# Halvorsen State University — Biosafety Incident Response Plan

Document HSU-EHS-BIO-01 · Office of Environmental Health and Safety (EHS) · Marlow Falls, Iowa · Revision 7, March 2026

## 1. Purpose and scope

This plan sets out how Halvorsen State University (HSU) responds to a suspected or confirmed exposure, spill, loss of containment or records discrepancy in any laboratory that works at Biosafety Level 1 or 2. It covers the four research buildings on the Marlow Falls campus: Kessler Hall, the Lindgren Life Sciences Building, the Aldous Veterinary Research Annex and the Prairie Genomics Center. HSU holds no select agents and no BSL-3 space. Any work that could involve a select agent or toxin is referred to the Institutional Official before it starts, and the Federal Select Agent Program reporting path in section 6 is kept only so that staff know what to do if a surprise occurs.

The plan covers what must be decided, by whom and by when. It does not describe how to handle any organism. Agent-specific handling is in each laboratory's approved protocol and biosafety manual.

## 3. Immediate actions at the bench (first 15 minutes)

1. Make the area safe and warn others in the room. Leave the laboratory if there is a spill outside the biosafety cabinet, and keep others out.
2. Wash the affected skin or flush the eyes for the time in the laboratory's protocol. Remove contaminated protective equipment.
3. Call Campus Police dispatch at ext. 5-0911 if anyone needs emergency medical care, then the BSO.
4. Post a "Do not enter: spill under evaluation" sign. Do not clean up a spill outside a cabinet until the BSO or the PI has agreed the plan.
5. Write down time, people present, what happened in plain words, and the container or sample identifier. Do not guess about causes.

## 4. Stop-work and medical evaluation

The BSO may order stop-work for the affected room, bench or procedure at any time, and the order stands until the BSO lifts it in writing. The target is a stop-work decision recorded within 30 minutes of the BSO receiving the report.

Any worker with a possible exposure is offered a medical evaluation. The target is that the worker is on the way to the Occupational Health Clinic, or has been telephoned by the clinic physician, within 60 minutes of the report. The worker may decline and the BSO records that. The clinic receives the protocol's agent information sheet from the PI; the BSO does not send a sample anywhere without the clinic physician's direction.

## 5. Classification

Within the first few hours the BSO, with the PI and the Director of Research Compliance, classifies the event. The choices are:

- **Reportable to NIH through the IBC.** Any significant problem, violation of the NIH Guidelines, or significant research-related accident or illness involving work covered by the Guidelines, including most exposures in laboratories that work with recombinant or synthetic nucleic acid molecules.
- **Internal incident, no external report.** A near miss or minor event without exposure that is reviewed by the BSO and logged.
- **Records discrepancy.** An inventory or log mismatch with no evidence of loss. Resolved by recount.
- **Reportable select agent incident.** Not expected at HSU; if any evidence points this way, the IO and the BSO treat it as immediate and go to section 6.

Where guidance conflicts, for example the PI's protocol says no medical follow-up is needed but the clinic physician advises evaluation, the more protective course is followed while the BSO resolves the disagreement with the IBC chair. The classification is recorded with its basis, and it is revisited if new facts arrive. A change of classification restarts the clocks that depend on it from the time of discovery, not from the time of the change.

## 6. Notification clocks

| Step | From | Due |
|---|---|---|
| PI reports to BSO | Discovery | 24 hours (target: within 2 hours for any exposure) |
| BSO reports to IBC chair | Receipt of the PI report | 3 calendar days |
| Notice to the sponsor | Discovery | 7 calendar days, or earlier if the award terms say so |
| IBC report to the NIH Office of Science Policy | Discovery | 30 days; earlier by telephone or e-mail when the event is serious |
| Select agent: immediate notice to the Federal Select Agent Program and law enforcement | Discovery | Immediately (exercise window 60 minutes) |
| Select agent: written report (Form 3 equivalent) | Discovery | 7 calendar days |

The Director of Research Compliance keeps the clock board and updates it each shift during an active incident.

## 7. Communications

University Communications speaks for HSU. PIs, students and laboratory staff refer all media and social media questions to ext. 5-0200. The first holding statement is cleared by the IO and the BSO within 3 hours of an event that draws outside attention. It names the facts known, says an evaluation is under way, and does not name the worker, the laboratory or the organism unless the IBC chair and the IO agree it is necessary for public health.

## 8. Records and review

The BSO opens an incident file for every report. Within 30 days the BSO holds a no-blame review with the laboratory, and the IBC receives a summary at its next meeting. Corrective actions are tracked to closure by EHS.

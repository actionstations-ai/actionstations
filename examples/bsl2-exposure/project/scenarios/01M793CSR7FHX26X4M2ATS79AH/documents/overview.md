# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Hairline Crack** (Centrifuge Failure, Exposure Clocks & Rumor)
Location: Lindgren Life Sciences Building, Halvorsen State University, Marlow Falls campus, Iowa · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Institutional Official (Vice President for Research), Biosafety Officer, IBC Chair, Principal Investigator, Director of Research Compliance and University Communications Director. The SimCell plays the clinic physician, the worker's advisor and lab mates, the sponsor program officer, NIH OSP, the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services, Marlow Falls Fire Department and reporters.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** The worker's health before the protocol, which deadline actually governs, deciding and recording what kind of event it is, and answering a "lab leak" claim without naming anyone.

**The Threat:** At 09:02 on Tuesday, 13 October 2026, before STARTEX, a tube cracked inside a centrifuge rotor in the shared centrifuge room on the third floor of the Lindgren Life Sciences Building. Graduate student Lena Park of the bacterial genetics laboratory may have had a splash to the face and a small cut to her gloved hand. The material is a Risk Group 2 culture covered by the laboratory's approved protocol. There was no attacker: the cause was an equipment failure. The failure stops work in a room that the bacterial genetics laboratory shares with a cell biology laboratory, and it starts the PI-to-BSO, IBC chair, sponsor and NIH notification clocks. That same morning a second laboratory finds a freezer inventory mismatch, and someone in the room raises select agents, even though HSU holds none.

**The Operational Impact:** The PI calls the Biosafety Officer at 09:20. The BSO must order stop-work in the shared centrifuge room within 30 minutes. That halts both laboratories' runs and leaves the cell biology group asking when it can get back in. The BSO must also send the worker for medical evaluation within 60 minutes. The approved protocol says no post-exposure prophylaxis is needed, but the Occupational Health physician wants to see her. The clinic closes at 17:00, and after hours she would have to go to Marlow Falls Regional Medical Center. Her advisor and lab mates call asking whether they are at risk. A federal sponsor's program officer says informally that no NIH report is needed. Meanwhile the Iowa Corn Growers Board award requires notice within 72 hours, which is shorter than HSU's 7 days. The Director of Research Compliance has to reconcile the clock board with that award term. The IBC chair must classify the event and write down the basis for the decision. In a second laboratory, the freezer count does not match the inventory records. That lab is stuck until someone confirms it is a records discrepancy and not an incident, and no select agent report is filed. At 11:30 a student posts a "lab leak" claim on the campus forum, and the student newspaper calls for comment. Communications needs a fact sheet from the BSO and a holding statement that names no worker, laboratory or organism.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Worker Care and Containment:** Demonstrate the ability to put the exposed graduate student's medical evaluation first by referring her to the Occupational Health Clinic within 60 minutes and before the 17:00 closing, with Marlow Falls Regional Medical Center as the after-hours fallback, while ordering and recording stop-work in the shared Lindgren third-floor centrifuge room within 30 minutes of the 09:20 call.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to classify the centrifuge tube failure in the IBC record, write down the basis for the classification, and separately confirm the second laboratory's freezer count mismatch as a records discrepancy without filing a select agent report, since HSU holds no select agents.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to reconcile the EHS portal clock board with the Iowa Corn Growers Board award's 72-hour notice term, state which deadline governs where the stricter rule applies, and set the PI-to-BSO, IBC chair, sponsor and NIH notifications as who, how and when, without relying on the federal program officer's informal advice.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to deliver the BSO's three-line fact sheet to University Communications within one hour and issue a holding statement and a single factual answer to the 11:30 campus-forum "lab leak" post and the student newspaper call, naming no worker, laboratory or organism and making no promise that nobody is at risk.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Institutional Official / Vice President for Research (IO):** Owns the institution's public posture, the decision with the IBC chair on whether anything about the organism is ever released, and the 72-hour telephone briefing to the Board of Regents' research committee chair.
- **Biosafety Officer (BSO):** Manages incident classification, containment of the shared centrifuge room, medical referral of the exposed worker, the incident file and the fact sheet for Communications.
- **Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC) chair (IBC):** Owns the classification of the event, the written basis for that classification, corrective actions, and what the committee reports to NIH and when.
- **Principal Investigator (PI):** Owns the first report to the BSO, stop-work in the bacterial genetics laboratory, accounting for materials, support for the student, and the corrective actions on the centrifuge.
- **Director of Research Compliance (RC):** Manages the EHS portal clock board, notices to sponsors and regulators, and the reconciliation of the Iowa Corn Growers Board award's 72-hour term with HSU's 7-day sponsor clock.
- **Director of University Communications (PIO):** Owns the holding statement, the one-time factual reply to the campus forum 'lab leak' post, the student newspaper inquiry and any use of HSUAlert.

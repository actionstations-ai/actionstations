# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Hairline Crack — Centrifuge Failure, Exposure Clocks & Rumor

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Lindgren Life Sciences Building, Halvorsen State University, Marlow Falls campus, Iowa · Duration: 4 Hours

### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise for the Halvorsen State University research safety leadership: the Institutional Official, the Biosafety Officer, the IBC chair, the Principal Investigator, the Director of Research Compliance and University Communications. Players talk through what they would decide, who they would call and what they would write down. Nobody touches real systems, real files or real people. The exercise runs 4 hours on a game clock that starts at 09:15 on Tuesday, 13 October 2026.

The scenario is an **infrastructure failure**: an equipment failure, not an attack. At 09:02 a tube cracks inside a centrifuge rotor in the shared third-floor centrifuge room of the Lindgren Life Sciences Building. A graduate student may have had a splash to the face and a small cut through her glove. The material is a Risk Group 2 culture under an approved protocol. The injects show how one broken tube spreads. The room is shared, so a stop-work order halts two laboratories and then the whole floor's bookings. The vendor cannot inspect until Friday. The PI-to-BSO, IBC chair, sponsor and NIH notification clocks start running. A fourth-floor freezer count mismatch and a "select agent" comment pull attention away. A "lab leak" rumor moves from a social post to the campus forum to a story in The Halvorsen Herald.

**Phase 1, The Cracked Tube (0:00–1:00, 09:15–10:15)**: putting the worker's medical evaluation first and recording containment. **Phase 2, Clocks and Crossed Signals (1:00–2:00)**: the spreading work stoppage, the Iowa Corn Growers Board 72-hour term set against HSU's 7-day default, and the freezer red herring. **Phase 3, Reopen or Hold (2:00–3:00)**: the signed decision on the shared room, the IBC classification and its written basis, and a public answer that names no worker, laboratory or organism. **Phase 4, Back in the Room (3:00–4:00)**: a phased, verified return to work, correcting the public record, and setting each notification as who, how and when against its governing deadline. Evaluators should watch whether:

- the student sees the physician before anyone talks about protocol
- the other people in the room (the badge log shows eight entries) get found and referred
- the stricter deadline is chosen and the reason is written down
- the program officer's informal advice is recorded but does not decide the NIH report
- players check the records before concluding anything about the freezer
- every decision has a name and a time next to it

**Objectives:**
- **Worker Care and Containment:** Demonstrate the ability to put the exposed graduate student's medical evaluation first by referring her to the Occupational Health Clinic within 60 minutes and before the 17:00 closing, with Marlow Falls Regional Medical Center as the after-hours fallback, while ordering and recording stop-work in the shared Lindgren third-floor centrifuge room within 30 minutes of the 09:20 call.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to classify the centrifuge tube failure in the IBC record, write down the basis for the classification, and separately confirm the second laboratory's freezer count mismatch as a records discrepancy without filing a select agent report, since HSU holds no select agents.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to reconcile the EHS portal clock board with the Iowa Corn Growers Board award's 72-hour notice term, state which deadline governs where the stricter rule applies, and set the PI-to-BSO, IBC chair, sponsor and NIH notifications as who, how and when, without relying on the federal program officer's informal advice.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to deliver the BSO's three-line fact sheet to University Communications within one hour and issue a holding statement and a single factual answer to the 11:30 campus-forum "lab leak" post and the student newspaper call, naming no worker, laboratory or organism and making no promise that nobody is at risk.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Arrange the room as islands that simulate the HSU incident room, which is the BSO's office acting as the institution's emergency operations center. Put one large table at the front for the incident core. Put satellite tables close to it so players can lean over and talk.
- **Front centre table (incident core):** Team B (Biosafety Officer) and Team C (IBC Chair). Put the ICS-201 / clock board whiteboard right behind them.
- **Front left table:** Team A (Institutional Official / VP for Research), next to the core. The IO approves statements, so they need to be within reach.
- **Front right table:** Team E (Director of Research Compliance), placed so they can see the clock board. They own it.
- **Side table near the door:** Team D (Principal Investigator). This represents the Lindgren third floor. Walk-in injects (the postdoc at the door, the front desk attendant) are delivered here.
- **Rear table, alone:** Team F (University Communications Director). Isolate the public-information role on purpose. In real life Communications is not in the BSO's office. It gets facts only when someone sends them. Communications has to walk to the core table and ask for the fact sheet, and the BSO has to remember to send it. That shows evaluators whether facts actually reach the PIO before the Herald's deadlines, or only after someone notices that Communications is missing.
- **SimCell / controller table:** At the side, out of the players' sight line, with the inject deck, phones and a clock showing game time.
- **Evaluator seats:** One evaluator behind the core table and one behind Team F. Each has an observation log.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and screen for main-screen injects: social posts, the forum thread, the Herald article, clock board screenshots.
- A whiteboard set up as the ICS-201 and clock board. Draw columns for step, due time, basis, who, how, status.
- A printed inject deck, in MSEL order, with one copy of each inject per addressed team. Mark every page "EXERCISE – EXERCISE – EXERCISE".
- Optional: simulated phones or radios so players can "call" the SimCell, and a desk phone labelled ext. 5-0142 at the BSO table.
- A wall clock or timer set to game time, starting at 09:15.

**Materials for players:** the situation manual, a role card for each team, the notification clock table (Biosafety document, section 6), the Communications document, sections 1 and 3, blank paper clearance sheets marked "Exercise worksheet, not the official form", pens and sticky notes.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role as SimCell**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff", you answer as the deputy. In this scenario that means the following:
- When a player says "I call the clinic", you are Dr. Mbeki.
- When they "email Prairieline", you are the service desk.
- When they "call the Corn Board", you are the Board office.
- When they "call CDC", you are the duty officer at the Federal Select Agent Program.

Answer in the first person, in that party's interest, with what that party would really know. If nobody plausible would know the answer, say "I'll have to get back to you" and bring it back 10–15 minutes later.

**Do not make it easy**
Real outside parties have their own priorities, deadlines and gaps. Do not hand players the right answer, and make them earn every concession. Here is an example from this scenario. At I-19 the cell biology postdoc calls: the second centrifuge threw an imbalance fault and the PI says rebalance and restart. Do not have the postdoc ask permission politely and then take "no" for an answer. Push back: "It's the Prairie Biologics deliverable, Thursday. It's a different machine. Who exactly is telling me no, and is that in writing?" The BSO has to give a clear, named instruction, record it and connect it to the vendor's written "do not run either centrifuge" (I-09). Likewise, Dr. Mbeki never clears the student by phone. The Board's program contact is out until Thursday, so players must find the office number.

**Managing time**
The game clock runs from 09:15 to 13:15. Use the time-jump cards (I-06, I-11, I-17) to keep the phases on schedule. If discussion stalls or runs long, announce it out loud:

> "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. It is now [time] on Tuesday, 13 October 2026. The stop-work sign is still on the centrifuge room door, the Herald's deadline is closer, and the clock board has not changed unless you changed it. RESUME."

After a jump, update the game clock on the wall. Remind Team E that anything they did not write on the clock board did not happen.

**Handling artificialities**
When a player says "I wouldn't use that system" or "we don't really do it that way", do not argue. Say:

> "Understood, note that for the hot wash. For today, assume it works the way the scenario describes and tell me what you would do next."

If the objection is about a real gap (for example, "nobody actually reads the award terms table"), have the evaluator record it as a finding.

**Delivering injects**
Do not explain an inject. Hand it over or put it on the screen. Say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." For phone injects, read the script in character and hang up when the character would. Do not answer "what does this mean?" questions about an inject. The players decide what it means.

**Exercise conventions**
- Start and end every simulated call, email or message to a real-sounding party with "This is an exercise." Mark all written products "EXERCISE".
- No real calls go to CDC, NIH, the Board, Prairieline or the press. All of them go to the SimCell.
- Do not name an organism. Use "Risk Group 2 material" only.
- If something real happens (a medical issue, a fire alarm, a real incident needing a participant), anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD" and play stops at once. Play resumes only when the lead controller says "Resume exercise."

  - Ask: "Prairieline: "Our records show the rotor inspection was due August 2026 and our reminder bounced off your address. Who at HSU is the contact on file now? I need that fixed before we take on any liability for that unit.""
  - Ask: "Prairieline: "Did anyone run either centrifuge after we told you in writing not to? If you did, I need the time and the error code for my file.""

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three that broke or failed during the response. Go around the room one team at a time (BSO and EHS, the laboratories, Research Compliance, Communications, Campus Police and leadership) and write every answer on the whiteboard in two columns before you discuss any of them.

**Key Discussion Questions for Hairline Crack:**
- **The Worker First:** At 09:30 (I-02), the cell biology group at the centrifuge room door asked to walk in for their tubes. They also told you that two people who were in the room when the tube cracked had heard nothing from anyone. Then at 10:55 (I-09b), the badge log showed eight entries and a visitor badge, while your list had three people. When did you stop trusting your list of who was exposed? Did Lena Park reach the Occupational Health Clinic within 60 minutes, with Marlow Falls Regional Medical Center as the after-hours fallback? Who followed up with the other people on the badge log, and when? _(Objective: Worker Care and Containment)_
- **Evidence or Ticket:** The 09:55 email (I-04) showed a rotor inspection two months overdue, a lid seated crooked and a film on the inside of the chamber window. Did you treat those facts as evidence for classifying the event in the IBC record, or as a maintenance ticket for Facilities? When Raj called at 11:50 (I-15) and accounted for the 14 vials as a paper transfer that never reached the portal, who confirmed in writing that it was a records discrepancy with no select agent report, given that HSU holds no select agents? Where did you write down the basis for each decision? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Clock:** The EHS portal clock board (I-08) showed the sponsor notice due in 7 days. The Award Terms tab in the same portal required notice to the Iowa Corn Growers Board within 72 hours of discovery at 09:02. When did someone notice that the board had never picked up that term? Who decided that the stricter deadline governs, and was that written down? Did the PI-to-BSO, IBC chair, sponsor and NIH notifications each get a named owner, a method and a time, or did the program officer's informal advice fill the gap? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Gap:** At 11:20 (I-12), the department head wanted a written choice between keeping the room closed until Friday (A) and reopening it that afternoon (B). Dr. Tomas Eklund cited a sponsor deadline, while the vendor had said not to run either machine and nobody yet knew why the tube cracked. Did the operations side understand what the technical team was telling them about the film and the uninspected rotor, and the other way round? Who signed the decision? Was the stop-work order from 09:20 recorded clearly enough to hold up under that pressure? _(Objective: Worker Care and Containment)_
- **Before You Knew:** The 'lab leak' post appeared at 11:30 (I-13). The Herald's 14:00 deadline followed, then the sergeant's offer of an HSUAlert at 11:53 (I-15). By 12:45 (I-21), the Herald article said 'the university did not respond.' What did HSU actually say publicly before you had confirmed facts, and when? Did the BSO's three-line fact sheet reach University Communications within the hour? What made you decide whether to send the HSUAlert? Did your holding statement avoid naming the worker, the laboratory or the organism, and avoid promising that nobody is at risk? _(Objective: Communication)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Lab safety**: Incident management for research laboratories: exposure and incident reports, notification matrices with clocks, inventory reconciliations, freezer alarms and access logs. Exercises concern what an organization decides and whom it tells. The toolkit carries guardrails that keep agent handling, production and modification detail out of every product.
  - Reference documents: 42 CFR part 73: Select Agents and Toxins (CFR 2025 edition); Biosafety in Microbiological and Biomedical Laboratories (BMBL), 6th edition; NIH Guidelines for Research Involving Recombinant or Synthetic Nucleic Acid Molecules; Incident Response Plan Guidance (Federal Select Agent Program)
- **Media environment**: The news cycle and social feeds around an incident: wire alerts, articles and broadcast segments, social posts whose reach and sentiment change over time, reporter calls and press conference questions, and verification tasks with provenance clues. Simulated spread and sentiment are teaching models, not predictions. All outlets, handles and people are fictional.
  - Data sources: gdelt
  - Reference documents: FCC report on the Hawaii false alert (2018): a correction case study

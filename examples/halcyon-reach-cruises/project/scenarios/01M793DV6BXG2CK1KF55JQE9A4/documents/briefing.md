# Player Briefing Packet
## Same Count, Every Channel — Norovirus Thresholds, Reporting & Turnaround

Location: MV Halcyon Dawn (Halcyon Reach Cruises), at sea between Cozumel, Mexico, and the Port of Galveston, Galveston, Galveston County, Texas · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 06:00 PM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Clear skies, wind east at 12 knots, seas 3 to 4 feet, 27 C. A mild February evening in the western Caribbean.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is Thursday, February 18, 2027, at 18:00 Central ship time. MV Halcyon Dawn, under Captain Anneliese Moreau, has just cleared Cozumel on schedule. This is day 5 of a seven-night western Caribbean voyage that left Galveston on Sunday, February 14. The ship is full, with 2,980 passengers and 1,120 crew as confirmed by the Purser at embarkation. Guests are coming back on board after a day ashore, and the dining rooms are opening for second seating. Tomorrow is a sea day with the formal-night gala, so Food and Beverage, Guest Services and the roughly 380 cabin stewards under the Chief Housekeeper are already planning around a heavy service day. Since Wednesday the ship has been running the Stage 1 enhanced cleaning and hygiene measures in HOPRPG 5 for a small number of stomach-illness reports. The medical center, led by Dr. Fiona Gallagher, is keeping its signed log as usual. The shoreside crisis team has not been stood up, and the 24-hour Fleet Operations Center is on its normal watch. The ship is due alongside in Galveston at 07:00 Sunday. The next voyage is planned to start boarding the same afternoon.

- Ship, bridge and navigation systems: GREEN: underway from Cozumel on schedule; ETA Galveston 07:00 Sunday, February 21
- Medical center and signed medical log: GREEN: 20 beds and 3 isolation rooms available; log is current and signed by the CMO
- Food service and hygiene measures: GREEN: dining rooms open for second seating; Stage 1 enhanced cleaning running since Wednesday; Friday gala on the schedule
- Housekeeping and steward staffing: GREEN: about 380 stewards on normal rotation; chlorine mixing station producing 1,000 ppm
- Satellite link to Fleet Operations Center: GREEN: phone link available 24 hours; duty officer on watch
- Guest Wi-Fi (third-party paid service): GREEN: operating normally

**The Anomaly:** **18:10 Thursday: routine paperwork from the medical center**

The Doctor on Duty sends the 18:00 medical log printout, signed by Dr. Fiona Gallagher, to the Chief Medical Officer and the Hotel Director (Raj Subramaniam). This is the regular end-of-afternoon update of reportable cases since embarkation, with passengers and crew counted separately.

A handwritten note from the Environmental Officer, Nikolai Petrenko, is clipped to the printout:

> "HD — public-area wipe-down log shows the 16:00 round on the Lido was not signed off. Are the 1% measures actually running everywhere?"

No one has raised anything else. Second seating is starting.

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep MV Halcyon Dawn running safely and normally for guests through the gala sea day and the Sunday turnaround in Galveston, while you investigate and resolve any illness reports under the ship's procedures. You must balance guest service against containment, and the speed of your reports and messages against checking them first. Every number you release must match the signed medical log on every channel.

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio call, phone call, satellite call to the Fleet Operations Center and PA rehearsal with "This is an exercise." Crew, guests or port contacts who overhear must never mistake a drill message for a real event on board.
- If a real emergency happens during play, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." The exercise stops at once and everyone switches to the real response.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to move the clock forward, for example from Thursday evening to the Galveston turnaround. Act on the situation as it stands at the new time, not where you left off.
- Treat every printed inject (medical log entries, cleaning records, emails, passenger posts, photos and reports) as real information. Act on what it says, and check it the way you would on a real voyage.

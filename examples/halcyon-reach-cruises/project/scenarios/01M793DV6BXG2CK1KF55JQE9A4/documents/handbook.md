## Same Count, Every Channel — Norovirus Thresholds, Reporting & Turnaround

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: MV Halcyon Dawn (Halcyon Reach Cruises), at sea between Cozumel, Mexico, and the Port of Galveston, Galveston, Galveston County, Texas · Duration: 4 Hours

### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise. It runs for four hours of exercise time starting at 18:00 and covers the outbreak of acute gastroenteritis on MV Halcyon Dawn, Halcyon Reach Cruises, from Thursday, February 18, 2027 (sailing from Cozumel) to the Sunday turnaround in Galveston. No equipment is moved and no real reports are sent. Players talk through decisions, notifications and handoffs using their own plans: HOPRPG 5 (stages of response), HR-HSP-02 5 and 6 (reports to the VSP, the quarantine station and other authorities), and HR-COM-03 7 and 8 (travel partners, next voyage and crew).

The scenario is an infrastructure failure. The injects show the ship's sanitation and hotel capacity giving way under a rising case count. The steward pool cannot cover isolation trays, the 2-hour public-area cycle and cabin turns at the same time. Isolation cabins, beds and gowns run out. The chlorine mixing station's dosing pump fails during the turnaround, and the terminal cannot finish its clean before boarding. Laid over these failures are reporting clocks timed against the 07:00 Sunday arrival, public pressure from false numbers on social media, and an unexplained death on board. **Phase 1 (0:00–1:00), The Count Climbs:** the count reaches 1 percent and then 2 percent, and the first special report is due. **Phase 2 (1:00–2:00), Every Channel, One Number:** a viral video, the lab result, the 24-hour report, the crew special report at 2 percent and the 3 percent outbreak report. **Phase 3 (2:00–3:00), Death and the Boarding Call:** the immediate death report, telling the family first, the 4-hour update, the VSP at the gangway and the 10:00 boarding decision. **Phase 4 (3:00–4:00), Turnaround Under Pressure:** cleaning order, the chlorine pump failure, the attestation and the notifications to all partners. Time jumps at 1:00, 2:00 and 3:00 move the story forward.

Evaluators should watch for the following:
- Every report and message uses the signed medical log entry and gives its time.
- Cumulative counts never go down. Watch for the Assistant Purser's suggestion to change the passenger total to 2,978, the galley chef's request for a separate "shore-acquired" column, and the draft 4-hour update that gives cases since the last report instead of cumulative cases.
- The lab confirmation does not change any count.
- Each report goes out on its clock: the special report within 1 hour at 2 percent and again at 3 percent, the 24-hour report in its window, the 4-hour update by 03:00 and the immediate death report.
- The family is told before anything is said in public.
- The boarding decision is made by 10:00.
- Nobody signs an attestation that the cleaning records cannot support.

**Objectives:**
- **Use of Doctrine (Reporting Clocks):** Demonstrate the ability to file the 2 percent and 3 percent special reports, the 24-hour and 4-hour pre-arrival reports and the immediate death report to the CDC Quarantine Station for Houston from the signed medical log, each timed against the 07:00 Sunday Galveston arrival and confirmed by the Director of Public Health and Vessel Sanitation before it is sent, as HOPRPG 8 and HR-HSP-02 require.
- **Containment:** Demonstrate the ability to carry out the HOPRPG 5 stage actions as passengers and crew pass 1, 2 and 3 percent: closing self-service, deciding on the show lounge matinee, the kids club shared craft and the formal-night gala buffet, and splitting about 380 stewards between meal-delivery routes, cabin work and cleaning public areas every 2 hours.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to give one signed count and time in the guest letter, family hotline script, media statement and crew notice, to answer the "300 sick" Lido video within 30 minutes with the count and no denial, and to answer the reporter's question about the death without guessing at the cause and only after the family has been told.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to decide by 10:00 Sunday whether the next voyage boards at 14:00 with an attestation, is delayed in 2-hour blocks, or is shortened or canceled, and to notify the 2,980 booked guests, travel agents, the Galveston terminal operator and the Galveston County Health District as HR-COM-03 7 requires.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Front island, Bridge and Medical Center (the ship):** Team A, Master (Captain), at the head. Team B, Chief Medical Officer, next to the Master with the printed medical log. Team C, Hotel Director, on the other side. These three are "on board" and talk face to face.
- **Side island, Fleet Operations Center (shoreside):** Team D, VP Fleet Operations (shoreside crisis lead), and Team E, Director of Public Health and Vessel Sanitation. Put this island at least 4 m from the ship island. Ship-to-shore contact goes by "satellite phone" (a SimCell handset or a runner), as HR-ORG-04 3 describes, not by talking across the room.
- **Rear table, Communications:** Team F, VP Communications, sits alone at the back of the room. COMMS must walk to the ship or shoreside island to get the signed count and its time. This is deliberate. In real life the communications team gets facts second-hand, and the exercise tests whether the media statement, hotline script, crew notice and guest messages carry the same signed number. If COMMS can overhear the medical log, that test does not work.
- **SimCell table:** at the side wall near the door, with phones and the inject deck, so the controller can reach any island fast. Evaluators sit behind each island with a clear view of the whiteboard.
- **Main screen:** at the front, visible from all islands. Social media posts, time-jump cards and the STARTEX and ENDEX cards appear here.

**Equipment needs**
- Projector and screen for main-screen injects (I-02b, I-05, I-17b and the time-jump cards).
- Whiteboard with an ICS-201 incident briefing layout: situation, signed count with time, report clock against the 07:00 arrival, resources (beds, isolation cabins, stewards, gowns, chlorine) and actions. Also a column for the boarding options.
- Printed inject deck in MSEL order, one copy for the controller, with spare copies of the medical log products to hand out.
- Optional: simulated radios or phones, one per island plus the SimCell, labelled "Ship-Shore Sat Link", "Hotline" and "Media Line".
- Wall clock showing exercise time, and a second card showing story time (ship time, Central).

**Materials for players:** HOPRPG 5 stage table, HR-HSP-02 5 and 6, HR-COM-03 7 and 8, HR-ORG-04 3 to 5 (officers, voyage table, outside parties), blank report forms (24-hour, 4-hour, special), the guest letter and hotline script templates, and name tents by team.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**The SimCell role**

You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the VSP", you answer as the VSP duty officer. If they say "I call the terminal", you are the Gulf Terminal Services duty manager. The same goes for the lab, the son of Guest A, the Gulf 8 assignment desk and the Bahamas Maritime Authority. Answer in the first person, in character, and briefly. If players call someone with no script, make the call short and realistic, and have that person ask for the one thing they would need.

**Do not make it easy**

Real partners do not fill gaps for players. Example from this scenario: at I-03 the doctor on duty logs passenger case 60 at 05:10, but the entry is "Awaiting CMO signature." If the CMO or Public Health phones the VSP with "about 60", answer as the VSP: "Is that from your signed log? What time was it signed? I need passengers and crew separately, cumulative since embarkation, and the written special report after this call." Do not accept a number that has not been signed. In the same way, if COMMS asks you (as the Gulf 8 desk) to "hold off until we call back", say: "We go live at noon. If I don't have something by 10:30, the story runs with the passenger's 300."

**Managing Time**

Read time-jump cards aloud, word for word, from the front:

"PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down for a moment. We are jumping forward. On the exercise clock it is now [19:00 / 20:00 / 21:00]. In the story it is [Friday 09:00 / Saturday 14:45 / Sunday 10:30] Central ship time. Whatever you decided before the pause is now in force. Here is what has changed… [read the card]. RESUME."

If a team is stuck, give a short warning ("Five minutes of story time until the 10:00 call") rather than solving the problem for them. If the room falls behind, drop the NOISE injects (I-01b, I-11c, I-18b) before any CORE_NARRATIVE inject.

**Handling artificialities**

If a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "We'd never be in that position", say:

"Noted. Write it down for the hot wash. For today, the system works the way the card describes. Tell me what you would do with it."

Do not argue about realism during play.

**Delivering injects**

Do not explain an inject. Hand it over and say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." Read phone calls in character. Do not hint at the right answer or say why the inject matters. If a player asks "What does this mean?", answer only in the voice of the sender.

**Conventions**

- Start and end every simulated outside call with "This is an exercise." No real call, email or post goes to any real agency, partner or media outlet.
- If anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD", all play stops at once. Deal with the real event, such as a medical issue or a real message, and resume only when the controller says "RESUME EXERCISE."

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** 3-Up, 3-Down: ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the exercise, so everyone sees both strengths and gaps before memories fade. Go around the room team by team (bridge, medical, hotel/F&B, communications, shoreside) and write each Up and Down on the whiteboard in that team's own words. Don't debate them until every team has spoken.

**Key Discussion Questions for Same Count, Every Channel:**
- **The Clock:** Passengers crossed 2 percent at about 05:10 Friday (case 60), crew crossed it at 22:00 Friday, and passengers hit 3 percent at 08:30 Saturday. Policy in HR-HSP-02 5 is a phone call plus a written special report within 1 hour of the count that reached the threshold. When did you actually see each crossing in the signed medical log, and when did each special report leave with the Director of Public Health's confirmation? You had the 24:00 log showing 52 cases in I-02. What would have let you see the 05:10 crossing coming instead of finding it afterwards? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine (Reporting Clocks))_
- **The Death:** At 15:00 Saturday a guest in her eighties with a cardiac history died in the medical center, and you did not know the cause. Under HR-HSP-02 5, the Master reports any death in the 15 days before arrival to the Houston quarantine station right away. Did you file it right away or wait for a cause? Who was told first: the family, the CDC, or the reporter? When the press asked, did anyone link the death to norovirus or rule it out before there was any evidence? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **One Count:** At 02:14 Friday the Roll Call post (I-02b) claimed 'over 100 locked in their cabins' and a cover-up, and a travel agent with 22 clients on the next sailing was following it. Later the '300 sick' Lido video gave you 30 minutes to respond. Did the guest letter, family hotline script, media statement and crew notice all quote the same signed count and time? Did any of them deny the outbreak or soften it? What did you tell the public before the 14:00 Friday lab result, and did it change once norovirus was confirmed? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **What Gives:** Over the 23:50 field report (I-02) and the 11:30 gala staffing memo (I-06), the hotel team kept asking the same thing: 380 stewards can't cover isolation trays, the 2-hour public-area cycle, cabin turns and a 64-server gala all at once. Gowns were down to 31 percent. Did you treat those messages as evidence that containment was failing, or as a staffing ticket to clear? What did you decide for the self-service buffet, the kids club craft, the matinee and the gala, and who owned the decision on what to drop? _(Objective: Containment)_
- **The Call:** At 08:50 Sunday (I-14) you had a full clean ending around 18:30, a 14:00 boarding that meant attesting on about 45 percent of cabins cleaned, and a terminal operator who needed an answer by 09:45, earlier than your 10:00 call. Which option did you pick: board with an attestation, delay in 2-hour blocks, shorten or cancel? Did the 2,980 booked guests, 318 agents, the terminal and the Galveston County Health District hear it from you before the news? Looking back, which input from Friday or Saturday would have let you make this call a day earlier? _(Objective: Decision Making)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Outbreak at sea**: Gastroenteritis outbreak response for ships and other congregate settings: the cumulative illness report and its 24-hour, 4-hour and 2 and 3 percent report points, the response checklist, guest letters, the port health notification and the boarding decision for the next voyage. It reuses the epidemiology toolkit for curves and case data and stays at reporting, isolation, cleaning and communication decisions, with no medical advice beyond the cited guidance.
  - Reference documents: Vessel Sanitation Program 2025 Environmental Public Health Standards (CDC); 42 CFR 71.21 Report of death or illness
- **Guest emergency**: Emergencies that involve guests away from home on a ship or in a property: muster and accounting, a person overboard, an alleged serious crime, a tower evacuation read against the in-house list, hurricane departures and shelter in place, a guest death or serious injury, a hotline script, and an outage of the property management system. Stays at coordination, accounting and notification; it carries no tactical or medical detail.
  - Reference documents: 46 U.S.C. 3507 Passenger vessel security and safety requirements

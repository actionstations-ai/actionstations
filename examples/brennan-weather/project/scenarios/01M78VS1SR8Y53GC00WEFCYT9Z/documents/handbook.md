# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Rain-Wrapped — Night Tornado Warning & Shelter Timing

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Brennan County EOC, Brennan County Public Safety Building, 900 Industrial Parkway, Ashford, Brennan County, Kentucky (alternate EOC: Corwin Fire Station 3) · Duration: 4 Hours

### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise for Brennan County Emergency Management Agency (BCEMA), Brennan County Communications (911), the fire districts and Sheriff's Office field command, and Channel 6 StormTeam. It covers four hours of exercise time, from 21:30 CST Friday, December 11, 2026 to 01:30 Saturday, and it is played in four one-hour phases. Players talk through decisions. Nothing is activated for real. No real siren, BrennanAlert, IPAWS/WEA or broadcast system is touched, and every product in the inject deck is marked EXERCISE. Radar and correlation-coefficient figures are simulated case data, and the county UGC code (KYC901) is fictional.

The injects simulate a natural hazard: a fast-moving December line of storms (QLCS) with rain-wrapped tornadoes embedded in it, crossing at night under a PDS Tornado Watch. **Phase 1, Under the PDS Watch (0:00–1:00, 21:30–22:30)**, holds the exercise's only true decision point. Players must decide whether to unlock the Ashford Middle School and Corwin Community Center safe rooms and move about 1,100 Clarks River manufactured-home residents into sturdy buildings before any warning, knowing sirens N-07 and N-11 are dead. **Phase 2, Rain-Wrapped Impact (1:00–2:00, 22:30–23:30)**, runs the 22:41 tornado warning, the upgrade to OBSERVED, the tornado emergency for Corwin, two worksite calls, a viral "locked shelter" rumor and Channel 6's move to its basement backup set. **Phase 3, Darkness and Entrapments (2:00–3:00, 23:30–00:30)**, stretches a two-seat 911 floor with entrapments at Corwin Wax & Candle and Pine Hollow, offers of mutual aid, and cell towers failing that cut off WEA, BrennanAlert and the spotter relay. **Phase 4, Full Activation and Recovery (3:00–4:00, 00:30–01:30)**, moves the EOC to full activation for damage assessment, the Red Cross shelter at Brennan County High School (which has no power), state resource requests, a generator failure at the Public Safety Building and support for the NWS Paducah storm survey.

Evaluators should watch for these behaviors:
- **Safe rooms:** whether the safe-room decision is made by about 22:00, so the doors are unlocked before the line arrives at 22:40.
- **Sirens:** whether 911 activates the zones inside the polygon at once under BWN 3, without waiting, and logs each activation and the N-07/N-11 failures.
- **WEA:** whether the Warning Officer refuses to send a county WEA that repeats the NWS warning, and finds another way to reach Pine Hollow (for example the Sheriff's PA).
- **Rumors and broadcast:** whether the rumor is verified before anyone repeats it, and whether Channel 6 stays consistent with NWS and county messages under CSSWCP 4.
- **Casualty figures:** whether death counts come only from the coroner through the county.
- **Mutual aid:** whether arriving units get a staging location, a contact and a radio channel.
- **Activation and recovery:** whether the EOC moves to Level 3 at the right time, hands management to the Deputy and sets up the after-storm work in EOP 3.4: damage assessment, utility counts every hour, one county message at least every two hours, and access for the NWS survey.

Note the moment each behavior happens, not just whether it happened.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to decide, ahead of any NWS Paducah tornado warning, to unlock the Ashford Middle School (20 minutes) and Corwin Community Center (15 minutes) safe rooms and to tell the roughly 1,100 residents of the Pine Hollow, Riverbend and Shady Acres manufactured home parks to move to a sturdy building, while the PDS tornado watch is in effect and the line is coming at 60 mph.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to carry out BWN 3 and the Level 3 triggers in EOP 3.1 when the 22:41 tornado warning arrives: sound the North zone sirens inside the polygon from Position 2 within one minute, log and work around failed sirens N-07 and N-11, and send BrennanAlert without sending a county WEA that repeats the NWS tornado warning.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to keep the county's public messages and Channel 6 StormTeam's coverage from its basement backup set matched to the NWS warning, the OBSERVED upgrade and the Corwin tornado emergency, and to check and correct the viral claim that the Corwin safe room is locked through the Warning Officer–broadcast contact named in CSSWCP 4.
- **Operational Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to prioritize and dispatch to entrapment calls at Corwin Wax & Candle and the Pine Hollow park with two telecommunicators and one supervisor as battery-powered cell towers fail, and then to move to full EOC activation for damage assessment, the Red Cross shelter request for Brennan County High School, the Kentucky Emergency Management resource requests and access for the NWS Paducah storm survey.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Set up islands that copy the overnight operations floor: an EOC cluster, a 911 console pod, a field command post and a broadcast desk. Put the main screen at the front where every island can see it. Put the facilitator/SimCell table at the front side with a clear line to every island.
- **Team A (EM Director / EOC Command):** the center island facing the screen, next to the whiteboard holding the ICS-201.
- **Team B (Warning Officer):** at the EOC island beside Team A, with the printed BWN 3 siren table and the warning log sheet in front of them.
- **Team D (911 Supervisor):** a separate pod a few steps from the EOC island, with two empty "Position 1 / Position 2" seats marked by place cards. The empty seats show how thin the overnight floor is. The supervisor has to choose what to answer.
- **Team E (Fire / Sheriff Field Operations):** a side table away from the EOC, the "field." They talk to the EOC by radio (or a passed message card) only, never by leaning across.
- **Team F (Channel 6 StormTeam):** its own table on the opposite side. In Phase 2, after I-11, move them to a corner with one chair and one laptop to stand for the basement backup set.
- **Team C (Public Information Officer):** a rear table, behind everyone, facing away from the screen. The PIO has to walk to the EOC island or the SimCell to get facts. **Why:** in a real activation, the PIO gets information second-hand and late. Isolating the role shows whether the EOC pushes verified facts out (the safe-room status, the siren failures, the official stance on casualties) or leaves the PIO to guess while Channel 6 and River Valley News 12 are pushing on deadlines. Each trip shows evaluators the flow of information.
- **Evaluators:** one standing evaluator per two islands, with a clear view and a timeline sheet.

**Equipment needs**
- Projector and screen for main-screen injects: NWSChat posts, Weather Wire products, siren poll and BrennanAlert console summaries, KPAH simulated radar figures, CAD board.
- Whiteboard (or flip chart) for the ICS-201, plus a second board for a running timeline of warning times, siren activations and messages sent.
- Printed inject deck in MSEL order, with phone slips and message cards for each team. Have spares for the time-jump cards (I-06, I-12, I-17).
- Optional: simulated radios or phones (walkie-talkies labeled "Fire dispatch," "SKYWARN 147.315" and "EOC line") and a desk bell or timer for the 911 ring-down.
- Wall clock set to exercise time, adjusted at each time jump.

**Materials for players:** BWN 2–3 (warning sources and siren table), EOP 3.4 and EOP 5 extracts, CSSWCP 4 (on-air rules), BCFPWS 6 partner list, Brennan County zone map with siren locations, blank ICS-201/213 forms, warning log sheet, pens and sticky notes.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role as SimCell**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the deputy. If they say "I post in pahchat," you answer as NWS Paducah. If they call McCracken County EM, KYEM Area 1, the Red Cross, River Valley News 12, the Corwin city clerk or a frantic plant supervisor, that is you too. Answer in the first person, in the character's voice and in the character's interest. Keep a name card for each character so you switch cleanly. If a player calls someone you have no script for, improvise from what that person would plausibly know and want, and never give them more than they would really have.

**Do not make it easy**
Real partners have their own priorities, missing information and deadlines. Do not hand players the answer they want. Make them earn it with the right action.
- **Example from this scenario (I-05, 22:10 CST):** the Ridgeline Distribution night supervisor asks, "If you tell me it's going to miss us, I keep running." Do not let the EOC get away with "it'll probably miss you." Push: "So you're telling me it misses us? You'll put that in writing?" A good answer gives no forecast and points to sheltering in place before the 22:30 break, citing the PDS watch and NWS Paducah's ~22:40 ± 10 minute estimate.
- **Second example (I-10):** the Channel 6 digital producer gets the anchor's "locked shelter" line on air in three minutes unless the county gives a confirmed status from someone who is actually at the Corwin Community Center doors.

**Managing time**
Run the clock hard. At each time jump (I-06, I-12, I-17), stand up, wait for quiet, and read:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down for a moment. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. It is now [time] CST. Here is what has changed: [read the time-jump card]. Decisions you made before the pause stand. Anything you didn't decide did not happen. RESUME."

Change the wall clock in front of them. If a team is stuck, give them one more minute, then say, "Time is up. The storm didn't wait. What do you do now?" Record any decision left unmade for the hot wash.

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says, "I wouldn't use that system" or "In real life we'd just do X," answer:
> "Noted. Write it on a card for the hot wash. For today, assume the system works as described in the inject and keep playing."

If they object that the radar figure "doesn't look right," remind them it is simulated case data. If they ask for a real county code, the exercise code KYC901 is fictional on purpose. Do not argue about realism during play.

**Delivering injects**
Do not explain. Do not hint at the right answer. Hand over the card or put it on screen and say only "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." For phone calls, pick up a phone and speak as the caller. For radio traffic, key a radio or read it aloud as the unit, with the background noise in your voice. If a team asks "What does this mean?", answer in character ("I'm just the clerk, ma'am, I'm reading the screen") or say "What would you do with it?"

**Conventions**
- **"This is an exercise."** Every inject, product and simulated message carries EXERCISE markings. Players open and close every simulated radio or phone message with "This is an exercise." No one sends anything on a real console, NWSChat room, IPAWS, BrennanAlert, social media account or broadcast channel. If the exercise is held in the real EOC or 911 center, cover or lock live consoles before STARTEX.
- **"REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD."** Anyone with a real emergency (a medical problem in the room, a real warning for the area, a live 911 surge) says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD" twice, loudly. All play stops at once. The facilitator handles the real event and restarts play only with "RESUME EXERCISE." Players on the real duty roster may leave the room without asking.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** 3-Up, 3-Down: ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed. Go around the room one team at a time (Warning Officer/EOC, 911 floor, fire/sheriff, PIO/broadcast liaison, NWS liaison), write every answer on the whiteboard in two columns, and don't debate an item until each team has had its turn.

**Key Discussion Questions for Rain-Wrapped:**
- **The Call:** The 20:15 PDS tornado watch and NWS Paducah's 22:00–02:00 window for embedded tornadoes were in hand well before the 22:41 tornado warning. When did you decide to unlock the Ashford Middle School safe room (20 minutes) and the Corwin Community Center safe room (15 minutes), and to tell the roughly 1,100 people in Pine Hollow, Riverbend and Shady Acres to move to a sturdy building? With the line moving at 60 mph, what were you waiting for? Would an earlier call with NWS Paducah or a pre-set watch-stage trigger have let you decide faster? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Gap:** When the 22:41 RADAR INDICATED / CONSIDERABLE tornado warning arrived, did Position 2 sound the North zone sirens inside the polygon within one minute, as BWN 3 and EOP 3.1 require? How did you log and cover the gap left by failed sirens N-07 and N-11? Who in that area was asleep and not reached? Did BrennanAlert go out without a county WEA repeating the NWS warning? Walk us through where the procedure helped you and where it slowed you down. _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **Trust:** At 22:46 (I-08), spotter Brennan-14 reported a rain-wrapped tornado with debris crossing the Clarks River near Pine Hollow. One minute later the severe weather statement upgraded to OBSERVED, and the room saw a correlation-coefficient debris signature sitting on the couplet. Did you treat the spotter report and the debris signature as evidence to act on right away, or did you wait for the product to arrive like a ticket? Did the operations side understand what the CC figure meant, and did the technical side explain it in terms of who needed to take shelter in Corwin? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The Message:** What did you tell the public before you knew for sure, and was it the same thing Channel 6 StormTeam was saying from its basement backup set as the warning moved to OBSERVED and then to the Corwin tornado emergency? When the viral claim spread that the Corwin safe room was locked, how quickly did you check and correct it through the Warning Officer–broadcast contact in CSSWCP 4? At 00:10 (I-15b), about 35 Riverbend residents were standing at a dark, locked church. What in your earlier messages sent them there, and how did you reach people who had no working phone? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **Two Seats:** At 23:35 (I-13) you had two telecommunicators, 9 calls holding and 4 abandoned. Corwin Wax & Candle had 24 people unaccounted for and a gas smell. The Pine Hollow lot 14 caller had a baby trapped and the line had dropped. Marshall County offered rollover but warned that its call takers didn't know your lot numbers or back roads. Did you accept, and why? How did you rank the two entrapments, the Shady Acres oxygen patient and the Corwin crew blocked by fallen oaks? At what moment did you move to full EOC activation for damage assessment, the Red Cross shelter at Brennan County High School, Kentucky Emergency Management resource requests and access for the NWS storm survey, and what would have let you make that move sooner? _(Objective: Operational Coordination)_

Used by a forecast office exercising its warnings, and by an airport, a utility, a venue or a school district that needs a storm to happen to it.
  - Data sources: nexrad-level2, spc-storm-reports, iem-warnings, iem-soundings, nhc-hurdat2

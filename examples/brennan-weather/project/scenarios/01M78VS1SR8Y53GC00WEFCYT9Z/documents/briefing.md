# Player Briefing Packet
## Rain-Wrapped — Night Tornado Warning & Shelter Timing

Location: Brennan County EOC, Brennan County Public Safety Building, 900 Industrial Parkway, Ashford, Brennan County, Kentucky (alternate EOC: Corwin Fire Station 3) · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 09:30 PM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Ashford: 71°F, dewpoint 66°F, south wind 20 mph gusting to 35 mph, light rain showers. Very warm and humid for a December night in western Kentucky.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is Friday night, December 11, 2026, and the Brennan County Emergency Management Agency EOC in the Brennan County Public Safety Building in Ashford is at **Level 2, partial activation** under EOP 3.1. The Storm Prediction Center's 3:00 PM Day 1 outlook puts the county in a **Moderate (4) risk**, with significant tornadoes possible after dark. At 8:15 PM, SPC issued a **Particularly Dangerous Situation tornado watch** for the county, valid until 4:00 AM. NWS Paducah's afternoon briefing called for a fast-moving line between 10:00 PM and 2:00 AM. In the EOC are EM Director Dana Whitfield, the on-duty Warning Officer and PIO Caleb Harmon. The NWSChat, WebEOC and BrennanAlert consoles are logged in, and the spotter net is open on the county VHF repeater. Across the building, the Brennan County Communications (911) overnight floor has its normal night crew of two telecommunicators and one supervisor, and call volume is routine. Fire districts and the Brennan County Sheriff's Office are on normal Friday-night patrol. On Channel 6, StormTeam Chief Meteorologist Marisa Kent is doing cut-ins between programs. No warning is in effect for Brennan County. Neither community safe room (Ashford Middle School, Corwin Community Center) is open. Nobody has reported an outage. It is a busy but orderly watch night, and the room is waiting for the next update from Paducah.

- BCEMA EOC (Public Safety Building, Ashford): GREEN: Level 2 partial activation per EOP 3.1. Generator tested, satellite phone checked. Alternate EOC (Corwin Fire Station 3) available.
- NWS Paducah feeds: KPAH WSR-88D, NOAA Weather Wire, NWSChat (pahchat): GREEN: all feeds current. No warnings in effect for Brennan County. PDS tornado watch in effect until 4:00 AM.
- Outdoor warning sirens and Position 2 / EOC encoders: GREEN: no faults reported. Encoders at 911 Position 2 and the EOC are online.
- BrennanAlert, county WEA via IPAWS, and battery-backed cell towers: GREEN: BrennanAlert console logged in, IPAWS connection normal, commercial power on all towers. No outages reported.
- Safe rooms: Ashford Middle School (FEMA P-361, 300) and Corwin Community Center: GREEN / CLOSED: normal night status. Locked and unstaffed until the county decides to open them.
- Overnight 911 floor, CAD, and Channel 6 StormTeam: GREEN: two telecommunicators and one supervisor, CAD normal, failover to Marshall County 911 available. Channel 6 on main studio power, doing cut-ins. Public mood calm.

**The Anomaly:** **9:35 PM CST: NWSChat, pahchat partner room**

The NWS Paducah decision support Meteorologist posts a routine IDSS update for Brennan County partners. It shows up on the EOC NWSChat console and on the 911 supervisor's screen:

> *** EXERCISE EXERCISE EXERCISE — NOT A REAL PRODUCT *** IDSS UPDATE for Brennan County partners. The QLCS is crossing the Tennessee River west of your county now. Line motion is NE at 60 mph (52 kt). KPAH radar loops (SIMULATED CASE DATA) show 2–3 couplets along the leading edge. Embedded, rain-wrapped tornadoes are LIKELY 22:00–02:00. Best estimate for the leading edge reaching northern Brennan County is ~22:40, give or take 10 minutes. Expect short warning lead times of 5–10 minutes or less on spin-ups. Anyone in manufactured homes should be in a sturdy building BEFORE the line arrives. We remain under PDS Tornado Watch until 04:00. *** EXERCISE ***

No warning has been issued. All of your systems still show normal. The line is about an hour away.

**YOUR MISSION:** Maintain timely, accurate warning, sheltering and emergency response for every person in Brennan County through the night, while investigating and resolving each problem that threatens those protective actions. You must balance **lead time against certainty**: by the time a warning confirms the threat, it is often too late to open shelters or move people. You must also balance **speed against verification**: every public message has to be fast and match the NWS product, and no rumor gets confirmed or denied until someone has checked it.

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio call, phone call, NWSChat post and BrennanAlert test with "This is an exercise." This room sits next to a live 911 floor and live weather channels, and a message without that tag could be taken for a real tornado alert and start real-world panic.
- If a real emergency happens, including real severe weather, a real 911 surge or a medical problem in the room, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." The exercise stops immediately so everyone can go back to their real duties.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]." When that happens, skip ahead to that time, assume routine work in between was done, and pick up the decisions in front of you then.
- Treat every printed inject (NWSChat posts, NWS products, emails, voicemails, social media posts, logs, photos and field reports) as real intelligence, and verify and act on it as you would on a real night. All radar and storm data in this exercise is simulated or historical case data, never a live feed.

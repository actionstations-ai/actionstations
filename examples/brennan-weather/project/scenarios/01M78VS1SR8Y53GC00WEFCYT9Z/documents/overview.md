# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Rain-Wrapped** (Night Tornado Warning & Shelter Timing)
Location: Brennan County EOC, Brennan County Public Safety Building, 900 Industrial Parkway, Ashford, Brennan County, Kentucky (alternate EOC: Corwin Fire Station 3) · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: EM Director / EOC Command, Warning Officer, Public Information Officer, 911 Supervisor, Fire / Sheriff Field Operations, and Channel 6 StormTeam (Broadcast Meteorologist and News Director). The SimCell plays NWS Paducah, Kentucky Emergency Management, neighboring counties, the American Red Cross, the employers, reporters and residents.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Acting before the warning, waking people who are asleep, and keeping county and broadcast messages the same as each power source, siren and cell tower fails.

**The Threat:** On an unusually warm, humid Friday night in mid-December, a fast-moving line of storms with tornadoes embedded in it crosses Brennan County. Before STARTEX, the Storm Prediction Center's 15:00 CST Day 1 outlook put the county in a Moderate (4) risk with significant tornadoes possible after dark. NWS Paducah's afternoon briefing warned of embedded tornadoes between 22:00 and 02:00, and at 20:15 a Particularly Dangerous Situation (PDS) tornado watch was issued until 04:00. At 22:41, NWS Paducah issues a tornado warning (RADAR INDICATED, CONSIDERABLE) for northern Brennan County, with the storm moving northeast at 60 mph. A spotter report near Corwin brings an upgrade to OBSERVED, and NWS Paducah later issues a tornado emergency for Corwin. The storms hit outdoor sirens (North zone sirens N-07 and N-11 are already out of service), BrennanAlert, Channel 6 StormTeam's studio, cell towers and the overnight 911 floor.

**The Operational Impact:** At 21:30 CST, the BCEMA EOC is at Level 2 with EM Director Dana Whitfield, the Warning Officer and PIO Caleb Harmon. Channel 6 StormTeam's Chief Meteorologist Marisa Kent is on air. Neither safe room is open. The Ashford Middle School safe room takes 20 minutes to unlock at night and the Corwin Community Center 15 minutes, so the opening decision has to come well before the line arrives. About 1,100 residents of the Clarks River manufactured home parks (Pine Hollow, Riverbend, Shady Acres) must be told to move to a sturdy building before any warning. Only Pine Hollow has a shelter, and it holds 60. The Pine Hollow siren (N-07) and the Clarks River bridge siren (N-11) will not sound.

When the 22:41 warning comes, 911 must activate the sirens in the zones inside the polygon within one minute. The Warning Officer sends BrennanAlert, but the voice calls take 12 minutes to finish against a storm moving at 60 mph. The county must not send its own WEA that repeats the NWS tornado warning. Ridgeline Distribution (310 workers on the overnight shift) and Corwin Wax & Candle (95 workers, no refuge area on the production floor) call to ask whether to shelter. A viral post claims the Corwin safe room is locked. Channel 6 loses studio power and moves to its basement backup set, and its meteorologist must stay consistent with the NWS warning while checking the rumor with the county.

After midnight, cell towers running on battery start to fail, which cuts off WEA, BrennanAlert and the spotter relay for parts of the county. The overnight 911 floor has two telecommunicators and one supervisor. It takes entrapment calls from the candle plant and the Pine Hollow park while fire and sheriff crews work in debris and darkness. Later, the EOC moves to full activation and must handle damage assessment, the Red Cross shelter request for Brennan County High School, resource requests to Kentucky Emergency Management, and support for NWS Paducah's storm survey.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to decide, ahead of any NWS Paducah tornado warning, to unlock the Ashford Middle School (20 minutes) and Corwin Community Center (15 minutes) safe rooms and to tell the roughly 1,100 residents of the Pine Hollow, Riverbend and Shady Acres manufactured home parks to move to a sturdy building, while the PDS tornado watch is in effect and the line is coming at 60 mph.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to carry out BWN 3 and the Level 3 triggers in EOP 3.1 when the 22:41 tornado warning arrives: sound the North zone sirens inside the polygon from Position 2 within one minute, log and work around failed sirens N-07 and N-11, and send BrennanAlert without sending a county WEA that repeats the NWS tornado warning.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to keep the county's public messages and Channel 6 StormTeam's coverage from its basement backup set matched to the NWS warning, the OBSERVED upgrade and the Corwin tornado emergency, and to check and correct the viral claim that the Corwin safe room is locked through the Warning Officer–broadcast contact named in CSSWCP 4.
- **Operational Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to prioritize and dispatch to entrapment calls at Corwin Wax & Candle and the Pine Hollow park with two telecommunicators and one supervisor as battery-powered cell towers fail, and then to move to full EOC activation for damage assessment, the Red Cross shelter request for Brennan County High School, the Kentucky Emergency Management resource requests and access for the NWS Paducah storm survey.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **EM Director (EOC Command) (EMD):** Owns EOC activation level, the safe room opening decision, any countywide siren activation outside NWS warnings, and requests for state assistance through the KYEM Area 1 Manager.
- **Warning Officer (WO):** Manages NWS product monitoring, siren activation confirmation, BrennanAlert and county WEA messages, and the warning log for the county.
- **Public Information Officer (PIO):** Owns the county's public messages, coordination with Channel 6 StormTeam and other media, and rumor monitoring on social media.
- **Brennan County Communications (911) Supervisor (SUP):** Manages the overnight 911 floor, siren activation from Position 2, spotter-triggered siren decisions and the triage of damage, injury and entrapment calls.
- **Field Incident Commander (Fire / Sheriff Field Operations) (FIC):** Manages field response, search and rescue, and scene security by the fire districts and the Sheriff's Office under NIMS incident command.
- **Broadcast Meteorologist on Duty (Channel 6 StormTeam) (CH6):** Owns Channel 6 StormTeam's wall-to-wall and tornado emergency coverage, the move to the basement backup set, and on-air consistency with NWS warnings and county messages.

# Brennan County Emergency Operations Plan — Annex H: Severe Weather and Tornado

Brennan County Emergency Management Agency (BCEMA) · Ashford, Kentucky · Revision 4, adopted by Brennan County Fiscal Court · Reviewed annually after the spring severe weather season

## 1. Purpose and scope

This annex describes how Brennan County prepares for, warns of, responds to and begins recovery from severe thunderstorms and tornadoes. It applies to every county department, the City of Ashford, the City of Corwin, the fire districts, the Brennan County Sheriff's Office and the volunteer organizations that sign the county's memorandum of understanding. It supports the Basic Plan and the Warning and Notification Standard Operating Procedure (BCEMA-SOP-02), which holds the step-by-step siren, alert and call-out instructions.

Brennan County lies in the Jackson Purchase of western Kentucky, in the warning area of the National Weather Service (NWS) Weather Forecast Office in Paducah. Severe weather is the county's most frequent disaster. Tornadoes have struck the county in every month of the year, and cool-season tornadoes at night, embedded in fast lines of storms, are the hardest to warn for: the storms move at 50 to 70 mph, the tornadoes are often wrapped in rain, and most residents are asleep.

## 2. Situation and assumptions

- Population about 41,000. Ashford (pop. 18,500) is the county seat. About 4,300 residents live in manufactured homes, most in nine parks listed in the Facilities and Systems Description.
- The county has 46 outdoor warning sirens in four zones. Sirens are an outdoor warning; they are not designed to wake people indoors.
- Most residents receive warnings from Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA), NOAA Weather Radio, local television and radio, and the county's opt-in mass notification system (BrennanAlert, about 14,200 subscribers).
- The NWS issues tornado and severe thunderstorm warnings. The county does not issue its own tornado warnings. It may activate sirens, send BrennanAlert messages and, through IPAWS, send its own WEA for protective actions (shelter openings, road closures, boil-water notices) that the NWS does not cover.
- Night events with power loss will cut television for many residents; cell towers on battery last four to eight hours.
- Large employers run night shifts. Ridgeline Distribution (a warehouse with up to 310 workers on the overnight shift) and Corwin Wax & Candle (up to 95 workers) have their own shelter plans, which BCEMA reviewed in 2025.

## 3. Concept of operations

### 3.1 Readiness levels

| Level | Trigger | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | No severe weather in the outlook | Routine monitoring |
| Level 1 — Watch for it | SPC Day 1 outlook of Enhanced (3) or higher for the county, or a Paducah hazardous weather outlook naming tornadoes | EM Director and Deputy review staffing; weather briefing to department heads by email; spotter coordinator on call |
| Level 2 — Partial activation | Tornado watch for the county, or a severe thunderstorm watch with a tornado threat at night | EOC partially activated: EM Director or Deputy, Warning Officer, Communications Lead; spotter net opened on the county VHF repeater; Channel 6 StormTeam and NWS Paducah chat joined |
| Level 3 — Full activation | Tornado warning for any part of the county, a confirmed tornado, or damage reports | Full EOC staffing per Section 4; Incident Command in the field; shelters on standby |

### 3.2 Warning sequence

When NWS Paducah issues a tornado warning whose polygon touches Brennan County, the 911 center (Brennan County Communications) activates the sirens of the zones inside the polygon within one minute, as BCEMA-SOP-02 Section 3 directs. The Warning Officer confirms the activation, sends a BrennanAlert message to the zones affected and posts the warning to the county's social media accounts. The county does not wait for spotter confirmation before activating sirens for a tornado warning.

For a severe thunderstorm warning with a destructive damage threat tag, or winds of 80 mph or more, the EM Director may activate sirens countywide (Section 3.4 of the SOP).

### 3.3 Protective actions

- **Shelter in place:** the default for tornado warnings. Messages tell people in manufactured homes and vehicles to go to a sturdy building before storms arrive, not after the warning.
- **Community storm shelters:** two safe rooms (Ashford Middle School and the Corwin Community Center) open when a tornado watch is issued at night or the SPC outlook is Moderate (4) or higher. Each holds 300 people. The Facilities Director or a designee unlocks them; opening is announced by BrennanAlert and Channel 6.
- **Post-event shelters:** the American Red Cross opens congregate shelters on request through the EOC. The first site is Brennan County High School.

### 3.4 After the storm

- Search and rescue starts with the fire districts in the damaged area; the Kentucky Emergency Management (KYEM) Area Manager is called for state resources.
- Damage assessment teams use the KYEM rapid damage assessment form; NWS Paducah's storm survey team is invited, and the county gives them access and its damage reports.
- Public information: one county message, coordinated with Ashford and Corwin, at least every two hours for the first day.
- Debris and utilities: county road department clears routes for emergency vehicles first; Brennan Rural Electric Cooperative and Ashford Municipal Utilities report outage counts to the EOC every hour.

## 4. Organization and responsibilities

- **EM Director (Dana Whitfield):** activates the EOC, decides countywide siren activation outside NWS warnings, requests state assistance, advises the County Judge/Executive on a local emergency declaration.
- **Deputy EM Director (Ray Okafor):** EOC manager on activation; leads damage assessment.
- **Warning Officer (on duty rotation):** monitors NWS products, confirms siren activations, sends BrennanAlert and county WEA messages, keeps the warning log.
- **Spotter Coordinator (Lena Brooks, volunteer):** runs the spotter net, relays reports to NWS Paducah by chat and phone.
- **Public Information Officer (Caleb Harmon):** writes and approves public messages, coordinates with Channel 6 StormTeam and other media, monitors social media for rumors.
- **Brennan County Communications (911):** activates sirens per the SOP, dispatches responders, logs calls reporting damage, injuries and entrapments.
- **Fire districts and Sheriff's Office:** field response, search and rescue, scene security.
- **Brennan County Schools:** safe room access at Ashford Middle School; school closures.
- **Health Department:** medical needs, mortality information for the coroner, public health messaging.

## 5. Direction, control and coordination

The EOC is at the Brennan County Public Safety Building, 900 Industrial Parkway, Ashford. The alternate EOC is the Corwin Fire Station 3 training room. Field incident command follows NIMS. The EOC coordinates with NWS Paducah (NWSChat and the EM hotline), the KYEM Area 1 Manager, neighboring McCracken, Graves and Marshall counties, and Channel 6 StormTeam's broadcast meteorologist on duty.

## 6. Training and exercises

The annex is exercised every year before spring. Exercises follow HSEEP. The 2025 after-action report found three gaps that this revision addresses: siren zone activation took four minutes on a night warning (target one minute); BrennanAlert messages were sent without the safe room status; and the county's storm shelter opening message conflicted with a broadcast saying shelters were closed.

# Channel 6 StormTeam — Severe Weather Coverage Plan

Channel 6 (Ashford, Kentucky) News and Weather · Owned by Purchase Area Broadcasting · Revision 2026-1

## 1. Purpose

This plan tells the Channel 6 newsroom, weather team, master control and digital staff what to do when severe weather threatens the station's viewing area: western Kentucky's Jackson Purchase and parts of southern Illinois and northwest Tennessee, eleven counties including Brennan County. It sets when we break into programming, who does what on air and online, and how we keep broadcasting if the station itself is hit.

Our first duty is to get the National Weather Service's warnings to viewers immediately and to help them act. We do not issue our own warnings and we never contradict an NWS warning on air.

## 2. Team

- **Chief Meteorologist (Marisa Kent):** leads severe weather coverage; decides wall-to-wall coverage.
- **Morning/Weekend Meteorologist (Tyler Gaines):** second on-air meteorologist; radar and storm tracking.
- **Weather producer (on call):** builds graphics, watches NWS products, NWSChat and the county partner rooms, feeds information to the on-air meteorologist.
- **Storm trackers (two contract crews):** live video and reports by phone and cellular bonding units. Trackers never drive into a tornado's path and never report from inside a warning polygon's core.
- **News Director (Andrea Lusk):** staffing, news crews, editorial calls on damage coverage.
- **Digital producer:** livestream, app push alerts, social media.
- **Master control operator:** inserts crawls, switches to the weather center, runs EAS.

## 3. Coverage levels

| Level | Trigger | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Alert day | SPC Day 1 Enhanced (3) or higher for the area, or tornado watch issued | "StormTeam Alert Day" graphics; extra weather segments; both meteorologists scheduled |
| Crawl and cut-ins | Severe thunderstorm warning in the area | Crawl with the warning text; map in the corner; cut-in at the top of the hour |
| Wall-to-wall | Any tornado warning in the area, or a severe thunderstorm warning with a DESTRUCTIVE tag in a populated area | Break programming, stay on air until no tornado warning remains, stream on the app and social media |
| Tornado emergency | NWS issues a tornado emergency (CATASTROPHIC tag) | Full-screen map, repeated life-safety instructions, name towns and times of arrival every two minutes |

At night, the duty meteorologist must be in the building within 30 minutes of a tornado watch for the area. If they cannot be, the producer airs the NWS warning text with the radar loop and calls in the on-call meteorologist by phone.

## 4. On-air content

- Lead with what the NWS warning says: which places, until when, the tag, and the action.
- Give arrival times for towns in the path from the station's storm-track tool, saying they are estimates.
- Repeat that sirens are an outdoor warning, and that people in manufactured homes and vehicles must move to a sturdy building now.
- Read county messages (safe room openings, shelter locations) from emergency management partners, naming the source. Brennan County EMA's Warning Officer is the contact for Brennan County.
- Do not air unconfirmed damage or casualty reports; say who reported anything we air (trained spotter, law enforcement, NWS).
- Correct errors on air immediately and say what was wrong.

## 5. Digital

- App push alerts for tornado warnings by county, set to the NWS polygon counties.
- Livestream during wall-to-wall coverage on the app, the website and social channels.
- The digital producer watches comments and posts for rumors (for example, "the sirens are broken", "the shelter is closed") and passes them to the meteorologist and the News Director, who check with the county before addressing them.

## 6. If the station is hit

- The weather center has a generator and a UPS; the transmitter site at Benton Ridge has its own generator with 72 hours of fuel.
- If the studio must be evacuated, the team shelters in the interior hallway of the basement. Coverage continues from the backup weather set in the basement with a single camera and a laptop radar feed, or by phone from a meteorologist at home.
- If the transmitter fails, coverage continues on the livestream and by simulcast on Ashford 98.1 FM under the Purchase Area Radio Group agreement.

## 7. After the storm

- Daylight damage coverage with names of the places hit and resources (shelters, how to report damage), from emergency management.
- The meteorologist explains the NWS storm survey when it is published (EF rating, path length and width).
- A debrief with the county emergency managers within two weeks, including what viewers told us about the warning.

# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Wet Stock — Cyclone Response, Coordination & Pipeline Integrity

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Harbourlight Relief International (HRI) country office, Beira, Mozambique · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for Harbourlight Relief International (HRI) Mozambique. It runs for four hours of real time and covers the first 72 hours after fictional Tropical Cyclone Tevra made landfall near Beira at about 22:00 on Thursday 11 February 2027. Six teams sit at the table: the Country Director, the Emergency Coordinator, the Information Management Officer, the Logistics Manager, the Security Focal Point and the Headquarters Crisis Management Team Chair in Geneva. Players talk through decisions, draft products and make calls. Nobody deploys, nobody moves stock and no real message leaves the room. You run the clock, deliver the injects and play everyone outside HRI.

The injects simulate a natural hazard and everything that follows from it. A GDACS red alert and INGD bulletins open the exercise. Three staff in Buzi district are out of contact. The Beira warehouse roof is damaged and about a fifth of the stock is wet or missing. The EN6 crossing at Dondo is cut. The numbers on the HCT agenda don't match. Cluster gap lists arrive with 12-hour and 48-hour clocks. Customs holds the tarpaulin container. On the second day, the Nhamissa distribution breaks into a crowd surge, a cash rumour spreads and acute watery diarrhoea cases rise. Along the way, reporters, a donor press office, families and community members press the players. There are four phases, each one hour. **Phase 1, Silence from Buzi (06:00–07:00 Friday):** accounting for staff and family calls. **Phase 2, Numbers That Disagree (07:00–08:00 Friday):** correcting the stock, the HCT caseload and resolving the Buzi search. **Phase 3, Gaps and the Container (08:00–09:00 Friday):** the one decision point, on gap commitments, plus the choice between the customs waiver and rerouting. **Phase 4, Pressure at Nhamissa (09:00–10:00 Saturday, after a one-day jump):** safe distribution, the rumour, AWD and the restart plan.

Evaluators should watch four things. First, staff accounting: do players act on the out-of-contact report before the facts are confirmed, report within the 1-hour window in HMSSSP 6, and keep staff names out of every external line? Second, numbers: do the cluster stock sheet, the 3W, Flash Update No. 1, the Geneva situation message and the donor's quote all show the Recount 1 figures (2,400 shelter kits, not 3,000)? Third, commitments: do players accept a gap only in writing, and only against stock, staff, access and funds they actually have, as SOP-COORD-01 6 requires? Fourth, safe programming: do they suspend movements, share a nameless alert, decide what happens to the national staff at Nhamissa, answer the rumour with one message, and keep the safeguarding complaint confidential? Write down the time of each decision, who made it, and whether they named the document behind it.

**Objectives:**
- **Duty of Care:** Demonstrate the ability to account for the three out-of-contact staff in Buzi district, report the incident to the Security Focal Point and Country Director within 1 hour, notify the HQ Crisis Management Team chair within 2 hours of activation, and give families only cleared information until the staff are found at 11:20.
- **Information Integrity:** Demonstrate the ability to correct the overstated Beira warehouse stock balance with the clusters and submit the first 3W entries by 17:00, so that they agree with the situation message to Geneva and the flash update and explain the gap between INGD's 412,000 and OCHA's 385,000 affected.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to decide which WASH and shelter gaps in Buzi and Nhamatanda HRI can take within the 12-hour assignment target, committing only the stock, staff, access and funds it actually has, and to choose between an INGD customs waiver and rerouting a shipment for the held container of 6,000 tarpaulins.
- **Safe Programming:** Demonstrate the ability to suspend movements and share a security alert without staff names under Saving Lives Together during the Nhamissa crowd surge, while countering the cash-for-registration rumour with one consistent message to the community committee and reporters.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** islands. Together they simulate the emergency coordination room at the HRI country office in Beira, with a separate "Geneva" island for headquarters. Leave space so players can walk between tables.
- **Island 1 (front centre), Team A Country Director, with Team E Security Focal Point beside it:** the CD and SFP work the staff accounting and movement decisions together. Put this island nearest the whiteboard.
- **Island 2 (front left), Team B Emergency Coordinator:** keep the seat empty or "in the air" until the 12:00 landing (game time) at the Phase 2/3 boundary. Before that, the player can only read the documents and cannot be contacted. This forces the CD to carry the 08:30 HCT meeting alone.
- **Island 3 (front right), Team C Information Management Officer and Team D Logistics Manager:** seat them side by side. The recount, the cluster stock sheet, the 3W and the flash update all pass between them.
- **Island 4 (set apart, side of the room), Team F Headquarters Crisis Management Team Chair (Geneva):** this team must reach the country office by "phone" (walking over, or using the simulated phones). They do not overhear the Beira discussion.
- **Rear table, public information (the Communications Officer role):** seat a spare player or observer here as the Communications Officer. If you have no spare player, leave a labelled tray here instead. Deliver all media slips and reporter emails to this table (I-02, I-10, I-16, I-20). The CD or CMT must physically walk over to clear a line and to get the facts. The point is to show the real friction: public information sits away from the decision-makers, and statements go wrong when the speaker hasn't been briefed on the latest confirmed figure, for example 64 of 64 located, or 2,400 shelter kits.
- **SimCell / Controller table (front corner, facing the players):** you, the inject deck, the MSEL and the scripts. Evaluators sit along the walls with a clear view of Islands 1 and 3.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and screen for main-screen injects: the STARTEX, time-jump and ENDEX cards, the GDACS and INGD bulletins, and the gap lists.
- A whiteboard laid out as an ICS-201-style status board, with these columns: staff roll call (61/64 → 64/64), situation, current stock (sheet vs Recount 1), open gaps with their clocks, decisions log and actions.
- A printed inject deck, one card per inject, in MSEL order, with extra copies of I-08, I-15 and I-21 for the teams named on them.
- Optional: simulated radios or phones, such as handsets or labelled cards, for the HF, VHF and satellite-phone calls (I-01b, I-11, I-19).
- A wall clock showing exercise time (CAT), and a printed time-jump banner.

**Materials for players:** name tents by team · extracts of HMSSSP 6, HMSCLP 2 and 6, SOP-COORD-01 6, and the HRI response plan sections on roles (2) and principles and accountability (6) · the Wednesday cluster stock sheet · a blank 3W template, flash update template and incident report form · pens, sticky notes and a decision log sheet per team.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**The SimCell role**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff", you answer as the deputy. Here that means: if the Security Focal Point says "I call UNDSS", you are the UNDSS field security officer in Beira. If the Logistics Manager says "I ring the clearing agent", you are Sra. Luísa Nhantumbo at Sofala Freight and Clearing. Answer in the first person, in that character's own interest and with only what that character would know. If players call a party with no script, improvise from what the character wants. A reporter wants a quote before deadline, a cluster coordinator wants the line closed, an agent wants paperwork and payment. Log every call: time, who called, what was asked and what was promised.

**Do not make it easy**
Players learn from friction. Don't hand over information or resources they haven't earned. For example, at 08:30 the HCT pre-read (I-07) asks for AVAILABLE stock, and the cluster sheet still shows "3,000 shelter kits, Beira – available". If the CD says "we have about 3,000 kits", play the OCHA officer and write it straight into the request to New York. Don't correct them. The error should come back in the donor's draft (I-16) and in Sitrep 2 (I-22). The same goes for the WASH Cluster Coordinator: if the IMO says "yes, HRI will take Buzi", don't accept it. Demand in writing the units, the staff, how they get there and a first-service date before 07:30 Sunday, as SOP-COORD-01 6 requires. If UNDSS is asked for a helicopter seat, it shares information but promises nothing. Under Saving Lives Together, the UN is not responsible for HRI staff.

**Managing time**
The MSEL has three time jumps: at 1:00 (to 07:00 Friday), at 2:00 (to 08:00 Friday) and at 3:00 (to 09:00 Saturday, a full-day jump). Stop the room, show the card on the main screen and read it word for word. Script:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down, please. We are jumping forward. It is now [07:00 / 08:00] Central Africa Time, Friday 12 February 2027 — [or: 09:00 Saturday 13 February 2027; the clock has moved forward one day]. Here is what has happened in the meantime." [Read the card.] "Anything you said you would do before the pause has been done as you described it. Anything you did not decide has not happened. Update your boards. RESUME."

For a short jump inside a phase, say: "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. The recount is finished and the boat has left the quay. RESUME." If one team is stuck, give them a two-minute warning ("The HCT starts in two minutes, what is HRI's line?"). Don't solve the problem for them. Hold the Emergency Coordinator out of contact until the 11:45 landing, even if they ask to "phone from the plane".

**Handling artificialities**
Players will push back on the exercise's assumptions. When a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "In real life we would just…", answer:
> "Understood. For today, assume it works the way the card says. Note your point on the parking-lot sheet and we will take it at the hot wash. What do you do now?"

Don't argue about whether the scenario is realistic. If the objection exposes a real gap in a plan, ask an evaluator to note it word for word.

**Delivering injects**
Don't explain. Say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." Hand the card to the team named on it, or read the call aloud in character. Don't hint at the right answer, and don't say which injects are noise or red herrings. I-01b, I-15b and I-21b are distractions. The committee member's "car in the river" call (I-09) is a red herring, and so is the 2025 flash update template. Let the players sort them. If a team ignores an inject, don't repeat it. Let the consequence arrive in a later inject.

**Exercise conventions**
- Start and end every simulated call, email or message with "This is an exercise". Write "EXERCISE EXERCISE EXERCISE" at the top and bottom of any product players draft. Nothing leaves the room, and nobody calls real numbers, including the feedback line or staff phones.
- If a real emergency or a real-world issue comes up (an injury, a fire alarm, a real security event, a real call that needs answering), anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD". Play stops at once. Resume only when the controller says "Back in exercise. RESUME."

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the first 72 hours after Tevra. Go around the room one team at a time (Country Office leadership, Logistics, Information Management, Programmes and Comms, HQ CMT) so every function speaks, and write each Up and each Down on the whiteboard in that team's words before you discuss any of them.

**Key Discussion Questions for Wet Stock:**
- **Trust:** At 0:30 (I-02) the reporter's email about three staff 'swept away' near Buzi reached the Geneva CMT chair and the Beira CD before anyone had confirmed anything. At 0:50 (I-04) UNDSS told you it could only share information and could not promise a seat on the helicopter or the INGD boats. When did you stop waiting for the UN search picture and treat finding your three Buzi staff as HRI's job? Did the Security Focal Point and CD hear within 1 hour, and the CMT chair within 2? And what exactly did you say to the families and to the reporter before the staff turned up at 11:20? Was every line cleared, or were you filling the silence? _(Objective: Duty of Care)_
- **The Number:** At 0:40 (I-03) the storekeeper guessed 'maybe one-fifth' was lost. At 1:20 (I-08) the unsigned recount showed 2,400 usable shelter kits, not 3,000, and the cluster logistics officer was still asking whether he could list all 3,000 for Nhamatanda. How long did the 3,000 figure stay live on the cluster sheet, and who owned the job of correcting it? By 17:00, did your 3W entries, the situation message to Geneva and the flash update all use the same stock figures? Could you explain the gap between INGD's 412,000 and OCHA's 385,000 affected in one sentence that every product used? _(Objective: Information Integrity)_
- **The Yes:** At 2:30 (I-15) the WASH cluster asked you to take G-W-01 in Buzi (18,000 people, figure unverified, and the EN6 cut at Dondo) and the Shelter cluster asked about G-S-02 in Nhamatanda (3,500 households). The deadline was 19:30, and declining meant an HCT escalation. When you answered, did you commit only what you actually had: 10 tested water treatment units, not 12, plus real staff, boat or helicopter access and money in hand? Or did the pressure not to leave the line unassigned push you to over-promise? For the 6,000 held tarpaulins, how did you choose between the INGD customs waiver and rerouting, and what information would have let you decide faster? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Rumour:** At 3:18 (I-20) the committee chair read you the '3,000 MT cash, registration costs 150 MT at gate 2' message. Within minutes, Rádio Voz do Centro was going live at 10:30, Diário do Chiveve was asking how many were hurt in the 'stampede', and the NGO platform was asking who runs cash in Nhamissa. Did the committee, the radio reporter and the other NGOs all hear the same single message from you? When did you suspend movements and share the security alert through Saving Lives Together, and did it go out without any staff names? _(Objective: Safe Programming)_
- **The Quiet Call:** In the middle of the rumour response, the safeguarding focal point called the EC on a personal line about a man in an HRI vest at registration and asked that the complaint stay with the EC and CD only. How did you keep that complaint confidential while you were also sending public messages and security alerts about the gate 2 'agent'? Did anyone treat the two issues as the same thing before you knew whether they were linked? _(Objective: Safe Programming)_

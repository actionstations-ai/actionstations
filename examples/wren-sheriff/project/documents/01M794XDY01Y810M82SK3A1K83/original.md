# Wren County Communications (911): Facilities, Systems and Procedures

Operated by the Wren County Sheriff's Office for the county, Alder Falls PD, Fire District 1 and Wren County EMS · Revision 3

## 1. The center

Wren County Communications is the county's only public safety answering point (PSAP). It is on the second floor of the Public Safety Building, 400 Cedar Street, Alder Falls, next to the Sheriff's Office. It has six consoles: four call-taking and dispatch positions, one supervisor position and one overflow position. The day shift (06:00 to 18:00) normally has four telecommunicators and the supervisor; the night shift has three and a supervisor. Priya Venkataraman is the day Communications Supervisor (extension 4410); Marcus Delacroix is the night supervisor (extension 4411). The center's published non-emergency number is 541-555-0100.

A backup center with two consoles is at the Pine Landing substation, 22 miles away. Moving to the backup takes about 20 minutes and is decided by the Communications Supervisor with the Undersheriff.

## 2. Systems

| System | What it does | Who owns it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Next generation 911 call handling | Takes 911 calls and texts, shows location | Wren County, hosted by the state | A text-to-911 session can be transferred between positions |
| Computer-aided dispatch (CAD) | Call entry, unit status, incident chronology, premise history | Sheriff's Office, vendor-hosted in the county data room | Every command change, decision time and alert is logged here |
| Radio system | Talkgroups WREN-1 (Sheriff), APD-1 (Alder Falls), FD1-1 (fire), EMS-1, WC-INTEROP (shared), tactical WREN-T1 to WREN-T6 | County, with tower sites at Alder Falls, Pine Landing and Gray Butte | Gray Butte site has a backup generator tested monthly |
| LEDS and NCIC terminal | State and national law enforcement data, including missing person entries | Oregon State Police | Entries need a second person's read-back |
| Records management system | Reports, case numbers, evidence and property | Sheriff's Office | Property room items are logged by barcode |
| Mass notification | A county text and email system, "WrenAlert" | Emergency Management | Used for evacuation and shelter in place, not for AMBER Alerts |
| Vehicle location and in-car terminals | Show unit positions and send messages | Sheriff's Office | Positions refresh every 15 seconds |
| Tip line | A dedicated queue on extension 4450 and public number 541-555-0190 | Communications Center | Three additional call-takers staffed from the conference room, which has six phones |
| Wireless Emergency Alert access | Sent to OSP through the state, not by the county for AMBER | Oregon State Police | The county can send local WEA for evacuation through Emergency Management |

## 3. Call taking and dispatch priorities

Calls are prioritized 1 to 4. A report of a child missing under suspicious circumstances is a priority 1 call. The telecommunicator enters it with the "missing child" flag, which alerts the supervisor and starts a timer on the CAD screen that shows the time since the first report. The flag is the starting point for the AMBER Alert timing measure in the after-action report.

During a call surge, the supervisor opens the overflow console and asks the Alder Falls PD watch commander and Fire District 1 captain which of their calls can wait. The center's service level target is to answer 90 percent of 911 calls in 10 seconds and to dispatch a priority 1 call within 60 seconds. A surge is declared when more than 12 calls are in the queue or the average hold exceeds 30 seconds. The supervisor then moves the tip line to the conference room, so that tips do not use 911 positions.

## 4. Interoperability and channel plan

For a multi-agency incident the supervisor assigns a tactical talkgroup, normally WREN-T3, and patches WC-INTEROP for outside agencies. The supervisor logs each patch with the time and the channel. A resource board in CAD lists each agency's units on the incident with the check-in time. Mutual aid units check in at staging by radio on WC-INTEROP and are added to the board.

## 5. Failure modes and responses

- **CAD outage.** The center switches to paper run cards and tracks units on a wall board. The supervisor tells the command post and the Undersheriff, and the center keeps a paper chronology, which is entered into CAD afterward with the original times.
- **Radio tower site failure.** Calls move to the remaining sites; if Gray Butte is down, coverage in the north county falls below standard and units use simplex channels.
- **Phone overload.** The incoming 911 queue is protected by the tip line; callers with a tip are asked to use extension 4450.
- **Loss of LEDS or NCIC access.** The center asks the OSP communications center to make entries by phone and logs the request time. A missing child entry made by OSP counts as done.

## 6. Who is told what

For any critical incident the supervisor makes the following notifications and logs the times: the Undersheriff (extension 4401), the Patrol Lieutenant, the Investigations Sergeant, the PIO (extension 4430), Fire District 1 and EMS, APD watch commander (extension 5501), and the OSP duty sergeant. For a missing child the list adds the Victim and Family Liaison (extension 4440), the NCMEC case line and the OSP AMBER Alert coordinator through the OSP Northern Communications Center.

## 7. Rumor and social media at the center

The center's social media policy states that dispatchers do not post. The supervisor tells the PIO about any rumor that is reaching the center as calls, with a count and the wording callers use, so the PIO can answer it. Calls that report sightings of a vehicle are taken as tips, not as emergencies, unless the caller sees the child.

## 8. Records and retention

Audio of 911 calls, radio traffic and CAD data are kept for 3 years, or longer for an open case. After-action reports use CAD chronology times, which is why every decision is typed into CAD at the time it is made.

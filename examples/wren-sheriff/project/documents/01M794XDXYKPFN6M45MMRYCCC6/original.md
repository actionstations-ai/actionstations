# Partner Agencies: Alder Falls Police Department, Fire District 1 and Neighbors

Prepared by the Wren County Sheriff's Office from the partners' own published descriptions · Revision 2

## 1. Alder Falls Police Department (APD)

APD serves the city of Alder Falls (population 18,500). Chief Gerald Mbeki leads 31 sworn officers and 8 civilians. APD's headquarters is at 120 Main Street. Its watch commander is on the shared line at extension 5501 around the clock; its PIO is Dana Kowalczyk (extension 5530); its victim advocate is Jonah Feldman (extension 5520). APD dispatches on the Wren County Communications radio system, on talkgroup APD-1.

APD and the Sheriff's Office have a written agreement: the agency in whose jurisdiction an incident begins has command until unified command is formed. When a missing child's last known location is in the city, APD commands and the Sheriff's Office joins; when the child may have been taken outside the city limits, the Sheriff's Office commands and APD joins. In a case of doubt the first deputy or officer asks the other agency's watch commander. APD has 4 detectives, one of whom is assigned to the child abuse team, and a school resource officer at Alder Falls High School and Alder Falls Middle School.

APD holds one drone, flown by two certified officers, and one K-9 handler. Any such resource is requested through the Incident Commander, with the reason recorded; its use is the APD commander's decision.

## 2. Wren County Fire District 1

Fire District 1 has 5 stations and about 60 career and volunteer members. Fire Chief Alicia Moreno is the district's commander; the day duty captain is on extension 5601. Its Station 1 on Main Street, Alder Falls, hosts the District's training room, which is the designated backup site for a family reunification and assistance center. The District's support for a missing child incident is ground search help, medical support at a search base, light and rehab for search volunteers, and staging. The District also provides EMS standby with Wren County EMS (Mercy Valley Ambulance, a private nonprofit) at the family assistance center on request.

## 3. Wren County Emergency Management

Emergency Management is led by Director Fernando Castellanos (extension 4700). It runs the county emergency operations center (EOC) in the Public Safety Building basement, the WrenAlert mass notification system and the local relationships with Oregon Emergency Management. The EOC is activated at the Undersheriff's request. It can host the JIC support, volunteer check-in and a shelter. Emergency Management can send a local Wireless Emergency Alert to a defined polygon for evacuation or shelter in place, but not an AMBER Alert.

## 4. Neighbors and the state

- **Deschutes County Sheriff's Office and Jefferson County Sheriff's Office** are called under the neighbor mutual aid agreement. Each can send 6 to 8 deputies within 90 minutes and a search coordinator. Their dispatch centers are on the interoperability network.
- **Oregon State Police** run the state AMBER Alert Plan, the Oregon Missing Children Clearinghouse, the LEDS system and state aircraft requests. OSP's Northern Communications Center takes alert requests at 555-0142. The Oregon State Police also supports Wren County with troopers on highway stretches on Highway 26 and Highway 97.
- **FBI Portland Field Office** is contacted if a child may have been taken across a state line or the facts suggest a federal offense. The FBI can deploy a Child Abduction Rapid Deployment team and victim specialists on request.
- **National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC)** provides case management support, poster production and tip analysis on request.
- **Oregon Department of Transportation** operates the highway message signs that carry alert text.
- **Wren County School District** (Superintendent Ines Barragan) must be told if the child is a student, so it can answer parents' questions with the same words as the public statement.
- **Wren County Juvenile Department** and the **District Attorney's office** (District Attorney Phillip Vance) are told once a suspect is identified, and the DA's office reviews warrants.

## 5. Contact routing for an incident

The Communications Supervisor holds the notification list. The Incident Commander decides who joins unified command; the Communications Supervisor logs the agency's check-in with the commander name and call-back number on the unified command briefing. Joint briefings take place at the command post at fixed times, normally on the hour; each agency's commander states its resources, its needs and any problem. Differences about who leads are resolved by the Sheriff and the APD Chief by phone, and the outcome is written into the CAD chronology.

## 6. Training and exercises

The agencies hold a joint tabletop each year in the spring. The most recent one tested family reunification at a school; the lessons were that the family assistance center needs a single owner, that the PIOs must agree text before posting, and that the NCMEC call should be made earlier. These items are carried into WCSO-SOP-12 and WCSO-PIO-02.

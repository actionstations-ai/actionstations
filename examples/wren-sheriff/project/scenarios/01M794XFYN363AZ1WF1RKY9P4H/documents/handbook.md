# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## North Gate — Fairgrounds Abduction, AMBER Decision & Unified Command

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Wren County Fairgrounds (command post at the Fairgrounds Exhibit Hall B office) and Wren County Communications, Wren County Sheriff's Office, Alder Falls, Wren County, Oregon · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for the Wren County Sheriff's Office and its partners. It runs about four hours of exercise time, from 14:05 to 18:05 on a simulated Saturday in mid-October. It tests coordination and decisions only: who commands, who is told, what gets entered and what gets said. No tactical methods are discussed or written down, as WCICMA 1 requires. If players drift into tactics, bring them back to the coordination decision. All people, case numbers and posts are fictional.

The injects simulate a physical security incident: a six-year-old girl (exercise name Lily Ashby) taken from the Wren County Fall Festival at the Wren County Fairgrounds. Witness accounts conflict, jurisdiction is shared with Alder Falls PD at the north gate road, the family posts on Facebook and false posts spread online. You deliver the radio calls, CAD supplementals, Facebook posts, wire bulletins, queue boards and phone calls from partners, and you play every outside party the players contact. The arc has four phases. **Phase 1, North Gate Report (0:00–1:00):** checking the first facts, the Child Abduction-flagged LEDS/NCIC entry with its read-back, a building-level command post and command across the city line. **Phase 2, Facts Pulling Apart (1:00–2:00):** keeping one picture while three truck colours, family posts and a viral post naming a local man pull the case apart. **Phase 3, The AMBER Call (2:00–3:00):** the one high-consequence decision (an OSP AMBER Alert, or a search-area WEA or Endangered Missing Person advisory), then the tip surge and the Highway 26 extension. **Phase 4, Cancel and Close (3:00–4:00):** a false "found" rumor, the LEDS outage, shift relief, the recovery, the cancellation and one joint closing statement.

Evaluators should watch for these things. Does the Incident Commander get named and the Exhibit Hall B command post announced before more units arrive? Is unified command with APD, Fire District 1 and OSP formed and written down? Is every AMBER criterion recorded on the worksheet as yes or no, with a reason and a time, and is the worksheet reviewed every 30 minutes (WCSO-SOP-12 4)? Does the entry come before any alert? Do players keep the search for the vehicle and the ground search separate? Does the family hear news from the Liaison before the public does? Does every agency release the same times and facts, with no person named or described? Do mutual aid requests follow the order in WCICMA 5 and name what is needed, how many, where to report and on which channel? Also watch whether the Communications Center uses its failure-mode steps in WCCFSP 5 when the 911 queue and LEDS fail.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to complete and update the AMBER worksheet under WCSO-SOP-12 section 4, recording each of the five criteria as met or not with a reason as the truck description and timeline keep changing, and to decide with the Undersheriff on the phone whether to request the OSP AMBER Alert or use a search-area Wireless Emergency Alert or Endangered Missing Person advisory instead.
- **Unified Command:** Demonstrate the ability to announce a building-level command post at the Fairgrounds Exhibit Hall B office within 20 minutes and form unified command with Alder Falls PD, Fire District 1 and Oregon State Police within 30 minutes, with every change of command announced on WREN-1 and logged in the CAD chronology.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to get the Child Abduction-flagged LEDS/NCIC entry made and read back before any alert request, and to protect the 911 queue during the tip surge by moving tips to extension 4450, following the Communications Center plan (WCCFSP 5).
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to open the joint information center and issue one accurate, agreed message that corrects the grandparent's conflicting truck description and answers the viral post naming a local man within 30 minutes, without releasing case detail the Investigations Sergeant has not cleared.

**Plan sections exercised:**
- WCSO-SOP-12: Missing and Endange… › 4. The five AMBER criteria
- WCSO-SOP-12: Missing and Endange… › 2. Every missing child report is taken
- WCSO-SOP-12: Missing and Endange… › 5. Requesting the alert
- WCICMA › 3. Establishing the command post
- Partner Agencies: Alder Falls Po… › 1. Alder Falls Police Department (APD)
- WCSO-PIO-02: Public Information,… › 7. Social media and rumor

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Use islands, not a U-shape, so each table feels like a separate place and information has to be carried or called across. Put the facilitator/SimCell table at the front beside the main screen.
- **Table 1: Exhibit Hall B command post (front center).** Team A, Incident Command (Patrol Lieutenant/IC). Leave three empty chairs labelled APD, Fire District 1 and OSP for the partner commanders who "arrive" at I-04. The SimCell sits in these chairs when playing them face to face.
- **Table 2: Wren County Communications (side wall).** Team B (Communications Center Supervisor). Put a phone or a "Console 1–4" card set on the table and a separate empty "conference room / tip line ext. 4450" spot next to it. Players have to physically staff that spot when they move the tip line.
- **Table 3: Investigations (near the command post, but a separate island).** Team C (Investigations Sergeant). This is where the printed scene photo (I-07) is handed over.
- **Table 4: Private family room (far corner, away from the command post).** Team E (Victim and Family Liaison). Its distance from Table 1 is deliberate. The plan wants the family out of earshot of the command post. If the Liaison is sitting at the command post, the family hears the radio (I-06).
- **Table 5: Executive (side, away from Table 1).** Team F (Undersheriff). The Undersheriff is reached "by phone" in the early phases. They talk to the IC through you or a handset, not across the table, until players decide otherwise.
- **Table 6: Public Information (rear of the room, by itself).** Team D (PIO). The PIO has to walk to the command post and to Investigations to get confirmed facts. This copies the real problem: the PIO is surrounded by reporters and posts but cut off from the decision makers. The walk exposes whether command pushes facts out or the PIO is left guessing while the wire and KWRN-TV deadlines run.

**Equipment needs**
- Projector and screen for main-screen injects: Facebook posts, wire bulletins, the 911 queue board (I-13), the LEDS error dialog (I-18) and the time-jump slides
- A whiteboard or flip chart at Table 1 for the ICS-201 (incident briefing: objectives, organization, resources, command post location), plus a second board for the AMBER worksheet with all five criteria and the 30-minute review times
- A printed inject deck in MSEL order with handouts cut and labelled by recipient. This includes the printed still of IMG_5126.HEIC for I-07 and the draft alert text for I-12, both marked EXERCISE PRODUCT
- Simulated radios or desk phones (optional). Two handsets let you play the OSP AMBER desk and APD at the same time.
- A wall clock showing exercise time, which you change at each time jump, and a timer for the real-time deadlines (the KWRN 15:00 live shot, the wire write-through at 17:00, the 10-minute reply on the alert text)

**Materials for players:** WCICMA, WCSO-SOP-12 (with the AMBER worksheet), WCCFSP, the partner agencies contact sheet, blank ICS-201 and ICS-214 forms, a decision log, a PIO release log and rumor-monitoring sheet, a mutual aid request form, a custody transfer form, name tents and pens.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role: the SimCell**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the Sheriff. If they say "I call APD," you are the APD watch commander on extension 5501. If they say "I call OSP," you are the trooper at the Northern Communications AMBER desk. Answer only what that person would know, in that person's voice and interests. Write down every call players make: the time, who called and what they asked for. Evaluators need this for the hot wash.

**Do not make it easy**
Real partners have their own rules, workload and doubts. Make players earn every answer. Example from this scenario: at I-08 and I-11 the OSP AMBER desk does not accept "dark pickup, maybe blue, maybe gray." The trooper will not broadcast three colours or anyone's name, and asks: "Which ONE description will your sergeant stand behind, what direction, what time window?" If Investigations has not yet worked the IMG_5126.HEIC photo against the witness accounts, the trooper tells them to call back. Do not invent a plate or a fourth witness to rescue them. Likewise, when Deschutes County asks "stage or stand by?", wait. If no one names what is needed, how many, where to report and the channel (WCICMA 5), the deputies stay where they are.

**Managing time**
Read the time jumps (I-05, I-10, I-16) word for word from the deck. Script:
"PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down for a moment. We are jumping forward 30 minutes [or one hour, as scripted]. It is now [time]. Here is what has happened since: [read the inject]. Anything you said you would do before the pause has been done, unless I tell you otherwise. The AMBER worksheet review is due now. RESUME."
If a team is stuck, tell them a partner is waiting on the line, and give them a two-minute deadline. If the room is ahead of the MSEL, add pressure (a second media call, another tip) instead of skipping core injects.

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "We'd never do it that way," answer: "Understood. For this exercise, assume the system works as written in the plan. Tell me what you would do instead and who you'd tell, and we'll capture the difference for the after-action report." Then move on. When a student asks for tactical detail, answer: "This exercise stays at coordination. Tell me the decision, who makes it and who needs to know."

**Delivering injects**
Do not explain or interpret. Hand over the slip and say "This just came in," or point to the screen and say "This is on the main screen." Deliver phone calls in character, standing at the team's table if possible. Give only the recipients listed on the MSEL. It is the players' job to share it.

**Conventions**
- Every printed product and every email or post on screen carries "EXERCISE — EXERCISE — EXERCISE." Any real-world contact made by mistake starts with "This is an exercise."
- If anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD," stop play at once. A real emergency or injury takes priority. Resume only when the facilitator says so.
- No real person's name is used for the man named in the posts. Read it as "[local man's name]".

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well (3-Up) and three things that broke or failed (3-Down). Go around the room one team at a time (Command Post, Communications Center, Investigations, PIO/JIC, Alder Falls PD, Fire District 1, OSP) and write every item on the whiteboard in two columns before anyone discusses them.

**Key Discussion Questions for North Gate:**
- **The Call:** At I-11 (16:07), Trooper Ellen Hask asked you to choose between an OSP AMBER request, a search-area WEA and an Endangered Missing Person advisory, and to give a decision time. Talk us through your AMBER worksheet at that moment. Was each of the five criteria in WCSO-SOP-12 section 4 marked yes or no with a reason? Did you review it every 30 minutes as the truck went from black to dark blue to gray? For the criteria that were only partly met, did you list what information was missing and who would get it? And when did the Undersheriff get on the phone: before the decision or after it? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **Trust:** Between I-03 (the volunteer changing her account to 'dark blue, maybe a Ford') and I-07 (the parking volunteer's photo IMG_5126 timestamped 13:51:22), when did you stop relying on the witness accounts and start relying on the photo? Did you handle that photo as evidence, by preserving the original file and metadata, identifying the volunteer and telling him not to post it? Or did it just go around the command post as another update? How did it shape the one vehicle description your sergeant was willing to stand behind? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Gap:** At I-14 (16:32), Jefferson County dispatch asked who the IC was, what channel to use and whether your alert area would include their county. Meanwhile a tipster was still behind a gray-blue pickup on Hwy 26. Could everyone in the room have answered that call right then? When was the Exhibit Hall B command post announced, and when did unified command with Alder Falls PD, Fire District 1 and OSP actually form? Was each change of command announced on WREN-1 and logged in the CAD chronology? What would have let you bring in a neighboring jurisdiction faster? _(Objective: Unified Command)_
- **Doctrine Under Load:** At I-03 (14:35), OSP asked whether the LEDS entry carried the Child Abduction flag and who did the read-back. Did the command post know the answer, or did the Communications Center have to tell you? Later, at I-17, 7 callers in 5 minutes asked the tip line whether the alert was cancelled. Did moving tips to extension 4450 under WCCFSP 5 actually keep the 911 queue clear all afternoon? If so, who was watching the 4450 line for real leads like the Hwy 26 tip, and who was answering the people calling because of rumors? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Message:** Three posts got ahead of you: the grandparent's 'silver-gray Chevy' post at I-03b, the 'black Dodge toward Redmond' update at I-06, and the false 'they found her' post at I-17. KWRN was also going live at 15:00, and a camera was filming the family through the lobby glass. What did you tell the public before you knew the facts, and who agreed to it? Did the JIC put out one message within 30 minutes that corrected the vehicle description and dealt with the viral post without releasing anything the Investigations Sergeant hadn't cleared? What did the Liaison tell the mother at 17:11 before the public heard it? _(Objective: Communication)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Law enforcement, corrections and courts**: Command, coordination and continuity for police and sheriff’s offices, jails and courts: unified command, communication with families, the public and the bar, AMBER Alert decisions, jail counts and court continuity. It stays at the coordination level by design and never teaches tactics.
  - Data sources: open-cad-calls, vera-jail-trends, fbi-cde-crime
- **Dispatch and command traffic**: The responders' view of dispatch: CAD incident detail, unit status board, pending calls, radio traffic across dispatch, command and tactical channels, station alerting and mutual aid requests, with the decisions of mutual aid, command transfer and a mayday. The 911 center's own view and CAD failure stay with the emergency communications domain.
  - Reference documents: Emergency Services Sector Tabletop Exercise, Situation Manual (CISA)
- **Media environment**: The news cycle and social feeds around an incident: wire alerts, articles and broadcast segments, social posts whose reach and sentiment change over time, reporter calls and press conference questions, and verification tasks with provenance clues. Simulated spread and sentiment are teaching models, not predictions. All outlets, handles and people are fictional.
  - Data sources: gdelt
  - Reference documents: FCC report on the Hawaii false alert (2018): a correction case study

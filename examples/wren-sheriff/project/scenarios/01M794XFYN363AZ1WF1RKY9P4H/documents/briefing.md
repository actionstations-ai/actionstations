# Player Briefing Packet
## North Gate — Fairgrounds Abduction, AMBER Decision & Unified Command

Location: Wren County Fairgrounds (command post at the Fairgrounds Exhibit Hall B office) and Wren County Communications, Wren County Sheriff's Office, Alder Falls, Wren County, Oregon · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 02:05 PM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Saturday in mid-October: cool and dry, about 54°F, clear skies, light northwest breeze. Daylight until early evening.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is a Saturday afternoon in mid-October and the Wren County Fall Festival is busy at the Wren County Fairgrounds, with about 3,000 visitors. There are families at the food booths, at the Exhibit Hall B petting area and on the shuttle between the parking lots. The Wren County Sheriff's Office has normal Saturday staffing (WCICMA 9): 5 deputies and one sergeant on patrol across the county, one deputy at jail intake, and Wren County Communications with 4 telecommunicators under Day Communications Supervisor Priya Venkataraman (ext. 4410). Patrol calls have been routine for a festival day: parking complaints, a few lost-and-found items and traffic on the roads near the fairgrounds. The Public Safety Building is quiet. The mobile command trailer CP-1 is parked at the Alder Falls substation. Alder Falls Police Department is handling its usual city calls. Sheriff Rosalind Teague and Undersheriff Calvin Okafor are off-site and can be reached by phone.

- Wren County Communications (911) and CAD: GREEN: 4 telecommunicators and the supervisor on duty, CAD normal, no calls waiting
- Radio talkgroups WREN-1, APD-1, WC-INTEROP: GREEN: all working, normal traffic
- LEDS/NCIC terminal: GREEN: online, no open entries pending
- Tip line (ext. 4450): GREEN: working, no calls
- Patrol and jail intake staffing: GREEN: 5 deputies and 1 sergeant on patrol, 1 deputy at jail intake, normal Saturday shift
- Mobile command trailer CP-1: GREEN: parked and ready at the Alder Falls substation (45 minutes to set up anywhere in the county)

**The Anomaly:** **14:00, Wren County Communications, 911 line.** A telecommunicator takes a 911 call from a grandparent at the Wren County Fall Festival. Their six-year-old granddaughter (exercise name **Lily Ashby**) was last seen at **13:40** near the **Exhibit Hall B petting area**, and the family cannot find her. The call is entered as **CAD 26-081377**, flagged "missing child." **Deputy Wren 14** is pulling into the fairgrounds now. Calls like this are common on busy festival days, and most end with the child found within minutes near a booth or an exhibit.

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep 911 service, patrol coverage across the county and safe festival operations running normally while you investigate and resolve the missing-child report at the Wren County Fairgrounds. You must balance speed against verification: act fast enough for the family and the public, but check every fact before you request resources, send alerts or make public statements, so that each decision is documented and defensible.

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio call, phone call and message with "This is an exercise." The Communications Center and the fairgrounds are real places, and an exercise message overheard on its own could cause real panic.
- If a real emergency happens to anyone in this room or at any real Sheriff's Office site, say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." The exercise stops immediately so that real people and real calls come first.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]." When that happens, move your clocks, logs and CAD times forward to match, so the exercise can cover four hours of the incident in the time we have.
- Treat printed injects (CAD entries, radio logs, social media posts, photos, tip forms and reports) as real information that arrived through that channel. Act on them, check them and log them exactly as you would on a real shift.

## SECTION 1: PURPOSE, AUTHORITY AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

This plan tells the Marston County Health Department (MCHD) how to recognize a public health emergency, organize its people under the Incident Command System, protect the residents of Marston County, and keep its essential functions running when its own buildings or systems are degraded. It is the department's part of the county's Emergency Support Function 8 (Public Health and Medical Services).

### 1.2 Authority

- Kansas statutes that give the local health officer authority to investigate and control infectious or contagious disease, including isolation and quarantine orders (cited by section in the health order templates kept by the County Counselor; Section 9).
- The Marston County Emergency Operations Plan (2024), ESF-8 annex, which names MCHD the primary agency for public health.
- The CDC Public Health Emergency Preparedness (PHEP) cooperative agreement, passed through the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE). The plan is organized around the 15 CDC preparedness capabilities (October 2018, updated January 2019).

### 1.3 Scope

All hazards affecting the health of the public in Marston County: communicable disease outbreaks, medical countermeasure campaigns, environmental and drinking-water emergencies, mass gatherings, natural hazards, and incidents that disable the department itself (cyber incident, loss of a building, staff shortage). Clinical care at hospitals is out of scope; the department coordinates with them through the Prairie Bend Healthcare Coalition.

## SECTION 2: SITUATION AND ASSUMPTIONS

### 2.1 Situation

Marston County has 142,600 residents. The City of Ellery (population 61,300) is the county seat; Brandt, Coyle and Lindell are the other towns. The county is divided into four health districts for field work and clinics (MCHD-FAC-SYS-2026, Section 2):

| Health District | Area | District office or clinic | Population |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health District 1 | City of Ellery | Ellery Health Center (headquarters) | 61,300 |
| Health District 2 | Brandt and the northern townships | North District Clinic | 33,800 |
| Health District 3 | Coyle and the southern townships | South District Clinic | 27,900 |
| Health District 4 | Lindell and the western townships | West District Office | 19,600 |

Kindergarten MMR coverage in Health District 2 was 86.4% in the 2025–26 school year, the lowest in the county; two private schools in Brandt report below 80%.

### 2.2 Assumptions

- The department runs on county IT (network, email, identity, file shares). A county-wide IT outage takes MCHD's local systems with it (Section 10; Cyber Annex).
- The state disease surveillance system and the state Immunization Information System (IIS) are hosted by KDHE. MCHD reaches them over the internet with individual accounts; they stay up when county systems are down, but MCHD's interfaces to them do not.
- Laboratories and hospitals report to KDHE electronically (ELR) and phone MCHD for urgent conditions. Fax to the department is a fallback only.
- The first 72 hours of a large outbreak need more disease investigators than MCHD has (eight); KDHE, neighboring counties and the Marston County Medical Reserve Corps fill the gap.

## SECTION 5: SURVEILLANCE, INVESTIGATION AND REPORTING

### 5.1 How reports reach MCHD

1. Electronic laboratory reports and electronic case reports arrive in the state surveillance system; MCHD investigators see new county cases in their work queue.
2. Clinicians and infection preventionists phone the MCHD Disease Reporting Line (555-0142, answered 24/7 through the after-hours service).
3. Fax to the MCHD Epidemiology fax (555-0149), received on the county fax server; used by smaller clinics and as a fallback when ELR is down.
4. Syndromic surveillance (ESSENCE through KDHE) for emergency department visits with fever and rash.

### 5.2 Outbreak numbers and line lists

The Lead Epidemiologist assigns an outbreak number (format OB-2026-0147) and opens the line list from template MCHD-F-12. The working line list lives on the county SharePoint site "MCHD Epi Response"; a printed copy is updated at each operational period. Individual cases carry the state system case number (format Case 26-04412) and lab specimens carry the state lab accession number (format PHL-26-031882).

### 5.3 Reporting up

- Suspect measles: telephone report to the KDHE Epidemiology Hotline as soon as the suspect case is known (MCHD practice: within 4 hours; the Lead Epidemiologist confirms the current state rule each January).
- Confirmed outbreak: daily line list update to KDHE by 15:00 during Level 1 or 2.
- KDHE reports to CDC; MCHD does not report to CDC directly.

## SECTION 6: COMMUNICABLE DISEASE RESPONSE (MEASLES APPENDIX)

### 6.1 Case definition and exposure windows

MCHD uses the KDHE measles case definition and investigation guideline in force. Investigators record for each case the infectious period (four days before through four days after rash onset) and every place the case visited during it.

### 6.2 Contacts and post-exposure prophylaxis

- Exposed contacts without evidence of immunity are offered MMR within 72 hours of exposure or immune globulin within 6 days, per the Medical Director's standing order SO-MEA-03.
- Susceptible contacts who do not receive prophylaxis are excluded from school, child care and work in health care settings for 21 days after the last exposure; the exclusion letter is template MCHD-F-31 and the quarantine order is template MCHD-F-33.
- Exposure sites (clinics, schools, stores, churches) are listed on the public exposure page after the Lead Epidemiologist and the PIO agree on wording.

### 6.3 Vaccination clinics

Rachel Holtz opens extra MMR clinics at the district clinics first; if demand exceeds 300 doses a day, Darnell Okafor recommends opening POD sites (Section 7). Appointments are booked in SlotWise; walk-ins are logged on paper form MCHD-F-40 and entered later. Every dose is entered in ClinicTrack EHR, which sends it to the state IIS overnight.

### 7.2 Requests and cold chain

Vaccine and immune globulin beyond MCHD stock are requested from the KDHE Immunization Program; Strategic National Stockpile materiel is requested through KDHE only. Vaccine is held in the pharmaceutical refrigerators at Ellery Health Center (units VF-01 to VF-04) and in each district clinic, monitored by ColdSentry sensors.

## SECTION 8: EMERGENCY PUBLIC INFORMATION AND WARNING

MCHD follows the CDC Crisis and Emergency Risk Communication principles: be first, be right, be credible, express empathy, promote action, show respect. The PIO clears every public message with the Incident Commander; clinical content is cleared by the Medical Director. Spanish versions go out at the same time as English (Marisol Ortega). Channels: news releases, the MCHD website and exposure page, Facebook and X accounts, the MCHD Information Line (555-0150) staffed by call-center volunteers, school-district messages through the superintendents, and the Health Alert Network to clinicians.

Rumor control: the PIO keeps a rumor log (MCHD-F-55) and answers recurring false claims on the website "What's true" panel within one operational period.

## SECTION 9: HEALTH ORDERS AND LEGAL PROCESS

Isolation, quarantine and exclusion orders are signed by the Health Officer and served by MCHD staff, or by the Marston County Sheriff's Office if a person refuses contact. The County Counselor reviews any order affecting a school or more than 25 people before it is signed.

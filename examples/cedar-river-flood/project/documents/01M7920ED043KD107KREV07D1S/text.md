# SOP-FLD-01: Flood Monitoring, Evacuation, Closures and Shelters

Tallis County Emergency Management Agency · Tallis County Sheriff's Office · Revision 4

## 1. Purpose

This procedure tells the EOC staff how to read river forecasts, how to decide on evacuation and closures, and how to run the evacuation, closure and shelter steps. It carries out Annex F.

## 2. Reading the forecast

The EOC reads three sources every hour once the Coordinator is in Monitoring: the observed stage at CFDI4 (the USGS gauge page), the National Weather Service hydrograph and text product for CFDI4, and the forecast discussion from the River Forecast Center. The text product lists the issuance time, the current stage, the forecast crest and its time, and a statement of the impacts. The EOC records each issuance in the River Status Board (a shared spreadsheet on the EOC wall screen) with: issuance time, observed stage, forecast crest, crest time, and change from the previous issuance.

Forecast crests carry uncertainty, mostly from rain that has not fallen yet. A revised forecast that moves a crest up by 2 feet or more, or earlier by 12 hours or more, is a "material revision": the Coordinator reconvenes the EOC and re-decides evacuation, closures and resources on the new issuance. The county does not delay a decision to wait for a better forecast when the order lead time (18 hours for Cedar Hollow) is running out.

## 3. Evacuation procedure for Cedar Hollow

1. The Coordinator records the decision: type (advisory or order), area, clearance time, time of decision, and the gauge reading, forecast issuance or report used.
2. TallisAlert text and voice go to the Cedar Hollow polygon. A Wireless Emergency Alert goes out for an order only. Voice calls take about 25 minutes for the whole county; the Cedar Hollow polygon takes 6 minutes.
3. The Sheriff's deputies and Cedar Ford Fire go door to door in Cedar Hollow beginning with the 22 households on the medical needs list; the list is kept by the Public Health nurse and held in the EOC.
4. Hollow Court Care Center (36 beds, Administrator Rosa Delgado) moves residents by the agreed vans and the county's three paratransit buses to Ferris Crossing Community Hall and then to the Meadowview Care Center in the neighboring county. The care home must start at least 12 hours before water.
5. Traffic: Hollow Road is made one-way outbound; barriers are placed by Public Works.
6. Reentry only after the Coordinator confirms the gauge below 17.0 ft and falling, the Fire Chief confirms utilities, and the Building Official has inspected.

## 4. Road closure procedure

Public Works (Alan Kessler, ext. 220) places barricades before the road floods, not after. The EOC gives each closure a basis and a stage. Reopening requires an inspection.

| Road or crossing | Floods at CFDI4 | Closed by |
|---|---|---|
| River Road south of Mill Street | 15.0 ft | County Public Works |
| Mill Street underpass | 17.0 ft | City of Cedar Ford |
| Route 38 bottoms | 19.0 ft | Iowa DOT with county barriers |
| Hollow Road | 19.5 ft | County |
| Levee Road (the Levee District access road) | 24.0 ft | Levee District |

The Cedar Ford Bridge (Route 38) is closed by Iowa DOT when the stage reaches 24.5 ft or on engineer's order for scour.

## 5. Sandbags, pumps and levee patrol

Requests for sandbags, sand, pumps, hoses and fuel, barriers and patrol crews go on an ICS 213 general message to the Logistics position and are logged: resource, quantity, delivery point and needed-by time. Needed-by is before the action stage at the delivery point (13.0 ft for the Levee Road staging site). The Levee District's own fill station at the county shop makes 3,000 bags an hour with the sand bagger; volunteers are directed to the shop, not to individual streets. Iowa HSEMD is asked for the rest of the bags and pumps through WebEOC.

## 6. Shelters

The Red Cross Iowa region opens Cedar Ford High School when the order is issued; the county's mass care lead is Deputy Coordinator Hannah Brandvold. The shelter includes: a general population area, an access and functional needs area with accessible cots and a power-dependent equipment charging station, and a pet area for 60 animals. Registration is by name only; no one is turned away. A pet owner may stay with their animal in the pet area. Shelter status is reported to the EOC every 4 hours with the count.

## 7. Facilities and livestock

The wastewater plant, the Tallis Prairie Cooperative (5 grain bins, 340 head of cattle at its feed yard at the river bend) and the water treatment plant are each asked, when the forecast crest is at or above moderate, to submit a plan to move stock and equipment at least 24 hours before the stage that floods them. The Coordinator logs each such move with the stage that floods it.

## 8. Messages and consistency

The PIO issues all public messages. Each message states: the category (Minor, Moderate or Major); the forecast crest with its time and issuance; the action asked of people; and where to get help. The PIO checks that the messages of the county, the city, the Levee District and the Red Cross agree on the crest and time before release. Rumors (a levee break, a closed shelter) are answered from the county's rumor log within 20 minutes.

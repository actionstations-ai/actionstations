# Player Briefing Packet
## Eighteen Hours — Rising Crest, Evacuation Clock & Levee Fight

Location: Tallis County Emergency Management Agency Emergency Operations Center, Cedar Ford, Tallis County, Iowa · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 06:00 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Overcast with light rain, 48°F, wind SE 10 mph, at the end of the wettest April in years. The ground is saturated across Tallis County.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is 06:00 CDT on Tuesday, 20 April 2027, a normal school and work day in Cedar Ford. Rain is falling steadily, and the Cedar River is having a spring rise. At 5:00 AM the CFDI4 gauge on the Route 38 bridge piers read 12.4 ft, still below the 13.0 ft action stage. The NWS Quad Cities 5:15 AM issuance is on the duty desk. Under EOP 5, TCEMA is at the **Monitoring** level. Coordinator Marcus Ellery and Levee Superintendent Bill Hoeksema are checking in by phone. Deputy Coordinator Hannah Brandvold is alone in the EOC at the Tallis County Courthouse annex, and PIO Josie Pruitt is on the way in. Levee and Drainage District No. 3 is running its normal patrol drive of the 3.8-mile levee. Pump Stations 1 and 2 are idle, and the Hollow Creek gated outlet is open. Morning commuters, school buses and farm traffic are using River Road, the Mill Street underpass, Route 38 through the bottoms and Hollow Road, and none of them has any restriction. The Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant is treating normal wet-weather flow. In Cedar Hollow, Hollow Court Care Center is starting its day shift, and Tallis Elementary and Cedar Ford High School are open. Public Works crews are out on routine rain-day work, and the Sheriff's Office and Cedar Ford Fire have normal shift staffing. The phones are quiet so far, but local businesses, farmers and parents have started watching the river and will want answers they can plan on.

- Cedar River at Cedar Ford (CFDI4): GREEN. 12.4 ft at 5:00 AM, below the 13.0 ft action stage. Gauge telemetry is reporting, and the NWS 5:15 AM issuance has been received and logged.
- Levee District No. 3: 3.8-mile levee, Pump Stations 1 and 2: GREEN. Routine patrol on county VHF channel 4. Landside toe dry, Hollow Creek gated outlet open with normal flow. Both pump stations idle on PUD power, and the Pump Station 1 day tank is full.
- Roads and crossings: River Road, Mill Street underpass, Route 38 bottoms, Hollow Road: GREEN. All open with no restrictions. Barricades and variable message signs are staged at Public Works and not deployed.
- TallisAlert, 911 and radio: GREEN. Normal 911 call load. TallisAlert, NOAA Weather Radio and county VHF are tested and working. No public messages sent today.
- Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant: GREEN. Treating normal wet-weather flow. Access road open, generator in its normal position, no bypass.
- Shelters, schools and EOC staffing: GREEN. Cedar Ford High School (400, with the field house pet area) and Ferris Crossing Community Hall (80) are not open. Schools are in session. The EOC is at Monitoring under EOP 5.

**The Anomaly:** **06:10 – Field Report by email (patrol.foreman@leveed3.example), from the District foreman to Superintendent Bill Hoeksema, cc TCEMA duty desk**

> "06:05 drive of the 3.8 miles. River is 12.4 at the bridge and up 0.3 since midnight. We're about 0.6 ft below action stage, likely there this afternoon. Toe is dry and the Hollow Creek gated outlet is open with flow normal. Stock count at the shop: 40,000 filled bags on pallets, and 2 of our 6-inch pumps are serviceable, both at Pump Station 1. The sand bagger works, but the sand pile is good for maybe 6 hours of bagging. PS1 day tank is full, so that's 36 hours of diesel once we start running. The flood fight plan calls for 80,000 bags and six pumps. Do we open the fill station today, and who's ordering the rest?"

Deputy Coordinator Hannah Brandvold has printed the email at the duty desk and put it next to the 5:15 AM NWS printout.

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep Tallis County running normally: roads open, schools in session, the wastewater plant treating and the public calm. At the same time, work out and resolve what the rising Cedar River means for Cedar Hollow, the levee and the county's critical facilities. Balance speed against verification. Act early enough to beat the lead times in EOP 6, base every decision on the latest forecast issuance and confirmed field reports, and don't let the public or partners act on rumor.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: EM Coordinator / EOC Command**— TCEMA Coordinator (EOC Command / Incident Commander) | You own the EOC activation level under EOP 5 and the evacuation, shelter and road-closure decisions under EOP 6. You also own requests to Iowa HSEMD and the final say on every public warning. | **Latest Issuance: base every decision on the most recent River Forecast Center issuance, and log the issuance time, the CFDI4 reading or the field report you used.** **Clock Before Water: count lead times back from when the river is expected to reach the trigger stage at CFDI4, not from the crest, and say out loud how many hours remain.** **Single Voice: send every public statement through the PIO and every outside request through the EOC. No position deals with the state or the media on its own.** |
| **Team B: Public Information Officer**— Public Information Officer | You own every public message on TallisAlert, the county website, Facebook and local radio. You check that the county, the city, the Levee District and the Red Cross are saying the same thing, and you keep the rumor log. | **Category Discipline: use only the flood category the CFDI4 forecast gives, and always quote the forecast crest with its time and issuance.** **Truth Over Reassurance: when a forecast changes, say "The forecast crest has been revised" and give the old and new numbers. Never soften a forecast to calm callers.** **Rumor Clock: answer any levee or shelter rumor within 20 minutes, using only facts the Levee Superintendent or the EOC has confirmed.** |
| **Team C: Levee District Superintendent**— Levee and Drainage District No. 3 Superintendent (Levee District) | You own the flood fight on the 3.8-mile levee. That covers patrols, sandbagging, seepage and sand boils, the Hollow Creek low spot and gated outlet, and Pump Stations 1 and 2. | **Trigger Discipline: step up patrols, close gates and stage sandbags at the stage triggers in the District plan, not by the clock or on gut feel.** **Report Early: report any boil, seepage, slump or crack to the Coordinator at once, with the station number (0+00 at Mill Street) and the stage. A boil with muddy water, or a slump, is an emergency report (Levee District plan 4).** **Fuel and Stock Ahead: order Pump Station 1 diesel through county Logistics at least 12 hours before the tank falls below one half. Tell the EOC early about any shortfall in bags, sand or pumps.** |
| **Team D: Sheriff and Fire Field Operations**— Sheriff and Cedar Ford Fire Chief (Sheriff and Fire Field Operations) | You own door-to-door notification, contact with medical-needs households, traffic control, 911 call surges and swift water rescue readiness across the county. | **Safety First: do not send crews or residents onto roads or crossings that are flooding. If dispatch and the field disagree, trust the field.** **Knock Every Door: when an evacuation is called, record contact with every home and medical-needs household, and report anyone who refuses to leave or cannot be reached.** **Verify Before Responding: send a unit to confirm any reported levee failure before starting a mass response, and pass what it finds to the EOC right away.** |
| **Team E: Public Works Director**— Public Works Director | You own road and crossing closures, barricades and variable message signs, county pumps, and coordination with the Iowa DOT liaison on Route 38. | **Close Before Water: close each road at its stated stage before water reaches it (EOP 6: River Road 15.0 ft, Mill Street underpass 17.0 ft, Route 38 bottoms 19.0 ft), even if motorists complain.** **Routes Matter: keep an evacuation route and a supply route open to the levee, the wastewater plant and the shelter. Tell the EOC before you cut any route.** **Report Status: give every closure the road name, its status, the stage at which it floods and the time, so the PIO can publish it.** |
| **Team F: Wastewater Plant Superintendent**— Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant Superintendent | You own operations at the Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant behind the levee. That covers protecting chemicals and the generator, keeping the access road usable, the bypass decision and the required regulatory notice. | **Protect Early: move portable chemicals and generators above 24.0 ft while the access road is still open, and don't wait for the river to force the move.** **Continuity vs. Containment: keep treating as long as it is safe, and base any bypass decision on the stage and the latest forecast, not on hope.** **Notify Through EOC: tell the Coordinator and the PIO before any bypass or notice to the Iowa Department of Natural Resources, so the public message matches.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio, phone or TallisAlert test call with "This is an exercise." Courthouse staff, dispatchers and residents who overhear us must not mistake a drill for a real flood warning.
- If a real emergency happens, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." The exercise stops at once and everyone turns to the real event.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to move the river and the clock forward. Accept the new time and stage, and act on the situation as it stands then.
- Treat every printed inject (NWS products, patrol field reports, emails, social media posts, photos and logs) as real intelligence and act on it as you would on the real thing, because in this room it is.

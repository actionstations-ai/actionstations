# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Eighteen Hours — Rising Crest, Evacuation Clock & Levee Fight

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Tallis County Emergency Management Agency Emergency Operations Center, Cedar Ford, Tallis County, Iowa · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for the Tallis County Emergency Management Agency (TCEMA) and its flood partners in Cedar Ford. Play runs four hours of exercise time, starting with the scenario clock at 06:00 CDT on Tuesday 20 April 2027. No one deploys, no barricades go up and no real alerts go out. Players say what they would do, who they would call and on what basis, and the SimCell answers as the outside world. Every forecast product in the inject deck is marked TEST and EXERCISE and must stay that way.

The injects simulate a natural hazard: a slow-rising spring flood on the Cedar River at Cedar Ford (CFDI4), after the wettest April in years. In **Phase 1 (0:00–1:00), The Rising Watch**, the forecast is minor (crest 18.2 ft). The first revision then raises it to moderate (21.8 ft). In **Phase 2 (1:00–2:00), Water in the Streets**, the river passes flood stage. River Road and the Mill Street underpass have to be closed on a stated stage, and the PIO has to fight a stale radio story. Phase 2 ends with the 12:00 Wednesday issuance: a major crest of 23.4 ft, and 19.0 ft reached about 18 hours earlier than the previous forecast. In **Phase 3 (2:00–3:00), The Eighteen-Hour Clock**, players must issue the mandatory Cedar Hollow order by 20:00 Wednesday against an uncertain forecast. They also handle the Hollow Court Care Center move, opening the shelter, the Iowa HSEMD request and the Pump Station 1 power loss. In **Phase 4 (3:00–4:00), Boil, Bypass, Return**, it is 08:00 Friday. Players deal with a muddy sand boil at station 18+50, a false "levee broke" post, the wastewater plant bypass decision, PS1 fuel, the Route 38 bridge scour closure, and finally the crest, damage assessment and a phased return.

Evaluators should watch whether each decision names the forecast issuance it rests on and is tied to a stage or a clearance time, not just the clock. Watch whether roads are closed before water reaches them, using the stages in SFMECS 4. Check that public messages use the category the current product states, and never a category the players work out for themselves. Watch whether the Levee District's emergency report (Levee District plan, section 4) reaches the Coordinator at once. Watch whether the wastewater plant moves its equipment above 24.0 ft before it has to decide on a bypass, and whether the Iowa Department of Natural Resources is told (TCCFFS 4). Finally, note how players cope with half the sandbags and a third of the pumps the plan calls for.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to set the EOC activation level and issue the mandatory Cedar Hollow evacuation order by 20:00 Wednesday, at least 18 hours before the river reaches 19.0 ft at CFDI4, and to name the River Forecast Center issuance and the gauge reading each decision is based on.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to send TallisAlert, website, radio and door-to-door messages that use the correct CFDI4 flood category and the latest forecast crest and time, state the old and new numbers when the forecast is revised, and answer the false levee-failure post from the rumor log within 20 minutes.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to carry out the stage triggers in the Levee District plan and the County EOP: close River Road, the Mill Street underpass, Route 38 bottoms and Hollow Road before the water reaches them, increase levee patrols, and report the district's judgement of the levee's condition once the forecast crest reaches 23.0 ft.
- **Infrastructure Protection:** Demonstrate the ability to protect Pump Station 1 and the Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant: order diesel fuel at least 12 hours before the tank falls below one half, decide the 24.0 ft bypass with notice to the Iowa Department of Natural Resources, and send the sand boil at station 18+50 up through the EOC to the Army Corps of Engineers and Iowa HSEMD as resource requests.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Set up islands (one table per team), arranged loosely in a U facing the main screen, to simulate the Tallis County EOC in the Courthouse annex. The open end of the U faces the screen and the facilitator.
- **Head of the U, Team A (TCEMA Coordinator / EOC Command):** Seat them closest to the screen and the whiteboard, so the Coordinator can see every team.
- **Left side, Team C (Levee District Superintendent) and Team F (Wastewater Plant Superintendent):** Seat them next to each other. They share the levee, the pump stations, the interior ponding and the flatbed truck, so they need to talk directly.
- **Right side, Team D (Sheriff and Fire Field Operations) and Team E (Public Works Director):** Seat them next to each other. They share the Hollow Road one-way route, the barricades, the closures and traffic control.
- **Rear table, Team B (Public Information Officer), set apart:** Put the PIO at a separate table behind the U, facing away from the screen. Main-screen injects do not reach them automatically. They must walk over to the Coordinator, or have a runner bring them the information, to get the current issuance and the measured stage. This simulates the real gap between operations and public information. It is what makes stale-number messages (the 18.2 ft "minor" story, I-07) and rumor responses (I-15b) a real test.
- **SimCell table:** Place it at the side of the room, with line of sight to every team and out of easy earshot. Players "call" the SimCell by walking over or using a simulated phone.
- **Evaluator positions:** Seat one evaluator at or behind each team table, with a clear view of the whiteboard.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and screen for main-screen injects: the TEST flood warnings, time-jump cards and the USGS gauge screenshot.
- A whiteboard or flip chart for the ICS-201, with a pre-drawn stage/time board. Label the rows: 15.0 River Road, 17.0 Mill St underpass, 19.0 Cedar Hollow / Route 38 bottoms, 19.5 Hollow Road, 22.0 / 23.0 / 24.0 levee triggers, 24.5 Route 38 bridge.
- A printed inject deck in MSEL order, with "deliver X minutes later" notes highlighted.
- Optional: simulated radios (for county VHF channel 4 and PW/S units) and desk phones or cell phones for SimCell calls.
- A wall clock showing both exercise time and scenario time.

**Materials for players:** Player handbook, objectives sheet, TCCFFS and SFMECS extracts, the Levee District stage-trigger table, the key contacts list (EOP 3), blank ICS-213 general message forms, levee patrol field report forms, a rumor log sheet and name tents.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**The SimCell role**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the deputy. If they say "I call the Weather Service," you are the NWS Quad Cities hydrologist. If they say "I call HSEMD," you are the regional duty officer. Answer in character, briefly, and in that party's interest. Do not step out of character to coach. Only answer calls the players actually make: if nobody calls Iowa DNR, Iowa DNR does not call them.

**Do not make it easy**
Real partners have their own constraints, so give players the friction those partners would really give. For example, when the Levee Superintendent or the Coordinator asks Iowa HSEMD "for sandbags and pumps" (I-13), the duty officer does not say yes. He asks which resource, how many, which delivery point and by what time. If the request goes in without a delivery point and a needed-by time, it goes to the bottom of the pile. The duty officer also says Guard sandbag crews will not be considered until the district submits a written statement of the levee's condition. In the same way, the fuel tanker driver at the Route 38 bottoms barrier (I-17) will not pass the closure without written authorization. Make players produce it. If players commit a resource the scenario says is short, such as the eight vans, the flatbed or the Prairie Barricade Rental stock, tell them it is not available, or it is already committed elsewhere.

**Managing time**
The scenario covers five days in four hours, so the clock jumps at 1:00, 2:00, 3:00 and at I-18. Use this script word for word:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down for a moment. We are jumping forward [30 minutes / to 07:00 Wednesday / to 14:00 Wednesday / to 08:00 Friday]. Here is where things stand: [read the time-jump card: CFDI4 stage, the forecast issuance in force, closures and conditions]. Anything you said you would do before the jump is done, unless I tell you otherwise. RESUME."

If a team is stuck on a minor detail, move them on: "Assume that is handled. What is your next decision?" If the room runs ahead of the MSEL, hold the next inject. If the room runs behind, compress the time but do not skip the Phase 3 decision point (I-11).

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says "I wouldn't use that system," "We don't do it that way," or "That gauge wouldn't fail," answer:
> "Noted. Put it in the parking lot for the hot wash. For today, the system works as written. Tell me what you would do with what you have in front of you."

Write the comment down for the after-action report and keep the exercise moving. Do not debate the scenario.

**Delivering injects**
Do not explain. Say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." Hand over the card or read it in the character's voice, then step back. Do not tell players what the inject means or what they should do. If they ask "Is this real?" or "Is this important?", answer in character: "I just took the message." Deliver noise injects (I-01b, I-13b, I-17b) with exactly the same seriousness as core injects.

**Exercise conventions**
- Start every call, radio message and printed product with **"This is an exercise."** Every forecast product carries TEST and EXERCISE markings. Never strip those markings, and never let a product leave the room.
- If a real emergency or a real-world issue arises (someone is hurt, a real call needs an answer, a real flood or weather warning is issued for this area), anyone may call **"REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD."** All play stops at once. The facilitator handles it, and play resumes only when the facilitator announces "Resume exercise."

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **NWS Quad Cities forecast office (DVN) hydrologist, speaking also for the North Central River Forecast Center forecast for CFDI4** — _Trigger: Any player calls or messages the Weather Service to ask what the CFDI4 forecast means, whether the crest will change, or when 19.0 ft will be reached, most likely after I-04, I-09 or during I-11._
  - Ask: "Which issuance are you working from? The 06:00 Tuesday product is two forecasts old. If you're quoting 18.2 ft, you're quoting a dead number."
  - Ask: "What decisions are you trying to make, and at what stage? Tell me your thresholds, like 19.0 ft for Cedar Hollow and 24.0 ft for the plant, and I'll give you timing against those. I can't tell you whether to evacuate."
  - Ask: "The crest is 23.4 ft, give or take 1.5 ft, and 19.0 ft arrives at 14:00 Thursday, give or take 4 hours, with the early side more likely. If you wait for the 18:00 issuance, does your 18-hour clock still work?"
  - Ask: "Who's checking the gauge? The USGS page for CFDI4 is provisional and hasn't updated since 07:00. Do you have eyes on the staff gauge?"
  - Concede only when: If the EOC gives its decision thresholds and asks for decision support, the hydrologist gives timing for each stage the county named and holds a short IDSS call with the EOC at each new issuance.
  - Concede only when: If the PIO asks, the hydrologist confirms which flood category the current product states, so county messages can use the same category. The hydrologist will not give the county a category of its own.
  - Concede only when: If the players ask about the upstream gauge (I-01b), the hydrologist confirms that the Gauge 2 maintenance gap does not affect CFDI4, and that missing upstream data does not mean zero flow.
- **City of Cedar Ford, City Administrator Fran Oyelaran, speaking for the city's emergency management and street crew** — _Trigger: Team A or Team E contacts the city about closing the Mill Street underpass, about Water Street basements, or about a joint public message, or does not answer the city's text in I-05._
  - Ask: "I've got four barricades and a two-man crew. What stage are we closing Mill Street on, and who's signing it? I'm not closing a state-funded underpass on a guess."
  - Ask: "You closed River Road at 07:30. Did you close it before the water got there, or after a commuter drove through it?"
  - Ask: "Water Street people are calling me, not you. Is the city saying minor, moderate or major? Your radio interview and my Facebook page had better use the same number."
  - Ask: "If Cedar Hollow goes mandatory, is the city's name on that order or just the county's? My council will ask."
  - Concede only when: If the EOC gives a written closure basis tied to a stage, such as 17.0 ft at CFDI4 per SFMECS 4, the city closes the Mill Street underpass on that stage and reports the time it closed.
  - Concede only when: If the PIO sends the city an approved message with the current issuance and category, the city posts it word for word on its own channels.
  - Concede only when: If Public Works confirms who will inspect the underpass before reopening, the city agrees not to reopen it on its own.
- **Iowa Homeland Security and Emergency Management (HSEMD) regional duty officer** — _Trigger: Team A or Team C asks the state for sandbags, pumps, Guard sandbag crews or a disaster declaration, most likely around I-13 or earlier if players think ahead._
  - Ask: "What exactly do you need: how many bags, how many 6-inch pumps, delivered where and needed by when? "As many as you can" goes to the bottom of the pile."
  - Ask: "Is it in WebEOC? I can't move anything off a phone call. And I need your declaration request by 18:00 if you want to be on tonight's proclamation."
  - Ask: "Where's the district's written statement on the levee's condition? No statement, no Guard crews."
  - Ask: "What have you already used locally? Have you exhausted your own sand, bags and contractors, or am I your first call?"
  - Concede only when: Once a WebEOC request names the resource, quantity, delivery point (for example, the district shop) and a needed-by time, the duty officer gives an estimated arrival time for the 40,000 bags and four pumps.
  - Concede only when: Once the Superintendent submits the written levee condition statement, Tallis is added to the list for Guard sandbag crews.
  - Concede only when: Once the declaration request is in by 18:00, Tallis County is included in the Governor's proclamation package.
- **US Army Corps of Engineers, Rock Island District, levee safety and geotechnical engineer** — _Trigger: Team C requests a technical assistance visit (Levee District plan, section 3, at 22.0 ft) or answers the I-13 email, or calls the Corps about the sand boil at station 18+50 in I-15._
  - Ask: "Who's asking? I need the request from the district as the levee sponsor, not from someone who heard about the levee secondhand."
  - Ask: "Give me the boil the way your patrol logged it: station number, distance from the toe, clear or muddy, size, flow, and the stage at the time. "There's a leak" doesn't help me."
  - Ask: "At the Hollow Creek low spot you're 1.1 ft lower than the rest of the crown. What is your freeboard there right now, and who is standing on it continuously?"
  - Ask: "Is the boil ringed? To what height? If you sealed it off, you just pushed the problem somewhere else."
  - Concede only when: Once the district formally requests the inspection, a geotechnical engineer is on site the next morning, or at once after a muddy boil is reported with a logged station and stage.
  - Concede only when: Once the patrol log is read back with the station, stage and boil description, the engineer advises ringing the boil to a height that slows the flow without sealing it, and watching the clear wet spot near 18+70.
  - Concede only when: After the crest, the engineer agrees to a written post-flood inspection recommendation for the Hollow Creek reach, which the declaration package can use.
- **Iowa Department of Natural Resources, wastewater field office duty staff** — _Trigger: Team F calls Iowa DNR about protecting the Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant or a possible shutdown and bypass at 24.0 ft (TCCFFS 4), or replies to the automated eDMR reminder (I-13b)._
  - Ask: "Are you telling me a bypass is happening, or that it might happen? Give me the start time, or the stage you'll start at, and how much flow is going untreated to the river."
  - Ask: "Who's downstream of your outfall? Have downstream communities and water users been told?"
  - Ask: "Where are your hypo and bisulfite totes right now? If they're still on the low pad and the yard floods, that's a second report."
  - Ask: "(If they bring up the eDMR) That's the compliance desk's automated reminder. I'm not the one to call about March paperwork. Tell me about this week."
  - Concede only when: Once the plant gives a stage-based plan with an estimated start time and the outfall location, DNR accepts a verbal notice now with a written follow-up.
  - Concede only when: Once the chemicals and generator are confirmed moved above 24.0 ft, DNR records the plant's protective actions in the file.
  - Concede only when: If the plant bypasses, DNR asks for a sampling plan and a return-to-service notice. DNR will discuss flexibility on the March eDMR deadline only after the bypass notice is on record.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** 3-Up, 3-Down: ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the exercise. Go around the room one team at a time (EOC, Levee District, Public Works, Fire/Sheriff, PIO, Public Health), write each item on the whiteboard as it is said, and don't debate them until every team has spoken.

**Key Discussion Questions for Eighteen Hours:**
- **The Call:** At I-11 (14:12–14:18 Wednesday), the hydrologist told you the 23.4 ft crest was ±1.5 ft and 19.0 ft could come as early as 10:00 Thursday. Then the Hollow Court Care Center administrator gave you until 15:00 to lock in Meadowview and the vans. Did you order the Cedar Hollow evacuation then, or did you wait for the 18:00 issuance? Which issuance and gauge reading did you write down as the basis, as SFMECS 3 requires? How did you weigh the care center's 12-hour start time against the cost of a false alarm? And what information would have let you decide faster? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **Trust:** At I-06, Patrol L-1 sent a staff-gauge photo reading about 14.9 ft at 07:17, while the USGS page was stuck at 14.81 ft from 07:00. Water was already over River Road and pickups were driving through it. Which reading did you trust, and when? Had Public Works barricaded River Road before the 15.0 ft trigger in SFMECS 4, or after the water got there? Did you treat the field photo as evidence to act on, or as a report to log? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Message:** From I-04b through I-07 and I-11b, the public heard three numbers: '20 feet already', the TV and radio's stale 18.2 ft minor crest, and 'whole south side out by midnight.' What did you tell the Tallis Valley Radio reporter before the 11:30 and 15:00 deadlines? Did your messages name the latest crest, its time, its category and the old and new numbers? Did you make clear that the order covered Cedar Hollow only and not everyone behind the levee? Did you answer each rumor within 20 minutes? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The Gap:** At I-12, the plan met the street. The man on oxygen at house 14 would not leave his dog. Two medical-needs households didn't answer. The Hollow Court night aides were stuck behind the one-way barrier on Hollow Road. Red Cross couldn't open the pet area before 19:00. Who in the EOC owned each of those problems? Did operations and the care center understand each other's limits? What would you change in SFMECS 3 steps 3–5 so that traffic control doesn't block the people the evacuation depends on? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Levee:** At I-15b, a video claimed the levee broke at Hollow Creek, and 911 took 61 calls in nine minutes with no confirmation. How long did it take the Levee District patrol to confirm or deny it? What did you give the 911 call-takers to tell callers? Had the district already reported its judgement of the levee's condition once the forecast crest reached 23.0 ft? And did the sand boil at station 18+50 and the Pump Station 1 fuel order go up through the EOC to the Army Corps of Engineers and Iowa HSEMD as resource requests, or stay inside the district? _(Objective: Infrastructure Protection)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Flooding and hydrology**: River gauges and forecast hydrographs, National Weather Service flood categories, flash flood warnings with damage tags, road closure and sandbag logistics, and decisions scored against the stage thresholds that matter. For any organization that has to act on a river forecast or a flash flood warning: counties, utilities, farms, schools, camps, transit and more.
  - Data sources: nwps, usgs-waterdata, fema-nfhl, nws-fim
  - Reference documents: NWS Manual 10-950: Definitions and General Terminology; NWS Instruction 10-922: Water Resources Products Specification; How Does a USGS Streamgage Work?; USGS Streamgaging; FEMA P-64: Emergency Action Planning for Dam Owners; FEMA P-946: Dam Inundation Mapping
- **Weather**: Real weather for any exercise: forecast and warning products (outlooks, watches, warnings, advisories, METARs and TAFs), instrument figures (radar, satellite, soundings, model guidance), replay of a past event from public archives or a simulated one, and warning verification. Used by a forecast office exercising its warnings, and by an airport, a utility, a venue or a school district that needs a storm to happen to it.
  - Data sources: nexrad-level2, spc-storm-reports, iem-warnings, iem-soundings, nhc-hurdat2
  - Reference documents: NWSI 10-511, WFO Severe Weather Products Specification; NWSI 10-512, National Severe Weather Products Specification; NWSI 10-501, WFO Statements, Summaries, Tables Products Specification; NWSI 10-601, Weather Forecast Office Tropical Cyclone Products; NWSI 10-602, Tropical Cyclone Coordination and Emergency Operations; NWSI 10-811, En Route Forecasts and Advisories; NWSI 10-813, Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts; NWSI 10-1701, Text Product Formats and Codes; NWSI 10-1702, Universal Geographic Code (UGC); NWSI 10-1703, Valid Time Event Code (VTEC); National Hurricane Center Product Description Document: A User's Guide to Hurricane Products; Impact-Based Warnings: Tornado; Hurricane Evacuation Studies (National Hurricane Program fact sheet); HURREVAC (National Hurricane Program fact sheet); Tri-State Integrated Warning Team Tabletop Exercise, Situation Manual; Tri-State Integrated Warning Team Tabletop Exercise, After-Action Report and Improvement Plan

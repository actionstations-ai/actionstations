# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Eighteen Hours** (Rising Crest, Evacuation Clock & Levee Fight)
Location: Tallis County Emergency Management Agency Emergency Operations Center, Cedar Ford, Tallis County, Iowa · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: EM Coordinator / EOC Command, Public Information Officer, Levee District Superintendent, Sheriff and Fire Field Operations, Public Works Director, Wastewater Plant Superintendent

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Making decisions on the latest forecast issuance before the lead time runs out, flood-fighting a levee with half the resources the plan needs, and keeping the public message tied to the measured stage and the right flood category.

**The Threat:** After a wet April, the Cedar River at Cedar Ford (CFDI4) is rising toward a spring flood. At STARTEX, 06:00 Tuesday 20 April 2027, the gauge reads 12.4 ft and a flood warning forecasts a minor crest of 18.2 ft at 06:00 Friday. After 2.4 inches of rain in the upper basin, the River Forecast Center raises the crest at 18:00 Tuesday to 21.8 ft (moderate) at 20:00 Friday. At 12:00 Wednesday it raises it again to 23.4 ft (major) at 06:00 Saturday, with the river reaching 19.0 ft at 14:00 Thursday. Cedar Hollow, the roads and crossings in the river reach, Pump Stations 1 and 2, the Levee and Drainage District No. 3 earthen levee and its Hollow Creek low spot, and the Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant behind the levee all come under threat.

**The Operational Impact:** The second revision leaves only eight hours, until 20:00 Wednesday, to issue the mandatory Cedar Hollow order. The order has to go out at least 18 hours before the river reaches 19.0 ft so the 12-hour clearance can finish in time. About 140 homes and 22 medical-needs households need door-to-door contact from deputies and Cedar Ford Fire. Hollow Court Care Center has to move 36 residents by van and paratransit bus, and Hollow Road becomes one-way outbound. TallisAlert voice calls take 25 minutes county-wide, so the PIO has to word each message with the correct category while residents and reporters call. Public Works has to close River Road at 15.0 ft, the Mill Street underpass at 17.0 ft, Route 38 bottoms at 19.0 ft and Hollow Road at 19.5 ft before the water reaches them. Motorists and farm families lose their routes, and Iowa DOT may close the Route 38 bridge at 24.5 ft or for scour. The Levee District has 40,000 of the 80,000 sandbags and 2 of the 6 pumps it needs, and Pump Station 1 has only 36 hours of fuel. Patrols move to around the clock. At 08:00 Friday, with the river at 22.0 ft, a patrol reports a sand boil carrying muddy water near station 18+50 at the Hollow Creek low spot. Within minutes, a social media post falsely claims the levee has failed, and 911 lines fill with calls from the 340 homes behind it. Tallis Prairie Cooperative needs 24 hours to move 340 cattle and secure its anhydrous ammonia tanks. The wastewater plant has to move chemicals and its generator above 24.0 ft and decide whether to bypass at 24.0 ft, which means notifying the Iowa Department of Natural Resources. Tallis Elementary closes, Cedar Ford High School opens as a shelter with a pet area, and Iowa HSEMD is asked for bags, pumps and a disaster declaration.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to set the EOC activation level and issue the mandatory Cedar Hollow evacuation order by 20:00 Wednesday, at least 18 hours before the river reaches 19.0 ft at CFDI4, and to name the River Forecast Center issuance and the gauge reading each decision is based on.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to send TallisAlert, website, radio and door-to-door messages that use the correct CFDI4 flood category and the latest forecast crest and time, state the old and new numbers when the forecast is revised, and answer the false levee-failure post from the rumor log within 20 minutes.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to carry out the stage triggers in the Levee District plan and the County EOP: close River Road, the Mill Street underpass, Route 38 bottoms and Hollow Road before the water reaches them, increase levee patrols, and report the district's judgement of the levee's condition once the forecast crest reaches 23.0 ft.
- **Infrastructure Protection:** Demonstrate the ability to protect Pump Station 1 and the Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant: order diesel fuel at least 12 hours before the tank falls below one half, decide the 24.0 ft bypass with notice to the Iowa Department of Natural Resources, and send the sand boil at station 18+50 up through the EOC to the Army Corps of Engineers and Iowa HSEMD as resource requests.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **TCEMA Coordinator (EOC Command / Incident Commander) (EM):** Owns the EOC activation level, the evacuation orders, shelter opening, requests to Iowa HSEMD for resources and a disaster declaration, and the final call on public warnings.
- **Public Information Officer (PIO):** Owns all public messages on TallisAlert, the county website, Facebook and local radio, checking that the county, city, Levee District and Red Cross messages agree, and keeping the rumor log.
- **Levee and Drainage District No. 3 Superintendent (Levee District) (LEV):** Owns the flood fight on the 3.8-mile levee: patrols, sandbagging, sand boils and seepage, the Hollow Creek low spot and gated outlet, and Pump Stations 1 and 2.
- **Sheriff and Cedar Ford Fire Chief (Sheriff and Fire Field Operations) (SFO):** Manages door-to-door evacuation notices, the 22 medical-needs households, traffic control on outbound Hollow Road, 911 call surges and swift water rescue readiness.
- **Public Works Director (PW):** Owns road and crossing closures, barricades and variable message signs, the Hollow Road one-way detour, county pumps and the coordination with the Iowa DOT liaison on Route 38.
- **Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant Superintendent (WWTP):** Manages plant operations behind the levee: moving chemicals and the generator above 24.0 ft, keeping the access road usable, the bypass decision and the notice to the Iowa Department of Natural Resources.

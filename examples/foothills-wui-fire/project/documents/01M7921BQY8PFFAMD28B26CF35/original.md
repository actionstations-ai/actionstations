# Foothills Electric Cooperative: Wildfire Mitigation and Public Safety Power Shutoff Plan

Foothills Electric Cooperative · Operations Director Rosalind Ames · Public Safety Partner Manager Daniel Okoye · Revision 3

## 1. The cooperative

Foothills Electric Cooperative is a member-owned utility serving 18,400 meters across Sawmill County and parts of the neighboring counties. Its 61 distribution circuits include 14 that run through the foothills in high fire-risk areas (Circuits 4 to 17). The operations center is at 20 Substation Road, Pine Gulch; the control room phone is 970-555-0178 and the public safety line for agencies is 970-555-0179.

## 2. Purpose of a shutoff

A Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) turns power off on selected circuits during dangerous fire weather to reduce the chance that electrical equipment starts a fire. It is a last resort. A shutoff has consequences: water pumps, cell towers, medical devices, gas furnaces and garage doors stop working. The co-op's plan weighs those consequences.

## 3. Thresholds

The Operations Director decides to de-energize a circuit when all of these hold: the National Weather Service has a Red Flag Warning for the area; the forecast or measured gusts at a co-op weather station are at or above **45 mph** (sustained 25 mph); fuel moisture is 8 percent or less; and field staff report that the circuit cannot be patrolled and cleared. Circuits 4 to 17 are scored in descending order; Circuits 6, 7, 11 and 12 are the first to go. Re-energization requires a patrol of the whole circuit by line crews and gusts below 35 mph for 2 hours; patrol takes 4 to 8 hours of daylight.

## 4. Notifications

| Audience | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Public safety partners (county emergency management, sheriff, fire districts, the water district, hospital, school district, telecommunications carriers) | 72 hours and 24 hours before, then at shutoff and at restoration | Phone call by Daniel Okoye, email, and the partner portal |
| All customers on affected circuits | 48 hours and 12 hours before, at shutoff, and at restoration | Text, email and automated call |
| Medical baseline customers (312 households) | 48 hours (phone call by a human), 12 hours, and at shutoff; a no-answer is retried 3 times and a door visit made for the 40 most critical | Phone call and door visit |
| Critical facilities (14 listed: the hospital, water well stations, 911, two cell towers, the care center) | 72 and 24 hours before, then on the hour during an outage | Direct call |

Each notice states the circuits or areas, the expected start, the expected duration (up to 24 to 48 hours), the cause (fire weather), the Community Resource Center (the Pine Gulch Fire Station community room, with charging, water and a cooling or heating area, open 07:00 to 21:00) and the phone number. Medical baseline customers are those who have registered a power-dependent medical device; they are also told how to get help from the Resource Center.

## 5. The partner coordination call

The Public Safety Partner Manager holds a partner call at 09:00 and 15:00 on days of a PSPS watch and the Emergency Manager joins. In it the co-op gives the circuits, the weather and the decision. The county checks the zone map: a circuit may serve a zone that is under an Evacuation Warning; when it is, the county tells the co-op and the co-op will not re-energize that circuit until the county says the zone is safe.

## 6. Fire on or near a de-energized circuit

If a fire is reported near an energized or de-energized line, the control room confirms the status of the circuit within 5 minutes and reports to the dispatcher whether the circuit is dead (with a switch operation time and a field verification). The co-op does not tell responders a line is dead until the switch is open and the line crew has tested it, because lines may be back-fed by customer generators.

## 7. After the event

Within 10 business days the co-op files a post-event report: the circuits, customers affected, the number of medical baseline customers contacted, the notification times, the duration, and the lessons. The report goes to the state utilities regulator and the county. The co-op counts the share of notices sent inside their windows and the medical baseline contact rate (the co-op's target is 100 percent of medical baseline customers reached or visited).

## 8. Mitigation program

Vegetation clearing on a 3-year cycle in the high-risk zones, covered conductors on 22 miles of line, 9 weather stations, and 6 fault-detection devices on Circuits 6, 7, 11, 12. The co-op also funds a cut-and-chip program with the county's fire districts.

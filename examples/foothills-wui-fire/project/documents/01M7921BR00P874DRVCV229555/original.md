# Sawmill County: Facilities and Resources

Reference sheet for exercise players · SCOEM · 2027

## 1. County and terrain

Sawmill County, Colorado: 24,000 residents, 810 square miles from the Front Range plains into the foothills. County seat Pine Gulch. One state highway (Highway 305) runs through the Mill Creek canyon to the plains; County Road 68 (Gulch Road) is the second way out of Pine Gulch and Kestrel Springs and is a single lane in two stretches.

## 2. Emergency operations center and dispatch

The EOC (400 Courthouse Plaza) is staffed by SCOEM and Sheriff's Office personnel; it has 18 workstations, a generator, amateur radio and a link to the state's WebEOC. The Regional Communications Center on the same campus handles 911 for the Sheriff, two fire districts and EMS; the dispatch supervisor is Joanne Pettibone (ext. 130). CAD records the zone status.

## 3. Fire and EMS

Pine Gulch Fire Protection District: 3 stations, 5 engines, 2 water tenders, 14 career firefighters and 30 volunteers. Kestrel Springs Volunteer Fire: 1 station, 2 engines. Mutual aid comes from the plains districts under a county-wide agreement. Helicopters and air tankers are requested through the interagency dispatch center (the Northern Colorado center) by the Incident Commander. The Sawmill County EMS ambulances (4 on a normal day) are staffed from the Pine Gulch base.

## 4. Healthcare

Pine Gulch Medical Center: 20 beds, critical access, 9 miles from Kestrel Springs; on a Red Flag day it keeps its oxygen reserve full and monitors air quality. Stonebridge Assisted Living (Kestrel Springs, KS-04, 40 residents, 12 non-ambulatory, Administrator Pauline Okafor, 970-555-0186) needs 120 minutes and four wheelchair vans to evacuate and a county bus; its only route is Kestrel Springs Road.

## 5. Schools

Sawmill County School District has Pine Gulch Elementary (140 students, in PG-03), Pine Gulch High School (480, the shelter) and Kestrel Springs K-8 (160, KS-02). On a Red Flag day buses are pre-positioned. The district decides on closures with the Sheriff.

## 6. Water

Pine Gulch Water District's wells are electric; its tank storage is 800,000 gallons. During a PSPS the Water District relies on tank storage and a generator at Well 2. Hydrants in Kestrel Springs depend on a pump station that has a generator for 8 hours. The Fire Chief tracks tank levels because a firefight and an evacuation can drain them.

## 7. Telecommunications

Two cell carriers have 6 towers in the foothills; two have battery backup for 4 hours and generators for 48 hours; four have only the batteries. Cell outages during a PSPS are reported to the county by the carriers.

## 8. Sheltering and animals

Pine Gulch High School (500), Kestrel Springs Recreation Center (80), Mountain View Fairgrounds (120 large-animal stalls, 60 small-animal crates). The Red Cross (Colorado and Wyoming region) staffs shelters; Mountain View Animal Rescue runs animal sheltering.

## 9. Outside parties

The National Weather Service forecast office in Boulder; the Colorado Division of Fire Prevention and Control; the interagency dispatch center and the Rocky Mountain Area Coordination Center; the US Forest Service (Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests); the Colorado Department of Transportation; the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (smoke); the Colorado Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management; neighboring counties; and the state utilities regulator.

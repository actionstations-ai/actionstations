# SOP-WF-01: Evacuation Notification, Traffic Control and Sheltering

Sawmill County Sheriff's Office · SCOEM · Regional Communications Center · Revision 3

## 1. Purpose

This procedure gives the dispatcher, the evacuation branch and the shelter team the steps to take for each zone status.

## 2. Evacuation Warning

1. The EOC (or the Incident Commander) sets the zone to Evacuation Warning in the zone map and records the time and basis.
2. The communications center sends an app push and a SawmillAlert text and voice message: "Evacuation Warning for Zone PG-03, Gulch Village. Be ready to leave. Wildfire near County Road 68. Leave now if you need help or have large animals. Sawmill County Sheriff."
3. Deputies begin notification in the zone on the medical needs and access list (the county keeps a voluntary registry of 214 households) and phone calls go to Stonebridge Assisted Living, Pine Gulch Elementary and the two campgrounds.
4. Large animal owners are asked to go to Mountain View Fairgrounds. The county's animal evacuation team (volunteers) stages at the fairgrounds.

## 3. Evacuation Order

1. The Sheriff or the Incident Commander orders the zone. The dispatcher records the time, sets the zone to Evacuation Order in the map, and sends a WEA, SawmillAlert text and voice message, a push and the EAS script. The WEA reads (360 characters or less): "EVACUATION ORDER Zone PG-03 Gulch Village. Wildfire approaching. Leave NOW via County Road 68 east. Do not return. Info: sawmillcounty.example/ready."
2. Law enforcement and fire sweep each house on a route plan. A house is marked with a single orange flag or tape and the address is logged; no house is entered by force without the Sheriff's authority.
3. Traffic control points are staffed at Gulch Road and Highway 305, at Kestrel Springs Road and the canyon entrance, and at County Road 68 and Mill Creek Bridge. Outbound lanes only for evacuation: contraflow on Highway 305 is ordered by the Undersheriff with the Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) and takes about 25 minutes to set up.
4. The Undersheriff reports zone clearance progress to the EOC every 15 minutes: houses swept, houses contacted, people refusing to leave.
5. People who refuse are told the risk, their names recorded, and not forced.

## 4. Shelter in Place

If the road is lost or evacuation would be more dangerous (an example is the Highway 305 canyon), the Sheriff orders Shelter in Place for the zone with a refuge: the Pine Gulch High School parking lot and the Kestrel Springs Recreation Center. The message tells people what to do: close all windows and doors, move to the room with the fewest windows, remove curtains, keep water and a phone. Dispatchers answer every call; 911 does not tell a caller their house is safe.

## 5. Roads

CDOT controls the state highways; the county Public Works Director controls the county roads. A road status (Open, Controlled, Closed, Contraflow) has a time. The county shares one list of road statuses on the hour with CDOT, the fire districts, the school district and the utility.

## 6. Shelters

- **Pine Gulch High School** (capacity 500; HVAC with MERV 13 filters; accessible; 20 pet crates). Opened by the Red Cross with the school district.
- **Mountain View Fairgrounds:** large animals and trailers; Mountain View Animal Rescue (a fictional nonprofit) runs it.
- **Kestrel Springs Recreation Center:** 80 people; a clean air shelter for people with respiratory conditions.

The Mass Care lead reports each shelter's count to the EOC every 2 hours.

## 7. Messages and rumors

The county never repeats a fire agency perimeter or acreage as its own; it quotes the Incident Commander and the time. The PIO keeps a rumor log; examples: "Kestrel Springs is burning" (not confirmed); "The Mill Creek Bridge is closed" (confirm with Public Works before replying). A zone is never described as "safe" while the fire is active; the county says the zone has been "repopulated" after the joint Sheriff and fire decision.

## 8. Repopulation

The Incident Commander and the Sheriff approve the return of residents. Sweeps check for hazards (downed lines, hot spots, hazard trees). The Utility, the Water District and the Fire Chief report. The county announces a time and a zone list; residents show identification. Re-entry may be escorted for homes in the burned area.

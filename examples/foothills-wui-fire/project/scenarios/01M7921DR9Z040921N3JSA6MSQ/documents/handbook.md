# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## One Road Out — PSPS, Evacuation Timing & Shared Zone Status

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Sawmill County Emergency Operations Center, 400 Courthouse Plaza, Pine Gulch, Sawmill County, Colorado · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise for the Sawmill County Office of Emergency Management and its partners. It runs for 4 hours of play. The scenario clock starts at 05:00 MDT on Wednesday 14 April 2027 and runs to 20:00 MDT, with three scripted time jumps in between. Players stay in their real roles: Emergency Manager / EOC Command, Sheriff and Evacuation Branch, PIO, Dispatch Supervisor, Pine Gulch FPD Fire Chief and the Foothills Electric Cooperative Operations Director. They talk through what they would decide, who they would call and what they would say. Nothing is deployed and no real alert goes out. Every weather, FIRMS and AirNow product in the deck is simulated and marked EXERCISE.

The injects simulate a natural hazard. First comes a Red Flag Warning day, with west gusts to 58 mph and relative humidity near 9 percent, which forces a Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) decision on Circuits 6, 7, 11 and 12. Then a wildfire starts at Coyote Draw on County Road 68 and grows to about 2,900 acres toward Gulch Village (PG-03) and Kestrel Springs. The phases run as follows:
- **Phase 1, Red Flag Morning (0:00–1:00, 05:00–09:00):** the 06:00 shutoff call against the co-op plan section 3 thresholds, and medical baseline outreach.
- **Phase 2, Smoke at Coyote Draw (1:00–2:00, 13:20–14:40):** size-up, confirming the line is dead before crews engage, getting Evacuation Warning out early and starting Stonebridge moving.
- **Phase 3, Go Now or Shelter (2:00–3:00, 15:00–16:15):** the single decision point, the PG-03 order at the 15:10 latest order time. Meanwhile the Mill Creek Bridge is blocked, contraflow is not yet set up, cell sites fail and Unified Command forms with DFPC.
- **Phase 4, Holding the Line (3:00–4:00, 17:00–20:00):** a mistaken re-energization in PG-01, pump station fuel, regulator and state notifications, and a phased restoration plan.

Evaluators should watch timing more than outcomes. Was each zone status set before its latest order time, which is projected arrival minus clearance time? Did Stonebridge (KS-04, 120-minute clearance) start moving before any order? Did shutoff and re-energization decisions stay tied to zone status, as the co-op plan section 5 partner call requires? Did every outward message name its zones, use the same status words and quote one acreage with a time? Note who owned each decision and where it was logged. Note when players assume a call was made but never made it, for example the unacknowledged bus and ambulance request in I-14.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to set Evacuation Warning, Evacuation Order or Shelter in Place for PG-03, KS-04 and the CR zones by the latest order time (projected fire arrival less zone clearance time, issued 30 minutes earlier where possible, per EOP 4), including moving Stonebridge Assisted Living before any order and deciding on shelter in place in the Highway 305 canyon.
- **Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to tie the Foothills Electric Cooperative de-energization and re-energization decisions on Circuits 6, 7, 11 and 12 to evacuation zone status, so that dispatch confirms line status before crews engage, no circuit is restored in a zone under Evacuation Warning, and Well 2, the Kestrel Springs pump station and the 71 medical baseline households are protected.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to put out one set of zone statuses, acreage and road statuses that the Sheriff, the fire agency, the co-op, the school district and Pine Gulch Medical Center all use, through WEAs of 360 characters or fewer, SawmillAlert and media briefings, and to correct the false report that Kestrel Springs is burning.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply the EOP 5 EOC decisions and the SENTCS 3 and 5 procedures (sweep reporting every 15 minutes, traffic control points, Highway 305 contraflow with CDOT, the hourly shared road status list, opening shelters at Pine Gulch High School and Mountain View Fairgrounds) when the Mill Creek Bridge is blocked and the foothill cell sites fail.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout: islands simulating the Sawmill County EOC in Pine Gulch.** Place four tables in a loose arc facing the main screen. The arc reproduces the EOC floor, with partner liaisons seated in it.
- **Center table (EOC Command):** Team A, Emergency Manager. Put this table nearest the whiteboard, because it owns the zone board and the decision log.
- **Left table (Law enforcement):** Team B, Sheriff / Evacuation Branch. Seat them next to Team E so that requests for evacuation have to be spoken across a short gap and can be heard and logged.
- **Right-front table (Fire):** Team E, Pine Gulch FPD Fire Chief. Give them the projection map of Coyote Draw, PG-03, KS-04 and the CR zones.
- **Right-rear table (Co-op):** Team F, Foothills Electric Cooperative Operations Director. Set this table slightly apart to simulate the co-op's own operations center. They reach the county by "phone" (walking to the SimCell desk) or through the 09:00 and 15:00 partner calls.
- **Side table (Dispatch):** Team D, Dispatch Supervisor, representing the Regional Communications Center. They receive radio and 911 injects and must relay them to Teams A, B and E themselves.
- **Rear table, isolated (Public Information):** Team C, PIO. Place it at the back of the room, out of earshot of the zone board. The PIO must walk to the EOC Command and Fire tables to confirm zone statuses and acreage before releasing anything. This reproduces the real friction behind the scenario's main failure mode: the reporter, the co-op, the school district and social media each holding a different number and different zone statuses. Evaluators should log every message the PIO drafts without first confirming it in person.
- **SimCell / Controller desk:** front corner, with a clear sightline to every table and the inject deck face down.
- **Evaluator positions:** one evaluator standing behind each pair of tables, with a clipboard and a timing sheet keyed to the MSEL clock.

**Equipment needs**
- Projector and screen for main-screen injects: the NWS fire weather message, simulated FIRMS and AirNow figures, the Channel 9 chyron, social media posts and the ICS 209-style summary.
- Whiteboard (or two) for the ICS-201 and a zone status board. Use columns for zone ID, status, time set, set by and basis, listed for PG-01, PG-03, KS-02, KS-04, CR-01 to CR-18 and the Highway 305 canyon. Add a strip for the shared road status list.
- Printed inject deck, one envelope per inject, marked with inject ID, time and recipient team.
- Optional simulated radios or phones: two handheld radios (Dispatch and SimCell) and a desk phone or bell at the SimCell desk for "calls".
- Wall clock set to scenario time, and a time-jump card for each of I-06, I-12 and I-17.

**Materials for players:** zone map with clearance times, circuit-to-zone list, co-op plan section 3 thresholds sheet, blank ICS-201, decision log, message templates, contact roster, name tents.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role as SimCell.** You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the deputy. If they say "I call the Weather Service," you are the Boulder fire weather forecaster. If they call DFPC, CDOT, Channel 9, the PUC, the neighboring county, Stonebridge, the water district, Peakline Wireless or High Plains Fuel, you are that person. Answer only what that person would know at that scenario time, in that person's interest. If players do not call someone, that person does not act. A request nobody makes stays unfilled.

**Do not make it easy.** Outside parties do not volunteer the answer, and they do not accept vague requests. Example: at I-07, Battalion 1 asks whether the Gulch Road line is dead. If Dispatch calls you as the co-op control room and asks "is it dead?", do not say yes. Answer as the operator: "The breaker opened at 08:02, but the Coyote Draw tap hasn't been field-tested, the ranch generator's transfer switch is unknown, and my nearest crew is 25 minutes out." Make the players decide whether Brush 33 goes under the line. The same applies at I-10: Stonebridge's van company needs an hour, and nobody "already" booked the county bus unless a player actually ordered it.

**Managing time.** Keep each phase to its hour. When a phase ends, or discussion stalls, read the time-jump announcement word for word:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. [Read the new time and situation from the inject card.] Decisions you were discussing have been carried out as you described them. Anything you did not decide did not happen. RESUME."

The scripted jumps are I-06 (to 13:20), I-12 (to 15:00) and I-17 (to 17:00). Use the same format for any shorter jump you need. Before you resume, update the zone board only with what the players actually set.

**Handling artificialities.** If a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "we don't do it that way," reply:
> "Noted, and thank you. Write that on your issue card for the hot wash. For the next 10 minutes, tell me what you *would* use and play it that way."

Do not argue about the design. Log the comment, because it is often a real finding.

**Delivering injects.** Do not explain. Hand the card over and say "This just came in." For projected items, say "This is on the main screen." Read radio and phone injects aloud in character. Do not hint at what the inject means, which team should act or whether it is a red herring. I-08 (burn barrel), I-14b (fairgrounds booking) and I-19b (fireworks stand) are delivered just like the others.

**Exercise conventions.**
- Every spoken call, radio message, email and post starts or ends with "This is an exercise." All projected products carry EXERCISE / SIMULATED markings. No player sends anything through SawmillAlert, WEA, EAS, a real social media account or a real phone number.
- If a real emergency or safety issue occurs in the building or the real world, anyone calls **"REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD."** Play stops immediately. Resume only when the lead controller says "Resume exercise."

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **National Weather Service Denver/Boulder forecast office, fire weather desk (forecaster on duty)** — _Trigger: The EM or the co-op Operations Director calls Boulder after I-01 to ask whether the 45 mph gusts will really arrive by 09:00, or asks for a spot forecast or IDSS briefing once the Coyote Draw Fire starts (I-07 / I-09)._
  - Ask: ""I can give you confidence, not certainty. Moderate to high that gusts top 45 by mid-morning, and the timing could slip an hour either way. What decision are you making, and by what time? Tell me that and I'll brief to it.""
  - Ask: ""You want a spot forecast for Coyote Draw? Send me the request with the fire's latitude and longitude, the elevation, the size and the time you need it. I can't build it from 'near Gulch Road.'""
  - Ask: ""Do your co-op stations at Coyote Ridge and Gulch Saddle report to us? If I can't see your observations, I'm forecasting blind on the canyon mouths.""
  - Ask: ""Who on your end gets my wind-shift update: the Fire Chief, the EOC or both? If I call one number and it doesn't reach the evacuation branch, that's on your side.""
  - Concede only when: Once the caller names the decision and its deadline, gives a scripted IDSS briefing with gust timing (strongest 9 AM to 4 PM along the foothills and canyon mouths) and the confidence level.
  - Concede only when: After a properly formatted spot forecast request with location and size, delivers a spot forecast. It keeps west-northwest winds of 28–31 mph gusting near 50 through the afternoon and gives a slow decrease after 20:00, with gusts below 35 mph from about 22:00.
  - Concede only when: Once the co-op agrees to share its station observations, adds Coyote Ridge and Gulch Saddle to the briefing and offers a callback if a wind shift is forecast.
- **Emergency manager of the neighboring plains county** — _Trigger: The players call to coordinate mutual aid or sheltering, or the neighboring EM calls the Sawmill EOC at 19:10 (I-20) about 140 foothills vehicles and 22 households asking for shelter at its fairgrounds rest stop._
  - Ask: ""I've got 22 households at my fairgrounds saying they were ordered out. Were they? Which zones: PG-03, KS-04, the CR zones? I need your zone list, not what they heard on Channel 9.""
  - Ask: ""If I open a shelter for your people, who's paying, and is that under a mutual aid request or just a phone call? I need it in writing to my EOC.""
  - Ask: ""Channel 9 says 3,000 acres and Kestrel Springs under evacuation. Your Nextdoor says 1,200. What number do I give my commissioners?""
  - Ask: ""Is anything coming my way? Do I need to stand up my own traffic control on the plains side of Highway 305?""
  - Concede only when: After receiving the current zone status list with the time it was set, agrees to direct those households to the Pine Gulch High School shelter or to hold them overnight.
  - Concede only when: After a written mutual aid or shelter request from the Sawmill EOC, opens an overflow site of up to 40 cots, which helps close the 31-cot gap.
  - Concede only when: Once given a single acreage figure with a time and source from Unified Command, uses only that figure in its own public messages.
- **Colorado Division of Fire Prevention and Control (DFPC) Northeast Region duty officer, also speaking for the Northern Colorado interagency dispatch center on resource status** — _Trigger: The Fire Chief orders air and a strike team through Northern Colorado (I-09), or asks DFPC for help once the fire is beyond district capacity. DFPC also calls proactively at 15:30 (I-14)._
  - Ask: ""Before I assume the lead, I need your current size-up: acres, containment, rate of spread, structures threatened and the time it was mapped. 'Big and running' doesn't go on a delegation.""
  - Ask: ""Under a Unified Command with the Sheriff, who runs the Evacuation Branch and the traffic control points, and who signs off on acreage? I need names before my IC trainee walks in at 16:15.""
  - Ask: ""Your Type 1 helicopter is unable to fill at preparedness level 3. What's your plan without it? And who acknowledged the open county bus and ambulance request for Stonebridge? My board shows nobody did.""
  - Ask: ""Are you going to ask for a Fire Management Assistance Grant? Then I need the threat to structures and infrastructure documented now, while the fire is still threatening them. A count after the fact won't support the request.""
  - Concede only when: After a complete size-up with a time stamp, accepts the fire lead and forms Unified Command with the Sheriff at the Pine Gulch EOC. The handover takes effect at 16:15.
  - Concede only when: After the county names its Evacuation Branch lead and a single acreage authority, confirms ST-CO-4417 (ETA 16:20) and the two SEATs from Fort Collins-Loveland (first drop about 15:55), and re-orders the Type 1 helicopter.
  - Concede only when: If threatened structures, the pump station and the critical infrastructure are documented, supports moving an FMAG request through the state.
- **Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) Region 4 traffic operations supervisor** — _Trigger: The Sheriff or EM asks CDOT about the stalled hay truck at the Highway 305 canyon mouth (I-04), or calls for Highway 305 contraflow or road closures during the Mill Creek Bridge block (I-13)._
  - Ask: ""Contraflow takes 25 minutes from your go to set the cones and get a pilot car through the canyon. Is this a go? And who's authorizing: the Sheriff, or the Fire Chief? I need one name.""
  - Ask: ""Smoke is down to a quarter mile at the canyon mouth. If you send people up 305 before contraflow is in, they're in a single lane under the smoke. Have you weighed shelter in place for the canyon?""
  - Ask: ""Your fire, your co-op and your school district each sent me a different road list. Which one is official? I'm not posting closures on the state message boards from three sources.""
  - Ask: ""Who's staffing the inbound end of the contraflow? I need your deputies at both ends before my crew starts setting cones.""
  - Concede only when: On a clear go from the named authority, starts Highway 305 contraflow (in place 25 minutes later) and posts it on CDOT message boards and COtrip.
  - Concede only when: Once deputies are confirmed at both ends, runs the pilot car and holds contraflow until the Sheriff releases it.
  - Concede only when: After it receives the EOC's single shared road status list with an issue time, mirrors that list on state boards and stops using partner lists.
- **Jenna Okafor, reporter, Channel 9 (Front Range regional TV)** — _Trigger: The PIO returns the 14:36 media slip (I-10b), or Channel 9 calls back after its 16:17 chyron (I-16b) or before the state representative's 18:30 appearance (I-18b)._
  - Ask: ""I'm on at five and my deadline is 15:00. I've got a scanner page saying 1,200 acres and Gulch Village being evacuated NOW. Confirm or deny, on the record, in the next ten minutes.""
  - Ask: ""Is Gulch Village under an order or a warning? What's the difference for my viewers? And which zones, by name? Your neighbors at the school district told callers 1,500 acres.""
  - Ask: ""Did the power shutoff start this fire? The caller said flames were under the line. If you won't answer, I'll say the county and the co-op declined to comment.""
  - Ask: ""The power came back on in PG-01 while it was under a warning. The state representative is going on my air at 18:30 to say the shutoff was mishandled. Do you want to respond before she does?""
  - Concede only when: Given a single acreage with a time and source and the zone statuses named by zone ID, runs those figures and corrects the 3,000-acre chyron on the next update.
  - Concede only when: Given a scheduled briefing time and a named spokesperson (the PIO or a Unified Command spokesperson), holds the 'declined to comment' line and promises to air the joint statement.
  - Concede only when: After a joint county–co-op statement on the PG-01 re-energization that names what happened, when it was de-energized again and the zones affected, airs it next to the representative's comments.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during play. Go around the room one team at a time (Sheriff, fire, co-op, EOC, PIO, schools and hospital, Red Cross) and write every answer on the whiteboard in two columns before anyone discusses them, so the loudest voice doesn't decide what the lessons are.

**Key Discussion Questions for One Road Out:**
- **The Call:** At I-13 (15:10), Battalion 1 told you the latest order time for PG-03 was now. At the same moment the Mill Creek Bridge was blocked, and CDOT needed 25 minutes to set up Highway 305 contraflow under quarter-mile smoke. At 14:30 (I-09), the chief had already given you a 14:50 EOC push. What stopped you from ordering PG-03 and the CR zones at 14:50? When did you decide between an order and shelter in place for the people who would end up in the 305 canyon, and where was Stonebridge Assisted Living at that point? What one piece of information would have let you decide 20 minutes earlier? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Gap:** At 05:15 (I-02), the station readings of 38 and 37 mph gusts were still below the co-op's 45 mph / 25 mph trigger, but the models and Dale Ostrowski's field report both pointed past it. Did you de-energize on the forecast or wait for the measured gust, and did the county EOC know your answer before six? Then at I-18b, power came back in PG-01 while the zone was still under warning, and families started driving back from the shelter. Who checked the zone status before that restore? Did dispatch ever confirm line status to crews working in the zones? _(Objective: Coordination)_
- **One Number:** By 16:17 (I-16b), the public had three different acreage figures: 1,500 from the school district, 3,000 on the Channel 9 chyron, which cited the co-op outage map, and about 2,300 at the fire line, plus a fake photo saying Kestrel Springs was burning. Who owned the single set of acreage, zone statuses and road statuses, and how fast did it reach the co-op call center, the school district and Pine Gulch Medical Center? How long did it take to put out a correction in a WEA of 360 characters or fewer, and what did the 911 callers asking which way to go hear in the meantime? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **Before You Knew:** At 06:36 (I-03b), Nextdoor was saying Well 2 would shut down at 8 and Gulch Village was being evacuated. At 14:36 (I-10b), Channel 9 had a 15:00 deadline and was asking whether the shutoff started the fire. What did you tell the public and the media before you had confirmed facts? Did your early holding statements about water, the Community Resource Center and the 71 medical baseline households hold up later? What would you say differently to the state representative's office by 18:15? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **Trust the Plan:** With the Mill Creek Bridge blocked and the foothill cell sites down, which of the SENTCS 3 and 5 procedures actually ran: sweep reports every 15 minutes, traffic control points, the hourly shared road status list and the shelter openings at Pine Gulch High School and Mountain View Fairgrounds? At what moment did you stop trusting the reports reaching the EOC? How did you decide on the 31 children in the unpowered elementary gym at AQI 174 (I-16)? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Wildfire**: Wildfire, power shutoff and smoke for exercises: the terms and sequence fire agencies, sheriffs, emergency managers and utilities use, the products they issue (incident summaries, zone-based evacuation notices, road and shelter boards, shutoff notices, smoke advisories), the decisions they make and the clocks they are measured on. Includes perimeter and spread maps with evacuation zone status, circuit and customer boards, AQI series, and simulated fires. Red flag warnings and fire weather come from the weather toolkit; floods after a fire from the flooding toolkit. Stays at coordination level: it carries no fireline tactics.
  - Data sources: wfigs-perimeters, firms-hotspots, hms-smoke, airnow-observations
  - Reference documents: NWSI 10-401, Fire Weather Services Product Specification; NWSI 10-403, Fire Weather Services Coordination and Outreach; Technical Assistance Document for the Reporting of Daily Air Quality: the Air Quality Index; Air Quality Guide for Particle Pollution; NWCG Incident Response Pocket Guide (IRPG), PMS 461; Wildfire Smoke Guide for Public Health Officials (EPA-452/B-26-001); Marshall Fire Operational After-Action Report; CPUC Decision 19-05-042, Phase 1 de-energization (power shutoff) guidelines; Ready, Set, Go! Used by a forecast office exercising its warnings, and by an airport, a utility, a venue or a school district that needs a storm to happen to it.
  - Data sources: nexrad-level2, spc-storm-reports, iem-warnings, iem-soundings, nhc-hurdat2

# Player Briefing Packet
## One Road Out — PSPS, Evacuation Timing & Shared Zone Status

Location: Sawmill County Emergency Operations Center, 400 Courthouse Plaza, Pine Gulch, Sawmill County, Colorado · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 05:00 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Still dark, 41°F, clear sky, light west breeze. Relative humidity is 18 percent and falling. The NWS Red Flag Warning for the Sawmill County foothills is in effect from 8 AM to 8 PM MDT.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is 05:00 MDT on Wednesday 14 April 2027, an ordinary spring weekday in Sawmill County. The SCOEM EOC in Pine Gulch is at normal status with the morning duty staff: Emergency Manager Elena Marchetti and PIO Maya Lindqvist. Sheriff Thomas Briggs and Undersheriff Raymond Cho are on call. There are no fires in the county, and all 41 evacuation zones (PG-01 to PG-14, KS-01 to KS-09, CR-01 to CR-18) show no status on the Sawmill Ready zone map. Foothills Electric Cooperative has sent its 72-hour and 24-hour partner notices and its 48-hour and 12-hour customer notices for a possible Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) on Circuits 6, 7, 11 and 12. Those circuits serve 3,100 customers, including 71 medical baseline households and Pine Gulch Water District Well 2. The co-op's de-energization decision is due at 06:00. If it goes ahead, the Community Resource Center in the Pine Gulch Fire Station community room opens at 07:00. Load is normal for a spring weekday. The Regional Communications Center has had quiet overnight traffic, and every foothill cell site and fire station reports normal. The morning is about to get busy: Pine Gulch Elementary buses (140 students) roll at 07:15, commuters will soon be on the one-road-out foothill routes, and the co-op's customer care line expects calls once the decision is announced.

- Co-op SCADA and Circuits 6, 7, 11, 12: GREEN: All circuits energized, normal spring weekday load. Partner and customer PSPS notices sent. Decision due 06:00.
- Kestrel Springs pump station generator: GREEN: On commercial power. Generator on standby with a full tank (8 hours of fuel). Hydrant pressure normal.
- Foothill cell sites (battery backup): GREEN: All sites on commercial power with batteries fully charged.
- Highway 305 contraflow and Mill Creek Bridge TCPs: GREEN: All roads open. No traffic control points staffed. Contraflow plan on file, not activated.
- SawmillAlert / WEA and Sawmill Ready zone map: GREEN: Online and tested. All 41 zones show no status. CAD synchronized.
- Pine Gulch High School shelter, Fairgrounds stalls, public mood: GREEN: Shelter and Mountain View Fairgrounds stalls available, not activated. No rumors circulating. Call volume normal.

**The Anomaly:** **05:15 MDT, phone call to the co-op Operations Director from Line Patrol Supervisor Dale Ostrowski, with the co-op's weather station feed**

The co-op system meteorologist's routine feed shows the **Circuit 6 Coyote Ridge** station at a 38 mph gust (22 mph sustained) and the **Circuit 11 Gulch Saddle** station at a 37 mph gust (21 mph sustained). Both are still below the trigger. Model guidance puts gusts at 45–52 mph at both stations by 09:00.

Dale calls in from the field: *"Fuel sticks on Circuits 7 and 12 read 7 percent. On Circuit 12 above Kestrel Springs, I can't get a bucket truck up the right-of-way in this wind, and once the gusts hit we can't patrol or clear that line. Do you want my crews staged or sent home? I need an answer by six."*

**YOUR MISSION:** Maintain power, water, communications, school operations and public trust across Pine Gulch, Kestrel Springs and the County Road 68 corridor while investigating and resolving whatever today's conditions bring. You must balance service against safety: keeping customers, wells and towers powered against shutting off circuits that could start a fire. You must also balance speed against verification: getting zone statuses and public messages out before they are too late, without sending anything that is wrong or that doesn't match the shared board.

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio call, phone call and message with "This is an exercise". Co-op, county and dispatch lines connect to the real public, and this prevents real-world alarm or a mistaken shutoff or evacuation.
- If a real emergency occurs at any time, whether a real fire, a medical problem or a real 911 surge, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD". The exercise stops immediately so everyone can respond to the real event.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to skip ahead. When this happens, update your clocks, zone timelines and fuel and battery estimates to the new time before you continue.
- Treat every printed inject, including weather products, co-op status boards, call logs, social media posts, photos and field reports, as real intelligence and act on it. Products marked EXERCISE stay inside this room and must never be sent on a live channel.

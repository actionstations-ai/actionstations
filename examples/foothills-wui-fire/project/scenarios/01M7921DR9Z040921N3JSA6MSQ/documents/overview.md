# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**One Road Out** (PSPS, Evacuation Timing & Shared Zone Status)
Location: Sawmill County Emergency Operations Center, 400 Courthouse Plaza, Pine Gulch, Sawmill County, Colorado · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Emergency Manager / EOC Command, Sheriff and Evacuation Branch, Public Information Officer, Dispatch Supervisor, Fire Chief, Electric Cooperative Operations Director

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Ordering evacuations early enough, measured against projected fire arrival and zone clearance time. Keeping power shutoff decisions and evacuation decisions tied together. Giving the public one consistent message, so the Sheriff, the fire agency, the co-op, schools and the hospital all use the same zone statuses and acreage.

**The Threat:** On Wednesday 14 April 2027, the National Weather Service Boulder office has a Red Flag Warning in effect for the Sawmill County foothills from 08:00 to 20:00 MDT. The forecast calls for west winds of 30 mph with gusts to 58 mph and relative humidity of 9 percent. Before STARTEX, Foothills Electric Cooperative sent its 72-hour and 24-hour partner notices and its 48-hour and 12-hour customer notices for Circuits 6, 7, 11 and 12. At 06:00 the co-op must decide whether to de-energize those circuits ahead of 45 mph gusts expected at 09:00. The shutoff would cut power to 3,100 customers, including 71 medical baseline households and the Pine Gulch Water District's Well 2. At 13:20 a wildfire is reported near Coyote Draw on County Road 68. By 14:30 it covers 420 acres and is spotting a mile ahead. By 17:00 it covers about 2,300 acres and is moving east-southeast toward Gulch Village (zone PG-03) and Kestrel Springs. It threatens the foothills road network, the Kestrel Springs water supply for firefighting, cell coverage and air quality in Pine Gulch. The narrative runs from 05:00 into the evening, with time jumps fitting it into 4 hours of exercise play.

**The Operational Impact:** From early morning, 3,100 co-op customers on Circuits 6, 7, 11 and 12 are told they may lose power for 24 to 48 hours. Medical baseline customers who rely on powered medical devices are the most urgent: 71 of them are on the affected circuits, and the co-op must reach or visit all 312 on its list. Each call that goes unanswered needs 3 retries and, for the 40 most critical customers, a door visit. Customers are calling the co-op's customer care agents about Well 2 and the Pine Gulch Fire Station community room, which serves as the Community Resource Center. After the 13:20 fire report, dispatchers at the Sawmill County Regional Communications Center must get the status of the nearby circuit (dead, or back-fed by customer generators) before crews engage. The EOC must issue each zone status against its latest possible order time. Gulch Village (PG-03, 55-minute clearance) includes Pine Gulch Elementary with 140 students, so families and buses are on the road. Stonebridge Assisted Living (KS-04) has 40 residents, a 120-minute clearance and one route out on Kestrel Springs Road, so it must start moving before any order. Deputies running zone sweeps and staffing traffic control points report that the Mill Creek Bridge is blocked, while contraflow on Highway 305 needs 25 minutes to set up. In the Highway 305 canyon, sheltering in place may be the safer choice. Engine and crew bosses are on Kestrel Springs hydrants that depend on a pump station with 8 hours of generator fuel. Two foothill cell towers go dark when their batteries run out, which cuts off residents and field units. In a zone under Evacuation Warning, a circuit is re-energized by mistake. By 16:00, smoke pushes the AQI in Pine Gulch above 150, and the school district and Pine Gulch Medical Center must act. Pine Gulch High School opens as a shelter and Mountain View Fairgrounds takes large animals. A social media post falsely claims Kestrel Springs is burning, and reporters are calling for acreage and zone statuses that the Sheriff and fire agency must report the same way.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to set Evacuation Warning, Evacuation Order or Shelter in Place for PG-03, KS-04 and the CR zones by the latest order time (projected fire arrival less zone clearance time, issued 30 minutes earlier where possible, per EOP 4), including moving Stonebridge Assisted Living before any order and deciding on shelter in place in the Highway 305 canyon.
- **Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to tie the Foothills Electric Cooperative de-energization and re-energization decisions on Circuits 6, 7, 11 and 12 to evacuation zone status, so that dispatch confirms line status before crews engage, no circuit is restored in a zone under Evacuation Warning, and Well 2, the Kestrel Springs pump station and the 71 medical baseline households are protected.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to put out one set of zone statuses, acreage and road statuses that the Sheriff, the fire agency, the co-op, the school district and Pine Gulch Medical Center all use, through WEAs of 360 characters or fewer, SawmillAlert and media briefings, and to correct the false report that Kestrel Springs is burning.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply the EOP 5 EOC decisions and the SENTCS 3 and 5 procedures (sweep reporting every 15 minutes, traffic control points, Highway 305 contraflow with CDOT, the hourly shared road status list, opening shelters at Pine Gulch High School and Mountain View Fairgrounds) when the Mill Creek Bridge is blocked and the foothill cell sites fail.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Emergency Manager (EOC Command) (EM):** Manages the county EOC: activation level, zone status records and their basis, shelters, resource requests and state and federal declaration requests.
- **Sheriff (Evacuation Branch) (SHF):** Owns evacuation orders and warnings, shelter in place decisions, zone sweeps, traffic control points, road control and Highway 305 contraflow through the Undersheriff.
- **Public Information Officer (PIO):** Owns public alerts, media statements, rumor control and the joint messaging with the co-op, the schools and the medical center so that every message names its zones and matches their status.
- **Dispatch Supervisor (Sawmill County Regional Communications Center) (DISP):** Manages the Regional Communications Center: call intake, alert transmission (WEA, SawmillAlert, EAS), the time each zone status is set on the map, unit status and line-status checks with the co-op.
- **Fire Chief (Pine Gulch Fire Protection District) (FC):** Owns fire strategy and tactics, spread projections and arrival times for each zone, requests for evacuation, resource orders and acreage and containment figures until Unified Command forms.
- **Operations Director (Foothills Electric Cooperative) (OPS):** Owns the decision to de-energize and re-energize Circuits 4 to 17, line patrols, customer and partner notices and medical baseline outreach, working with the Public Safety Partner Manager.

## Phase 3: Go Now or Shelter (Hour 2:00 – 3:00)

Goal: Tests the exercise's single decision point: the Sheriff and EOC set Evacuation Order or Shelter in Place for PG-03, KS-04, the CR zones and the Highway 305 canyon by the latest order time. They do this while the Mill Creek Bridge is blocked, cell sites fail and a Unified Command forms with the state.

Beats: DECISION POINT (15:10): the fire's projected arrival at Gulch Village leaves little margin over PG-03's 55-minute clearance. Deputies under Undersheriff Raymond Cho report the Mill Creek Bridge blocked, and Highway 305 contraflow with CDOT needs 25 minutes to set up. Sheriff Thomas Briggs must choose. One option is an Evacuation Order now, which puts Pine Gulch Elementary's 140 students on buses and families on a single road under smoke. The other is Shelter in Place for the Highway 305 canyon while waiting for contraflow. That leaves residents in the fire's path if the projection is late. Both choices have a real cost in lives at risk.; (15:30) The fire exceeds local district capacity.

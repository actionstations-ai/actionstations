# CCEM-SOP-07 — Control Area, Checkpoints and Movement Permits

Calder County Emergency Management · Standard Operating Procedure · Effective March 2026

## 1. Purpose

Describe how the county puts the Board of Animal Health's quarantine and control area on the ground: checkpoints, permit checks, the records at the EOC and the rule that unaffected farms keep working.

## 2. Premises designations the EOC tracks

The Board's epidemiologists designate every premises. The EOC keeps a board in the planning section with one code per premises and a fictional farm name in public documents.

- **Infected premises (IP):** confirmed or presumptive positive; under quarantine.
- **Contact premises (CP):** a premises with a trace-in or trace-out link to an IP; quarantined or on hold.
- **Suspect premises (SP):** clinical signs or a pending test.
- **At-risk premises (ARP):** within the control area with no sign of disease. Only an at-risk premises can be moved to monitored.
- **Monitored premises (MP):** at-risk premises tested negative and eligible to move under permit.
- **Free premises (FP):** outside the control area with no link.

The EOC never changes a designation. The Board does, and the EOC records the time of the Board's notice.

## 3. Checkpoints

Eleven checkpoints are pre-designated on county roads and one state highway (see the Annex K appendix). Each checkpoint has two Sheriff's deputies or reserve officers, a county highway worker, a sign set, a handwash station and a cleaning and disinfection station with a pressure sprayer. They are staffed by:

- Hours 0 to 3: the four primary sites on County Roads 12, 14, 21 and State Highway 9.
- Hours 3 to 8: the remaining seven.
- After hour 8: the Sheriff's mutual aid arrangement with the Pell and Ritter county sheriffs provides relief shifts.

A vehicle entering or leaving the control area must show a movement permit or a statement that it is traveling on an unrestricted category (for example a school bus or a resident's car). Livestock haulers, feed trucks, shavings trucks, egg carriers, rendering trucks, and crews are asked for a permit number; a vehicle without one is turned back or held while the permit desk is called. The deputy never decides whether a movement is allowed; the permit desk does.

## 4. Permit desk

The permit coordinator is a Board employee seated in the EOC. County liaison: Alina Petrova's deputy, Sam Idowu (555-0118). A permit request has the origin premises code, destination, species or product, quantity, route, driver and truck identifiers, requested date and a statement of biosecurity (cleaning and disinfection, covered load, no stops). The Board decides with conditions such as a negative test within a number of days, a single route, a wash station at exit, or that birds go directly to a processing plant. Decisions: issue, issue with conditions, or deny. Service target: a decision within 24 hours of a complete request; critical categories (feed to a premises with birds in the barns, a plant that is processing birds already moved) are decided within 4 hours.

## 5. Common requests

- **Feed deliveries** to unaffected premises: allowed under permit with a clean truck and a route that does not pass through an infected zone.
- **Finished birds to the processing plant** (the Prairie Gold plant in Dunmore, outside the control area): allowed under permit if the flock has tested negative, with the load sealed.
- **Egg and litter movements:** by permit; litter movements from at-risk premises may be denied.
- **Dead birds (routine mortality):** composting on farm or rendering under permit.
- **Rendering and mail:** the rendering company's truck is treated as a high-risk vehicle, with clean-and-disinfect at exit.
- **Small flocks and 4-H:** shows and swaps in the control area are canceled; the county fair board reports any event; the Extension Educator contacts every registered flock owner in the zone.

## 6. EOC records

For each premises: code, designation, designation time, the test results and times, the permit decisions, the checkpoint visits. The counts the PIO and the producers see (number of premises in the control area, infected, at-risk, monitored, permits issued and denied) are taken from this board at a stated time. The board is updated at the top of each hour and at each Board notice.

## 7. Sampling schedule

The Board and APHIS set the surveillance schedule; the county supports with staff when asked. The planning assumption used by CCEM (confirm against the Board's current plan): at-risk premises in the control area are sampled by the date given for that designation; any premises that misses its due date is reported to the Operations Chief at 07:00 the next day.

## 8. Disputes

A producer unhappy with a permit decision calls the permit coordinator, then the Board's duty line; the EOC does not review animal health decisions. County staff record complaints and the PIO reports patterns to the Board.

## 9. Briefings and cadence

06:30 operational briefing; 10:00 permit and traffic check; 16:00 planning meeting; 18:30 press update; 19:00 shift change. The EOC log notes every call from a producer or a member of the public to the hotline, 555-0160.

## 10. Public messaging rules

A producer notice and a consumer notice use the same counts, dates and distances. The PIO does not announce the size of a control area before the Board does. The notice names no farm or farmer. When the state and the cooperative publish different numbers, the PIO calls the Board's public information officer before replying.

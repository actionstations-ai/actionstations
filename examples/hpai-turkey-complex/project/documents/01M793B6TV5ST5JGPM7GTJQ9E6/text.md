# Calder County — Annex K: Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza and Animal Disease Response

Calder County Emergency Management (CCEM) · Calder County, Minnesota · Adopted March 2026

## 1. Purpose and authority

This annex describes how Calder County supports the State of Minnesota and USDA APHIS when highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) or another foreign animal disease is found in the county. The Minnesota Board of Animal Health (the Board) holds legal authority to order quarantine, set the control area and issue movement permits; USDA APHIS Veterinary Services provides the area veterinarian in charge, the national laboratory confirmation, indemnity and incident management support. The county does not make animal health decisions. It runs checkpoints with the Sheriff, opens and staffs the county emergency operations center, gives roads, disposal sites and public messaging, and protects the people doing the work.

Calder County is a fictional county in west-central Minnesota with 21,400 residents and the highest density of turkeys per square mile in its region: 48 commercial turkey premises, about 2.9 million birds placed per year, mainly under contract with the Prairie Gold Turkey Cooperative, and roughly 190 registered small flocks. County seat: Larkin.

## 3. Triggers

- **Suspect:** a producer or veterinarian reports high mortality, drop in egg or feed consumption, or neurologic signs and the state veterinarian places a hold order. CCEM goes to monitoring.
- **Presumptive positive:** a National Animal Health Laboratory Network laboratory (for Minnesota, the Minnesota Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory) reports an H5 or H7 result. CCEM opens the EOC at Level 2 within 60 minutes of notice from the Board. The plan clocks start at the presumptive result.
- **Confirmed:** the national reference laboratory in Ames confirms. Presumptive positives found later inside an established control area can be acted on without waiting for confirmation under the state plan.

## 4. First 24 hours after a presumptive positive

1. The Board orders a quarantine on the infected premises; CCEM confirms it knows the address, the barn count and the access roads. Target: quarantine ordered within 4 hours of the result.
2. The Board and APHIS draw the control area. Plan sizes (from the federal HPAI response plan, to be confirmed against the Minnesota plan): an infected zone of 3 km around the infected premises, a buffer zone of 7 km beyond it (together the 10 km control area), and a surveillance zone at least 10 km wide outside it. The epidemiology section may widen any of them. Target: control area set within 8 hours.
3. The Sheriff and Highway Engineer place checkpoints on the control area boundary roads, starting with the roads that carry turkey, feed, shavings and rendering traffic. Checkpoint plan: 11 sites (Annex K Appendix 2); the first four are staffed within 3 hours, all 11 within 8 hours.
4. The EOC establishes the permit desk with the Board's permit coordinator, and an industry liaison seat held by the Prairie Gold Turkey Cooperative biosecurity manager.
5. Depopulation: the plan goal is every barn on an infected premises depopulated within 24 hours of the presumptive result. The state and APHIS pick the method (foam, carbon dioxide or another method as the incident command decides) and the crews; CCEM provides security, water, power, traffic control and worker welfare.
6. Choose the disposal method and site (see the logistics SOP): on-farm composting or burial if the premises can host it, the Calder County Regional Landfill if the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency approves, or rendering or incineration.
7. The PIO publishes a first producer and consumer notice with the state within 12 hours; the message states that eggs and poultry products remain safe to eat when handled and cooked properly, the risk to the general public is low, and backyard flock owners should keep birds inside.
8. County Public Health contacts every worker who entered the barns, offers antiviral prophylaxis through the Minnesota Department of Health and arranges daily symptom monitoring for 10 days after last exposure.

## 5. Unified command

The Board and APHIS run unified command from the state's incident management team; the county EOC is a supporting node. The county's Incident Commander is Alina Petrova on site and Marcus Lindqvist at the EOC. Daily operational period: 07:00 to 19:00, with the planning meeting at 16:00 and the briefing at 06:30.

## 6. Requests

County requests go through the state emergency operations center in St. Paul. The Director may request a county emergency declaration from the County Board of Commissioners and then ask the Governor for a state emergency declaration through Minnesota Homeland Security and Emergency Management when the response needs the National Guard for checkpoints, state-funded mutual aid or waivers on hauling hours. A federal request (for example more depopulation crews or indemnity processing) is made by the Board to APHIS, not by the county.

## 7. Continuity of business

Unaffected producers inside a control area continue to operate under a permit. The Board issues movement permits for birds, eggs, feed, litter, dead haul and people, based on testing and biosecurity conditions; the service target is a decision within 24 hours of a complete request. The county does not close a whole sector without a stated reason. A suspended transport route is reported to the EOC so the Highway Engineer can reroute.

## 8. Protection of responders

Workers use the PPE set the state specifies (coverall, N95 or higher respirator, goggles, gloves, boots) and take the monitoring path in section 4. Heat, fatigue and rotation limits are tracked by the Operations Section; no crew works more than a 10-hour shift without rest.

## 9. Demobilization

When the state releases the control area (after cleaning and disinfection of infected premises, surveillance negative for the required period, and APHIS and the Board concurring), CCEM removes the checkpoints within 24 hours, informs producers and the public with the Board's release notice and sets the after-action meeting within 30 days.

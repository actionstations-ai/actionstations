### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise for Calder County Emergency Management and its partners. It runs in the county EOC in the Public Safety Building Training Room, Larkin, for 4 hours of exercise time starting at 07:50 on Tuesday, October 20, 2026. Nobody deploys and no equipment moves. Players talk through what they would decide, who they would call and what they would put in writing. You run the room and play the SimCell, which means everyone outside it: the Minnesota Board of Animal Health, USDA APHIS, the state EOC, the Minnesota Department of Health, the landfills, neighboring sheriffs, growers and reporters.

The injects simulate a natural hazard: highly pathogenic avian influenza carried in by wild waterfowl during the fall migration. The first report is an H5 presumptive positive from the Minnesota Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory at the Larkin Ridge Turkey Complex (IP-1, public name "Ridgeline Farm", four barns, 62,000 toms). Ames does not confirm it until hour 3:40. Players face the Annex K 4 clocks that start at the presumptive result:
- the first four checkpoints by 10:50
- the quarantine order by 11:50
- the control area and all 11 checkpoints by 15:50
- the joint notice by 19:50
- every barn depopulated by 07:50 Wednesday

The injects also bring wrong numbers, rumors, a disputed access road, a blown transformer, a line of trucks at the landfill and a pile of permit requests.

The day runs in four phases:
- **Phase 1, The Presumptive Call (0:00–1:00):** open the EOC and start the clocks without waiting for Ames.
- **Phase 2, Drawing the Lines (1:00–2:00, jump to 08:50):** checkpoints, access roads, catch-crew tracing and the first public message.
- **Phase 3, Holding the Perimeter (2:00–3:00, jump to 09:50):** the barn 3 power failure, depopulation support, the National Guard and declaration decision point (I-14) and the exposed workers.
- **Phase 4, Queues and Permits (3:00–4:00, jump to 05:30 Wednesday):** the landfill backlog, the permit queue, confirmation and the 10 km vs. 10 miles error, ending at 08:30 Wednesday.

Evaluators should watch for these things:
- **Acting on the presumptive result:** the county acts at once but does not make the Board's or APHIS's animal health decisions, such as tagging contact premises, moving a premises to monitored, choosing the depopulation method or widening zones.
- **Sending requests the right way:** county requests go through the state EOC, and federal requests go from the Board to APHIS (Annex K 6).
- **Protecting workers:** crews keep to the 10-hour shift cap (CDSDSL 6), and workers who don't speak English are reached in their own language (Annex K 4 step 8).
- **Keeping health information private:** worker health data stays with Public Health.
- **One set of numbers:** the county, the Board and Prairie Gold use the same counts, distances and times in every product.

**Objectives:**
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to meet the Annex K 4 clocks from the 07:50 presumptive result: open the EOC at Level 2 within 60 minutes, confirm the IP-1 address, barn count and access roads before the Board's 11:50 quarantine, and staff the first four checkpoints by 10:50 and all 11 by hour 8.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to support the Minnesota Board of Animal Health's decisions without making them: send the shared catch-crew evidence to the Board's epidemiologists for a decision on widening the zone, route disputed permits through the permit coordinator under CCACMP 8, and decide on Pell and Ritter mutual aid, a National Guard request and a county emergency declaration under CDSDSL 10.
- **Logistics and Continuity:** Demonstrate the ability to keep IP-1 depopulation and disposal moving: send county generators and the Larkin Fire District tanker when Barn 3 loses power, staff crews on shifts of 10 hours or less, escalate when the landfill queue passes 12 hours, and help process feed, finished-tom, rendering and egg-carrier permits for unaffected premises within the 24-hour service target.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to keep one set of counts, dates and distances across the Board, the cooperative and the county by issuing the 12-hour producer and consumer notice with the state, answering the social media claims that eggs and turkey are unsafe, coordinating replies to the grocery distributor and export broker, and reaching all 37 barn workers in a language they understand.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role as SimCell.** You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the Sheriff in Pell," you answer as Sergeant Halvorsen. If they say "I call the Board," you are the duty veterinarian. If they say "I call MPCA," you are a project manager who has not signed the letter yet. Answer in the first person and in that party's interest, and only with what that party would know. If players don't pick up the phone, nobody calls them back to help. Keep a short log of every call: who called, the time, what was asked and what you gave.

**Do not make it easy.** Every outside party has its own priorities, limits and paperwork. Give players only what the right action earns. For example, when Ops asks the County Highway Engineer to "just close" the 230th Street field lane (I-02, I-08), Pete Vasquez answers: "I can put up Type III barricades by 10:30, but only if someone tells me it's a controlled gate and not a closure. That lane is the only route for loaded rendering and depop equipment trucks. If you close it, the depop trucks don't get in. And has the Board put the lane in the quarantine order or not?" The same goes for the fourth checkpoint: Pell's deputies come at 11:30, not 10:50, however hard the Sheriff pushes. Ritter sends nobody until Wednesday.

**Managing time.** Use the time-jump cards at 1:00, 2:00 and 3:00. Stand, raise a hand and read aloud:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. It is now [time]. Here is what has happened in the meantime… [read the card]. Any action you started before the pause has been carried out unless I tell you otherwise. RESUME."

Change the minutes to match the card. The 3:00 jump (I-17) moves to 05:30 Wednesday, so say "jumping forward to 05:30 Wednesday." If a team is stuck in a debate, give them a two-minute warning and then a SimCell call that forces a decision, such as the state EOC asking for an answer by noon. Never let the room fall behind the clocks without consequences.

**Handling artificialities.** If a student says "I wouldn't use that system," "We'd never get that email," or "In real life the Board would just tell us," answer:
> "Understood. Note it for the hot wash. For today, the system and the call are as shown. Tell me what you would do with the information you have."

Write the comment on the parking-lot sheet and move on. Do not argue about how realistic it is during play.

**Delivering injects.** Do not explain injects, hint or summarize. Hand the card over or read it, and say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." If a player asks "What does this mean?", answer in character ("I'm the landfill manager. I need to know where these trucks go.") or say "Work it out with your team." Deliver noise injects (I-02b, I-10b, I-15b, I-19b) with the same weight as core injects.

**"This is an exercise."** Every outside phone call or email a player actually makes, and every written product, starts and ends with "This is an exercise." No player calls a real Board, APHIS, MDH or media number.

**"REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD."** If a real emergency or real-world event happens, such as a medical problem in the room, a real activation of a participant or a real call to the EOC, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." Play stops at once. The controller handles the real event and then decides whether and when to resume with "RESUME EXERCISE."

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three that broke or failed during the response. Go around the room one team at a time (county EOC, sheriff and checkpoints, cooperative, communications), write each item on the whiteboard in two columns, and don't debate items until every team has spoken.

**Key Discussion Questions for Flyway:**
- **The Gap:** At I-02, the Board asked you to confirm IP-1's address, barns and access roads by 09:30. The complex manager said the CR 12 drive was the only way in. The County Highway Engineer said loaded trucks had been using an ungated field lane off 230th Street. Whose answer did you send to the Board, when did you send it, and who put a checkpoint or gate on the 230th lane? Did you treat the engineer's reply as evidence that changed the quarantine order, or as a side note in an email thread? Under Annex K 3, the clocks started at 07:50. Which of them did you actually meet? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Call:** At I-14 (10:15), the state EOC told you that tonight you would have 11 checkpoint sites and about 4 people. They also said a Guard request with a county declaration had to reach them by noon to get soldiers there around 18:00. Did you decide on the declaration and the Guard request before noon? If not, what stopped you? When the commissioners chair warned that reporters would ask for the farm name, who decided what could be said? Did you keep that call with the Board and the cooperative, or did the county end up answering it? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **Thin Lines:** At I-07b, Pell could send two deputies starting at 11:30, Ritter could send nobody until tomorrow, and the Pell deputies had never run an ag checkpoint. Who briefed them on what a Board permit looks like, where the cleaning and disinfection station is, and what PPE to wear? How did you staff the first four checkpoints by 10:50 without putting anyone on a shift longer than 10 hours? What would have had to be ready before this morning for you to fill the night shift? _(Objective: Logistics and Continuity)_
- **Before You Knew:** At I-05, a Facebook post with 84 shares claimed that every turkey within 10 miles was being killed and that eggs and turkey were unsafe. Meanwhile, a 60-vendor poultry swap meet was going ahead unless someone official said otherwise. What did you tell the public, and the fair board, while the result was still only presumptive? Who decided about the swap meet: the Board or the county? Did the county, the cooperative and the Board all use the same counts and control-area distance, or did each one put out its own numbers? _(Objective: Communication)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Animal disease response**: Foreign animal disease response for exercises: premises designations and the zones they sit in, movement permits, sampling schedules, depopulation and disposal, indemnity, and the notices producers, exhibitors and consumers receive. Rules are tagged with the response plan edition they come from. Disease dynamics, epidemic curves and tracing come from the epidemiology toolkit. Stays at coordination level: no pathogen handling detail, fictional premises only.
  - Data sources: aphis-hpai
  - Reference documents: HPAI Response Plan: The Red Book (May 2017); FMD Response Plan: The Red Book (October 2020); APHIS Foreign Animal Disease Framework: Roles and Communication (FAD PReP Manual 1-0) (September 2022); APHIS Foreign Animal Disease Framework: Response Strategies (FAD PReP Manual 2-0) (October 2015); FAD PReP Manual 3-0: Incident Information Management and Reporting (February 2018); FAD PReP Manual 6-0: Permitted Movement (February 2017)
- **Media environment**: The news cycle and social feeds around an incident: wire alerts, articles and broadcast segments, social posts whose reach and sentiment change over time, reporter calls and press conference questions, and verification tasks with provenance clues. Simulated spread and sentiment are teaching models, not predictions. All outlets, handles and people are fictional.
  - Data sources: gdelt
  - Reference documents: FCC report on the Hawaii false alert (2018): a correction case study

## Ninety Minutes — Flash Flood, Evacuation & Campus Shelter

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Marlowe State University (MSU) campus along Sugar Camp Run, with the EOC in Campus Services Building room 108; Marlowe, Athens County, Ohio · Duration: 4 Hours

### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise for EMGT 4320 Exercise 3. It runs for four hours of exercise time on the exercise clock, starting at 05:30 EDT on Thursday, March 25, 2027, at Marlowe State University (MSU) in Marlowe, Athens County, Ohio. Six student teams sit in a simulated campus Emergency Operations Center (EOC) in Campus Services Building room 108: Incident Command, Public Information, Campus Police, Housing and Mass Care, Facilities and the Student Evaluators. They work the MSU Emergency Operations Plan Flood Annex (Annex F) and the Notification and Shelter SOP. Nothing is deployed and nobody leaves the room. Every decision is spoken, written in a log or drafted as a message. The exercise is no-fault (EEDECS 5): it tests the plan and the decisions, not the people. A team that makes a sound call on the information it had is scored on that call, even if the water still reaches Riverside.

The injects simulate a natural hazard. Sugar Camp Run floods in a flash flood after 2 inches of overnight rain, with 2 to 3 more inches due by noon. The first phase brings a NWS Charleston flash flood watch and a gauge rising at the Mill Street culvert. The Mill Street gauge then passes the Annex F section 2 stages in order: action 5.0, Dairy Lane closure 6.5, flood stage 7.0, crossing under water 8.0, then the Lower Quad, Lot C and the Riverside basement. A CONSIDERABLE-tag Flash Flood Warning arrives at 06:40, and the 0720 AM Ohio River Forecast Center issuance moves the crest 3 hours earlier, to 10.8 ft near 10:30. Around the water come the other injects: a stranded diesel tanker, a basement-water red herring, a viral "trapped on the roof" rumor, media deadlines, a parent line that keeps filling, a student who is on no list, and recovery asks from the Red Cross, the Marlowe building inspector and the Ohio EMA.

The four phases are Phase 1, The Watch (0:00–1:00), Phase 2, Warning and Rising Water (1:00–2:00), Phase 3, The Riverside Decision (2:00–3:00), and Phase 4, Crest and Return (3:00–4:00). Time jumps open Phases 2 to 4. From I-16 on, the scenario runs compressed, through the crest and into Thursday afternoon. Evaluators should watch for these things:
- Seats act before the gauge forces them to.
- Each decision names the stage or the forecast issuance it rests on.
- Closures and moves are tied to stages and clearance times, not the clock alone.
- Clery classification goes through the Clery Compliance Officer.
- The university holds one consistent public message against the rumor.
- The two residents on oxygen concentrators and the deaf resident get support in place inside the general population shelter.
- The count reconciles (start + arrivals − departures).
- Reoccupation waits for the inspector's written clearance.

Record times to the minute and quote exact words.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to raise the EOC activation level and issue the Riverside Hall evacuation order by about 08:00, on the latest Ohio River Forecast Center crest forecast (10.8 ft at about 10:30) rather than waiting for the Mill Street gauge to reach the trigger, and to record the time and the forecast issuance behind each decision as EOP 4 requires.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to send a first MSU Alert within 15 minutes of the flash flood warning, and to answer the 07:50 viral 'students trapped on the roof' post with one confirmed correction, checked with Campus Police and Facilities, on the same channel and consistent with the statements given to Channel 9, The Hilltop and the parent line.
- **Mass Care:** Demonstrate the ability to open Kessler Fieldhouse as a general population shelter, support the two students who use oxygen concentrators and the deaf student in place, keep a shelter count that matches the Riverside Hall roster, and resolve the 09:30 missing-student report within the FERPA limits in MENSO 6.
- **Infrastructure Protection:** Demonstrate the ability to tie the Dairy Lane closure, the Lot C fleet and generator move, the Riverside Hall power isolation and the Whitcomb Library equipment relocation to stage thresholds and clearance times, and to re-route the stranded central plant fuel delivery.

**Plan sections exercised:**
- MSU Emergency Operations Plan, A… › 2. Flood profile
- MSU Emergency Operations Plan, A… › 4. Activation levels
- MSU Emergency Operations Plan, A… › 5. Protective actions
- MSU Emergency Operations Plan, A… › 3. Roles and authorities
- MSU Emergency Operations Plan, A… › 6. Notification
- MENSO › 6. Media, parents and rumors

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Set the tables as a U-shape or as islands to simulate the MSU EOC in Campus Services Building room 108. The projector screen sits at the open end of the U and acts as the EOC "main screen" (exercise clock, Facilities dashboard, NWS and Ohio River Forecast Center products, scene photos).
- **Head of the U, facing the screen:** Team A, Incident Command (IC seat), next to the ICS-201 whiteboard.
- **Left arm:** Team C, Campus Police, next to Team E, Facilities. They share closure stages, the tanker and the Riverside power isolation, so they need to talk across the table.
- **Right arm:** Team D, Housing and Mass Care, with room to spread out shelter rosters, the Riverside roster and the access list.
- **Rear table, away from the U:** Team B, Public Information Officer. Isolate this seat on purpose. A real PIO is rarely in the room where the gauge numbers arrive, so they must walk over to Incident Command, Campus Police or Facilities to confirm facts before anything goes out. That walk is what exposes the rumor test (the 2019 "evacuated before" claim in I-03, the roof post in I-13) and makes one consistent message an earned result.
- **Control table, side or rear corner:** The SimCell (instructor), the teaching assistant and Team F, Student Evaluators. Evaluators can see every table but do not join any of them. The control table is also a quieter seat for any student with an approved accommodation (EEDECS 7). Apply accommodations as written and never announce them.
- **Wall space:** A printed Annex F section 2 stage table and the Annex F section 8 shelter facts (Kessler 1,800, 36-hour generator; ballroom 400) posted where every team can see them.

**Equipment needs**
- Projector and screen for main-screen injects (exercise clock, dashboard alarms, forecast products, the Upper Quad photo)
- Whiteboard and markers for the ICS-201 (situation, objectives, organization, resources)
- Printed inject deck in MSEL order, with spare copies of each card to hand to the target teams
- Optional: simulated radios or phones (handheld radios or a desk phone at the control table) for SimCell calls, and a phone tone or recording for the WEA in I-07
- Wall clock or timer showing exercise time, plus a printed time-jump card for each phase

**Materials for players:** Annex F excerpts, Notification and Shelter SOP, role rubrics, ICS-201 and ICS-214 forms, decision log sheets, blank MSU Alert / message templates, campus map with Dairy Lane, Lot C, the Lower Quad, Riverside and Kessler marked, pens and name tents.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**The SimCell role.** You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff", you answer as the deputy. In this exercise you will be:
- the NWS Charleston forecaster and the Ohio River Forecast Center
- Marlowe Fire and Marlowe Police dispatch
- the diesel supplier, the central plant supervisor, Tech 2 and Tech 4
- the Riverside Director, the Kessler building manager and the Student Health Center nurse
- the Vice President for Student Affairs and the Clery Compliance Officer
- the Channel 9 desk, The Hilltop, and a parent on the line
- the Red Cross Southeast Ohio chapter, the Marlowe building inspector and the Ohio EMA watch desk

Answer only what you are asked, as that person would, with that person's interests. If players don't call, nobody calls them back with the answer.

**Do not make it easy.** Your outside characters should not hand the players the plan's answer. Make them earn it. Example: at I-12 the Vice President for Student Affairs leans on the IC to "hold 20 minutes, half an hour" until the gauge reaches 8.0 and the 9 AM issuance comes out. Do not fold just because the IC says "safety first." Fold only when the IC names the 0720 AM forecast (10.5 ft near 10 AM), works backwards from the 90-minute clearance plus the margin before the basement floods, and states the deadline aloud. If the IC agrees to wait, accept it, let the clock run, and have the Riverside Director call back at I-15 with less time. The no-fault rule (EEDECS 5) means you let the consequence happen without commentary. The evaluators score the decision, not the outcome.

**Managing time.** Read the time-jump cards (I-06, I-11, I-16) word for word. If a team is stuck or play runs long, use this announcement:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. The Mill Street gauge now reads [stage per the MSEL]. Anything you ordered is in force; anything you did not decide is still undecided. RESUME."

Never undo a decision during a jump, and never make one on a team's behalf. Move stages forward only along the MSEL, at about +1.7 ft/hr, and keep the forecast tied to its issuance time. From I-16 on, say the scenario time out loud with each inject.

**Handling artificialities.** When a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "we'd just check the real dashboard," answer:
> "Understood. For this exercise, that system is what you have. Tell me what you would do with it, or tell me which tool you would use instead and what it would show, and we'll play that. Note the gap in your log for the hot wash."

Don't argue about it. Write the point down as a possible plan finding.

**Delivering injects.** Do not explain. Say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." Hand the card to the target teams or put it on the projector, then step back. Do not say why an inject matters or what the right answer is, and do not point out that I-08 is a lint-clogged drain. Reveal Tech 2's follow-up only if Facilities sends someone to look. If a player asks "is this real?", answer in character with only what that person would know.

**"This is an exercise" and "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD."** Every scripted message, drafted MSU Alert, email and post starts and ends with "EXERCISE EXERCISE EXERCISE". Any player message drafted for the public must carry "This is an exercise." Never send anything from a real MSU Alert account, real social media or a real phone line. If a real emergency or a real injury happens, or if someone is in real distress, anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD" plus the problem. All play stops right away and the instructor deals with it. Resume only when the instructor says "RESUME EXERCISE."

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **NWS Charleston WV forecast office duty forecaster and the Ohio River Forecast Center hydrologist (weather and hazard authority)** — _Trigger: IC, Facilities or PIO calls the NWS Charleston office or the Ohio River Forecast Center to confirm the watch or warning, ask how sure the crest forecast is, or report the Mill Street gauge readings_
  - Ask: "What's your gauge reading right now, and what time was it taken? I need a number and a time, not 'it's high.' If your dashboard has gone flat, tell me. Missing data isn't zero."
  - Ask: "Which issuance are you working from? The 0540 AM crest was 2.3 feet lower and 3 hours later. If your plan is built on that one, it's out of date."
  - Ask: "You're asking if 10.8 is a sure thing. No forecast is. The 0720 includes 1.5 to 2 inches of QPF through 10 AM. Do you have a decision you can make on that, or are you waiting for one I can't give you?"
  - Ask: "Are you seeing debris racking on the Mill Street culvert? That can push your local stage above anything in my forecast. Have you got anyone watching it?"
  - Concede only when: When Facilities gives timed gauge readings and the debris report, the forecaster logs them as a ground truth report and confirms the 0720 AM issuance is the current forecast. The next issuance is due by 09:00 or sooner.
  - Concede only when: When the IC explains the Riverside 10.5 ft impact, the hydrologist confirms the forecast puts 10.5 ft near 10 AM. They will not say whether to evacuate: 'That's your call, and you've got the numbers to make it.'
  - Concede only when: After 10:15, if asked, they say the second rain band might bring a rise to near 12.0 ft. They call it possible and say it is not in the forecast.
- **Ohio Emergency Management Agency watch desk (with Athens County EMA duty officer on the same bridge line)** — _Trigger: IC or Housing and Mass Care calls county or state emergency management to report the activation, ask for resources (fuel, interpreter, cots), or answer the 09:32 and 15:40 data requests_
  - Ask: "What's your activation level, and since when? I need a time and a reason for the log."
  - Ask: "How many people are displaced right now, from Riverside and from the Sugar Run Apartments? How many are actually in Kessler? Don't give me 680 if 61 signed out to friends."
  - Ask: "You want a fuel delivery for the Kessler generator. What's your tank level, your burn rate and your hours remaining? And which road can a tanker actually use, with Dairy Lane under water?"
  - Ask: "Your 11:00 numbers are late. Do you have a single point of contact for the county, or am I calling four people?"
  - Concede only when: Once the shelter count is reconciled (start + arrivals − departures) and broken out by Riverside and Sugar Run Apartments, the desk takes it as the preliminary displaced figure and stops calling for it.
  - Concede only when: Once Facilities gives the generator fuel level, burn rate and a usable access route, the county logs a fuel resource request and gives an ETA window. It will not arrive before the crossing reopens unless MSU names another route.
  - Concede only when: Once the IC names one point of contact per shift, all county and state requests go through that person.
- **Marlowe Fire dispatcher (also relays for Marlowe Police)** — _Trigger: Campus Police announces or considers the Dairy Lane closure, asks for help with the Riverside sweep, or Marlowe Fire calls in at I-09_
  - Ask: "Dairy Lane is our first-due route to your south side. Which route do you want Engine 2 and the medic units on, and does it still work at 9.0 when your Lower Quad goes under?"
  - Ask: "Is there an officer at the barricade, or just cones? People drive around cones. We've pulled them out of the water before."
  - Ask: "You're asking for a crew on the Riverside sweep. How many floors are left, how many people are unaccounted for, and who's my one contact in your EOC?"
  - Ask: "Is anyone actually on the Riverside roof? I've got a viral post and two citizen calls. Confirm it or rule it out. I'm not sending a ladder truck on a rumor."
  - Concede only when: Once Campus Police names the alternate route and a staffed barricade, dispatch updates its run cards and confirms Engine 2 has the route.
  - Concede only when: Once the Chief names a single point of contact and shares the evacuation area and the list of unaccounted residents, Marlowe Fire sends one crew to join the floor-by-floor sweep.
  - Concede only when: Once the sweep reports the roof hatch padlocked from inside and no one on the roof, Marlowe Fire tells its own public line 'no roof rescue in progress.' It refers questions to MSU.
- **American Red Cross, Southeast Ohio chapter, disaster program manager (shelter handover)** — _Trigger: Housing and Mass Care or the IC asks the Red Cross for shelter support or a handover of Kessler Fieldhouse, or receives the I-20 email_
  - Ask: "Annex F section 8 says we support after the first 12 hours, on request. When did Kessler open, and who is formally making the request?"
  - Ask: "Show me your daily count: residents at start, plus arrivals, minus departures. Does it match your registration log? It doesn't? Then I can't take the shelter tonight."
  - Ask: "What FNSS supports are in place right now? Who's on the generator outlets for the oxygen concentrators, how many spare cylinders do you have, and do you have an interpreter or text announcements for your deaf resident, or still 'requested'?"
  - Ask: "Are you opening the Student Union ballroom? Who cancelled the career fair, and who is shelter manager there on the night shift?"
  - Concede only when: When the count reconciles with the registration log, the chapter confirms it will assume shelter management at 20:30. It will not do so before.
  - Concede only when: When MSU lists the FNSS supports with an owner for each (generator-backed outlets, spare cylinders, interpreter or text alerts) and names a contact for each shift, the chapter commits a shelter team and an interpreter request through its network.
  - Concede only when: If the pets-in-carriers area and the co-located space are already set, the chapter keeps that arrangement and does not separate owners from their animals.
- **Channel 9 assignment desk and The Hilltop news editor (media)** — _Trigger: PIO answers or returns the I-13 media inquiries, or Channel 9 / The Hilltop call again on the reoccupation question at I-20_
  - Ask: "We go live at 8:00 with or without you. Is anyone on the Riverside roof, yes or no? I need a name and a title for the quote."
  - Ask: "Our reporters in Riverside say RAs are banging on doors. Is that an evacuation? Why did my phone get a county warning at 6:40 and nothing from MSU until now?"
  - Ask: "A parent says their kid lives on the fourth floor and isn't on any list. Is the university missing a student?"
  - Ask: "Students on our live blog say they're going back to Riverside tonight. True or not? And are Friday classes on? We publish at 08:15."
  - Concede only when: A short on-the-record statement from the named university spokesperson before the deadline becomes the lead. It should say what is happening, where students should go and when the next update comes. The desk drops the 'roof rescue' framing and says MSU denies it.
  - Concede only when: Given a set time for the next update and a single media contact, the desk stops calling other offices and waits for the briefing.
  - Concede only when: Given a clear line that Riverside stays closed until the Marlowe building inspector's written clearance, the desk runs it in place of the 'tonight' rumor.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the flood response. Go around the room one team at a time (EOC command, Facilities, Police, Communications, Student Affairs/shelter), write each item on the whiteboard in two columns, and don't let anyone repeat an item until every team has spoken.

**Key Discussion Questions for Ninety Minutes:**
- **The Call:** At 07:35 (I-12) the Vice President for Student Affairs asked you to hold the Riverside evacuation until the Mill Street gauge actually reached 8.0 and the 9 AM forecast came out. The Hall Director had just told you a full sweep takes about 90 minutes, and longer for floors 9 to 11 and the three residents on the access list. Did you decide on the 10.8 ft crest forecast, or did you wait for the gauge? What time did you give the order, and which forecast issuance did you write down as the basis, as EOP 4 requires? What would have let you decide faster? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Gap:** At 06:10 (I-04) the gauge passed action stage at 5.02 ft and was rising 1.2 ft/hr. Then the central plant needed an answer about the 08:00 diesel tanker by 06:45, and the fleet coordinator asked whether to start the 40-minute Lot C move now or wait for 8.5 (I-05). Did you work back from the 6.5 Dairy Lane closure and the 9.5 Lot C stage to a start time, or did you wait for each threshold to arrive? When Tech 4 reported two inches in the low corner of Lot C at 07:20, were the trucks and generators already moving? Did the tanker reach Kessler's half-full day tank? _(Objective: Infrastructure Protection)_
- **The Rumor:** At 07:50 (I-13) the 'trapped on the roof' post had 900 reposts in 8 minutes. Channel 9 was going live at 8:00, The Hilltop was publishing at 08:15, and 64 voicemails were waiting on the parent line. What had you already told the public when the flash flood warning came out, and was it within 15 minutes? Who checked the roof claim with Campus Police and Facilities before you answered? Was your correction one message, on the same channel, saying the same thing to Channel 9, The Hilltop and parents, or did different versions go out? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The Count:** When Riverside emptied into Kessler Fieldhouse, how did you keep the shelter count tied to the 680-resident roster? How did you support the two students on oxygen concentrators and the deaf student, both during the evacuation and at the shelter? When the missing-student report came in at 09:30, what were you able to confirm, and to whom, within the FERPA limits in MENSO 6? Where did the shelter team depend on the residence hall staff or the police for information, and what was missing? _(Objective: Mass Care)_
- **Trust:** At 10:32 (I-18) the gauge read 10.81 ft with a 'CREST?' flag, and the 10:15 forecast said the crest was over but a second rise toward 12.0 ft was possible late in the afternoon. Did operations and the technical team read that forecast the same way? Did the 48 boxes still on the lower level and the floor-level UPS and switches in Whitcomb get moved, or did 'not in this forecast' lead you to stand down? When did you bring in the Marlowe and Athens County emergency management liaisons and NWS Charleston, and was that early enough? _(Objective: Infrastructure Protection)_

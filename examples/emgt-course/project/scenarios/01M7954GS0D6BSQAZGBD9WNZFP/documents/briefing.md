# Player Briefing Packet
## Ninety Minutes — Flash Flood, Evacuation & Campus Shelter

Location: Marlowe State University (MSU) campus along Sugar Camp Run, with the EOC in Campus Services Building room 108; Marlowe, Athens County, Ohio · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 05:30 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Steady moderate rain, 48°F, light south wind; about 2 inches of rain fell overnight on the Sugar Camp Run basin north of campus.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is 05:30 EDT on Thursday, March 25, 2027, at Marlowe State University in Marlowe, Athens County, Ohio. It is still dark and the campus is quiet. The residence halls are asleep: 680 residents are in Riverside Hall and 210 are in the Sugar Run Apartments. The central plant is running on its night shift, a Campus Police night dispatcher is on the operations line, and Facilities has a night technician on call. Day staff and faculty start arriving in about two hours for a full Thursday of 08:00 classes, and the plant's regular 08:00 fuel delivery is on the schedule. The campus is at Level 3 Monitoring. The Emergency Operations Center in Campus Services Building room 108 is not open, the Facilities dashboard is up and every system reports normal. The clock on the projector is the exercise clock. The SimCell at the control table plays everyone outside this room, including the National Weather Service, the Marlowe Police and Fire Departments, the media, parents and students.

- Campus status and EOC: GREEN: Level 3 Monitoring. The EOC (Campus Services Building room 108) is not activated and the 08:00 class schedule stands.
- Mill Street stage gauge and Facilities dashboard: GREEN: Online and reporting. 4.2 ft at 05:30. Action stage is 5.0 ft and flood stage is 7.0 ft.
- Dairy Lane crossing, Lot C fleet and Whitcomb Library network room: GREEN: The crossing is open to plant deliveries and Marlowe Fire. The Facilities trucks and mobile generators are parked in Lot C. The library network gear and Special Collections are normal.
- Riverside Hall and Sugar Run Apartments: GREEN: Occupied (680 and 210 residents), with RAs on duty. Elevators and electrical gear are normal.
- Kessler Fieldhouse shelter and Student Union ballroom: GREEN: Standby, not open. Kessler holds 1,800 and the ballroom holds 400. The 500 kW generator has 36 hours of fuel.
- MSU Alert, website banner, parent line and social channels: GREEN: The system is ready. No messages have gone out today, the parent line is quiet and there is no media contact.

**The Anomaly:** **05:30, Emergency Management duty phone (to the IC, copied to Facilities).** The duty phone receives the routine overnight forward from the National Weather Service Charleston WV office, issued at 5:17 AM EDT. It says the Flash Flood Watch for Athens County remains in effect through this afternoon and that another 2 to 3 inches of rain is expected by noon.

A minute later the Facilities night technician sends a screenshot of the Facilities dashboard for the MSU stage gauge at the Mill Street culvert:

- 05:00: **3.9 ft**
- 05:15: **4.0 ft**
- 05:30: **4.2 ft**

The trend reads RISING. Action stage is 5.0 ft and flood stage is 7.0 ft.

No one has called in, and every other system reads normal.

**YOUR MISSION:** Maintain normal campus operations, student safety and the 08:00 class day while you investigate and resolve the situation developing around the morning's weather. You must balance continuity against protective action and speed against verification. Decide on the latest information early enough that the plan's lead times still work, without causing disruption or putting out messages that the facts do not support.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: Incident Command**— Director of Emergency Management (Incident Commander) | You own the campus response, the activation level of the EOC in Campus Services Building room 108, and the orders to close campus areas and evacuate residence halls under EOP section 3. You set the shared picture that every other seat works from. | **Forecast, Not Gauge: base each decision on the latest forecast issuance and the plan's clearance times, not on the current gauge reading alone.** **Log It: record the time, the forecast issuance and the reason for every change of level and every order you give.** **One Picture: consult the Vice President for Student Affairs when time allows, bring the Marlowe Police and Fire liaisons in early, and keep the Ohio Emergency Management Agency informed rather than running the response alone.** |
| **Team B: Public Information**— Director of University Communications (Public Information Officer) | You own MSU Alert content, the website banner, social media, media statements and parent messages. You are the university's only spokesperson. | **Holding Line: get a first message out quickly and say when the next update will come, because silence feeds rumors.** **Verify Before You Deny: check every rumor with Campus Police or Facilities before you answer it, and correct it on the channel where it spread.** **One Voice: say what is known, what is not known and what is being done; give every outlet the same statement; and never promise a student is safe without confirmation.** |
| **Team C: Campus Police**— Chief of Campus Police | You own road and crossing closures, access control, building sweeps and coordination with the Marlowe Police and Fire Departments. You also decide with the Clery Compliance Officer which type of notification is required. | **Close Early: close the Dairy Lane crossing at the stage set in EOP section 5, even if that strands traffic or a fire access route.** **Field Over Feed: if a social post and an officer on scene disagree, trust the officer and report back to the PIO.** **Right Message Type: decide with the Clery Compliance Officer whether a message is an emergency notification or a timely warning before the wording goes out, and agree on any alternate access with Marlowe Fire before you need it.** |
| **Team D: Housing and Mass Care**— Dean of Students (Housing and Mass Care) | You own the residence halls, the Kessler Fieldhouse shelter and its Student Union ballroom alternate, student welfare, students with access and functional needs, and communication with parents about students. | **Count Everyone: reconcile hall rosters, RA sweeps and shelter registration, and treat missing data as unknown, not as a confirmed absence.** **Support in Place: keep students with access and functional needs in the general population shelter with power and communication access, and never move anyone because of a disability.** **FERPA Before Comfort: confirm a student's status to a parent only with consent or under the health and safety exception, decided with University Counsel. Plan any overflow before the primary shelter fills.** |
| **Team E: Facilities**— Associate Vice President for Facilities | You own the central plant, electrical systems, building automation, flood barriers, pumps, the Mill Street gauge dashboard and damage assessment. | **Move by Stage: move vulnerable assets in Lot C and Whitcomb Library by the stages set in EOP section 5. The stage sets your deadline, not the clock.** **People Before Plant: isolate power to any occupied building only after the IC confirms the elevators are clear of people.** **Report Gaps: report a gauge failure or a gap in the data as missing data, never as a steady or falling stage. Keep generator fuel and plant deliveries supplied.** |
| **Team F: Student Evaluator**— Student Evaluator | You own the observation record. You time each seat's decisions and score them against the role and team rubrics from the control table. | **No Fault: score the decision and its basis, not how the event turns out.** **Timestamp Everything: record the exercise time of each trigger, the first public message and each order so the timeliness criteria can be scored.** **Stay Silent: tie each score to a quoted action or message, and do not coach the seats or answer their questions during play.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio call, phone call, text and drafted message in this room with "This is an exercise," so nobody overhearing or receiving it mistakes it for a real MSU emergency.
- If a real emergency happens anywhere in Hargrove Hall or on campus, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD" and the exercise stops immediately, because real safety always comes before training.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to skip ahead. When that happens, update your picture to the new time and keep going, so the exercise can cover the later phases in the session we have.
- Treat every printed inject the SimCell hands you, including posts, logs, dashboard screenshots, photos and reports, as real intelligence and act on it as you would on the day, because that is how the scenario gives you information.

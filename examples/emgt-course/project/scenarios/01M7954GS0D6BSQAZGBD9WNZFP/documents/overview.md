# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Ninety Minutes** (Flash Flood, Evacuation & Campus Shelter)
Location: Marlowe State University (MSU) campus along Sugar Camp Run, with the EOC in Campus Services Building room 108; Marlowe, Athens County, Ohio · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Student teams in six seats: Incident Commander (Emergency Manager), Public Information Officer (Director of University Communications), Chief of Campus Police, Dean of Students (Housing and Mass Care), Associate Vice President for Facilities, and Student Evaluators

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Deciding ahead of the trigger, one consistent public message against a fast-moving rumor, and caring for evacuated students, including students with access and functional needs, without losing count of anyone.

**The Threat:** Before STARTEX, 2 inches of rain fell overnight on the 9-square-mile Sugar Camp Run basin north of campus. At 05:30 EDT on a Thursday in late March 2027, NWS Charleston has a flash flood watch in effect for Athens County, with 2 to 3 more inches forecast by noon. The Mill Street gauge reads 4.2 ft and is rising toward action stage (5.0) and flood stage (7.0). The creek can rise 1.5 feet per hour. By 06:40 an NWS flash flood warning covers the basin. The Ohio River Forecast Center then forecasts a crest of 10.8 ft at about 10:30. At that stage water reaches the Dairy Lane crossing, the Sugar Run Apartments, Parking Lot C and the Lower Quad, then floods the Riverside Hall basement (mechanical room, transformer vault, main electrical gear). Whitcomb Library's lower level floods only if the creek reaches 12.0, which stays a threat in play rather than the forecast.

**The Operational Impact:** Campus Police must close the Dairy Lane crossing at 6.5. That closure strands the central plant's 08:00 fuel delivery truck and cuts one Marlowe Fire access route. The 210 Sugar Run Apartments residents go on advisory at 7.0 with a 45-minute clearance. The Riverside Hall order for 680 residents across 11 floors has to go out by about 08:00. The hall needs 90 minutes to clear, longer once power isolation stops the elevators, and must be empty 30 minutes before the basement floods. Kessler Fieldhouse needs 25 minutes to open by day. It holds 1,800 and has 36 hours of generator power, and the Student Union ballroom (400) becomes the alternate once the count reaches 1,500. Shelter staff must support two students who use oxygen concentrators and a deaf student in the general population shelter. Facilities technicians race the stage to move the truck fleet and mobile generators out of Lot C before 8.5 and the library network gear and low-shelf Special Collections boxes to the second floor before 10.5. At 07:50 a viral post claims Riverside Hall is under water and students are trapped on the roof. Channel 9 and The Hilltop call for statements and the parent line fills with calls. At 09:30 a student is on no list while a parent insists the student is in Riverside Hall. After the time jump, the seats must plan the Red Cross handover and get the Marlowe building inspector to clear Riverside Hall before anyone moves back in. The exercise is no-fault. Evaluators score each decision against the role rubrics, not how the flood turns out.

- MSU Emergency Operations Plan, A… › 5. Protective actions
- MSU Emergency Operations Plan, A… › 3. Roles and authorities

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to raise the EOC activation level and issue the Riverside Hall evacuation order by about 08:00, on the latest Ohio River Forecast Center crest forecast (10.8 ft at about 10:30) rather than waiting for the Mill Street gauge to reach the trigger, and to record the time and the forecast issuance behind each decision as EOP 4 requires.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to send a first MSU Alert within 15 minutes of the flash flood warning, and to answer the 07:50 viral 'students trapped on the roof' post with one confirmed correction, checked with Campus Police and Facilities, on the same channel and consistent with the statements given to Channel 9, The Hilltop and the parent line.
- **Mass Care:** Demonstrate the ability to open Kessler Fieldhouse as a general population shelter, support the two students who use oxygen concentrators and the deaf student in place, keep a shelter count that matches the Riverside Hall roster, and resolve the 09:30 missing-student report within the FERPA limits in MENSO 6.
- **Infrastructure Protection:** Demonstrate the ability to tie the Dairy Lane closure, the Lot C fleet and generator move, the Riverside Hall power isolation and the Whitcomb Library equipment relocation to stage thresholds and clearance times, and to re-route the stranded central plant fuel delivery.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Director of Emergency Management (Incident Commander) (IC):** Owns the campus flood response, the EOC in Campus Services Building room 108, the activation level and the orders to close campus areas and evacuate residence halls.
- **Director of University Communications (Public Information Officer) (PIO):** Owns MSU Alert content, the website banner, social media, media statements and parent messages as the only university spokesperson.
- **Chief of Campus Police (CPD):** Owns road and crossing closures, access control, the Riverside Hall sweep, coordination with Marlowe Police and Fire, and notification decisions with the Clery Compliance Officer.
- **Dean of Students (Housing and Mass Care) (DoS):** Owns residence halls, the Kessler Fieldhouse shelter, student welfare, students with access and functional needs, and communication with parents about students.
- **Associate Vice President for Facilities (Fac):** Owns the central plant, electrical systems, building automation, flood barriers, pumps, the Mill Street gauge dashboard and damage assessment.
- **Student Evaluator (EVAL):** Owns the observation record, timing each seat's decisions and scoring them against the role rubrics from the control table.

## Phase 3: The Riverside Decision (Hour 2:00 – 3:00)

Goal: Tests the one decision point: ordering the evacuation of Riverside Hall's 680 residents by about 08:00 on the forecast crest rather than waiting for the gauge, opening Kessler Fieldhouse, and holding one consistent message against a viral rumor.

Beats: DECISION POINT (07:30–08:00): The IC must decide whether to order the Riverside Hall evacuation now, on the 10.8 ft crest forecast, or wait for the EOP 2 trigger of 8.0 ft plus a forecast of 10.5 ft within 3 hours. Ordering now means 680 students across 11 floors move before dawn classes, on a forecast that could fall.

# EMGT 4320: Exercise Design and Evaluation — Course Syllabus

Marlowe State University · Department of Emergency Management and Homeland Security · Spring 2027 · Revision 2

## 1. Course description

EMGT 4320 teaches undergraduates how a tabletop exercise is designed, run and evaluated under the Homeland Security Exercise and Evaluation Program (HSEEP). The class is a lab: students do not read about response, they take the seats of an incident commander, a public information officer, a police chief, a student affairs dean and a facilities leader, make decisions against a running scenario, and then evaluate each other with rubrics. The course runs on the university's own plans. Every exercise in the term is grounded in the Marlowe State University (MSU) Emergency Operations Plan and its annexes, which the class holds as course documents.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of the course a student can:

- **LO1 Decide and state the basis.** Decide whether to activate a plan or take a protective action, and state the information the decision rests on. (Bloom: apply)
- **LO2 Communicate under time pressure.** Write and send an alert, a statement or a briefing that is accurate, specific and consistent with other messages in play. (Bloom: apply)
- **LO3 Use the plan.** Find the relevant section of a plan or SOP in play and use it, and say where the plan is wrong or silent. (Bloom: analyze)
- **LO4 Coordinate.** Keep a shared picture of the situation with other roles and resolve conflicts between what two roles intend to do. (Bloom: analyze)
- **LO5 Evaluate.** Record an observation that is specific, timed, tied to an objective and free of opinion about the person. (Bloom: evaluate)
- **LO6 Reflect.** Explain what decision they would change and what their role depended on from others. (Bloom: evaluate)

These map to the program outcomes of the B.S. in Emergency Management: PO2 (plans and capabilities), PO4 (communication), PO5 (assessment and improvement).

## 4. Roles and rotation

Six seats are staffed in every session: Incident Commander (the Emergency Manager seat), Public Information Officer, Chief of Campus Police, Dean of Students (housing and mass care), Associate Vice President for Facilities, and Student Evaluator. A seat is played by a team of three or four students who share one voice: one student speaks for the seat during each phase, and the team rotates the speaker every 20 minutes of exercise play. The Student Evaluator seat is staffed by one or two students who use the evaluator form and are graded on the quality of their observations.

Over the term each student plays four different seats in the four graded sessions, one of them the Student Evaluator seat, and a fifth seat in the practice run, so no seat is repeated until all others have been tried (the six seats and five sessions leave one seat unplayed by each student; the rotation plan in document 5 varies which). Seat teams change each session. A student who is absent takes a make-up version of the session, with the same objectives and rubrics but different facts, names and inject content.

## 5. Grading

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Exercise 1 to 4 performance (scored by rubric from evidence) | 48 percent (12 each) |
| Evaluator observation quality (one session as Student Evaluator) | 12 percent |
| Reflections after each session (2 to 5 prompts) | 16 percent |
| Written after-action report for the session of your choice | 16 percent |
| Participation in hot washes | 8 percent |

A student's score for a session comes from the role rubric for the seat they played and the team rubric for the session (document 2). The instructor, not software, assigns every score. Where the teaching assistant recorded evidence, the evidence is shown beside the criterion in the score report the student receives. The suggested level in the app follows a fixed rule (the highest level supported by at least two pieces of evidence) and is a prompt for the instructor, never the grade.

The practice run in Week 2 is not graded and writes nothing to the gradebook. Its report shows students what the rubric looks for.

### The no-fault rule

HSEEP exercises are open, low-stress, no-fault learning environments. They test plans and capabilities, not people. If the plan fails in play, that is a finding about the plan. Grades measure what a student did against the rubric: the reasoning they gave, the messages they wrote, the coordination they attempted. Grades never depend on how the scenario turned out. A team that makes a sound decision on the information it had and still sees the flood reach the residence hall is scored on the decision.

## 6. Evidence and reflection

Three kinds of evidence count toward a score.

1. **Evaluator observations**, recorded by the teaching assistant and the Student Evaluators, each with a student or seat, a criterion, a level and a short note.
2. **Decisions and messages** each seat sends through the exercise tool. Each one is linked to the student speaking at the time.
3. **Hot wash contributions**, tallied by the teaching assistant with one count per contribution. The audio transcript of the hot wash is not used to attribute statements to students.

After each hot wash, students answer the reflection prompts in the lab or on the course page: what decision would you change, what did your role depend on from others, and how does this connect to LO1 to LO6.

## 7. Accommodations

Students with approved accommodations from Student Accessibility Services give Dr. Castellan the letter in the first two weeks. Typical adjustments in this course are extended time on individual prompts (not on the shared exercise clock), a written response instead of a spoken one, a screen-reader-compatible packet or large print, scheduled breaks, and a quieter seat such as Student Evaluator with an equivalent rubric. Adjustments are applied as written and are not announced to the class. The course record stores the adjustment, not the reason for it.

## 8. Records and privacy

Student names, identifiers, scores, reflections and accommodations are education records under FERPA. They stay on the instructor's computer in the course project folder, are left out of shared exercise files, and are exported only by the instructor, to the gradebook as a CSV file of scores. Exercise materials in this course use no real student names; the people in the scenario are fictional.

## 9. Academic integrity

Work in the exercise is the team's and the speaker's. The after-action report is individual work. If you miss a session you take the make-up version; do not ask classmates what the facts were.

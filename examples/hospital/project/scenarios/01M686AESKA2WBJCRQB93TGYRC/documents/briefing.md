# Player Briefing Packet
## Core Collapse — Mass Casualty Surge During a Network Outage

Location: Ottawa River Regional Medical Center, Toledo, Lucas County, Ohio · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 16:45 PM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Overcast with a light drizzle, temperature 41°F

### 1. Situation Brief

It is a busy Friday afternoon at Ottawa River Regional Medical Center, with the campus operating at near-full capacity. The Emergency Department holds 31 patients, both Intensive Care Units are at maximum occupancy, and two operating rooms are actively in use. The hospital is managing a steady flow of patients across all major units, supported by a full complement of clinical and administrative staff. The campus remains functional under normal operations, with routine workflows underway in all departments, including the Outpatient Dialysis Center and Central Plant. All systems are reporting as stable, and no immediate threats have been identified.

- Campus Status: Green
- Building Automation (HVAC, Medical Gas, Generators): Green
- PACS Access Control and Infant Security: Green
- CCTV and Elevators: Green
- Medical Devices on Network (Pumps, Monitors, Imaging): Green
- Pneumatic Tube System: Green
- Fire Alarm Panels: Green
- Water Treatment for Dialysis: Green
- Duress Buttons: Green
- IT Systems (EHR, LIS, PACS, Pharmacy): Green
- Security Operations: Green
- Staffing Levels: Green

**The Anomaly:** At 16:41, the Nursing Supervisor reported a system-wide downtime after the core network switch in Room A-B02 failed. The backup core in Room C-212 did not activate due to outdated configuration. The telephones remain operational, but the EHR, laboratory system, PACS, and secure messaging are unreachable across most of the campus. The IT on-call team has initiated a priority-1 helpdesk ticket and confirmed that the replacement hardware is expected in four hours.

**YOUR MISSION:** Maintain critical patient care and operational continuity while investigating and resolving the network failure, ensuring that patient safety is not compromised by delays in treatment, medication administration, or diagnostic imaging.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: Operations (Command)** | You own the Incident Action Plan and the overall command structure. You decide on activation level, diversion, and coordination across all departments. You report to the Policy Group within 30 minutes of any Cyber 1 event. | **Decide with the CMO when a technical isolation changes care protocols.** **Keep the Policy Group informed within 30 minutes of a Cyber 1 event.** **Maintain the HCC and report to the HICS structure every four hours at Level 1.** |
| **Team B: Technology** | You own the restoration of the network core and clinical systems. You assess whether the failure is internal or external, determine realistic restore times, and validate system integrity before reintroducing any clinical function. | **Tell the HCC the time you will report next, even when you have no news.** **Do not bring a partly restored system back into clinical use without telling the CNO what it will and will not show.** **Report all changes in system status immediately, even if they are negative.** |
| **Team C: Clinical** | You own patient care continuity during system downtime. You enforce downtime procedures, manage medication safety, and ensure unit censuses and acuity levels are reported hourly. | **Patient safety first: two-person checks replace barcode scanning for high-alert drugs and blood.** **Units report census, acuity, and open beds at the top of every hour at Level 1.** **Ensure all staff follow the EHR Downtime Procedures Annex and use the downtime cart when necessary.** |
| **Team D: Medical Care** | You own triage, treatment, and movement in the Emergency Department. You decide on diversion, expectant categories, and patient placement in surge spaces, in coordination with the AOC. | **Keep the ED moving: discharge and move admitted patients out of the ED to make room.** **Triage decisions in a crisis-standards situation are made by a physician, never a registration clerk.** **Assign and track surge space utilization in real time using paper logs.** |
| **Team E: Communications** | You own public messaging and media coordination. You release information only after clearance by the Information Security Officer and the Chief Privacy Officer. You maintain a media log and ensure no patient-specific data is disclosed without approval. | **Never say patient data was not accessed unless the Chief Privacy Officer has cleared the words.** **Keep a media log; all public statements are timestamped and approved.** **Release information only from facts cleared by the IS and Privacy Officers.** |
| **Team F: Security and Facilities** | You own facility safety, access control, and physical security. You monitor for unauthorized access, manage shelter-in-place and evacuation readiness, and ensure that all critical systems (including generators and water reserves) are functional and monitored. | **Report any suspicious activity or unauthorized access immediately.** **Ensure all doors, locks, and access points are functioning and logged.** **Activate shelter-in-place protocols if directed by the Incident Commander.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- All radio and phone communications must begin and end with 'This is an exercise' to prevent real-world panic.
- If a real emergency occurs, immediately say 'REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD' and the exercise will stop.
- The facilitator may announce 'Time Jump: it is now [time]' to skip ahead and simulate the passage of time.
- Printed injects (posts, logs, photos, reports) are intelligence and must be treated as real-time information.

# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Water Line, No Drink — A Regional Water Advisory That Reaches Dialysis

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Ottawa River Regional Medical Center, Toledo, Lucas County, Ohio · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise simulating a regional infrastructure failure involving a Do Not Drink advisory that impacts critical hospital systems, particularly dialysis and water-dependent clinical operations. The scenario unfolds over four hours, beginning at 06:15 on a Saturday, March 14, 2026, as the City of Toledo issues a water advisory due to a disruption at its treatment plant. The injects simulate cascading operational, clinical, and public information challenges tied to a failure in a core utility—potable water—throughout a large healthcare system. The arc progresses from initial awareness (Phase 1) through conservation and crisis management (Phases 2–3) to long-term planning (Phase 4), testing the hospital’s ability to sustain care under resource constraints. Evaluators should watch for alignment with HICS Level 2 activation, proper use of downtime procedures, documentation of decisions, coordination with external agencies, and the accuracy of communication—especially regarding dialysis continuity, infant feeding, and public messaging. The exercise emphasizes a single voice, evidence preservation, and the need to balance competing priorities under pressure.

The core challenges involve water rationing, dialysis continuity, infection control, and public trust. Evaluators should assess whether the Incident Action Plan includes measurable objectives for each operational period, whether conservation measures are implemented systematically, and whether decisions to transfer patients or accept external support are justified and documented. The exercise also tests the ability to manage misinformation and maintain a consistent public narrative despite external pressure. The SimCell roles are critical to testing these elements, and evaluators must track whether players rely on established protocols (e.g., EOP 4.3 for activation levels) or improvise under stress.

**Objectives:**
- **Stored Water and Conservation:** Demonstrate the ability to protect the 40,000-gallon supply, set conservation levels and name the points at which services are reduced (EOP Section 8).
- **Dialysis Continuity:** Demonstrate the ability to decide which dialysis patients must be treated first, where, and how they get there when the hospital's loop fails its tests.
- **Infection Prevention and Sterile Processing:** Demonstrate the ability to adapt sterile processing, food service, hand hygiene and patient care to a Do Not Drink advisory with public health guidance.
- **One Voice:** Demonstrate the ability to give staff, patients and the press the same facts, and to coordinate with the City of Toledo Department of Public Utilities, the Lucas County EMA and the Toledo-Lucas County Health Department.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- U-shape table arrangement simulating the Hospital Command Center (HCC), with the Incident Commander (AOC) at the head, flanked by Infrastructure (DFEM), Clinical (CNO), Medical Care (NEPH), and Communications (PIO) teams.
- Each team occupies a designated table: Team A (Command) at the head, Team B (Infrastructure) to the right, Team C (Clinical) to the left, Team D (Medical Care) behind CNO, and Team E (Communications) at a separate rear table, isolated from the main HCC.
- The Public Information Officer (PIO) role is seated at the rear table to simulate real-world separation from command and to test their need to walk to the HCC for information, reinforcing the principle of a single voice and avoiding premature public statements.
- Equipment needs: Projector for main-screen injects, whiteboard for real-time ICS-201 form, printed inject deck (laminated, with color-coded tabs), optional simulated radios/phones for role-play.
- Materials for players: Printed HCC binder, EOP 4.3 (activation levels), downtime procedures, and a one-page cheat sheet of key contact numbers and reporting channels.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

You are the SimCell: you represent everyone outside the hospital. When a player says, 'I call the city water department,' you respond as the City of Toledo Department of Public Utilities. When they say, 'I call the FBI,' you answer as the FBI Cleveland Field Office. You are not a guide; you are a source of information, pressure, or constraint. Do not explain, justify, or clarify what a player says. Your role is to reflect the world as it would be: reactive, limited, and bound by policy.

Do not make it easy. If a player says, 'I’ll just call the vendor and get a new RO unit,' you respond: 'The vendor says they don’t ship to Ohio until Tuesday and require a signed access request and a ticket number. No exceptions.' This prevents over-reliance on vendor promises and forces players to follow process.

Managing Time: At the scheduled time-jump points (e.g., 07:15, 08:15, 09:15), announce: 'PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. The tank is at 37,300 gallons. Use is down to 1,300 gallons per hour. Resumes at 07:16.' This maintains momentum and prevents players from over-clarifying.

Handling artificialities: If a player says, 'I wouldn’t use that system,' respond in character: 'We’re not here to debate. We’re here to run the hospital. You’re on the system. Use it.' This reinforces that the exercise is real and internal decisions must be made under constraints.

Delivering injects: When an inject appears on screen, say: 'This just came in.' or 'This is on the main screen.' Do not preface with context or explain the source. Let the players react to the information as it arrives.

This is an exercise: If a player says, 'I’m going to the ER now,' respond: 'You’re in the HCC. The ER is on lockdown. You’re not leaving.' This maintains situational control.

REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD: If a player says, 'I’d never do that,' or 'That’s not how it works,' respond: 'REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD. You’re doing it now. That’s how it works. Let’s proceed.' This keeps the simulation moving and avoids meta-commentary.

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **City of Toledo Department of Public Utilities** — _Trigger: AOC calls for water advisory status update_
  - Ask: "What is your timeline for lifting the advisory? We need a date to plan dialysis resumption."
  - Ask: "Do you have a sample protocol for hospitals to follow when testing for safe water? We need to validate your lab results."
  - Ask: "Are you issuing a flushing instruction for the hospital’s internal lines? If so, when?"
  - Ask: "Do you have a plan for transporting clean water to facilities that cannot self-supply?"
  - Concede only when: Will confirm that two clean sample sets are required to lift the advisory.
  - Concede only when: Will provide a flushing instruction after two clean samples are confirmed.
  - Concede only when: Will accept a formal request for water tankers if the hospital submits a priority list and connection details.
- **Regional Healthcare Coalition (RHC)** — _Trigger: AOC requests mutual aid for dialysis capacity_
  - Ask: "What is your plan for transporting patients across the advisory area? We cannot accept a patient without a documented transfer protocol."
  - Ask: "Do you have a list of partner facilities with clean water and operating dialysis units? We need to validate their readiness."
  - Ask: "What is your role in verifying that a receiving facility has not been exposed to the advisory water?"
  - Ask: "How do you ensure that a patient transferred from one facility to another does not carry contamination risks?"
  - Concede only when: Will provide a list of verified partner facilities with clean water and active dialysis capacity.
  - Concede only when: Will coordinate with the Ohio Hospital Association to validate transfer protocols.
  - Concede only when: Will accept a signed transfer agreement and a 48-hour lab history from the sending hospital.
- **FBI Cleveland Field Office** — _Trigger: AOC contacts FBI after suspecting a cyberattack on water treatment systems_
  - Ask: "What is your jurisdiction over a public utility’s water treatment system? We are not a healthcare entity."
  - Ask: "Do you have forensic access to the city’s network logs? We need to see if this was a sabotage or a malware event."
  - Ask: "How do you define a cyber incident that crosses into public health? We need to know if this is a threat to patient safety."
  - Ask: "What is your process for notifying healthcare facilities when a utility is compromised?"
  - Concede only when: Will provide a preliminary assessment of the incident as a cyber threat if the city confirms a breach.
  - Concede only when: Will accept a formal request for investigation with a case number and a signed access agreement.
  - Concede only when: Will share threat indicators if the city provides a timeline of the compromise.
- **Neighboring Hospital Transfer Center** — _Trigger: AOC asks to transfer inpatients for dialysis_
  - Ask: "What is your policy on accepting patients from facilities under a Do Not Drink advisory? We need to know your risk assessment."
  - Ask: "Do you have a protocol for receiving patients with contaminated water exposure in their medical record? We need to validate your infection control procedures."
  - Ask: "What is your timeline for admitting a patient with a potassium of 6.1? We need to know your capacity and triage process."
  - Ask: "Can you confirm that your dialysis unit uses clean water and that your RO system is not connected to the advisory area?"
  - Concede only when: Will accept inpatients for transfer if the sending hospital provides a physician-to-physician handoff and lab data from the past 48 hours.
  - Concede only when: Will confirm that its dialysis unit is on a separate water source and has passed its own RO testing.
  - Concede only when: Will accept a signed transfer agreement and a copy of the patient’s most recent lab results.
- **Ottawa River Regional Medical Center EHR Vendor (HealthSyst Inc.)** — _Trigger: AOC contacts vendor to request EHR access for patient data during downtime_
  - Ask: "Do you have a break-glass account for emergency EHR access? We need to see the log of your last use."
  - Ask: "What is your policy on remote access during a system outage? We need to know if you’re still connected."
  - Ask: "Do you have a backup of the EHR database from before the outage? We need to verify integrity."
  - Ask: "How do you ensure that no unauthorized user has accessed the system during the downtime?"
  - Concede only when: Will provide a copy of the EHR database from the last clean backup if the hospital submits a signed request and a ticket number.
  - Concede only when: Will grant temporary remote access only after a signed access request and a verification of the ISO’s approval.
  - Concede only when: Will accept a copy of the hospital’s downtime policy and a log of system access attempts.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Use the '3-Up, 3-Down' method by asking each team to share three things that went well and three that broke or failed during the exercise. Go around the room by team, capturing responses on a whiteboard to ensure all voices are heard. Keep the discussion focused on operational performance, communication, and decision-making under pressure.

**Key Discussion Questions for Water Line, No Drink:**
- **Trust:** When did you first stop trusting the water test results from the dialysis loop, and what changed your mind about whether to trust the city’s field samples or the hospital’s internal lab data? _(Objective: Dialysis Continuity)_
- **The Gap:** At the moment you decided to close the city fill valve, what information was missing that made it hard to decide whether to protect stored water or risk contamination for conservation? How did that gap affect your confidence in the decision? _(Objective: Stored Water and Conservation)_
- **The Call:** When you received the Facebook post about a patient stuck in a wheelchair with no ride, who did you call, and what would have allowed you to respond faster and prevent misinformation from spreading? _(Objective: One Voice)_
- **The Evidence:** When the field report said the city water smelled stronger and the RO outlet conductivity was normal, did you treat that as evidence or as a ticket to keep operating? At what point did you decide to stop relying on sensory or partial data and act on public health guidance? _(Objective: Infection Prevention and Sterile Processing)_
- **The Partner:** When you learned the neighboring hospital had open beds but no transport plan, what would have allowed you to decide faster to activate a regional transport protocol or involve the regional healthcare coalition? _(Objective: One Voice)_

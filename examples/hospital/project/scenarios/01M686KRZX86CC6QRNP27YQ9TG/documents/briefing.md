# Player Briefing Packet
## Water Line, No Drink — A Regional Water Advisory That Reaches Dialysis

Location: Ottawa River Regional Medical Center, Toledo, Lucas County, Ohio · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 06:15 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Partly cloudy, 42°F with a light breeze

### 1. Situation Brief

It is a busy Saturday morning at Ottawa River Regional Medical Center, with a full inpatient census of 322 and nine outpatient dialysis appointments scheduled for 07:00. The campus is operating under normal weekday conditions, with surgical teams preparing for six elective procedures beginning at 07:30. The Central Plant is holding a full 40,000-gallon potable water tank, and all utility systems—power, medical gas, HVAC, and building automation—are functioning as expected. The hospital’s infrastructure and clinical teams are staffed at normal levels, and the facility is in a state of routine operational readiness.

- Campus Status: Normal / Green
- Power (2 utility feeds, 3 generators): Normal / Green
- Water Supply (municipal + 40,000-gal tank): Normal / Green
- Medical Gas (bulk oxygen, air, vacuum): Normal / Green
- HVAC and Building Automation (BMS, ORRMC-BMS01): Normal / Green
- PACS Access Control and Infant Security (ORRMC-PACS01): Normal / Green
- CCTV and Elevator Systems: Normal / Green
- Medical Devices on Network (pumps, monitors, imaging): Normal / Green
- Pneumatic Tube System: Normal / Green
- Fire Alarm Panels and Water Treatment for Dialysis: Normal / Green
- IT Systems (EHR, LIS, SQL, BMS, PACS): Normal / Green
- Security and Duress Buttons: Normal / Green
- Staffing Levels (clinical and facilities): Normal / Green

**The Anomaly:** At 06:02, the City of Toledo Department of Public Utilities issued a public notice to the Lucas County Emergency Management Agency stating that a disruption at a water treatment plant has led to a Do Not Drink advisory for the region, including the Ottawa River Regional Medical Center. The advisory cites no known cause, but water pressure remains normal. The Central Plant’s 40,000-gallon potable water storage tank is currently full and confirmed to be uncontaminated.

**YOUR MISSION:** Maintain uninterrupted patient care and critical medical operations while investigating and resolving the water supply disruption, balancing the need for immediate action against the risk of compromising patient safety, particularly in dialysis, infection control, and medication administration.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: Operations (Command)** | You own the overall incident command and decision-making authority under HICS. You are responsible for activating the appropriate level of response, issuing the Incident Action Plan, and coordinating all sections. You ensure that all command decisions are made with input from the Chief Medical Officer and the Policy Group. | **Decide: Convene the HCC within 30 minutes of activation and determine the activation level.** **Lead: Coordinate all section chiefs and ensure information flows through the HCC.** **Maintain: The Incident Action Plan is updated every operational period and approved by the Chief Medical Officer.** |
| **Team B: Infrastructure** | You own the management of the Central Plant, water conservation, utility continuity, and logistics for external support. You are responsible for protecting the stored potable water, prioritizing system shutdowns by clinical impact, and verifying any external water source before connection. | **Protect: The 40,000-gallon storage tank is the highest priority; never compromise its integrity.** **Decide: What to disconnect in order of clinical impact if conservation is required.** **Verify: No external water tanker may be connected without prior Infection Prevention verification of certification and chlorine residual.** |
| **Team C: Clinical** | You own the execution of patient care during system outages. You are responsible for implementing downtime procedures, ensuring medication safety, and maintaining unit census and acuity reporting. You oversee staffing and care continuity across all inpatient units. | **Patient: Safety is paramount—two-person checks replace barcode scanning for high-alert drugs and blood.** **Report: Census, acuity, and open beds are reported to the HCC at the top of every hour.** **Maintain: All units follow downtime protocols until the HCC declares a new status.** |
| **Team D: Medical Care** | You own the triage and treatment decisions for dialysis patients. You are responsible for determining which patients can safely wait, which must be treated immediately, and where treatment can be conducted using safe water sources. You ensure all dialysis is conducted only on water that has passed loop testing. | **Treat: Patients with dangerous potassium or fluid overload first, regardless of location.** **Reject: Any dialysis run on water that has not passed the reverse osmosis loop tests.** **Coordinate: With the DFEM to determine if alternate water sources can be used safely.** |
| **Team E: Communications** | You own the public release of information and media coordination. You are responsible for ensuring all public statements are cleared by the Information Security Officer and the Chief Privacy Officer. You maintain a media log and track all public inquiries. | **Release: Only information cleared by the CPO and ISO may be shared.** **Log: All media contacts and inquiries are recorded in real time.** **Never: Use the word 'exposed' or 'compromised' without explicit clearance from the Chief Privacy Officer.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- All radio and phone communications must begin and end with 'This is an exercise' to prevent real-world panic.
- If a real emergency occurs, immediately say 'REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD' and the exercise will stop immediately.
- The facilitator may announce 'Time Jump: it is now [time]' to skip forward in the timeline and simulate passage of time.
- Printed injects (such as logs, reports, photos, and messages) are to be treated as real intelligence and must be acted upon accordingly.

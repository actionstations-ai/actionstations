# Player Briefing Packet
## Paper Charts — EHR Downtime and Trust in the Record

Location: Ottawa River Regional Medical Center, Toledo, Lucas County, Ohio · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 05:40 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Overcast, light drizzle, 42°F

### 1. Situation Brief

It is a busy Tuesday morning at Ottawa River Regional Medical Center, with a full inpatient census of 331 and 21 patients in the Emergency Department. The day shift is beginning its routine, and nine surgical cases are scheduled to begin at 07:30. The Outpatient Dialysis Center is preparing for 11 scheduled patients at 08:00. Staffing levels are at full capacity across all units, and all departments are operating under normal workflows. The campus is quiet, with no reports of external hazards, utility outages, or security incidents. All systems appear functional—power, water, medical gas, and telephone lines are operating normally. The EHR remains unresponsive across multiple units, but no formal alert has yet been issued.

- Campus Status: Green
- Building Automation (HVAC, Medical Gas, Generators): Green
- PACS Access Control and Infant Security: Green
- CCTV and Elevator Systems: Green
- Networked Medical Devices (Pumps, Monitors, Imaging): Green
- Pneumatic Tube System: Green
- Fire Alarm Panels: Green
- Water Treatment for Dialysis: Green
- Duress Buttons (All Units): Green
- IT Infrastructure (EHR, SQL, Application Servers): Red (EHR Unresponsive)
- Pharmacy Medication Cache (Mass Casualty): Green
- Central Supply Surge Cache (Cots, Ventilator Circuits, Supplies): Green

**The Anomaly:** At 05:40, the night nursing supervisor, Gail Moreau, contacts the Chief Nursing Officer via phone: 'CNO, it is Gail Moreau on nights. The EHR will not open on 4 West, 5 East, ICU 3 North, or in the ED. eMAR is frozen, and 06:00 medications are due on every unit. The last printed medication packet is from 01:00. IT says they are looking at it. Do I declare downtime or wait for IT?'

**YOUR MISSION:** Maintain patient safety and continuity of care while investigating and resolving the EHR downtime, balancing urgent clinical needs with the need to preserve evidence and prevent further system compromise.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: Operations (Command)** | You own the overall command structure, including HICS activation, Incident Action Plan development, and decisions on diversion, downtime level, and public messaging. You ensure no data is altered or erased before forensic review. | **Preserve evidence before anything is wiped or rebuilt.** **A restore is not an all-clear: watch restored systems for at least 72 hours.** **Coordinate all external reporting through CISA, FBI, and HHS as directed by the AOC.** |
| **Team C: Clinical** | You own patient care continuity during downtime, including medication safety, census reporting, staffing, and adherence to downtime procedures. You ensure all units report acuity and bed availability hourly. | **Patient safety first: two-person checks replace barcode scanning for high-alert drugs and blood.** **Units report census, acuity, and open beds at the top of every hour at Level 1.** **Activate downtime carts and paper workflows immediately upon declaration.** |
| **Team D: Infrastructure** | You own the operation and manual fallbacks for networked medical devices, including infusion pumps, monitors, ventilators, and analyzers. You ensure no device is replaced at the bedside without CNO approval. | **Never trade a device that is working at the bedside for central visibility without the CNO agreeing.** **Verify all manual fallbacks are accessible and functional in every unit.** **Report any device failure or instability to the CNO within 10 minutes.** |
| **Team E: Communications** | You own all public and internal messaging, including media releases, staff communications, and external coordination. You ensure all statements are cleared by the ISO and CPO. | **Never say patient data was not accessed unless the Chief Privacy Officer has cleared the words.** **Keep a media log; patient-specific information is released only as the Chief Privacy Officer allows.** **All external statements must be approved by the AOC and ISO before release.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- All radio and phone communications must begin and end with 'This is an exercise' to prevent confusion with real emergencies.
- If a real emergency occurs, immediately say 'REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD' and the exercise will stop immediately.
- The facilitator may announce 'Time Jump: it is now [time]' to skip forward in the scenario.
- Printed injects (posts, logs, photos, reports) are to be treated as real intelligence and used to inform decisions.

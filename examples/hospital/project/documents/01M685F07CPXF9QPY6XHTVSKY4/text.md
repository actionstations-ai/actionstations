## SECTION 1: PURPOSE

This annex sets out how ORRMC increases capacity for a surge of patients, protects patients inside the building by sheltering in place, and moves patients horizontally, vertically or out of the campus. It meets the evacuation, shelter-in-place, tracking and arrangement-with-other-facilities elements of 42 CFR 482.15(b).

## SECTION 2: SURGE TRIGGERS AND LEVELS

| Surge level | Trigger | Capacity actions |
|---|---|---|
| Level A (Conventional) | ED holds more than 12 admitted patients for 4 hours, or the census exceeds 90% | Open the PACU and Infusion Center as extra space; expedite discharges before 11:00 |
| Level B (Contingency) | A mass casualty incident of 20 or more patients, or the census exceeds 100% | Cancel elective cases; discharge-ready patients leave within 2 hours; convert single rooms to double; open the Surgical Pavilion pre-op area, Cardiac Rehabilitation and the Pavilion C waiting areas as treatment space; recall off-duty staff |
| Level C (Crisis) | Demand exceeds all resources, or the EHR or other critical systems are down during a surge | AOC activates crisis standards of care with the Chief Medical Officer; triage team in the ED; request transfers to other hospitals through the regional coalition |

ED capacity: 48 treatment spaces, extendable to 90 with hallway and Pavilion C overflow. Inpatient surge: licensed 412 beds, staffed 340; surge to 460 with additional beds and cots. Operating rooms 1–12: 12 rooms, six staffed overnight.

## SECTION 3: MASS CASUALTY RESPONSE

On notice from Lucas County EMS or Toledo Fire and Rescue of a mass casualty incident, the ED charge nurse and the ED physician: call Code Triage; the Nursing Supervisor opens the HCC; the Triage Officer stands at the ambulance entrance; the Decontamination and Isolation Suite is made ready for contaminated patients; the helipad is closed to non-trauma landings. Security closes all but two public entrances and sets up a family reunification point in the Tower A lobby. Patient tracking uses the paper tracking board when the EHR is not available, with a trauma number for every arriving patient.

Blood bank, OR, imaging and ICU leaders report capacity to the HCC every 30 minutes. Patients with minor injuries go to the Pavilion C urgent care space.

## SECTION 4: SHELTER IN PLACE AND EVACUATION DECISIONS

Shelter in place when the hazard is outside (tornado, civil disturbance, outdoor release) or when moving patients is riskier than leaving them. Evacuate when a fire, flood, structural damage, loss of utilities for more than the available reserve, or a hazardous material inside makes the unit unsafe.

**Evacuation phases:** (1) Horizontal: move patients behind the nearest fire doors on the same floor; (2) Vertical: move patients down by stairs and, where elevators are safe, in beds; (3) External: move patients to an assembly area and onto vehicles to receiving hospitals. Only the Incident Commander orders external evacuation, after notice to Toledo Fire and Rescue and the Lucas County EMA.

**Order of movement:** patients who can walk first; then patients who need assistance; then bed-bound patients; critical care patients, ventilator-dependent patients, infants and patients in the OR last, with a nurse and portable equipment. The charge nurse assigns evacuation sleds, portable oxygen and manual ventilation bags.

**Special populations:** ICU 3 North and 3 South (32 beds): patients on ventilators and infusions; 6 West: mothers and 18 bassinets, infant protection tags; 7 West: locked behavioral health patients, with Security escort; the Outpatient Dialysis Center: patients stopped safely and disconnected; the OR: finish or stabilize the case.

## SECTION 5: PATIENT TRACKING DURING EVACUATION

Every evacuated patient wears the evacuation tag with their name, medical record number or DT- number, allergies, code status, diagnosis and the receiving facility. The Patient Movement Log records time out, destination, transport and receiving contact. The HCC keeps a master list and shares it, as allowed by HIPAA, with receiving hospitals and the Lucas County EMA. Families receive information from the family reunification center.

## SECTION 6: RECEIVING FACILITIES AND TRANSPORT

Receiving hospitals are chosen with the regional healthcare coalition by capability: trauma, critical care, perinatal, pediatric, behavioral health, dialysis. Lucas County EMS coordinates ambulance transport; school buses and wheelchair vans from the transportation contract move walking patients. Staff accompany their patients as far as the receiving unit.

## SECTION 7: SUPPLIES AND UTILITIES DURING SURGE AND EVACUATION

Central Supply maintains a surge cache: 100 cots, 60 ventilator circuits, and 7 days of med-surg supplies; the pharmacy maintains a mass casualty medication cache. If power fails, the generators carry the campus (see Section 3 of the Facilities Description). If municipal water is lost or advised against use, the Central Plant tank supplies essential areas; dialysis is paused or moved; the water-hauling contract is activated.

## SECTION 8: COMMUNICATIONS AND RETURN

The PIO releases a statement within 60 minutes of activation: what happened, the status of the hospital, and a number for families. After the hazard is gone, the Safety Officer and the Director of Facilities inspect each unit and the AOC authorizes return; patients are returned in reverse order of acuity.

## ANNEX A: DIVERSION CRITERIA

The hospital goes on diversion for ambulance traffic when the ED cannot safely accept more patients, or a critical system (the EHR, CT, power, water) is down. Diversion is requested by the ED charge nurse, approved by the AOC and the ED Medical Director, and reported to Lucas County EMS dispatch and the coalition. Diversion is reviewed every two hours and never applies to patients in extremis or to inbound trauma activations already in the building.

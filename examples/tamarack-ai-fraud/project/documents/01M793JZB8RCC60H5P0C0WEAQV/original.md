# TPC-COM-03: Executive Impersonation, Verification and External Statements

Tamarack Precision Components, Inc. · Corporate Communications and Security · Revision 2

## 1. Why this policy exists

Executives of manufacturers are easy to imitate. Their voices are on earnings-style town halls, conference talks and the company's YouTube channel; their photographs are on the website; their travel is published by trade media. Tamarack assumes that a convincing video or voice of any executive can be produced by an outsider.

## 2. Verification rules for staff

- A video meeting, call or voice message is not proof of identity.
- Any request involving money, credentials, access, confidential deal information or secrecy is verified by a second channel the staff member chooses, using the Tamarack directory.
- Staff who receive an instruction to keep a payment or acquisition "confidential" from colleagues must tell the Controller or the CISO, who will not treat that as disloyal.
- Executives will not use video calls from personal accounts to give financial instructions. Meeting invitations from outside the Tamarack tenant for internal-looking subjects are flagged to the recipient.
- No staff member is disciplined for pausing a payment or refusing an instruction to verify it.

## 3. Executives' own safeguards

Executives agree a passphrase with their assistant and the Treasury Manager, updated quarterly and kept off email. The executive assistants (CFO's assistant: Naomi Vandenberg, 864-555-0131) keep travel calendars so Treasury can check where an executive actually is. Executive voice samples are not posted beyond what is already public.

## 4. When a fake involving Tamarack circulates

Communications is told when a synthetic video, voice or image of a Tamarack person or product is found externally, whether sent by a reporter, a customer or seen on social platforms. The sequence:

1. **Verify:** security confirms with the person depicted and checks the file. A clip is never described as fake until verification is complete; before then, the language is "we are looking into a video that has been brought to our attention".
2. **Preserve:** the clip is saved, the URL, account, time seen, and number of views are recorded.
3. **Holding statement:** within 60 minutes of the first reporter inquiry or public spread, Communications issues a holding statement that says what is known, what is not known and what is being done. It does not repeat the false content more than necessary.
4. **Takedown:** the CISO and Communications send the platform trust and safety team a request with the evidence and the platform's impersonation form. Requests are logged with time and reference.
5. **Correction:** if a false claim has been published, Communications sends a correction naming what was wrong and what is right to the outlet, and posts it on the newsroom page.

## 5. Statement approvals

Statements that mention a loss, fraud, a criminal investigation or an AI system require the General Counsel's approval and the CFO's approval of any figure. The Director of Corporate Communications (Dorothea Skarsgaard-Blum, 864-555-0120) issues. Amounts are given only as signed by the Controller. The employee message is sent no later than the external statement. Customers and suppliers who may have been contacted by the same fraudsters (the "vendor" in the scheme) are warned by the account owners using a script, not an email blast.

## 6. Media

The company does not comment on law enforcement matters beyond "we have reported the matter to the FBI." Reporters from the Greenville business press and wire services receive the holding statement. Social listening monitors mentions of Tamarack and its executives hourly during an incident. The Director of Corporate Communications decides whether to respond to individual posts.

## 7. The board

The audit committee chair is told of any incident above $1 million within 24 hours. The full board is briefed at its next meeting or sooner.

# PART I: BASIC PLAN

## SECTION 1: PROMULGATION AND AUTHORITY

### 1.1 Legal Authority
This Airport Emergency Plan (AEP) is developed, promulgated, and maintained under the authority of the North Coast Regional Airport Authority (NCRAA). It has been developed in direct compliance with: Title 14 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 139.325, Airport Emergency Plan; FAA Advisory Circular 150/5200-31C, Airport Emergency Plan; Homeland Security Presidential Directive 5 (HSPD-5), Management of Domestic Incidents; the Robert T. ## SECTION 2: PURPOSE AND SCOPE

### 2.1 Purpose
The primary purpose of this AEP is to establish a comprehensive framework for the response to, management of, and recovery from emergencies occurring at or affecting North Coast International Airport. Specifically, this plan seeks to:
- **Coordinate Response:** Establish a unified command structure integrating airport, local, state, and federal agencies.
- **Assign Responsibility:** Clearly define the duties and legal authorities of all responding agencies to prevent duplication of effort or jurisdictional confusion.
- **Ensure Compliance:** Satisfy the requirements of the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) for airport certification.
- **Protect Critical Assets:** Minimize the impact of emergencies on airport operations, infrastructure, and the regional economy.

### 2.2 Scope
This plan applies to all emergencies, hazards, and critical incidents occurring within the jurisdictional boundaries of North Coast International Airport, defined as:
- **The Air Operations Area (AOA):** Including Runways 5/23 and 1/19, all taxiways, ramps, and safety areas.
- **The Landside Area:** Including the Main Terminal Building, Cargo Facilities, Fuel Farm, Parking Decks, and Airport Boulevard (up to the intersection with I-77).
- **The Airspace:** Immediate airspace (Class C) under the control of the NCIA Air Traffic Control Tower (ATCT), specifically regarding aircraft emergencies originating or terminating at NCIA.

### 2.3 Limitations
This plan does not supersede the specific tactical Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) of mutual aid agencies (e.g., Akron Fire Department firefighting tactics or FBI hostage negotiation tactics). Rather, it provides the coordination interface between those agencies and the Airport Authority.

## SECTION 3: SITUATION AND ASSUMPTIONS

### 3.1 Situation Overview

**A. General Location and Description.** North Coast International Airport (NCIA) is a public-use airport owned and operated by the North Coast Regional Airport Authority (NCRAA). Location: Summit County, Ohio, approximately 10 miles southeast of the City of Akron and 35 miles south of Cleveland. Coordinates: 41° 02' N / 81° 28' W. Elevation: 1,228 feet MSL. Certification: Class I Airport Operating Certificate under 14 CFR Part 139, authorizing scheduled passenger operations of large air carrier aircraft.

**B. Airside Facilities.** The AOA encompasses 1,800 acres enclosed by an 8-foot chain-link security perimeter fence topped with 3-strand barbed wire.
- **Runway 5/23 (Primary):** 10,001 ft × 150 ft. Grooved asphalt/concrete. HIRL and centerline lights. Runway 23 served by a Category II ILS.
- **Runway 1/19 (Crosswind):** 7,600 ft × 150 ft. Grooved asphalt. MIRL. Runway 1 served by a Category I ILS.
- **Taxiways:** Full parallel taxiway system (Taxiway A and B) supports both runways.
- **Aprons:** Main Terminal Ramp: 18 gates (A1–A8, B1–B10). North Cargo Ramp: heavy cargo operations (SwiftFreight/ParcelAir feeder). General Aviation (GA) Ramp: southeast quadrant, managed by FBO "North Coast Jet Center."

**C. Landside Facilities.** Terminal Building: two-level structure. Level 1: Arrivals/Baggage Claim. Level 2: Ticketing/Departures. Fuel Farm: Northeast perimeter. Capacity: 2.5 million gallons Jet-A and 50,000 gallons 100LL, fed by the Buckeye Pipeline spur. ARFF Station: mid-field between Runway 5/23 and Taxiway A. Access Roads: Airport Boulevard, connecting directly to Interstate 77 (Exit 122).

**D. Demographic Profile.** Passengers: 3.2 million annual enplanements. Peak hours 0600–0900 and 1600–1900. Employees: approximately 2,200 badged employees on-site daily. Transient population can exceed 15,000 during holiday peaks.

### 3.2 Critical Infrastructure Dependencies

**Electrical Power.** Primary: Northcoast Electric via two separate substations (North and South Feeds). Backup: three diesel generators —
- Gen-1 (2000 kW): Airfield Lighting Vault (runway lights/NAVAIDS). Runtime 48 hours at full load.
- Gen-2 (1500 kW): Terminal Life Safety (fire/emergency lighting) and Security (PACS/CCTV). Runtime 24 hours.
- Gen-3 (500 kW): ARFF Station and Emergency Operations Center (EOC).
- **Gap:** Terminal HVAC and general concourse power outlets are NOT on backup power.

**Communications.** Voice: Summit County 800 MHz P25 Digital Radio System. Data: redundant fiber optic loops from AT&T and Spectrum Enterprise. Notification: the "Crash Phone" (Red Phone) hardlines the Control Tower to ARFF, AOC, and Dispatch.

**Water/Sanitation.** City of Akron Water Department; a 24-inch main feeds the terminal and the fire hydrant loop.

Law Enforcement: Airport Police Department, 4 officers on duty 24/7 — initial containment, not sustained tactical operations. Operations: 2 Duty Managers on shift — airfield inspection and initial ICS setup.

**B. Mutual Aid Gaps.**

|---|---|---|

### 3.5 Planning Assumptions
- **Notification:** Incidents may occur with little or no warning. Initial notification will likely come from the ATCT or a 911 call.
- **Initial Overwhelm:** A significant incident (Alert 3 or Mass Casualty) will immediately exhaust all on-duty airport resources.
- **Mutual Aid Lag:** Full mobilization of a "2nd Alarm" response will take 12–15 minutes to stage on the airfield.
- **Self-Sufficiency:** In a regional disaster, mutual aid may be unavailable. The airport must be prepared to operate in island mode for up to 72 hours.
- **Jurisdiction:** The Airport Authority retains control of the airfield and airport operations. The Fire Department assumes Command of fire/rescue/hazmat scenes. The Police Department assumes Command of security/criminal scenes. The FBI assumes jurisdiction over acts of terrorism or aircraft piracy.
- **Preservation of Evidence:** For aircraft accidents, the NTSB has absolute authority over the wreckage.

## SECTION 4: CONCEPT OF OPERATIONS (CONOPS)

### 4.1 General Response Strategy
- **Phase 1: Discovery and Notification.** Incident detected (ATC, 911, sensor alarm). The AOC initiates the "Crash Phone" or mass notification system. Initial responders (ARFF/Police) deploy.
- **Phase 2: Response and Stabilization.** First arriving unit establishes the Incident Command Post (ICP). Immediate life safety actions. Unified Command if multiple agencies. EOC activated if necessary to support the field.
- **Phase 3: Recovery and Restoration.** Threat eliminated or contained. Investigative bodies (NTSB, FBI) take control of the specific scene. Airport Operations focuses on reopening unaffected runways and terminals. Demobilization begins.

### 4.2 National Incident Management System (NIMS)
NCIA utilizes NIMS for all emergency events. All responding agencies must utilize the Incident Command System (ICS). **Plain Language:** "10-codes" are prohibited during multi-agency response. **Span of Control:** No supervisor shall manage more than 5–7 subordinates. **Integrated Communications:** All agencies use the Summit County 800 MHz Interoperability Talkgroups.

### 4.3 Direction and Control
**A. Incident Commander (IC).** Jurisdiction determined by the primary threat: Fire/HAZMAT/Aircraft Crash → Akron Fire Department (Battalion Chief). Security/Terrorism/Criminal Act → Akron Police Department (Watch Commander). Airfield Operations/Snow/Power → Airport Operations (Duty Manager).

**B. Unified Command (UC).** For complex incidents involving overlapping jurisdictions, a Unified Command is established at the ICP. Example: an aircraft crash (Fire) caused by a hijacking (FBI/Police) on the runway (Airport).

**C. Transfer of Command.** Must be clearly transferred with a briefing covering current situation status, unit locations and assignments, operational objectives, and safety considerations.

### 4.4 Operational Centers
**A. Airport Operations Center (AOC).** 24/7 tactical warning point. Terminal Building, Ground Level (Room 104). Monitors the Crash Phone and fire alarms; dispatches Airport Police and Operations; issues NOTAMs; initiates the "Everbridge" Mass Notification System.

**B. Emergency Operations Center (EOC).** Strategic support hub. The EOC does NOT command tactical units on the scene; it supports the on-scene IC with resources, policy decisions, media coordination, and legal support. Location: Administration Building, 3rd Floor Conference Room. Backup: ARFF Station 9 Training Room.

### 4.5 EOC Activation Levels

| Level | Name | Description & Triggers | Staffing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Monitoring | Potential threat or minor incident. Severe Weather Watch, VIP visit, credible intel, "Alert 1" | AOC monitors. Key staff notified via SMS but remain at normal duty stations |
| 2 | Partial | Incident affecting airport operations but contained. "Alert 2", Terminal Evacuation, **Cyber-Intrusion**, Extended Power Outage | EOC opens. Airport Director, Ops Chief, Fire Chief, Police Chief, IT Director, PIO |
| 1 | Full | Catastrophic event requiring regional resources. "Alert 3", Mass Casualty, Terrorist Act, Natural Disaster | All Level 2 staff plus County EMA, Red Cross, Airline Station Managers, FEMA Liaison, Mayor's Rep |

### 4.6 Alert and Notification
**A. The "Crash Phone" (Red Phone).** Dedicated hardline connecting the ATCT to ARFF Station 9, AOC, Akron Fire Dispatch (911), and Airport Police Dispatch.

**B. Alert Classifications (Aircraft).** Alert 1 (Local Standby): minor defect; ARFF stands by at station. Alert 2 (Full Emergency): defect affecting flight safety; ARFF deploys to standby positions. Alert 3 (Aircraft Accident): full response.

**C. Mass Notification System (Everbridge).** Pre-scripted alerts to Group A (Airport Leadership), Group B (Tenants: airlines, TSA, concessions), Group C (Mutual Aid Partners).

## SECTION 5: ORGANIZATION AND ASSIGNMENT OF RESPONSIBILITIES

### 5.1 Airport Authority Departments
**A. Airport Director (Chief Executive).** EOC Manager upon activation. Final authority on all policy decisions, including airport closure. Authorizes emergency expenditures. Approves all press releases and media interviews. Liaises with the Mayor of Akron and Summit County Executive.

**B. Airport Operations Department.** Compliance with 14 CFR Part 139. Issue NOTAMs closing runways/taxiways. Coordinate aircraft movements with ATCT. Escort mutual aid vehicles on the movement area. Runway friction testing and snow removal. Primary liaison to the NTSB IIC.

**C. Airport Maintenance Department.** Infrastructure continuity and repair. Heavy equipment for debris removal. Manage critical utilities: HVAC shutdown (HAZMAT), power switching (grid failure). Perimeter fence repairs. Portable lighting and generators.

**D. Information Technology (IT) Department.** Protection and restoration of Cyber-Physical Systems.
- **Cyber Incident Response:** Identify, contain, and eradicate cyber-threats targeting NCIA systems (FIDS, PACS, AODB, BMS).
- **Digital Forensics:** Preserve server logs and digital evidence for law enforcement.
- **Continuity:** Ensure EOC communications (VoIP, radio, internet) remain operational.
- **Advisory:** Advise the EOC Manager on the operational impact of IT system shutdowns (e.g., "If we isolate the network, the fuel farm stops pumping").

**E. Airport Police Department.** Site security and traffic management. Inner and outer perimeters. Control access points to the airfield (Gates 1–20). Traffic flow on Airport Boulevard. Coordinate with FBI and TSA on security threats.

**F. Public Information Officer (PIO).** Establish the Joint Information Center (JIC). Monitor social media for misinformation and rumors. Conduct scheduled media briefings at the designated media staging area.

### 5.2 Mutual Aid Agencies
**A. Akron Fire Department (AFD):** fire suppression, rescue, HAZMAT mitigation; Incident Command for fire/crash/medical; staffs ARFF Station 9 per agreement; triage/treatment/transport; hazardous materials spills.
**B. Akron Police Department (APD):** tactical law enforcement and investigation; SWAT; Bomb Squad (EOD); criminal investigations.
**C. Summit County Emergency Management Agency (EMA):** resource coordination; state/federal assets (Ohio National Guard, FEMA); Family Assistance Center (off-site); mass casualty transport.

### 5.3 Federal Agencies
**A. FAA – Air Traffic Control:** control airspace and aircraft movement; notify AOC via the Crash Phone; stop arrivals/departures upon request.
**B. TSA:** secure the sterile area and checkpoints; assist with repopulation and re-screening after evacuation; aviation security intelligence.
**C. FBI:** jurisdiction and command for incidents confirmed as terrorism, aircraft piracy, or federal cyber-crimes; establishes a Joint Operations Center (JOC).

# PART II: HAZARD-SPECIFIC APPENDICES

## APPENDIX 1: AIRCRAFT INCIDENTS

### 1.1 Introduction
Primary compliance document for 14 CFR Part 139.325(b).

### 1.2 Alert Classifications

| Alert | Description | Response |
|---|---|---|
| ALERT 1 | Local Standby. Defect not normally causing serious difficulty | ARFF mans vehicles at Station 9. Ops conducts runway debris check |
| ALERT 2 | Full Emergency. Defect affecting flight safety | ARFF deploys to standby positions. EOC Level 2. Mutual aid notified (standby) |
| ALERT 3 | Aircraft Accident on or off the airport | Full response. Mutual aid dispatched. EOC Level 1 |

### 1.3 Operational Procedures
**A. Communications.** ARFF Discrete Frequency (121.9 MHz) or Summit County 800 MHz Fireground Channel. Upon Alert 3, ATC silences non-emergency ground traffic.
**B. Grid Maps.** All agencies use the NCIA Emergency Grid Map ("Crash site is located in Grid E-5").
**C. ARFF Priorities.** 1 — survivable evacuation path; 2 — rescue of trapped occupants; 3 — protection of evidence (NTSB).
**D. Triage and Medical.** Triage Area upwind, minimum 300 ft. Green → Passenger Collection Point (bus). Red/Yellow → stabilize and transport. Black → do not move; cover in place.

## APPENDIX 2: NATURAL HAZARDS

### 2.1 Severe Winter Storm
Trigger: NWS Winter Storm Warning or snowfall > 1 inch/hour. Activate the Snow and Ice Control Plan (SICP). Runway friction testing (Mu) every 30 minutes. Closure criteria: friction below 20 (Nil) or visibility below 1/4 mile → runway closed via NOTAM. Terminal coordination per TIRP Section 5 (stranded passenger protocols).

### 2.2 Severe Convective Weather (Tornado / Microburst)
Trigger: NWS Tornado Warning or winds > 50 knots. Stop fueling. If wind exceeds 65 knots the ATCT may be evacuated (airport becomes uncontrolled, Class G). Ramp personnel shelter in designated concrete shelters (bag rooms, tunnels).

## APPENDIX 3: MAN-MADE HAZARDS (HAZMAT)

### 3.1 Fuel Farm / Large Spill
Rupture of the Buckeye Pipeline spur or catastrophic tank failure (2.5M gallons). Ops shuts the Emergency Fuel Cutoff (EFSO) at the Fuel Farm gate. ARFF blocks storm drains with mats and booms. 1,000-foot isolation zone.

### 3.2 Cargo Aircraft HAZMAT
Aircraft directed to the Designated Isolated Parking Position (DIPP) on the remote De-icing Pad. Engines shut down. Crew evacuates upwind. Akron Fire HAZMAT Team assumes entry/mitigation.

## APPENDIX 4: SECURITY INCIDENTS

### 4.1 Bomb Threat (Aircraft)
Aircraft directed to the Designated Aircraft Parking Area (DAPA) on the North Cargo Ramp. Passengers deplane leaving carry-ons. EOD commands the search.

### 4.2 Security Breach (Perimeter)
ATC stops all aircraft movement. Police intercept. After containment, Ops conducts a full Runway Safety Inspection for FOD or devices.

### 4.3 Active Shooter
Immediate suspension of all aircraft movement. Perimeter gates locked down (LEO entry only). See TIRP Section 12 for terminal-specific actions.

## APPENDIX 5: UTILITY & INFRASTRUCTURE FAILURE

### 5.1 Power Grid Failure
Generators 1 and 2 auto-start within 15 seconds. If auto-start fails, Maintenance manually transfers load at the Airfield Lighting Vault. If runway edge lights cannot be restored, the runway is CLOSED for night operations.

## APPENDIX 6: CYBER-PHYSICAL INCIDENTS

### 6.1 Introduction
As NCIA relies on interconnected Operational Technology (OT), a cyber-attack may have physical safety consequences.

### 6.2 Critical Systems & Impact

| System | Function | Impact of Failure/Compromise | Response Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| ALCS | Airfield Lighting Control | Lights turn off, change intensity, or cannot be controlled by ATC | STOP all aircraft movement. Switch to Manual Lighting Panel in Vault |
| PACS | Physical Access Control | Doors fail "Open" (breach) or "Closed" (entrapment) | Deploy LEOs to manually guard critical portals. Switch to physical keys |
| AODB/FIDS | Flight Info & Database | Incorrect gate info, fake flights, passenger chaos | Shut down FIDS screens ("Go Dark"). Use whiteboards/megaphones |
| Fuel SCADA | Fuel Farm Control | Tank overfill (spill), pump failure, or quality sensor tampering | STOP all fueling. Isolate Fuel Farm network. Manually gauge tanks |

### 6.3 Response Protocol (Cyber)
- **Detection:** IT or Operations notices anomaly (mouse moving on its own, ransom note, lighting pattern change).
- **Containment (The "Disconnect"):** The Airport Director or IT Director has the authority to sever the internet connection (air gap) to critical systems to stop lateral movement. This will degrade operations but save infrastructure.
- **Continuity (Manual Mode):** Operations switches to analog backups (radios, paper logs, manual switches).
- **Forensics:** IT preserves server logs. Do NOT reboot infected machines unless necessary for life safety.
- **Recovery:** Systems rebuilt from "clean" backups (gold images) and patched before reconnection.

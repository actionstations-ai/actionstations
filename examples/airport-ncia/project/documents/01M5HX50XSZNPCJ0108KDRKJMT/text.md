## SECTION 1: GUIDANCE FOR ALL TERMINAL INCIDENT PLANS

### 1.1 Introduction

This TIRP provides a framework for managing emergencies and incidents affecting the NCIA terminal, with a primary focus on the safety and security of passengers, employees, and the public.

### 1.2 Scope

Actions required to manage passengers, employees (airport, airline, concessionaires, vendors), and the public in an orderly, safe, and secure manner during terminal incidents. Not intended to address the tactical responses of outside agencies, which are governed by their own SOPs.

### 1.4 Guidance Applicable for All Incident Plans

- **1.4.1 Training:** all responsible employees trained on this plan; annual refresher.
- **1.4.2 Drills:** tabletop and functional drills, including cybersecurity scenarios; tabletop at least annually.
- **1.4.3 After-Action Reviews (AARs):** after each incident or significant drill.
- **1.4.4 ADA Compliance:** account for occupants with mobility, hearing, or visual impairments, language barriers, and other access or functional needs.
- **1.4.5 Use of Communication Systems:** Airport Operations shall use all available means — Public Address (PA) system (plain language), Flight Information Display Systems (FIDS), social media (X/Twitter, Facebook), NCIA website, direct communication with tenants and stakeholders.

### 1.5 Annual Review of Plans

Update contacts (Annex A), regulatory changes, related documents, and AAR modifications.

## SECTION 2: SHELTER IN PLACE (SIP)

### 2.1 Purpose

Take cover in pre-designated, structurally safe areas to protect occupants from an external hazard until it passes.

### 2.2 Situation and Assumptions

Triggers: Tornado Warning; Severe Thunderstorm Warning (80+ mph winds, large hail); external HAZMAT incident (I-80/I-90, rail, industrial); security incident as directed by law enforcement.

| Location | Terminal Area | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shelter A | Main Terminal | Level 1 / Baggage Claim | Restrooms & interior corridors |
| Shelter B | Concourse A | Concourse Level | Stairwells A3, A4 & interior offices |
| Shelter C | Concourse B | Concourse Level | Restrooms, Stairwells B1, B2 |

### 2.3 Responsibilities

Airport Operations initiates SIP notification via PA and all channels. LEO/Security secures the perimeter, prevents entry/exit, secures shelters. Fire/EMS stages. Facilities Maintenance shuts down/adjusts HVAC for HAZMAT. Airlines/tenants direct their passengers and staff to shelters.

### 2.4 Checklist: Shelter in Place

- Ops: monitor threat (NWS, LEO); announce "Shelter in Place" with instructions; notify tenants; [HAZMAT] confirm HVAC shutdown; announce "All Clear" on IC advice.
- LEO/Security: secure all terminal entrances; move public from windowed areas to shelters.
- Facilities: [HAZMAT] immediately shut down/isolate HVAC air intake; prepare generators.
- Airlines/Tenants: cease boarding; direct passengers and staff to shelters; account for staff.

## SECTION 3: TERMINAL EVACUATION

### 3.1 Purpose

Orderly and safe movement of occupants out of the terminal to an Assembly Point during an internal threat.

### 3.2 Triggers

Structural fire; internal HAZMAT spill; bomb threat (LEO direction); significant structural failure; security breach (LEO/TSA direction).

### 3.3 Operations

Evacuation decision rests with the Incident Commander. **Public Assembly Point:** Rideshare Pickup Area (West End). **Employee Assembly Point:** Employee Parking Lot C (North). Staff direct spontaneous evacuees to official Assembly Points.

### 3.4 Responsibilities

Ops announces via PA and deploys staff. Fire/EMS leads fire evacuations and assists access/functional needs. LEO secures the evacuated area and traffic control. Airlines/tenants evacuate leased areas. CBP controls unprocessed international passengers.

### 3.5 Checklist: Evacuation

- Ops: announce "Evacuate" on IC order (exits, assembly points); notify tenants; deploy "Follow Me" vests/signs; coordinate with LEO to stop inbound traffic.
- LEO/Security: crowd direction; secure evacuated areas; traffic control for mutual aid.
- Airlines/Tenants: evacuate employees and passengers; prevent re-entry; shut down critical operations if time permits.

## SECTION 4: REPOPULATION

### 4.1 Purpose

Re-initiate normal or partial operations after an evacuation or extended SIP.

### 4.2 Assumptions

IC has declared "All Clear"; building inspections complete; utilities (power, water, IT) functional; security incidents require a full sweep and re-screening of the sterile area.

### 4.3 Operations

Phased re-entry: (1) Security employees (LEO, TSA, CBP); (2) Airport/airline employees; (3) Passengers by priority (flight, original screening time). All passengers and employees re-screened by TSA after any sterile-area evacuation.

### 4.4 Responsibilities

Ops coordinates and communicates. LEO controls queues; TSA re-opens checkpoints; LEO/Security sweep the sterile area first. Airlines re-staff and inform passengers. CBP re-establishes FIS.

### 4.5 Checklist: Repopulation

- Ops: receive "All Clear"; coordinate plan with LEO/TSA/Airlines; announce phased plan; manage queues.
- LEO/Security/TSA: sweep sterile area; re-establish checkpoints; maintain orderly queues.
- Airlines/Tenants: re-staff; **update FIDS and provide gate announcements**; prioritize access/functional needs.

## SECTION 5: NATURAL HAZARD – SEVERE WINTER STORM / BLIZZARD

### 5.1–5.2 Purpose and Assumptions

Heavy lake-effect snow, high winds, low visibility, cold. Long warning period (12–48 hours). Primary impact: cancellations and stranded passengers; IROPS plan activated. Secondary: power failures, HVAC strain, passenger care (cots, blankets, food), staff access.

### 5.3 Responsibilities

Ops coordinates with NWS, activates IROPS, manages communications, coordinates passenger care. Facilities ensures generators and HVAC; clears entrances/exits. LEO assists crowd management. Airlines manage cancellations/re-booking; coordinate care needs with Ops.

### 5.4 Checklist: Severe Winter Storm

- Ops: regular NWS calls; activate IROPS; updates via PA, FIDS, social media; coordinate passenger care; activate "essential personnel" plan.
- Facilities: generators fueled and tested; HVAC at full capacity; clear access/egress at all doors.
- Airlines/Tenants: implement IROPS plan; timely passenger info; report care needs to Ops.

## SECTION 6: NATURAL HAZARD – TORNADO / SEVERE THUNDERSTORM

### 6.2 Operations

On Tornado Warning, activate Section 2 (SIP) immediately. PA broadcast ordering occupants to shelters. All operations cease. Move away from windows, skylights, large open spans (main ticketing hall).

### 6.4 Checklist: Tornado

- Ops: monitor NWS; ON WARNING activate SIP; announce "Tornado Warning. This is not a drill. Move to the nearest shelter immediately. Move away from all windows."; notify tenants.
- All Staff: verbally direct passengers; assist access/functional needs.
- LEO/Security: clear windowed areas.

## SECTION 7: MAN-MADE HAZARD – HAZMAT INCIDENT

### 7.2 Situation

**Scenario A: Internal Release** (spill inside the terminal) → EVACUATION (Section 3). **Scenario B: External Release** (I-80/I-90 tanker, rail car, industrial plume) → SHELTER IN PLACE (Section 2) with immediate HVAC intake shutdown.

### 7.4 Responsibilities

Fire/EMS assumes IC, identifies material and correct response. Ops relays IC orders. Facilities shuts down HVAC intakes for external release.

### 7.5 Checklist: HAZMAT

- Fire/EMS: assume IC; identify hazard; determine internal/external; order EVACUATION or SIP.
- Ops: await IC direction; activate Section 3 or Section 2 accordingly.
- Facilities: if external, shut down all HVAC air intakes on order.

## SECTION 8: FACILITY INCIDENT – STRUCTURAL FIRE

### 8.2 Operations

Pull nearest alarm, report to 911 or the ACC. Fire alarm activation mandates full immediate EVACUATION (Section 3). Lakeport Fire Department (AFD) assumes IC on arrival.

### 8.4 Checklist: Structural Fire

- Ops: confirm 911 notification; activate EVACUATION; announce "A fire has been reported. Evacuate the terminal immediately. Use the nearest exit."; establish contact with Fire IC at Command Post.
- LEO/Security: crowd direction; perimeter; clear path for mutual aid.
- Facilities: report to Fire IC with building system information (fire panel, HVAC, utilities); shut down utilities as directed.

## SECTION 9: FACILITY INCIDENT – ELECTRICAL OUTAGE

### 9.2 Assumptions

Backup generators power critical life safety (emergency lighting, fire panel) and some essential systems. **Not all systems are on backup power: FIDS, baggage handling (BHS), jet bridges, and general concession power may fail.** Primary provider: Northcoast Electric.

### 9.3 Responsibilities

Facilities identifies cause, confirms generators, coordinates with Northcoast Electric. Ops activates manual procedures; PA or bullhorns; staff to dark restrooms/stairwells. IT verifies critical systems on UPS/generator; prepares restart. LEO/TSA monitor dark areas; checkpoint closes if screening equipment is unpowered.

### 9.4 Checklist: Electrical Outage

- Facilities: confirm generators accepted load; identify cause (internal/external); contact Northcoast Electric for ETR.
- Ops: assess which systems are down; notify tenants of outage and ETR; PA announcement or bullhorns; deploy staff with flashlights.
- IT: verify network and critical servers; prepare restart sequence.
- LEO/Security/TSA: deploy staff to key areas; determine checkpoint status.

## SECTION 10: SECURITY INCIDENT – BOMB THREAT

### 10.2 Operations

All threats treated as real until proven otherwise. Receiver uses the Bomb Threat Checklist; reports to Airport Police or the ACC. Airport Police assume IC; with FBI/TSA assess as **Non-Specific** (heightened awareness, low-visibility searches) or **Specific** (search and likely evacuation). Evacuation decision rests solely with the LEO IC. If a device is found: evacuate, perimeter, request Bomb Squad (EOD).

### 10.4 Checklist: Bomb Threat

- LEO/Security: assume IC; threat assessment; decide evacuate/search/heighten awareness; notify FBI, TSA, request Bomb Squad; if device found, perimeter and evacuate.
- Ops: await LEO IC; PA announcements only as directed; prepare EVACUATION.
- All Staff: use the Bomb Threat Checklist; report immediately; do not touch suspicious items.

## SECTION 11: SECURITY INCIDENT – SECURITY BREACH

### 11.2 Operations

Report "running the checkpoint" or "piggybacking" immediately. LEOs respond to last known location. **"Concourse Dump":** LEO IC with ASC and TSA decides to evacuate the sterile area — default response if the individual is not immediately contained. All occupants to the public side; LEO/Security sweep; TSA re-screens everyone. Repopulate per Section 4.

### 11.4 Checklist: Security Breach

- LEO/Security: assume IC; respond to contain; coordinate with TSA/ASC on dump decision; direct occupants to public side; full sweep.
- Ops: await LEO IC; PA only as directed; prepare EVACUATION.
- TSA: secure checkpoint; stop screening; prepare full re-screen.

## SECTION 12: SECURITY INCIDENT – ACTIVE SHOOTER

### 12.2 Guiding Principle: RUN. HIDE. FIGHT.

RUN if a clear path exists. HIDE: lock/barricade, lights off, phones silent. FIGHT as an absolute last resort.

### 12.3 Operations

LEOs move toward the threat first. Fire/EMS stage in a cold zone until LEOs declare "secure." Then LEOs secure the scene and Fire/EMS triage.

### 12.5 Checklist: Active Shooter

- All Staff: RUN. HIDE. FIGHT.; call 911 or ACC with shooter location/description when safe.
- LEO/Security: assume IC; contact teams; establish cold/warm/hot zones; declare scene secure for EMS.
- Ops: relay ACC information to 911/LEO IC; PA only as directed ("Police emergency, evacuate if able."); prepare family reunification.
- Fire/EMS: stage in cold zone; on "scene secure," establish triage.

## SECTION 13: TECHNICAL INCIDENT – CYBERSECURITY & IT SYSTEMS FAILURE

### 13.1 Purpose

Guidance for a significant IT system failure, whether caused by a malicious cyber-attack (e.g., ransomware) or a technical failure. Focus: operational continuity.

### 13.2 Command Structure

A technical incident creates two parallel command structures that must be in constant communication:

- **Technical Incident Command:** led by the IT Director or designee — identification, containment, eradication, recovery of systems.
- **Operational Incident Command:** led by the Airport Operations Manager — manual procedures, passenger flow, communications.

### 13.3 General Operations

First priority: isolate affected systems to prevent spread. Second: activate manual (analog) procedures to continue operations. Public communication coordinated between the IT Director (technical details) and the PIO/Ops Manager (operational impacts).

### 13.4 Scenario A: Ransomware / Critical Network Failure

**Impact:** airport computers locked; files encrypted; airline systems (separate networks) may be unaffected, but airport-provided systems (FIDS, BHS) fail. **Response:** IT — isolate the network; order all staff to power down workstations; **DO NOT PAY RANSOM**; contact FBI/CISA. Ops — manual procedures; radios, bullhorns, whiteboards. LEO/Security — prepare for Access Control failure (13.5). Airlines — own continuity plans.

### 13.5 Scenario B: Access Control System Failure

**Impact:** all airport-controlled doors fail "locked" (no movement) or "unlocked" (massive security breach). **"Fail Unlocked":** LEO/Security immediately dispatch all available staff to physically secure all sterile/secure access points — this is a critical security breach; Ops ceases operations until doors are manually secured. **"Fail Locked":** Facilities uses manual key overrides; LEO may need to breach doors.

### 13.6 Scenario C: FIDS / BHS System Failure

**Impact:** FIDS go dark; BHS stops. **FIDS Failure:** Airlines make all announcements manually at gates; Ops uses whiteboards and PA announcements in the terminal to provide general flight information. **BHS Failure:** Airlines/Ops activate manual baggage sort procedures (severe delays); TSA reverts to manual bag screening.

### 13.7 Checklist: Cybersecurity Incident

- **IT Department:** assume Technical Incident Command (IT Director); identify and isolate affected systems; notify FBI (Cyber Task Force) and CISA; begin recovery from backups on a clean system.
- **Airport Operations:** assume Operational Incident Command (Ops Manager); notify all tenants of system failure and operational impact; activate manual/analog workarounds (whiteboards, bullhorns); manage passenger flow and provide information.
- **Law Enforcement / Security:** [if Access Control fails] deploy all staff to physically secure doors.

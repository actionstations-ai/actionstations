# Northern Lakes Transmission Operations: System and Control Room Description

Reference sheet for exercise players · NLTO System Operations · 2027

## 1. Company

Northern Lakes Transmission Operations (NLTO) is an investor-owned transmission company serving northern Minnesota and Wisconsin. President: Raymond Okonkwo-Hale. Director of System Operations: Frank Delacroix. Transmission Operations Manager: Gloria Banerjee (218-555-0122). Reliability Engineer: Dr. Imani Okeke. Control room supervisors: Walter Nakamura (day), Silvia Hartnell (night). Communications Manager: Tess Lindholm (218-555-0125).

## 2. Network

- 4,100 miles of line: 345 kV (780 miles), 230 kV (1,900), 115 kV (1,420).
- 38 substations. Key substations: Iron Range (345/230 kV, 3 autotransformers), Hartley (345 kV switching, 2 autotransformers), Cloquet (static var compensator, 2 autotransformers), Arrowhead (HVDC converter tie, owned by another company), North Shore, Virginia.
- 11 345/230 kV autotransformers, 9 with GIC monitors. The oldest was built in 1978; four have single-phase units with a large core. A spare 345/230 kV autotransformer is stored at Hartley.
- Peak load 4,300 MW; March load about 2,900 MW. Generation: Taconite Ridge (coal and gas, 900 MW), Lake Superior Hydro (320 MW), Baraga Wind Farm (210 MW), plus imports over the Arrowhead and North Shore interfaces (up to 1,300 MW).
- Large customers: Mesabi Taconite Works (a mining pellet plant, 180 MW), Duluth Harbor Terminal (40 MW).

## 3. Control room

Two control rooms (primary Duluth, backup Virginia, 60 miles away) use an energy management system (EMS) with SCADA, a state estimator and contingency analysis. Neutral current monitors report to the EMS every 10 seconds, and the display has alarms at 75, 150 and 250 amperes. Voltage and reactive power are on the main one-line. Phasor measurement units at 27 substations feed a wide-area monitoring screen. The control room has two supervisors on the day shift and one on the night shift; the reliability engineer is on call.

## 4. Time and communications

GPS-disciplined clocks serve the substation protection and phasor equipment. Holdover clocks are fitted at 14 substations (24-hour holdover); a backup time source from a network time server over the fiber ring is at the Duluth control center. The control room uses the NLTO operations telephone system, a satellite phone and the entity conference bridge; the company's microwave and fiber network are the main data paths. The Duluth Harbor Terminal's operations room has a hotline to the control room.

## 5. Reactive power resources

The static var compensator at Cloquet (±200 MVAR), two capacitor banks at Iron Range (2 x 100 MVAR) and Hartley (2 x 75 MVAR). These must be energized before the storm; reactive devices cannot be switched during peak GIC without control room approval.

## 6. Interfaces

The Arrowhead interface (ties to the neighboring system, limit 1,000 MW) and the North Shore interface (limit 600 MW) are the main transfer limits. In a storm the limits are reduced by the percentages in the procedure.

# NLTO Communications Plan: Space Weather and Customer Messages

NLTO Communications · Communications Manager Tess Lindholm · Revision 3

## 1. Purpose

This plan tells NLTO how to talk about a geomagnetic storm to customers, the news media, the state and the public. The key rule is that messages describe the effects and the actions, not solar physics, and that every number NLTO uses agrees with NOAA's and the reliability coordinator's.

## 2. Who speaks

- NLTO's spokesperson is the Communications Manager. The Director of System Operations speaks for the control room at media briefings.
- NLTO does not speak for NOAA. It quotes the SWPC product by its time and level and links to SWPC.
- The RC speaks about wide-area operations; NLTO speaks about its own system.

## 3. What NLTO says at each condition

| Condition | Public statement |
|---|---|
| Condition 1 (watch) | No public message unless asked. A media statement is ready: "NLTO is monitoring a space weather forecast and has procedures in place. We do not expect customer impact." |
| Condition 2 (warning) | A statement on the website and to the state duty officer: NLTO has begun operating its system more conservatively. |
| Condition 3 (storm) | A statement to customers: the level (G3, strong) as reported by NOAA, the likely duration (hours), that outages are not expected and how to report one. |
| Condition 4 (severe) | A statement for the news media and the state with a briefing every 2 hours. |

The statement uses the NOAA scale name and number ("G4, severe") and the duration given in the SWPC warning; it never gives a time for the end of the storm that is earlier than SWPC's. The statement says that auroras may be visible at lower latitudes than usual and that this is not a hazard.

## 4. Customer notices

NLTO's retail customers are served by distribution utilities, so NLTO notifies: the two industrial customers (Mesabi Taconite Works, Duluth Harbor Terminal), the cooperatives that are directly connected, and the Minnesota duty officer. Notices say the condition, what NLTO has done and what customers might need to do: Mesabi Taconite Works asked to keep its power factor correction in service and tell NLTO of any change in its load; cooperatives asked to avoid planned switching.

## 5. Misinformation

Common false claims during a storm are that a nationwide blackout is coming, that the storm will destroy transformers across the grid, that the grid is down because of an attack, and that mobile phones will not work. The Communications Manager keeps a rumor log, replies from NLTO's accounts with the effects and actions, and refers the question of the storm itself to SWPC. NLTO does not discuss security.

## 6. Consistency checks

Before release, the Communications Manager compares each message to the SWPC product (level and time), the RC's latest notice and the state's message. The checks: the level (G scale) and Kp agree; the duration agrees; the term "outage" is used only for a loss of load; the numbers of customers are from the EMS state estimator. A discrepancy is fixed before the message is sent.

## 7. Other sectors

Space weather also affects GNSS users and aviation. NLTO does not give advice to airlines, farmers or telecommunications operators; it refers them to SWPC and to the FAA and their own providers. NLTO does tell its fiber and telecommunications team about GNSS-degradation notices, because the company's own timing equipment is affected.

## 8. After the event

The Communications Manager records every message and the time and compiles the list with the post-event summary.

# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Aurora Over Texas** (GIC, Conservative Operations & Timing Holdover)
Location: Northern Lakes Transmission Operations primary control room, Duluth, Minnesota (backup control room at Virginia) · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Control Room Shift Supervisor, Reliability Engineer, Transmission Operations Manager, Communications Manager, Reliability Coordinator Shift Manager, Substation and Timing Engineer. The SimCell plays NOAA SWPC, the neighboring operators, the generator owners, the state duty officer, the customers, the transformer manufacturer, reporters and the public.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Acting on the forecast before the Kp arrives, keeping a stressed 345 kV system and its transformers in service, and making sure NLTO, the RC, NOAA and the state give the same numbers to entities and the public.

**The Threat:** An X-class flare on Monday evening, 15 March 2027, caused a brief R3 radio blackout and launched a fast halo CME. At 22:30 CDT Monday, before STARTEX, SWPC issued a G3 Geomagnetic Storm Watch (WATA50) for Tuesday. At 02:10 CDT Tuesday its CME discussion gives an arrival window of 11:00 to 17:00 CDT. The CME arrives as a sudden impulse at 13:24 CDT (WARSUD). Kp 7 (G3) is observed at 14:06, the Kp 8 or greater warning is issued at 14:40, Kp 8 (G4) arrives at 15:10 and Kp 9 (G5) at 17:47. Geomagnetically induced currents flow in the 345/230 kV autotransformers of the Northern Lakes Transmission Operations network, the reactive power swings across the Arrowhead and North Shore interfaces, and GNSS timing at the substations degrades. The storm data are a replay of the Kp record from a real severe storm (case data) shifted to these dates. All SWPC products are exercise products, with times in UTC.

**The Operational Impact:** The exercise starts at 02:00 CDT Tuesday in the Duluth control room with the night shift, after the watch has been issued. Day shift supervisor Walter Nakamura comes on at 06:00. The narrative moves in time jumps from the early-morning watch through the afternoon storm to the end of the procedure on Wednesday morning.

Before arrival, operators must apply Condition 1 and then Condition 2 ahead of the Kp they trigger on, recording the basis and the time. They must also defer six planned 345 kV outages, including a 14-hour transformer replacement at Hartley, and bring the Cloquet static var compensator and the Iron Range and Hartley capacitor banks on line.

After the impulse:
- Neutral-current alarms climb on the EMS display, and the two transfer cuts follow: transfers across the Arrowhead (1,000 MW limit) and North Shore (600 MW limit) interfaces are cut by 10 percent and then by another 10 percent.
- A 345 kV line trips at 16:20 and a relay misoperates at Hartley, so the picture on the one-line becomes unclear.
- After 16:00, three substation GPS clocks lose lock. PMU data on the wide-area screen become suspect until the supervisor switches time sources and declares holdover.
- At 17:47 the Iron Range T2 autotransformer reaches 168 amperes neutral current, with a gas alarm and a 108 C hot-spot reading. Transmission Operations Manager Gloria Banerjee must decide whether to remove it from service, knowing the only spare is stored at Hartley. Reliability engineer Dr. Imani Okeke advises, and the SimCell transformer manufacturer's engineer is on the phone.

Each condition change starts a 60-minute clock for notifying 14 entities. The UMRC (Shift Manager Hugo Villanueva) raises its level from Alert to Warning to Emergency and runs hourly calls, then calls every 30 minutes. The Mesabi Taconite Works duty manager calls to ask whether to shut a furnace. Reporters call about aurora seen as far south as Texas. Communications Manager Tess Lindholm faces social posts claiming a nationwide blackout and a grid attack. Kp falls to 4 by 23:00, and the exercise ends Wednesday morning when the procedure is terminated. The exercise gives no engineering advice on protection settings.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply NGDOP Conditions 1 through 4 ahead of the Kp they trigger on when SWPC lead time allows, including deferring the six planned 345 kV outages and the Hartley transformer replacement, bringing the Cloquet SVC and the Iron Range and Hartley capacitor banks on line, and logging the basis and time of each condition change and of the termination.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to decide whether to remove the Iron Range T2 autotransformer from service at 168 A neutral current with a gas alarm and a 108 C hot-spot reading, weighing the loss of the only spare stored at Hartley against system security and agreeing the related transfer cuts on the Arrowhead and North Shore interfaces with the UMRC.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to keep an accurate operating picture after the 16:20 line trip, the Hartley relay misoperation and the loss of lock at three substation GPS clocks, by identifying suspect PMU data, switching to the backup network time source and declaring holdover within the 24-hour holdover limit.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to notify all 14 NGDOP entities within 60 minutes of each condition change and to issue public statements under the NLTO Communications Plan that match the UMRC and SWPC on G level and duration, while answering the Mesabi Taconite Works furnace question and correcting posts that claim a nationwide blackout or a grid attack.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Control Room Shift Supervisor (CRSS):** Owns the NGDOP operating procedure in the Duluth control room: declares each condition, takes its actions, keeps the log, starts each 60-minute notification clock and proposes when to end the procedure.
- **Reliability Engineer (RE):** Owns the GIC and transformer risk picture, including neutral-current trends, transformer thermal and gas indications, contingency analysis and the post-event review of the Hartley relay misoperation.
- **Transmission Operations Manager (TOM):** Owns the high-consequence decisions: deferring outages including the Hartley replacement, approving transfer cuts and extra reserve, removing the Iron Range T2 autotransformer and escalating to the Director of System Operations and the executive on call.
- **Communications Manager (COM):** Owns NLTO's public voice: the condition-based statements in the Communications Plan, the media briefings with the Director of System Operations, and the answers to rumors of a nationwide blackout or a grid attack.
- **UMRC Shift Manager (Reliability Coordinator Shift Manager) (RCSM):** Owns the wide-area GMD picture under UGFNWC: issues the Alert, Warning and Emergency notices, runs the hourly and then 30-minute conference bridge, and directs interface transfer reductions, redispatch and the sharing of reactive reserves.
- **Substation and Timing Engineer (NLTO Fiber and Telecommunications Timing Team) (TIME):** Owns the GPS-disciplined substation clocks, the 24-hour holdover clocks, the backup network time source over the fiber ring at Duluth and the timing alarms that feed protection and the PMUs.

It also tests whether the transfer cuts on the Arrowhead and North Shore interfaces are agreed with the UMRC and the 14 entities are kept informed.

Beats: Time jump to 13:24 CDT: an exercise WARSUD sudden-impulse warning sounds the control room alarm. The UMRC raises its level to Warning, applies the second condition and starts hourly calls.

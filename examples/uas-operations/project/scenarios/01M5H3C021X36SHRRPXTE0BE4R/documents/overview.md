# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Ghost Flight at Birch Hollow** (GNSS Spoofing & Command Hijack)
Location: Harwick UAS Operations Group, Birch Hollow Range, Harwick, Calder County, New York · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Flight Operations, Systems / Cybersecurity, Aviation Safety, Corporate Communications, Security / CCSO liaison, Client Liaison (Data Deliverables)

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Command Integrity, Cyber-Physical Trust, and Interagency Coordination

**The Threat:** A cyber-physical incident involving GNSS spoofing and unauthorized command injection disrupted the multispectral and hyperspectral sensor campaign at the Lakeshore Flight Area of the Birch Hollow Range. The hijack caused UAS-11 to execute uncommanded waypoints, lose RTK-1 and aircraft GNSS fix simultaneously under clear sky, show a second ground station in telemetry, and alter the home point. The incident originated from a compromised 915 MHz link and a vehicle with RF equipment departing the lodge parking area.

**The Operational Impact:** The RPIC and VO on UAS-11 experience a sudden, unexplained aircraft position jump and loss of RTK fix, conflicting with the GCS and DAA-1. The flight must be landed in attitude mode. The GCS and radio state must be preserved. The crew is unable to confirm the aircraft’s true position, and the Payload Operator cannot verify calibration status. The flight test site is shut down. The ASO is notified. The CP&L client is alerted about potential data integrity loss for Deliverable D-3. The site manager reports a vehicle with antennas leaving the lodge. Security and CCSO are engaged. Corporate Communications receives a trainee video from the site, and HUOG managed security provider is activated.

**Core Plan Sections Tested:**
- ISP › Section 9 › 9.1 C2 link compromise, interference or GNSS spoofing
- ISP › Section 5 › 5.2 Link monitoring
- ISP › Table of Contents
- ERP › Section 11 › 11.3 Records
- SITE › 9. Contacts (exercise numbers)
- ERP › Section 10 › 10.3 Social media and trainees

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Command Integrity:** Demonstrate the ability to verify and restore command authority over a flight system after a suspected GNSS spoofing and unauthorized command injection, using telemetry logs and radio state preservation.
- **Cyber-Physical Trust:** Demonstrate the ability to resolve conflicting data between the GCS, DAA-1, and aircraft telemetry during a cyber-physical incident, prioritizing field observations and forensic preservation.
- **Interagency Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to synchronize reporting and action with law enforcement (CCSO), base safety, the FSDO, and the client (CP&L) while maintaining regulatory compliance and data integrity.
- **Incident Response Continuity:** Demonstrate the ability to maintain operational continuity under a cascading failure of C2 links, RTK base, and GNSS, by activating pre-defined emergency procedures and preserving evidence.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Director of Flight Operations (Incident Commander) (IC):** Manages flight operations, crew, and go/no-go decisions; owns the flight test site and the aircraft's operational status.
- **Systems & Cybersecurity Lead (Technical Lead) (TL):** Owns the GCS, C2 links, telemetry pipeline, and the investigation into cyber incidents; leads technical forensics.
- **Aviation Safety Officer (ASO) (ASO):** Owns the safety case, airspace authorizations, and regulatory reporting; ensures compliance with the FOM and ERP.
- **Communications Lead (PIO) (PIO):** Owns the narrative, media, and public messaging; coordinates with Corporate Communications and the client.
- **Security / CCSO Liaison (Sec):** Owns the hangar, test site perimeter, and law enforcement liaison; ensures physical order and safe access.
- **Client Liaison (CP&L Program Office) (SPO):** Owns the data deliverables, client reporting, and controlled technology; ensures compliance with federal program requirements.

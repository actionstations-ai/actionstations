# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Over the Fairgrounds** (A drone over the Harwick Fairgrounds crowd on a NO FLY day)
Location: Millbrook Road hangar complex and Horton Field, beside the Harwick Fairgrounds, Harwick, Calder County, New York · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Flight Operations, Aviation Safety, Leadership, Corporate Communications, Security / Harwick Police liaison

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Proving a negative fast, helping the police without touching another aircraft, and telling the truth when the operator turns out to be one of your own people

**The Threat:** On Saturday, September 19, the Harwick Fall Festival draws about 6,000 people to the Harwick Fairgrounds beside Horton Field. It is a NO FLY day for the Group: the flight operations desk logged all twelve aircraft in Hangar 1 at 0800. Shortly after 11:00 Harwick Police’s contracted detection vendor shows a small quadcopter broadcasting Remote ID and circling over the crowd at about 240 ft. Its operator position sits in the north lot by the Group’s fence, a bystander post blames “the drone company next door”, and the aircraft looks like the Group’s own small quadcopters.

**The Operational Impact:** The Group has no counter-UAS capability and must not launch, chase, jam or interfere with the aircraft (HUOG-ERP 8.4). Its job is to prove within five minutes from the schedule and the fleet register that every Group aircraft is grounded, lend an RPIC to Harwick Police’s command post if asked to read the Remote ID data, and say only true things through Corporate Communications. The incident turns when police find the operator: a part-time visual observer of the Group flying his personal drone on his day off. The Group then has to decide what to say publicly, what to tell the FAA and its clients, and what to do about the crew member, without letting “no Group aircraft were flying” become a misleading answer.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Communication:** Answer Harwick Police within five minutes from the schedule and the fleet register, and keep Flight Operations, Aviation Safety, Leadership, the police liaison and Corporate Communications on one set of facts.
- **Decision Making:** Decide what the Group says publicly once its own crew member is identified as the operator, and how the Group handles him, without misleading statements (decision point).
- **Use of Doctrine:** Help Harwick Police interpret the Remote ID data and compare it with the fleet register, without launching, tracking with Group aircraft or interfering with the drone (HUOG-ERP 8.4).
- **Technical Analysis:** Follow HUOG-ERP 8.4 and HUOG-FOM 7.6, the Section 6 notifications (Harwick Police, the FSDO, Corporate Communications) and the UC-CP-2025 rule that only Corporate Communications speaks to the media.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Director of Flight Operations (Flight Operations) (FOM):** Owns the flight schedule, the fleet register and crew rosters; Incident Commander when aircraft are involved; answers whether any Group aircraft is airborne.
- **General Manager (Leadership) (GM):** Accountable executive and spokesperson with Corporate Communications; joins the Harwick Police call when a reported drone is suspected to be a Group aircraft; decides on client and regulator notification.
- **Aviation Safety Officer (Aviation Safety) (ASO):** Owns the safety case, regulator reporting and the event record; the Group’s contact with the FSDO.
- **Corporate Communications (Communications) (COMMS):** Owns all external messaging under UC-CP-2025; the Group never speaks to the media directly.
- **Fleet & Logistics Manager (Security / Harwick Police liaison) (LIAISON):** Owns Hangar 1 and the physical security of the hangars, keys and the perimeter road; liaison to Harwick Police and CCSO.

# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Cut Lock — Tampering at Orchard Standpipe and a Precautionary Advisory

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: City of Millbrook Department of Water & Sewer, Millbrook, Carver County, Ohio · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based tabletop exercise for the City of Millbrook Department of Water & Sewer. Someone entered Orchard Standpipe on Saturday night: the gate contact tripped twice and was dismissed, the hatch switch has been out of service for two years, and the camera only covers the gate. The exercise opens at 07:00 on Sunday with the day operator’s review of the overnight log and a chlorine residual that has been falling for five hours.

Phase 1 tests whether the alarms and the trend are read together. Phase 2 confirms the entry and turns on sampling before conclusions and on how to isolate the standpipe without losing Zone 2 pressure, inside a police scene. Phase 3 carries the decision point: the precautionary advisory, its type and its area, inside the two-hour window ERP 8.2 sets. Phase 4 runs the advisory and the investigation side by side. Evaluators should watch for the two-hour clock, the order sample–isolate–advise, critical customers before the public, and outside agencies advising rather than deciding.

**Objectives:**
- **Communication:** Keep Operations, Distribution, the lab, Security and the PIO on one set of facts from the first look at the overnight alarm log to the advisory release, and reach the Annex B critical customers before the public.
- **Decision Making:** Decide within two hours of the sign of entry whether to issue a precautionary advisory, of which type (Boil Water or Do Not Drink) and for which streets, after consulting DDAGW and Public Health, and record the basis on ERP-2 (ERP Sections 8.2 and 9).
- **Technical Analysis:** Treat the AIT-611 drift and the site evidence as a possible contamination event: sample per ERP-3 before draining anything, isolate the standpipe without dropping Zone 2 below 20 psi, and preserve video and RTU logs for law enforcement.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Follow ERP Section 8.2, the ERP-3 sampling plan, the PCP 10.3 precautionary template and the Section 9.5 notification sequence, and keep the department, not an outside agency, as the decision-maker for operations and advisories.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

You are the SimCell: the night operator, SecureLine, Millbrook Police, the FBI, DDAGW, Public Health, the City Manager, customers and the press. Deliver injects with “This just came in.”

The evidence is ambiguous on purpose. Do not tell players whether the jug held anything; nobody will know before Wednesday. If they ask the FBI or DDAGW what to do, the answer is a question back: “What is your plan, and what is it based on?”

Police want the scene untouched; the department needs the valves closed. Let the players negotiate it; grant escorted access to the valve vault if they ask for it with a reason.

Time jumps at 1:00, 2:00 and 3:00. At 2:00 remind the room that 10:05 is the two-hour mark.

If a real emergency occurs, say “REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD” and stop the exercise.

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **Millbrook Police detective** — _Trigger: A player asks for access to the site, or for the police view of the threat._
  - Ask: "Who has been inside that fence since 07:00, and is it written down?"
  - Ask: "Your crew wants to close a valve inside my scene. Why can’t it wait an hour?"
  - Ask: "Did anyone touch the jug or the hatch?"
  - Concede only when: Escorts one crew member to the valve vault once told isolation protects the public and stays off the roof.
  - Concede only when: Shares what the department needs for its advisory, but never confirms a substance to the press.
- **Ohio EPA DDAGW district engineer (Paul Grieve)** — _Trigger: The Water Quality Manager consults DDAGW, or no one has by 09:30._
  - Ask: "What is the area, by streets? How many people?"
  - Ask: "Boil Water or Do Not Drink — and why that one for an uncharacterized threat?"
  - Ask: "What is your sampling plan, and when will you have two satisfactory rounds?"
  - Concede only when: Concurs with a precautionary advisory and records the Tier 1 consultation.
  - Concede only when: Advises; the advisory decision stays with the Director.
- **Summit Care nursing home administrator** — _Trigger: Customer Service or the PIO calls with the advisory._
  - Ask: "Lunch is at 11:30 for 120 residents. What do I cook with?"
  - Ask: "Can my residents shower? Some are on oxygen and can’t go without bathing."
  - Concede only when: Follows the advisory once told what to do and when the next call will come.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Each team names three things that went well and three that did not; the facilitator writes them up and looks for the same issue in more than one team.

**Key Discussion Questions for Cut Lock:**
- **The Night Shift:** Two gate trips were acknowledged as wind and the patrol saw “nothing unusual.” What would have caught this at 02:00 instead of 07:15? _(Objective: Technical Analysis)_
- **The Two-Hour Rule:** ERP 8.2 says the advisory decision does not wait for lab results. When did your clock start, when did you decide, and what was the deciding fact? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **Boil or Do Not Drink:** Which advisory did you choose and for which streets? What would have changed your mind? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **Evidence and Public Health:** How did you reconcile the police scene with the need to close the standpipe valves? Who made that call? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **What We Promised:** What did you tell Summit Care, Channel 4 and the public about how long this would last, and was it realistic given the lab timeline? _(Objective: Communication)_

# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Ransomware Riptide** (Ransomware at City Hall and the Island Decision)
Location: Riverside Water Treatment Plant, Millbrook, Carver County, Ohio · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Water Operations, SCADA / IT, Public Information, Water Quality / Lab, Customer Service

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Business-network ransomware, the paths into the control network, and running the plant without the City’s systems

**The Threat:** Ransomware detonates on the City of Millbrook business network (the MILLBROOK domain) on a Monday morning and encrypts the billing system MBW-CIS-01, the AMI head-end MBW-AMI-01 and the file server MBW-FS-01 through the backup service account. The same account reaches toward the control network along the two paths the OT plan already lists as finding F-5: the nightly SMB rule FW-R-17 between MBW-EWS-01 and the backup server MBW-BKP-01, and the MBWSCADA → MILLBROOK domain trust on FW-R-14. A payload is staged on the hot-standby SCADA server MBW-SCADA-SRV02 to run at noon. MBW-BKP-01, which holds every recovery copy the City has, is encrypted too.

**The Operational Impact:** Customer Service loses billing, the customer portal and the outage map; the lab loses LIMS; the PIO loses the file server and may lose the MillbrookAlert console. The plant itself keeps running in automatic, but the SCADA / IT Security Manager must decide within 30 minutes whether to island the control network (OT Plan 10.3), and the Incident Commander must decide whether the plant stays in automatic with an operator watching MBW-HMI-01 or goes to manual operations with two operators per shift (ERP 7.5.1, OT Plan 8). The Mayor, the media and a dialysis center ask whether the water is safe; the answer has to come from grab samples, not from the absence of alarms. Recovery takes days: the SCADA servers are rebuilt from clean media, the PLC-02 flow-pacing logic exists only in the PLC and on the integrator’s laptop (finding F-2), and the City does not pay.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Communication:** Within 15 minutes of the first report, move DWS coordination to phone and radio DWS-1 (ERP Section 5: assume the City email tenant and MBW-FS-01 are read by the intruder) and keep the Control Room, Customer Service and the PIO on one set of facts.
- **Decision Making:** Decide within 30 minutes of the SIEM alert whether to island the control network (OT Plan 10.3), and decide on the evidence whether the plant stays in automatic with an operator watching MBW-HMI-01 or moves to manual operations (ERP 7.5.1), with the staffing that requires.
- **Technical Analysis:** Find and close the paths from the MILLBROOK domain into the control network (FW-R-17, FW-R-14 and the MBWSCADA trust) without powering off or reimaging the affected hosts (OT Plan 10.7), and establish which recovery copies survive.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Run the OT Plan 10.5 ransomware checklist, the Section 12 reporting clocks (CISA, FBI, Ohio EPA DDAGW, WaterISAC, the insurance carrier) and the Customer Service workarounds, and answer “is the water safe?” from sample results.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Director of Water Operations (Incident Commander) (IC):** Manages the water treatment and distribution system, including the decision to declare a Level 3 emergency and to initiate manual operations.
- **SCADA / IT Security Manager (Technical Lead) (TL):** Owns the control network, its firewall rules and remote access, the islanding decision and the technical investigation; works with City IT, which owns the business network.
- **Public Information Officer (PIO) (PIO):** Owns customer notices, boil-water advisories, social media, and the press.
- **Water Quality Manager (Compliance) (WQ):** Owns sampling, the lab, regulatory reporting, and public-notice triggers.
- **Customer Service Lead (Customer Service) (CS):** Owns the customer service center, the surge phone line, the printed critical-customer list and the manual intake and billing workarounds while MBW-CIS-01, the portal and the outage map are down.

# PFC-FS-04: Recall Notification, Regulatory Contact and Public Statements

Pinecrest Fresh Cut, LLC · Food Safety and Quality with Customer Service and Communications · Revision 5

## 1. Purpose

This procedure says how Pinecrest tells customers, regulators and the public about a recall, what the recall letter must contain, and how the counts are reconciled so that every document says the same thing.

## 2. Order and timing

| Step | Who | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Hold notice to Plant 1 and the three DCs | Recall Coordinator, by phone and email | within 30 minutes of a signal |
| Call to the FDA and the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Food and Drug Branch; Reportable Food Registry entry if required | Recall Coordinator | within 24 hours of a signal, earlier if illnesses are known |
| Phone call to each direct customer's food safety and category contact | Customer Service Manager | within 4 hours of the recall decision |
| Recall letter by email and overnight courier | Customer Service Manager | within 4 hours of the decision |
| Press release and consumer notice on the website | Communications Manager | at the same time as the customer notice, once the Coordinator confirms the facts |
| Customer acknowledgment tracked | Customer Service | every customer acknowledged within 24 hours; non-responders called again |
| Records request from FDA | Traceability and Systems Lead | sortable spreadsheet within 24 hours of the request |
| Status reports to FDA | Recall Coordinator | at the frequency FDA sets, usually weekly |

Plant 1 and the DCs are told before customers are told; customers are told before the press release posts, except where a public health agency announces first, in which case customers and the press release go within one hour.

## 3. The recall letter

A recall letter must contain: the words "URGENT: FOOD RECALL" in the subject line; the product name, size, UPC and packaging; the lot codes and use-by dates affected, in a table; the reason for the recall in one sentence (for example: "Product may be contaminated with E. coli O157:H7"); the hazard to health; the quantity shipped to the customer and the dates; the action the customer must take (stop sale, segregate and hold, do not donate, remove from store shelves, notify downstream customers); the instruction for returning or destroying product and for documenting it; a reply form to confirm receipt, the quantity on hand and the quantity destroyed, due within 24 hours; and a named Pinecrest contact with 24-hour phone numbers. Lot codes follow the format in PFC-QA-03 exactly, for example PF1-27278-L2-B, and dates are written in the month-day-year form.

## 4. Counts that must reconcile

Every letter and release states quantities from the reconciliation table. The total of cases shipped to all customers equals the total of cases produced for the affected lots, less cases held at Pinecrest locations at the time of the hold. If counts differ between documents by even one case the document is not sent until corrected. A number the team cannot back with the table is described as an estimate and marked so.

## 5. Press release and consumer notice

The press release is written by the Communications Manager, Tom Haskell, from the Recall Coordinator's fact sheet; the General Counsel reviews it; the President approves it. It says: the product, the lot codes and where it was sold (by state and chain, as the customers and the agencies agree); the reason for the recall; whether illnesses have been reported (stated only as confirmed by a public health agency); what consumers should do (do not eat, throw away or return, symptoms and when to call a doctor); and the consumer line. The consumer line is (831) 555-0145 and the web page is pinecrestfresh.example/recall. The release does not name a retailer unless the retailer agrees, does not blame a grower, and does not say product is "safe" unless a negative result is documented. A UPC and photo of the package are posted.

## 6. Regulators and public health

- **FDA:** the District Recall Coordinator for the Pacific region and the FDA Division of Food Defense Targeting (for traceability) are contacted by the Recall Coordinator; recall classification and strategy are FDA's decision. The firm provides the product description, lot codes, quantities, distribution list, and the recall letter.
- **California Department of Public Health, Food and Drug Branch:** notified at the same time as FDA; the state asks for the same distribution list for California and may issue its own warning.
- **Other states** (Arizona, Nevada, Texas) are contacted by FDA and by the Recall Coordinator where product was shipped to them.
- **CDC** is not contacted directly by the firm; the Recall Coordinator answers its questions through FDA and the state.
- The Coordinator keeps the log of each contact, who spoke, what was said and when.

## 7. Customers' questions

Customer Service uses the approved script: "Stop selling and set aside the five lots listed in the letter. Do not donate. Please confirm by 5:00 p.m." It does not speculate on the cause, the grower or the number of illnesses. Questions on contracts and credit go to the VP Supply Chain, who decides replacement product and credit within one business day. The distributor and the co-packer are told they must notify their own downstream customers and send their lists within 12 hours.

## 8. Insurance, employees and the grower

The General Counsel gives notice to the insurer and checks contracts for the grower's liability. The Communications Manager sends an employee message when the press release posts: what happened, what employees may say (nothing beyond the release), where to send questions. Plant 1's sanitation verification and the investigation (root cause review with the grower, the environmental sampling plan, and a third-party review) are managed by the Plant Manager and the Recall Coordinator and are reported in the final recall effectiveness report.

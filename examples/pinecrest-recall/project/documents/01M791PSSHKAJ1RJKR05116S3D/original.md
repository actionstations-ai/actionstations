# PFC-FS-01: Product Hold, Trace and Recall Plan

Pinecrest Fresh Cut, LLC · Food Safety and Quality · Salinas, California · Revision 8

## 1. Purpose

Pinecrest Fresh Cut processes romaine, spring mix and spinach into bagged and bulk fresh-cut products at its single plant in Salinas, California (Plant 1), and ships through three distribution centers (DCs) to retail chains, a foodservice distributor and a co-packer. About 2.1 million pounds a week go out the door. This plan says who decides to hold product, how the team traces where it went, who decides to recall, and how customers and regulators are told. It is written for a recall of a ready-to-eat leafy greens product where a pathogen is found or suspected.

## 3. Recall signals

Any of these opens a Recall Team call within one hour: a positive finished-product or retained sample for a pathogen (Salmonella, Listeria monocytogenes, E. coli O157:H7 or other Shiga toxin-producing E. coli); a state or federal agency report of illnesses linked to a product; a supplier notice about raw material; a customer complaint of foreign material or illness; a positive environmental sample on a food contact surface. The Recall Coordinator records the signal time; the clock for the measures in Section 7 starts then.

## 4. Decisions and who makes them

1. **Hold.** The Recall Coordinator or the Plant Manager may place a hold on any lot at any time, without waiting for approval. Target: lots on hold in the MES within 30 minutes of a signal and physical holds confirmed at plant and DCs within 4 hours. A hold is by lot code and location, with quantity.
2. **Trace.** The Traceability and Systems Lead produces the list of every lot made on the same line on the same days, and of every shipment of those lots by customer and ship-to location. Target: the complete list in 4 hours. The FDA's rule allows 24 hours for records; the plan sets the shorter target.
3. **Recall.** The President decides on the Coordinator's recommendation. The recommendation states the recall class (FDA classifies; the firm proposes), the depth (to the consignee, retail or consumer level), the lot codes, the quantity, and whether a public warning is needed. A product that may cause serious illness or death is proposed Class I with a consumer-level recall and a public warning.
4. **Notify.** Customers are notified by phone and email as set in PFC-FS-04. Target: every direct customer reached within 4 hours of the recall decision.
5. **Stop supply.** The VP Supply Chain fills unaffected demand from other lots and stops shipments of lots that may share the cause.

## 5. Scope rules

- A lot is a production lot by line, shift and date. Any lot made on the same line within 24 hours after a positive result on that line, before sanitation was verified, is in scope. Raw material lots from the same field and harvest date are in scope.
- Products that include recalled product as an ingredient are in scope, including salad kits made by co-packers.
- When the lot boundaries are uncertain the Coordinator widens the scope, and narrows it only on documented evidence such as a negative test series and sanitation records.

## 6. Disposal, returns and effectiveness

Held product is segregated, tagged, and held in the cooler at 34 to 38 degrees Fahrenheit until disposition. The Director of Logistics arranges returns or destruction with a certificate. The Coordinator counts: cases made, shipped, held, returned, destroyed and unaccounted for. The counts reconcile in every update. Effectiveness checks (customer acknowledgment, store checks and inventory confirmation) are done by Customer Service and reported to FDA in the periodic recall status report.

## 7. Measures used after an event

Time from signal to hold; time from signal to recall decision; time to complete trace and the share of the shipments traced; time to notify customers; reconciliation of counts; consistency of lot codes, dates and counts across the hold notice, recall letter, press release and FDA submission.

## 8. Legal and insurance

The General Counsel notifies the product contamination insurer, Trellis Mutual (fictional), within 24 hours, and checks supply contracts for notice duties. The firm is a food facility registered with FDA; the Reportable Food Registry report is made within 24 hours when there is a reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences.

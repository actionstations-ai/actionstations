# PFC-OPS-02: Plant, Distribution Network and Cold Chain

Pinecrest Fresh Cut, LLC · Supply Chain and Logistics · Revision 5

## 1. Company and plant

Pinecrest Fresh Cut, LLC was formed in 1994 and is owned by a family and a regional agricultural cooperative. It buys romaine, spring mix and baby spinach from growers in the Salinas Valley (October to November) and from growers around Yuma, Arizona (December to March), cuts, washes and packs them at Plant 1 in Salinas, and ships to customers in California, Arizona, Nevada and Texas. Plant 1 has four packing lines (L1 to L4), runs two shifts, and employs 640 people. The plant is certified to a global food safety scheme and holds annual third-party audits.

Weekly volume is about 2.1 million pounds of finished product, about 187,000 cases. Typical shelf life from pack is 14 days; product is shipped within 1 to 3 days, so most product in the network is under a week old. Product is held at 34 to 38 degrees Fahrenheit throughout.

## 4. Transportation

Pinecrest uses two truckload carriers (Cascade Reefer Lines and Sonoran Cold Freight, both fictional) with refrigerated trailers that carry a temperature recorder; a reefer trailer carries 22 pallets or 1,320 cases to a DC. Plant to Stockton is 3 hours, to Phoenix 11 hours and to Dallas 24 hours. The Director of Logistics, Carla Estrada, can stop a trailer in transit by calling the carrier's dispatch and by an instruction in the transportation management system. Trailers on the road at a hold are redirected to the nearest DC or a cold storage warehouse.

## 5. Cold chain

Product is cooled to under 38 degrees within 2 hours of pack. DC coolers have continuous temperature monitoring with alarms to the DC manager; the corporate alarm desk is staffed 24 hours at (831) 555-0136. Backup power for the Stockton and Dallas coolers is a diesel generator with 48 hours of fuel; Phoenix has a battery bank and a contract for a rental generator within 6 hours. Held product stays in the cooler under tags and seals in a separate hold area. Destroyed product is sent to a composter in Salinas.

## 6. Raw material and supply protection

Santa Lucia Growers (Ranch 14, Ranch 22) supplies 38 percent of romaine; Monterey Ridge Farms (fictional) 30 percent; a spot market supplies the rest. A recall linked to a field removes that supplier's volume; the VP Supply Chain can replace about 60 percent of the volume in 48 hours from spot growers and Yuma, at a premium. Days of supply at the retail customers are 2 to 4 days; a recall that removes romaine from shelves in 24 hours creates a shortage within 48.

## 7. Systems and people at the DC

Each DC has a WMS, a labeling station and a records clerk on the day shift; the night shift of 12 people handles receiving. Radio scanners read the lot code on the case label. DC managers send a hold count to the Director of Logistics within 2 hours of a hold notice: cases by lot code, in cooler, in transit, picked for orders, shipped and unaccounted for.

## 8. Insurance and finance

The CFO, Harriet Lowe, maintains product contamination insurance with Trellis Mutual (fictional): limit 25 million dollars, retention 250,000. The policy requires notice within 24 hours and the insurer's consent for the recall consultant. The average value of a case is 31 dollars at wholesale.

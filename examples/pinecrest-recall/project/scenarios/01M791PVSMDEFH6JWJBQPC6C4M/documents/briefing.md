# Player Briefing Packet
## Presumptive — Lot Trace, Holds & Recall Notification

Location: Pinecrest Fresh Cut, LLC — Plant 1 conference room and Recall Team bridge · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 06:00 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Salinas: marine fog, 52°F, light onshore breeze, expected to clear by 10:00 AM. Dry across the Stockton, Phoenix and Dallas lanes.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is Tuesday, October 12, 2027, and the Salinas Valley is in the middle of harvest, the busiest stretch of Pinecrest Fresh Cut's year. Plant 1 in Salinas is starting shift A on lines L1 to L4, cutting, washing and packing romaine, spring mix and baby spinach from Salinas Valley growers, including Santa Lucia Growers (Ranch 14 and Ranch 22) and Monterey Ridge Farms. About 2.1 million pounds a week, or roughly 187,000 cases, leave the plant. Of those cases, about 78,000 go through the Stockton DC cross-dock (manager Luis Carrillo) to Harvest Table Markets in northern California and Nevada and to Westline Foodservice. About 52,000 go through the Phoenix DC (manager Angela Whitfield) to Harvest Table's Arizona stores and to university and school food service. About 45,000 go through the Dallas DC (manager Terrence Boyd) to Cornerstone Grocers' 140 Texas stores and Westline's Texas branch. About 12,000 go direct to Rivermark Kits in Fresno for Caesar salad kits. Retail shelves hold only 2 to 4 days of stock, so every DC is working tight morning schedules. Dallas has been loading store deliveries since 04:00, and Stockton has reefers lined up at its 41 dock doors for early departures. The day crews are in at the plant and all three DCs. Dr. Imani Brooks, the Recall Coordinator, has just paged the Recall Team to the bridge line, (831) 555-0131, code 2200. You are joining that call now in your assigned seats.

- Plant 1 production, lines L1 to L4: GREEN: Shift A starting on all four lines with romaine, spring mix and baby spinach. Production log on PFC-OPS-02 is normal.
- MES lot hold status, MES-to-WMS interface and reconciliation table: GREEN: MES, ERP and the WMS at all three DCs show normal status. Interface is synchronized. No open lot holds.
- DC coolers, hold coolers and quarantine areas (Stockton, Phoenix, Dallas): GREEN: Coolers reading 34 to 38°F. Quarantine areas empty and available.
- Outbound lanes and in-transit reefers, including Sonoran Cold Freight: GREEN: Dallas store deliveries under way since 04:00. Stockton departures and in-transit trailers on schedule.
- Customer acknowledgment tracker (Harvest Table, Cornerstone, Westline, Rivermark): GREEN: No open notices. Customer food safety contacts current per PFC-FS-04.
- Romaine supply and public mood: GREEN: Grower intake on harvest plan. No unusual media or social media attention on Pinecrest or leafy greens.

**The Anomaly:** **05:50 PT, QA inbox, Plant 1.** Dr. Imani Brooks, the Recall Coordinator, has an email from **Coastal Analytical Labs, Watsonville** about **Work Order CAL-27-114582**. This is the routine screen on a retained finished-product sample: chopped romaine, lot **PF1-27278-L2-B**, from Plant 1 line 2. Shortly afterward, Dr. Brooks paged the Recall Team to the bridge. The email will be forwarded to the Recall Coordinator and the Traceability and Systems Lead in the first minutes of the call. Until it arrives, you have no other details.

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep product moving safely to Harvest Table Markets, Cornerstone Grocers, Westline Foodservice and Rivermark Kits while you investigate and resolve this morning's lab report on lot PF1-27278-L2-B and anything that turns out to be linked to it. You will need to balance service against safety and speed against verification. The PPHTRP 4 targets will not wait for perfect information: lots on hold in the MES within 30 minutes, physical holds confirmed and the full trace in 4 hours, and every direct customer reached within 4 hours of any recall decision. Every number you release must still be one you can defend.

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every call on the bridge line, every DC radio call and every phone call to a customer, grower or carrier with "This is an exercise." Plant and DC staff outside this room may overhear a hold or a recall being discussed and take real action.
- If a real emergency happens, such as an injury at Plant 1, a real cooler or ammonia alarm, or a real food safety issue, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." The exercise stops immediately so everyone can deal with the real event.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]." When that happens, assume the work in progress has carried on normally, and update your holds, counts, trailer positions and customer contacts to the new clock time, including how much of the 4-hour targets is left.
- Treat every printed inject as real intelligence and act on it as if it were real. Injects include lab emails, MES and WMS exports, DC count sheets, radio logs, customer emails, regulator calls, social media posts and news articles.

# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**The Second Wave** (Cascadia Rupture, Tsunami Evacuation & Message Discipline)
Location: Marlow County Emergency Operations Center, 410 Hilltop Road, Kellerton, Marlow County, Oregon · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: EM Director / EOC Command, Tsunami and Warning Coordinator, Public Information Officer, 911 Supervisor, Fire District and Sheriff Field Operations, Building Official and Public Works. The SimCell plays the NTWC, USGS, Oregon Emergency Management, ODOT District 2, the Coast Guard, Tillamook and Lincoln counties, Cape Marlow PUD, the hospital and school, the Red Cross, ARES, reporters and residents.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Acting on shaking rather than waiting for a message; one consistent message (same zone names, same magnitude) while magnitude, ShakeMap and PAGER products keep changing; protecting people who cannot self-evacuate; and holding the evacuation until the county gives a deliberate return order while later waves are larger.

**The Threat:** At 10:14 PDT on Tuesday 13 April 2027, during spring break, a magnitude 9.0 earthquake ruptures the Cascadia Subduction Zone about 80 km west of Port Alder. ShakeAlert gives the coast only seconds of warning, and the first automatic magnitude is lower than the final reviewed value. Shaking of intensity IX (MMI) lasts more than four minutes and knocks out power across the county. Three of the 18 tsunami sirens do not sound, cell service degrades, the 1936 Highway 101 Alder River bridge and the gas main it carries are damaged, and a landslide blocks the Highway 6 Neskin grade. At about 10:19 the National Tsunami Warning Center issues a Tsunami Warning for the Oregon coast with first arrival at Port Alder around 10:36 to 10:40. The first wave reaches the harbor tide gauge at 10:38 and later waves are larger. A magnitude 7.6 aftershock follows at 11:52.

**The Operational Impact:** At STARTEX (10:00) the Marlow County EOC is in normal status and about 3,800 spring-break visitors are in the tsunami zone. After the 10:14 rupture:
- **Early warning:** the 911 center and some phones get the ShakeAlert alert only seconds before shaking. Fire station bay doors, hospital elevators and the school drop-cover-hold-on bell must be confirmed and logged with completion times.
- **Sirens and alerts:** two sirens in Port Alder and one at the state park stay silent. As cell service fails within the hour, MarlowAlert and the county WEA reach fewer people.
- **Hospital:** Marlow Coast Community Hospital (25 beds, inside the XL zone) walks ambulatory patients uphill and drives non-ambulatory patients to the Kellerton Station 3 apron.
- **School:** 210 Port Alder Elementary students walk to the Spruce Hill assembly area, and parents then call about reunification at Marlow Coast High School.
- **Cedar Court:** the assisted-living facility calls for vans for 48 residents who cannot reach the assembly area before the first wave.
- **Harbor:** with about 20 minutes left, the Harbor Master asks whether to send boats to deep water.
- **911 center:** the primary center at 118 Front Street floods and telecommunicators move to the backup positions at the EOC in the middle of the call surge.
- **Utilities and roads:** Cape Marlow PUD reports about 14,500 meters out. The Alder River bridge and Neskin grade closures cut Port Alder off to the south and east.
- **Rumor:** a viral post says the tsunami ended after the first wave, and people start walking back toward the harbor.
- **Changing products:** ShakeMap and PAGER versions are revised twice and the magnitude changes, which puts the county's numbers at risk of drifting.
- **Aftershock (11:52):** the magnitude 7.6 aftershock and the USGS aftershock forecast send the Building Official to placard the hospital, the 911 backup, the Marlow Coast High School shelter and downtown masonry. Residents read a green placard as meaning "safe."
- **Return order:** the NTWC warning stays in effect, and the county must decide when, and whether, to issue a return order.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to activate the Marlow County EOC at Full Activation and order evacuation of the whole XL and XXL tsunami zones from the first strong-shaking report, before the 10:19 NTWC Tsunami Warning arrives, and to commit transport for Cedar Court and the hospital's non-ambulatory patients and give the Harbor Master an answer on boats inside the 20-minute window.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to keep one consistent public message across the PIO, the Warning position and 911, using the same zone names and a labeled, versioned magnitude, while ShakeMap, PAGER and the magnitude are revised, three sirens are silent, MarlowAlert and WEA reach fewer people as cell service fails, and the viral post saying the tsunami ended after the first wave spreads.
- **Operational Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to move 911 call-taking from the flooding Port Alder primary center to the backup positions at the EOC during the call surge, and to coordinate Fire District and Sheriff field units on door-to-door warning, access control and the closures that cut off the Highway 101 Alder River bridge and the Highway 6 Neskin grade.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply county doctrine after the M 7.6 aftershock: set ATC-20 placarding priorities (hospital, 911 backup, Marlow Coast High School shelter, Front Street masonry), state the USGS aftershock forecast as probabilities while making clear a green INSPECTED placard is not a guarantee, and decide a deliberate local return order while the NTWC warning stays in effect and later waves are larger, without changing the NTWC message itself.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **MCEM Director (EM Director / EOC Command) (DIR):** Owns overall direction from the EOC Command position: Full Activation, evacuation priorities, the local emergency declaration and the state request, and the timing of any return order.
- **Tsunami and Warning Coordinator (TWC):** Owns tsunami messages and evacuation orders: sirens, MarlowAlert, WEA and EAS, the zone names used in every message, and monitoring of NTWC messages and the harbor tide gauge.
- **Public Information Officer (PIO):** Owns public statements, media, social posts, rumor control and parent reunification messaging, consistent with the Warning position and the EOC.
- **Marlow County Communications (911) Supervisor (911S):** Manages 911 call-taking and dispatch, including the move from the Port Alder primary center to the backup positions at the EOC during the call surge.
- **Port Alder Fire District Chief (Fire District and Sheriff Field Operations) (FOPS):** Manages Fire District and Sheriff field units for search and rescue, door-to-door warning, evacuation help, access control and keeping people out of the zone.
- **Building Official (Building Official and Public Works) (BO):** Owns ATC-20 building safety placards and inspection priorities, and with the Public Works Director, county road and route status and closures.

## Phase 3: Hold the High Ground (Hour 2:00 – 3:00)

Goal: Tests the exercise's one decision point: whether to keep the evacuation in place while the NTWC warning stands and later waves are larger, or allow a limited early return. The phase also tests mutual aid, the local declaration and care for people who cannot help themselves.

Beats: (12:05) DECISION POINT: Pacific Reach Seafoods, the hospital and about 3,800 cold visitors on Spruce Hill press for re-entry to the waterfront and the Alder River flats. Holding the evacuation means overcrowded assembly areas, Cedar Court residents exposed outside, patients on the Station 3 apron and an economic cost. Allowing early return risks people being in the zone for a larger wave while the NTWC warning stays in effect. The county may set local instructions but may not cancel or downgrade the NTWC message (STWE 2).; (12:15) Command makes the local emergency declaration within the four-hour window (EOP 6.5) and asks for a state declaration through the OEM Regional Coordination Center. Staff from south of the Alder River bridge cannot get to the EOC.; (12:40) The USGS releases its first aftershock forecast through the state RCC Duty Seismologist. The PIO must put out probability ranges by 13:00 (EOP 6.6) using the same zone names and the current magnitude version, and correct the 'tsunami is over' rumor again across the WebEOC boards, 911 scripts and the satellite and Starlink channels..

## Phase 4: Placards and Return (Hour 3:00 – 4:00)

Goal: Tests ATC-20 placarding priorities, partner notifications and a deliberate phased return decision made by local officials, including the message that a green placard is not a guarantee against aftershocks.

Beats: (13:05) Building Official Ines Calloway sends inspectors and SEA volunteer engineers out in SOP-EQ-02 4 priority order for the sites above the line: the 911 backup at the EOC, the Marlow Coast High School shelter, the Fire stations, then the roughly 40 Front Street unreinforced masonry buildings. Placards come back dated: the 911 backup and the high school INSPECTED, Station 3 RESTRICTED USE; the hospital and Front Street are observed from outside only, because the Warning is still in effect.; (13:20) Hurdle: the Marlow Coast High School shelter reaches capacity and its generator fails. Red Cross Oregon cannot deliver cots because both highways are cut. Residents post that the green INSPECTED placard means the shelter is 'safe forever', and the PIO must correct this.; (13:35) Notifications: Lifelines and Shelters status goes to OEM, DOGAMI and ODOT District 2.

# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## The Second Wave — Cascadia Rupture, Tsunami Evacuation & Message Discipline

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Marlow County Emergency Operations Center, 410 Hilltop Road, Kellerton, Marlow County, Oregon · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for Marlow County Emergency Management (MCEM) and its partners. It covers four hours of play, from 10:00 to 14:00 PDT on Tuesday 13 April 2027. It is not a functional or full-scale exercise. No sirens sound, no MarlowAlert or WEA messages go out, and no units move. Players talk through decisions, write the products they would issue and say who they would call. You, as Controller/SimCell, answer for everyone outside the room. The plans in play are the Marlow County Emergency Operations Plan tsunami annex (situation in section 2, roles in section 4, concept of operations in section 5), SOP-EQ-02 for early-warning actions, placards and routes, and the MCFS outside-party list in section 8.

The injects simulate a natural hazard: a magnitude 9.0 Cascadia Subduction Zone rupture about 80 km west of Port Alder, followed by a local tsunami with several waves where the later waves are larger. Several things keep changing during play. The magnitude moves from M 7.3 (ShakeAlert, automatic) to M 8.0 (NTWC preliminary) to M 8.9 and then M 9.0 (USGS reviewed versions 2 and 3). ShakeMap and PAGER versions rise. The tide gauge goes silent and then reports larger waves. A M 7.6 automatic aftershock (M 7.5 reviewed) hits at 11:52. Field reports, rumors and a false TV chyron add pressure. The exercise runs in four one-hour phases:
- **Phase 1, Four Minutes of Shaking (0:00–1:00):** act on the shaking itself, get transport for people who cannot walk out, and handle the 911 center flooding.
- **Phase 2, The Larger Waves (1:00–2:00):** message discipline while products change, route closures and the aftershock.
- **Phase 3, Hold the High Ground (2:00–3:00):** the single decision point (keep the evacuation or allow an early return), plus the local declaration and mutual aid.
- **Phase 4, Placards and Return (3:00–4:00):** ATC-20 placarding, the green-placard message and a deliberate phased return order.

Evaluators should watch for these behaviors:
- **Before the NTWC message:** Command and the Warning Coordinator order the evacuation because of the shaking, before the 10:19 NTWC message arrives (EOP section 5, Phase 1).
- **Early-warning log:** ShakeAlert actions are logged with completion times, and the unconfirmed ones (Station 3 doors, hospital elevator recall) are chased down.
- **People who cannot walk out:** vans for Cedar Court and help for the hospital are committed inside the 20-minute window.
- **One message:** the PIO, the Warning desk and the 911 caller script use the same zone names and a magnitude labeled with its version and status (automatic or reviewed). Wave heights are given only when observed, and modeled estimates stay separate from field reports.
- **Return decision:** the evacuation holds while the NTWC warning stands and the gauge keeps rising. Players should recognize that the local all clear belongs to the county, not to the NTWC, the Coast Guard or a TV station.
- **Placards:** placards are not described as guarantees.
- **Shared lists:** the route, shelter and lifeline lists match ODOT District 2 and Oregon OEM and are updated on the hour (SOP-EQ-02 section 5).

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to activate the Marlow County EOC at Full Activation and order evacuation of the whole XL and XXL tsunami zones from the first strong-shaking report, before the 10:19 NTWC Tsunami Warning arrives, and to commit transport for Cedar Court and the hospital's non-ambulatory patients and give the Harbor Master an answer on boats inside the 20-minute window.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to keep one consistent public message across the PIO, the Warning position and 911, using the same zone names and a labeled, versioned magnitude, while ShakeMap, PAGER and the magnitude are revised, three sirens are silent, MarlowAlert and WEA reach fewer people as cell service fails, and the viral post saying the tsunami ended after the first wave spreads.
- **Operational Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to move 911 call-taking from the flooding Port Alder primary center to the backup positions at the EOC during the call surge, and to coordinate Fire District and Sheriff field units on door-to-door warning, access control and the closures that cut off the Highway 101 Alder River bridge and the Highway 6 Neskin grade.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply county doctrine after the M 7.6 aftershock: set ATC-20 placarding priorities (hospital, 911 backup, Marlow Coast High School shelter, Front Street masonry), state the USGS aftershock forecast as probabilities while making clear a green INSPECTED placard is not a guarantee, and decide a deliberate local return order while the NTWC warning stays in effect and later waves are larger, without changing the NTWC message itself.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** set the room as islands, not one big table. Each island stands for a real place, so information has to physically move between them.
- **Island 1, Warning and 911 desk (simulates Position 2 and the EOC backup consoles):** Team B (Tsunami and Warning Coordinator) and Team D (911 Supervisor) sit together. The printed NOAA Weather Wire / EAS / ShakeAlert / tide-gauge injects are delivered here first.
- **Island 2, EOC Command (simulates the Kellerton EOC floor):** Team A (MCEM Director) at the head, with Team E (Fire District and Sheriff Field Operations) and Team F (Building Official and Public Works) on either side. The whiteboard with the ICS-201 stands behind this island.
- **Rear table, Public Information:** Team C (PIO) sits alone at a table at the back of the room, facing away from the main screen if possible. The isolation is deliberate. A real PIO is not handed the Warning desk's NTWC message number or the latest USGS version. The PIO has to get up and go to the other islands to confirm magnitude, zone names and return times. This is what exposes message drift: M 7.3 on a draft post, M 8.0 on the 911 caller script.
- **SimCell table:** at the front side of the room, near the screen, with the inject deck, phones and the C/E handbook. From here you speak as every outside party.
- **Evaluator positions:** chairs behind Islands 1 and 2 and next to the PIO table, with a clear view of the players' writing.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and screen for main-screen injects: chyrons, social posts, ShakeMap/PAGER summaries, the MarlowAlert status page and time-jump cards.
- A whiteboard (or flip chart) set up as the ICS-201: situation map with the XL/XXL zones, objectives, organization, resources and actions. Add one column for the early-warning action log (each action with its time) and one for the route list (Open / Restricted / Closed, with basis and time).
- A printed inject deck: one card per inject, each marked EXERCISE, sorted by recipient team and time.
- Optional: simulated radios or phones (handhelds or desk phones on a closed loop) so calls from the Harbor Master, Cedar Court, the deputies and the RCC are spoken, not handed over.
- A wall clock or a projected exercise clock showing exercise time (PDT).

**Materials for players:** EOP tsunami annex sections 2–5, SOP-EQ-02, the MCFS contact list in section 8, an XL/XXL zone map of Port Alder, blank message forms (MarlowAlert/WEA/EAS, press release, caller script), blank ATC-20 placard forms, name tents, pens and sticky notes.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role: the SimCell**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the deputy. If they call the Harbor Master, the Coast Guard, ODOT District 2, the Oregon OEM Regional Coordination Center, the National Tsunami Warning Center, a reporter or Dr. Naomi Fitch at the hospital, you are that person, with that person's knowledge, priorities and patience.
- Answer only what was asked.
- Answer in the character's voice.
- Give only information that character would actually have.
- If players do not call someone, that party does not get the information. For example, ODOT does not learn the county's route list unless it is sent to them.

**Do not make it easy**
Real partners have their own interests, and players should not get easy yeses or a clean world. One example from this scenario is I-14 at 12:05:
- **The pitch:** the Pacific Reach Seafoods plant manager says the water is "way out" and this is "the window" for four workers to close the ammonia king valve.
- **If players say no:** don't accept it quietly. Push back: "So who's liable when that ammonia lets go? Put your name on it."
- **If players say yes:** come back eight minutes later as the plant manager: "My guys are on the flats. Gauge? What gauge?" Let the next gauge reading (+4.1 m at 11:58 was larger than the earlier waves) land on them.

Similarly, when the Harbor Master asks at I-04 "Do I send the fleet to deep water?", do not accept "use your judgment." Ask for a yes or no and a time.

**Managing time**
The exercise has three scripted time jumps: I-07 (11:00), I-13 (12:00) and I-19 (13:00). When you reach one, stand up, get the room's attention and read:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down. We are jumping forward [30 / 60] minutes. It is now [time] PDT, [elapsed] after the earthquake. [Read the time-jump card.] Anything you had in motion has happened as planned unless I tell you otherwise. RESUME."

- **If a team is stuck:** give one in-character nudge, such as a call from the Spruce Hill volunteer lead. Then move on.
- **If the room falls behind:** drop NOISE and RED_HERRING injects before CORE_NARRATIVE, CLUE or DECISION_POINT injects. Never skip I-14, the decision point.

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says "I wouldn't use that system," "We don't have WebEOC set up that way" or "Our CAD vendor would never…", answer:
> "Good point. Write it on a sticky note for the hot wash. For today, the system exists and works as the card describes. Tell me what you do with it."

Do not argue about the realism of a detail during play. Log it.

**Delivering injects**
- Do not explain an inject, interpret it or point out what matters in it. Hand it over or put it up and say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen."
- For radio and phone injects, read them aloud in character. If you have simulated radios, use them.
- If players ask "What does this mean?", answer as the sender would. A tide gauge does not explain itself, and the duty seismologist only says "Please quote the version number."

**Exercise conventions**
- Every product, call and message in play is preceded or followed by "This is an exercise." Every printed inject carries EXERCISE EXERCISE EXERCISE.
- Players who draft public messages must mark them EXERCISE. Nothing leaves the room.
- **REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD:** if anyone has a real emergency (medical, fire, a real earthquake or tsunami alert, a real call that needs action), they say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." All play stops immediately and the real event is handled. Play resumes only when the facilitator says "RESUME EXERCISE."
- If a real ShakeAlert or NTWC message arrives during the exercise, treat it as REAL WORLD.

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **Oregon Emergency Management (OEM) Regional Coordination Center duty officer (and, on request, Tillamook County Emergency Management for mutual aid)** — _Trigger: Players call the RCC bridge line to request state resources, a state declaration, Coast Guard or Red Cross support, or mutual aid; or the RCC calls in at I-15 (12:15) and I-22 (13:35)._
  - Ask: "Do you have a signed local emergency declaration? No? Then I can't take a state declaration request to the Governor's office. Who is your incident commander, and who signs if your Director can't be reached?"
  - Ask: "Every coastal county from Clatsop to Curry is on this line. What are your top three priorities, in order? 'Everything' is not an order."
  - Ask: "Your route list says 101 is 'restricted' and ODOT District 2 says 'closed.' Which is it, and when was it stamped? I need one route, shelter and lifeline list with a time stamp by 14:00."
  - Ask: "Tillamook here: our own waterfront is under water. We can send a 6-person CERT team and one ambulance, but how do they get to you? We can't cross the Neskin grade."
  - Concede only when: Once the county gives a signed declaration and names the IC and a successor signer, the RCC takes the state declaration request to the Governor's office and logs Marlow County's resource request with a tracking number.
  - Concede only when: Once the county sends a ranked list of priorities with locations and patient counts, the RCC passes it to the Coast Guard helicopter tasking and asks the Red Cross about a sling load of cots and blankets.
  - Concede only when: Tillamook commits its CERT team and ambulance once the county names a staging point and an open route, as shown on the county's hourly route list.
- **NWS National Tsunami Warning Center (Palmer, AK) duty scientist, reached through the NWS Portland forecast office** — _Trigger: The Warning Coordinator or Director calls to ask whether the warning still stands, how big the waves will be, when it will be cancelled, or whether it is safe to let people return (most likely around I-11, I-14, I-18 or I-19)._
  - Ask: "Which message are you reading? We're on Message Number 6. If your desk still has Number 1 or 4, you're behind."
  - Ask: "We lost your Port Alder gauge at 10:41. What has it shown since it came back, with times, and is that observed or estimated?"
  - Ask: "I can't give you a wave height for the next wave, and I don't give your local all clear. That's your call. What are you basing your return decision on?"
  - Ask: "That TV chyron about the Coast Guard saying the threat is passing didn't come from us. The warning is in effect. Who is correcting it on your end?"
  - Concede only when: Once the county passes observed gauge readings and the field water line with times, the duty scientist confirms the current message number and the time of the next message, and notes that the county's observations are in the next update.
  - Concede only when: If asked directly, the duty scientist explains that a downgrade to a Tsunami Advisory is possible as gauges trend down, but an Advisory still means strong currents and danger in or near the water and harbors. It does not mean the county should allow a return to the XL zone.
  - Concede only when: NWS Portland will put the county's own return-order wording on its dissemination channels only after the county sends it in writing and the NTWC message in effect allows it.
- **ODOT District 2 maintenance lead / bridge section** — _Trigger: Team F (Building Official and Public Works) or the Director calls about the Highway 101 Alder River bridge, the Highway 6 Neskin grade slide, a bridge engineer, or reopening a route for Red Cross trucks or relief staff (I-10, I-16, I-21, I-22)._
  - Ask: "That's a state highway, so closure is our call. Is the Sheriff physically holding both ends of the Alder River bridge? I've got reports of cars turning around at the north end."
  - Ask: "No bridge engineer has looked at that 1936 span, and it moved about a foot at the south abutment. It stays closed until we inspect, and we can't get an engineer there by road. Can you get one to us?"
  - Ask: "Cape Marlow Natural Gas is reading 30 percent LEL near the south abutment. Who is keeping people 300 feet back while my crew works?"
  - Ask: "Your partners are getting three different route lists. Send me yours on the hour, every hour, with the basis for each closure."
  - Concede only when: Once the county sends a time-stamped route list that matches the state's status on 101 and 6, ODOT confirms it and the two agencies issue matching lists on the hour.
  - Concede only when: Once the Sheriff confirms access control at both ends of the bridge and the gas exclusion is in place, ODOT puts barricades at the north end and asks the RCC to prioritize a bridge engineer by air.
  - Concede only when: ODOT will start a geotechnical look at the Neskin grade slide from the east side, but it will give no reopening time until the geotech has seen it.
- **Coast Guard command center (Sector Columbia River, Astoria), relayed through the Oregon OEM RCC, plus the Port Alder Harbor Master Luis Ferreira on the Port channel** — _Trigger: Players ask for helicopter support (hypothermic or oxygen-dependent patients, Cedar Court wheelchair users, a sling load of Red Cross supplies), ask about the harbor fleet (I-04), or try to confirm the Channel 8 chyron attributed to a Coast Guard spokesperson (I-18)._
  - Ask: "We have one helicopter for the whole Oregon coast and can't commit before 14:00. Send me priorities with patient counts, conditions, a landing zone and coordinates. 'Spruce Hill' is not a landing zone."
  - Ask: "Harbor Master again. You've got 15 minutes before the wave. Do I send the 42 boats to deep water or tie up and walk my 120 cannery workers uphill? Yes or no."
  - Ask: "We did not tell anyone to go home. We don't give the all clear on land. Who at the county is going to say that on air, and when?"
  - Ask: "Is the harbor basin closed to boats coming back in? Skippers are calling me asking whether they can return to port."
  - Concede only when: Once the county sends a ranked medevac list with a marked, cleared landing zone and a ground contact on a working radio channel, the Coast Guard tasks the helicopter to the top priority after 14:00 and considers a later sling load.
  - Concede only when: Once the PIO asks in writing, the Coast Guard public affairs office issues a statement that the Coast Guard has not cleared any return and that local officials decide when people may go back.
  - Concede only when: If the county closes the harbor and tells the Harbor Master clearly, the Coast Guard broadcasts a Safety Marine Information Broadcast telling vessels at sea to stay in deep water and not return to Port Alder.
- **News media: Marlow Coast Radio 1 news director, Lena Ortiz of the Coast Ledger, and the Channel 8 Coast News newsroom** — _Trigger: The PIO picks up or returns a media call (I-05, I-11, I-18), or any player gives a number, wave height or return time that reaches the press._
  - Ask: "I go live in two minutes. My phone alert said 7.3, the tsunami warning says 8.0 and someone just read me 8.7. Give me one number I can put on air."
  - Ask: "Is the tsunami over? People are saying the first wave was the worst. How high did the water get? Is it 30 feet?"
  - Ask: "We're quoting a Coast Guard spokesperson from Astoria saying people can start going home. Confirm a return time for our 1 o'clock or we run what we have."
  - Ask: "Your website says the high school is INSPECTED. Is that the same as safe? People are bringing their dogs."
  - Concede only when: If the PIO gives a named spokesperson and an on-record statement with the magnitude labeled by version (for example 'M 9.0, USGS reviewed, version 3'), the outlet reads it word for word on its next break and keeps reading it until the next update.
  - Concede only when: If the county gives a written correction with a named source before the deadline, Channel 8 pulls the chyron and runs a correction that the evacuation stays in place and only local officials can order a return.
  - Concede only when: If the PIO gives observed water levels from the gauge or a field water line, labeled with times, the reporter uses those numbers instead of a guessed wave height.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** 3-Up, 3-Down asks the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the exercise. Go around the room one team at a time (Command, Warning/PIO, 911, Fire/Sheriff, Shelter/Hospital, Building) and write every answer on the whiteboard in two columns before anyone discusses them, so the loudest team doesn't set the list.

**Key Discussion Questions for The Second Wave:**
- **The Call:** The shaking lasted more than four minutes and the NTWC Tsunami Warning didn't arrive until about 10:19. Who on your team ordered evacuation of the whole XL and XXL zones, and when? Was it on the strong-shaking report or only after the message came in? STWE 2 says nobody waits for the message. If you waited, what were you waiting for? Within that 20-minute window, when did you commit transport for the Cedar Court residents and the hospital's non-ambulatory patients, and when did the Harbor Master get an answer on the boats? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **One Message:** At I-18, the TV chyron said the 'Coast Guard says tsunami threat passing'. At the same moment, the 911 backup caller script still read 'M 8.0' and people at the Spruce Hill aid station were packing up. Earlier, at I-11, the @SurfDadOregon post and the Coast Ledger reporter asked whether the first wave was the worst. When did the PIO, the Warning position and 911 last check that they were using the same zone names and the same labeled, versioned magnitude? Who owned updating the caller script, and what did you tell Deputy 9's crowd of 40 and the Channel 8 reporter while the gauge showed later waves were larger? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The Window:** At I-14, three requests arrived within four minutes: the seafood plant manager wanted four people on the Alder River flats 'while the water's way out', the CMO wanted staff sent back into the hospital basement for oxygen, and 3,800 people on Spruce Hill were threatening to walk down. NTWC Message Number 4 still read WARNING. Did you treat the drawdown as a safe window or as a sign of the next wave? What did you give each of them instead of a 'yes', for example oxygen from Station 3 or shelter and warmth for the Cedar Court residents? What would have let you make that decision faster? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Evidence:** At I-10, the tide gauge showed +3.6 m at 11:06 compared with +2.4 m at the first wave. The Engine 31 photo put the water two blocks past Front Street. Deputy 14 reported that the 101 bridge abutment had dropped a foot and the gas smell was getting stronger, and ODOT reported the Highway 6 Neskin slide. Did you use those reports to reposition field units, access control and closures, or did they just become log entries? How did the 911 move from the flooding primary center to the EOC backup positions hold up during the call surge? At I-22, how quickly did your route list match ODOT's for the OEM sitrep? _(Objective: Operational Coordination)_
- **Trust:** At I-21, the @PortAlderMom_Kris post told people the green INSPECTED placard on the high school meant it 'can't fall down'. The shelter was already at its 400 limit with no generator, and the Red Cross truck couldn't get past the 101 and 6 closures. Then the M 5.4 ShakeAlert at 13:31 hit at I-22. How did you set ATC-20 placarding priorities after the M 7.6? Did the PIO, the Building Official and the shelter manager quote the same USGS aftershock forecast issuance, as probabilities with the time it was issued, as SOP-EQ-02 section 7 requires? Was the deliberate local return order you gave or held back stated as your own local instruction, without appearing to change the NTWC warning? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Earthquakes and early warning**: Earthquake products and decisions: the event summary, PAGER, aftershock forecasts, early warning alerts and the automated actions they trigger, building safety placards and lifeline status. For seismic networks, early warning operations and every organization that has buildings, people and lifelines in shaking.

  - Reference documents: ShakeAlert—An Earthquake Early Warning System for the United States West Coast (Fact Sheet 2014-3083); Comparison of Earthquake Early Warning Systems and the National Volcano Early Warning System at the U.S. Geological Survey (Fact Sheet 2023-3033); PAGER—Rapid Assessment of an Earthquake’s Impact (Fact Sheet 2010-3036); The U.S. Geological Survey's Guide to Communicating Aftershock Forecasts: A Primer (draft)
- **Tsunamis**: Tsunami messages and decisions for local sources (minutes to the first wave) and distant sources (hours): warning, advisory, watch and information statement messages, international threat messages, evacuation timing and cancellation. For warning centers, coastal counties, ports and harbors.
  - Data sources: tsunami-events, coops-tides, ndbc-dart, comcat

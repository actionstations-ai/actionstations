# Player Briefing Packet
## The Second Wave — Cascadia Rupture, Tsunami Evacuation & Message Discipline

Location: Marlow County Emergency Operations Center, 410 Hilltop Road, Kellerton, Marlow County, Oregon · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 10:00 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Overcast with light rain, 52°F, west wind 10 mph, tide falling; Small Craft Advisory on coastal waters.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is Tuesday 13 April 2027, the middle of spring break on the Marlow County coast. The Marlow County Emergency Operations Center in Kellerton is in normal monitoring status. Director Gwen Halvorsen is at her desk. Tsunami and Warning Coordinator Priya Natarajan is going through the siren battery report, and PIO Dale Whitcomb is writing a spring-break beach safety post. Marlow County Communications (911) is fully staffed at the Port Alder primary center at 118 Front Street, and the CAD mirrors to the EOC backup consoles every 5 minutes. It's a busy morning. About 3,800 spring-break visitors are on the Port Alder waterfront, in the Front Street shops, at the Alder River mobile home park and at the Cape Marlow State Park campground, which is nearly full. Marlow Coast Community Hospital has 18 inpatients. Port Alder Elementary is running its spring-break day camp with 210 students. Cedar Court assisted living is at its usual 48 residents. The Port of Port Alder fishing fleet and the Pacific Reach Seafoods plant are working normally. Cape Marlow PUD reports no outages, and Highway 101 and Highway 6 are both open. The day's routine work is visitor safety messages, sneaker-wave reminders and normal call volume.

- Marlow County EOC (Kellerton): monitoring status; WebEOC boards (Tsunami Status, Lifelines, Shelters, Road Status, Resource Board) current: GREEN – Normal
- 911 primary center (Port Alder): fully staffed; CAD mirroring every 5 min to the 4 backup consoles at the EOC: GREEN – Normal
- Warning feeds at Position 2: NOAA Weather Wire, EAS receiver, NWS Portland relay, ShakeAlert alarm via delivery partner: GREEN – Receiving; no active tsunami messages
- Public warning: tsunami sirens (battery report under routine review), MarlowAlert, county WEA, coastal cell service: GREEN – Available
- County VHF radio (9 repeaters: 3 on propane generators, 6 on 8-hour batteries): GREEN – All repeaters up
- EOC power and backup communications (generator with 5 days of fuel, 2 Starlink terminals, satellite phone, amateur radio, conference bridge); Cape Marlow PUD grid; Highway 101 and Highway 6: GREEN – Normal, no outages, roads open

**The Anomaly:** **10:05 AM: two routine items arrive within a minute of each other**

1. **Email to warning@marlowcountyor.example from the NWS Portland forecast office relay (Warning Coordinator):**
 *"MARINE WEATHER STATEMENT, NWS Portland OR, 958 AM PDT Tue Apr 13 2027. Small Craft Advisory for hazardous seas remains in effect until 5 PM PDT for coastal waters from Cape Falcon to Cascade Head out 10 NM. West swell 11 ft at 12 seconds. Sneaker waves possible on west-facing beaches this afternoon."*

2. **Phone call to the PIO line from Gary, the Cape Marlow State Park campground host in Loop B (cell 503-555-0147):**
 *"Hi, this is Gary, the host in Loop B. We've got about forty kids planning the big spring-break bonfire on the beach tonight. Somebody told them the county pulled the burn permit because of that sneaker wave thing. Is the permit still good? They want to know before they haul the wood down."*

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep warning, 911 and public information services running for Marlow County's residents and its 3,800 spring-break visitors while you investigate and resolve whatever develops today. Balance speed against verification: act decisively on what the field and your own feeds tell you, and keep every number and zone name in your public messages consistent and accurate. Protect the people who can't move themselves, and never let the pressure to restore service put responders or the public back in harm's way.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: EM Director / EOC Command**— MCEM Director (EM Director / EOC Command) | You own overall direction from the EOC Command position: activation level, evacuation priorities, any local emergency declaration and state request, and the timing of any decision to let people return to evacuated areas. | **Shaking Is the Warning: if long or strong shaking is reported, order the evacuation zones immediately; never wait for an NTWC message.** **People Who Cannot Walk First: send vans, vehicles and staff to the hospital, Cedar Court and the school before anything else.** **No Early Return: nobody goes back toward the harbor until you give a deliberate, logged return order, because the largest wave is often not the first.** |
| **Team B: Tsunami and Warning Coordinator**— Tsunami and Warning Coordinator | You own tsunami messages and evacuation orders: sirens, MarlowAlert, WEA and EAS, the zone names used in every message, and monitoring of NTWC messages and the Port Alder harbor tide gauge. | **Send Now: issue the evacuation order for the named zones as soon as it's warranted, and fix the wording afterward.** **Same Words Every Time: use the exact zone names and the latest labeled product version in every alert; the county adds local instruction but never upgrades, downgrades or cancels an NTWC message.** **Fill the Gaps: if a siren or cell site goes silent, ask the field for door-to-door, loudspeaker or radio coverage instead of assuming the message got through; report wave arrivals as observations only after a gauge or buoy shows them.** |
| **Team C: Public Information Officer**— Public Information Officer | You own public statements, media, social posts, rumor control and parent reunification messaging, and you keep them consistent with the Warning Coordinator and the EOC. | **One Voice: clear every number (magnitude version, zone names, times) with the Warning Coordinator before it goes out.** **Truth Over Reassurance: tell people plainly what is known and what is still coming; never imply a danger has ended until the county says so.** **Kill the Rumor Fast: answer false or confusing posts within minutes on every channel that still works, and say that a green placard does not guarantee safety from later aftershocks.** |
| **Team D: 911 Supervisor**— Marlow County Communications (911) Supervisor | You own 911 call-taking and dispatch at Marlow County Communications, including any move from the Port Alder primary center to the backup positions at the EOC. | **Get Out First: if the primary center is threatened, telecommunicators move to the EOC backup positions; a call surge doesn't justify staying in the zone.** **Log Everything: record times for alerts, fire station bay doors, hospital elevators, the school bell and any transfer to backup.** **Feed the EOC: pass reports of collapse, people trapped, silent sirens and people moving toward the harbor to Command right away.** |
| **Team E: Fire District and Sheriff Field Operations**— Port Alder Fire District Chief (Fire District and Sheriff Field Operations) | You own Port Alder Fire District and Sheriff field units for search and rescue, door-to-door warning, evacuation help, access control and keeping people out of hazard areas. | **Crews Above the Line: no unit works in the inundation zone after the expected first arrival; rescue resumes only on Command's word.** **Trust the Field: if the board and what you see disagree, report what you see and act on it.** **Hold the Perimeter: deputies stop people from going back toward the harbor until the county issues a return order, whatever social media says.** |
| **Team F: Building Official and Public Works**— Building Official (Building Official and Public Works) | You own ATC-20 building safety placards and inspection priorities. With the Public Works Director, you also own county road and route status and closures. | **Priority List: inspect the hospital and the 911 backup first, then the shelter school, fire stations, the EOC and the Front Street masonry, then houses.** **Date and Sign: every placard carries the date and the inspector's name; reassess after any aftershock of M 6.0 or larger.** **Closed Until Inspected: damaged bridges and grades stay closed until an inspection reopens them; publish one route list every hour on the hour, send state highway closures through ODOT and leave access control to the Sheriff.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio call, phone call and message in this room with "This is an exercise," so nobody outside the EOC mistakes a practice tsunami warning for a real one and panics.
- If a real emergency happens during play, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD". The exercise stops immediately and everyone turns to the real event.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to skip ahead. Accept the new time and the conditions the facilitator describes, then carry on from there.
- Treat printed injects (CAD logs, NTWC messages, social media posts, photos, field reports) as real intelligence and act on them as you would on live information arriving at your position.

# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Clearance Time** (Evacuation Decision & Vertical Critical-Care Moves)
Location: Mariner Bay Medical Center, Ocean Drive, Corpus Christi, Nueces County, Texas · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Hospital Incident Commander / COO, Chief Nursing Officer, House Supervisor, NICU Charge Nurse, ICU Charge Nurse, Transfer Center Liaison

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Deciding under a shrinking clock, keeping fragile patients safe when they have to move, and keeping trust with staff and families during a storm.

**The Threat:** Hurricane Quinlan is a fictional Category 3 storm in the western Gulf. At 06:00 Tuesday, September 15, 2026, the exercise National Hurricane Center advisory forecasts landfall near Corpus Christi on Wednesday evening, with 34-knot winds (H) reaching the city at 18:00 Wednesday CDT and a storm surge of 4 to 6 feet. Before STARTEX, at 04:00, the Nueces County Judge ordered Zone A evacuated. Zone B, where Mariner Bay Medical Center sits 1.4 miles from the bay, has not been ordered out. Under the 12-hour clearance rule, the horizontal evacuation decision is due by 06:00 Wednesday. At 07:00 Tuesday a forecast shift moves H forward 6 hours, to 12:00 Wednesday. That moves the decision deadline to midnight Tuesday and Vertical Evacuation Phase 1 to 06:00 Wednesday. The causeways close and the road to San Antonio floods at its low points. After landfall, surge water reaches the ground floor at 20:00 Wednesday. The ground floor holds the ED, imaging and the main pharmacy, so the ED has to move to the second floor.

**The Operational Impact:** At STARTEX, the COO serving as Hospital Incident Commander and the CNO are in the command center (ext. 4520). In house are 12 NICU infants (3 on ventilators) and 19 ICU patients (6 ventilated). 120 ride-out staff are scheduled and the fuel tanks are filling. The Transfer Center Liaison (ext. 4700) can find only 5 critical care ground ambulances and one helicopter, which stops flying at 34 knots, and the San Antonio receiving hospitals are filling. The Medical Director for Critical Care and one family disagree about where two infants fall in the move order. The roster is 14 nurses short because the Staffing Coordinator's agency nurses cannot cross the bridge, which leaves the NICU and ICU at 1:3 instead of 1:2 on the night of landfall. Some staff ask to leave to protect their own families. During Phase 1, the NICU moves to 7 West (90-minute target) and the ICU to 6 West (3-hour target), with only 2 of 6 elevators on generator power. The last drill took 74 minutes for 8 patients. During the move, a ventilated NICU infant's early warning score jumps. An ICU patient's infusion pump battery fails on the stairs while the patient is on 3 infusions. Two contact-isolation patients need separate placement. A handoff to 7 West leaves out an allergy and a pending culture. A medication delay requires a disclosure conversation with the family within 60 minutes. One family member refuses to leave the NICU. A social media post claims all babies are being evacuated to Houston, and the PIO has to correct it. Families, reporters, the county, the Regional Advisory Council and the receiving hospitals call in throughout.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to make and document the horizontal evacuation or shelter-in-place decision by the revised midnight Tuesday deadline after H moves to 12:00 Wednesday, weighing the NHC advisory, the Nueces County Judge's order, the 12-hour clearance rule, the 5 available critical care ground ambulances and the one helicopter, as set out in the Hurricane Plan sections 1 and 2.
- **Operational Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to carry out Vertical Evacuation Phase 1 at 06:00 Wednesday, moving the NICU to 7 West within 90 minutes and the ICU to 6 West within 3 hours using only the 2 elevators on generator power, while handling the infusion pump battery failure and keeping the two contact-isolation patients apart as NUR-EM-07 6 requires.
- **Patient Safety and Clinical Escalation:** Demonstrate the ability to recognize and escalate the ventilated NICU infant's rising early warning score to a physician at the bedside within 15 minutes, catch and correct the missing allergy and pending culture in the 7 West handoff by structured read-back, and start the medication-delay disclosure conversation with the family within 60 minutes.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to keep the CNO, the Transfer Center Liaison and the PIO on the same numbers (patients in house, patients transferred, staff on site, H), as Hurricane Plan 5 requires, and get a correction cleared and issued for the false post claiming that all babies are going to Houston.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Incident Commander (Hospital Incident Commander / COO) (IC):** Owns the shelter-or-evacuate decision, the 6-hour operational period briefings in the command center (ext. 4520) and coordination with Nueces County OEM and the Regional Advisory Council.
- **Chief Nursing Officer (Operations Section Chief) (CNO):** Owns nursing operations, the vertical evacuation, ride-out staffing ratios and the shared census numbers.
- **House Supervisor (nursing supervisor) (HS):** Manages the hourly unit status reports, cross-unit moves of staff and equipment, and the night-of-landfall leadership on the floor.
- **NICU Charge Nurse (NICU):** Leads the NICU move to 7 West within the 90-minute target, including the order of moves, hourly early warning scores and handoffs.
- **ICU Charge Nurse (ICU):** Leads the ICU move to 6 West within the 3-hour target, covering ventilated patients, infusions, isolation placement and the medication-delay disclosure.
- **Transfer Center Liaison (TCL):** Owns the transfer center (ext. 4700): receiving hospital beds, ambulance and air assets, the level of care and mode for each patient, and the transfer counts.

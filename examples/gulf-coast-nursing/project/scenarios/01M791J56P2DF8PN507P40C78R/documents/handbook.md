# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Clearance Time — Evacuation Decision & Vertical Critical-Care Moves

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Mariner Bay Medical Center, Ocean Drive, Corpus Christi, Nueces County, Texas · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for Mariner Bay Medical Center nursing and command leaders. It runs 4 hours of exercise time and starts at 06:00 CDT on Tuesday, September 15, 2026. Nobody moves a patient, starts a call tree or touches a real system. Players talk through the decisions, calls and handoffs they would make under the Hurricane Plan (EOP Annex F) and NUR-EM-07. The hazard is **Hurricane Quinlan**, a fictional Category 3 storm. All exercise products are marked EXERCISE and use simulated data. The injects simulate National Hurricane Center advisories and NWS Corpus Christi local statements, calls from the Nueces County Office of Emergency Management, the Coastal Bend Regional Advisory Council (RAC) bed board, a staffing agency that cannot cross the bridge, worried families, false social media posts and reporters on deadline. They also simulate what happens at the bedside during a stairwell and elevator move.

The exercise has four phases, one hour each. **Phase 1, The Watch (0:00–1:00):** players read an uncertain forecast against the H-based timeline in Hurricane Plan section 1 and start pre-positioning while Zone B has no evacuation order. **Phase 2, The Clock Moves Up (1:00–2:00):** a time jump to 07:00 Tuesday. H moves forward 6 hours to 12:00 Wednesday, so the shelter-or-evacuate deadline is now midnight. This is the exercise's single decision point. **Phase 3, Vertical Evacuation Phase 1 (2:00–3:00):** a time jump to 08:00 Wednesday. The NICU moves to 7 West against a 90-minute target and the ICU moves to 6 West against a 3-hour target, with only 2 elevators on generator power. During the move, players face a deteriorating infant, a dead infusion pump and isolation placement, and they receive a handoff with missing information. **Phase 4, Landfall and Return (3:00–4:00):** a time jump to 09:00 Thursday, after the overnight surge reached the ground floor, and players handle a late dose and disclosure, a second wave of rumors, a failed elevator, staff who ask to leave, and the first criteria for returning units to service.

Evaluators should watch for these things:
- **Time anchoring:** do players re-anchor every deadline to the new H, or keep working from the old one?
- **One shared census:** do all teams use the same patient numbers (the 9-versus-11 conflict)?
- **Escalation chain:** do staffing gaps go charge nurse → House Supervisor → Staffing Coordinator → CNO, with the time limits in NUR-EM-07 section 5?
- **Bedside safety during moves:** SBAR to the provider, closed-loop read-back, and a structured handoff that includes allergies and pending results.
- **Information flow to the PIO:** does the PIO get verified facts quickly enough to correct rumors before media deadlines?

Score what players actually say and document, not what they say they would "probably" do.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to make and document the horizontal evacuation or shelter-in-place decision by the revised midnight Tuesday deadline after H moves to 12:00 Wednesday, weighing the NHC advisory, the Nueces County Judge's order, the 12-hour clearance rule, the 5 available critical care ground ambulances and the one helicopter, as set out in the Hurricane Plan sections 1 and 2.
- **Operational Coordination:** Demonstrate the ability to carry out Vertical Evacuation Phase 1 at 06:00 Wednesday, moving the NICU to 7 West within 90 minutes and the ICU to 6 West within 3 hours using only the 2 elevators on generator power, while handling the infusion pump battery failure and keeping the two contact-isolation patients apart as NUR-EM-07 6 requires.
- **Patient Safety and Clinical Escalation:** Demonstrate the ability to recognize and escalate the ventilated NICU infant's rising early warning score to a physician at the bedside within 15 minutes, catch and correct the missing allergy and pending culture in the 7 West handoff by structured read-back, and start the medication-delay disclosure conversation with the family within 60 minutes.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to keep the CNO, the Transfer Center Liaison and the PIO on the same numbers (patients in house, patients transferred, staff on site, H), as Hurricane Plan 5 requires, and get a correction cleared and issued for the false post claiming that all babies are going to Houston.

**Plan sections exercised:**
- Mariner Bay Medical Center — Hur… › 2. Decision to shelter in place or evacuate horizontally
- Mariner Bay Medical Center — Hur… › 1. Why a timeline
- Mariner Bay Medical Center — Hur… › 3. Vertical evacuation procedure
- NUR-EM-07 › 3. Priority and sequence
- NUR-EM-07 › 4. Early warning and rapid response
- Mariner Bay Medical Center — Hur… › 7. Communication

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** set the tables as islands in a U-shape. The front of the U simulates the bedside: the **NICU Charge Nurse desk** (Team D) and the **ICU Charge Nurse desk** (Team E) side by side.
- **Middle of the U:** the **House Supervisor** table (Team C), with the hourly unit status board beside it.
- **Back of the U, the Hospital Command Center (ext. 4520):** **Incident Commander / COO** (Team A) and **CNO / Operations Section Chief** (Team B) at one long table facing the main screen.
- **Transfer Center Liaison (ext. 4700, Team F):** a separate island next to command, with its own phone and the RAC bed board printout.
- **Public Information Officer (Colin Reyes):** an isolated table at the rear of the room, away from command. The PIO must walk over to get confirmed numbers before answering a reporter or correcting a post. This makes the information flow visible and tests whether command pushes verified facts out or makes the PIO chase them. Do not let command shout numbers across the room.
- **SimCell / controller table:** off to the side, near the facilitator. It holds the phones/radios, the inject deck and the call log. Evaluators sit behind the teams they score, with a clear view of each team's notes.

**Equipment needs**
- Projector and screen for main-screen injects (NHC advisories, HLS products, social media screenshots, time-jump cards)
- Whiteboard or flip chart for the ICS-201 (incident briefing), plus a second board for the shared census (NICU and ICU, by bed and transfer status)
- Printed inject deck in MSEL order, with the controller notes (I-01 to I-21, plus STARTEX, time jumps and ENDEX)
- Optional: simulated radios labeled channel 2 (command) and channel 3 (nursing), desk phones or handsets labeled with extensions 4520, 4700 and 4410, a satellite phone prop for command
- Wall clock that shows exercise time, which the controller updates at each time jump

**Materials for players:** Hurricane Plan (EOP Annex F) sections 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7; NUR-EM-07; ICS-201 and blank status report forms; a paper downtime MAR and handoff cards; unit floor plans for floors 2, 3, 6 West and 7 West; name tents by role.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role as SimCell**
You play everyone else in the world. If a player says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the deputy. In this exercise you will most often be:
- the Nueces County OEM duty officer
- NWS Corpus Christi, through OEM briefings
- the Coastal Bend RAC hospital coordination desk and the Alamo Heights Children's transfer nurse
- Gulf Coast Critical Transport dispatch
- Gulf Nurse Staffing
- the pharmacist on call
- the 6 West and 7 West charge nurses
- NICU and ICU families
- Bay Tribune and Coastal Bend 6 News reporters

Answer in the first person, in the character's voice and in the character's own interest. Write down every call with its exercise time. Evaluators need that log.

**Do not make it easy**
Real partners want specifics, they have deadlines of their own, and they do not wait. Do not accept vague answers. For example, at I-07 the Alamo Heights Children's transfer nurse is holding one NICU bed until 23:00. If the Transfer Center Liaison says "we'll probably send one or two babies, we're still working on it," do not hold the bed. Say: "Your morning list said 9, your CNO's board says 11. I need a name, a bed and a ventilator status, or I give the bed to Victoria's request at 23:01." Players get the bed only when they give a reconciled, named list. In the same way, the OEM duty officer does not accept "we're evaluating" as a shelter-or-evacuate status for the 23:00 state situation report.

**Managing time**
There are three time jumps, at 1:00 (I-06), 2:00 (I-11) and 3:00 (I-17). Read each one aloud, word for word:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down, phones down. We are jumping forward [the hours on the card]. It is now [07:00 Tuesday / 08:00 Wednesday / 09:00 Thursday]. Here is what happened while we were away. [Read the time-jump card.] Anything you decided before the pause is assumed done unless I tell you otherwise. RESUME."

Update the wall clock and the census board before you say RESUME. Inside a phase, if a team stalls for more than about 5 minutes, push them with the next inject or a SimCell call. Do not wait. For example: "OEM is calling back, they still need your number."

**Handling artificialities**
If a player says "I wouldn't use that system," "we'd just text the doc," or "in real life we'd have more ambulances," answer:
> "Noted. We'll capture that for the hot wash. For today, play it as written: what do you do with what you have in front of you?"

Write the comment on the parking-lot sheet and move on. Do not argue about the scenario.

**Delivering injects**
Do not explain. Hand over the card or the call and say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." Do not hint at what the inject tests, do not point out the missing allergy in I-16, and do not tell players a rumor is false. Let them verify it by calling OEM, as in I-07b. If players ask a question the inject does not answer, answer in character, or say "You don't know that yet. Who would you ask?"

**Conventions**
- Every product, call and card is **EXERCISE**. If you speak to anyone outside the room, start and end with "This is an exercise."
- If a real emergency or real patient-care need comes up, anyone calls out **"REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD."** Play stops at once, and the facilitator handles it. Restart only when the facilitator says so.
- Do not use real patient names, real phone numbers or live alert channels. All numbers are exercise numbers.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the storm response. Go around the room one team at a time (command, nursing and critical care, transfer center, PIO, facilities), and write each item on the whiteboard under "Up" or "Down" before you move to the discussion questions.

**Key Discussion Questions for Clearance Time:**
- **The Clock:** At 07:00 Tuesday, the forecast shift moved H to 12:00 Wednesday. That moved your horizontal evacuation deadline from 06:00 Wednesday to midnight Tuesday, and you lost six hours you thought you had. When did you stop trusting the original Hurricane Plan 1 timeline? Did you call the Nueces County OEM and NWS Corpus Christi before you decided, or after? Zone B had not been ordered out, and you had only 5 critical care ambulances and one helicopter. What fact, call or pre-set trigger would have let you make and document the shelter-or-evacuate decision sooner? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Bays:** At I-14, the 6 West charge nurse told you she had 19 usable spaces. The intensivist then gave you the call on 3-07, who is on three pressors, and 3-14, who is proned, and asked for an answer before the 06:30 elevator slot. Did command understand the clinical risk of unproning a patient in a hallway? Did the clinicians understand the floor 3 problem: an isolated floor at 1:3 once the ground floor flooded? Who actually made that call? How did you keep 3-11 and 3-18 separate as NUR-EM-07 6 requires, and still fit the NICU move into 90 minutes and the ICU move into 3 hours on two generator-powered elevators? _(Objective: Operational Coordination)_
- **The Handoff:** In the middle of Vertical Evacuation Phase 1, the infusion pump battery failed and the ventilated NICU infant's early warning score kept rising. How long did it take to get a physician to the bedside, and what slowed it down? In the 7 West handoff, did you treat the structured read-back as real evidence and catch the missing allergy and the pending culture? Or was it a box you ticked to clear the elevator? Who started the medication-delay disclosure with the family, and was that inside 60 minutes? _(Objective: Patient Safety and Clinical Escalation)_
- **The Post:** At I-09, the Bay Tribune had a 21:00 deadline. At I-19, Coastal Bend 6 News was going live at 21:10. Both cited posts saying all the babies were going to Houston and that you were 14 nurses short. What did you tell the public before you knew the true numbers? Were the CNO, the Transfer Center Liaison and the PIO all working from the same count of patients in house, patients transferred, staff on site and H, as Hurricane Plan 5 requires? How long did it take to clear and issue a correction? Did those same numbers go into the status report the RAC wanted by 22:00 at I-21? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **Trust:** At I-04b, Dana asked to leave by noon to get her children out of Zone A. Luis, who is on the ride-out list, said his wife was 8 months pregnant in Flour Bluff. Everyone in the 'Storm Alert' group thread could see how you answered. What did you decide for each of them, and who decided it? How did your answer change ICU staffing once H moved forward six hours? Did the NICU and ICU rosters still keep a single-competency nurse in place, so that no off-unit nurse was ever left alone with a ventilated patient? _(Objective: Operational Coordination)_

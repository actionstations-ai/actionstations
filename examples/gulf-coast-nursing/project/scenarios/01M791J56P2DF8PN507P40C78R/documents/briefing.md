# Player Briefing Packet
## Clearance Time — Evacuation Decision & Vertical Critical-Care Moves

Location: Mariner Bay Medical Center, Ocean Drive, Corpus Christi, Nueces County, Texas · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 06:00 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Corpus Christi: overcast, 84°F, wind SE 12 mph gusting 20 mph, scattered showers moving in off the bay, seas building.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is a Tuesday morning in mid-September, and Mariner Bay Medical Center on Ocean Drive is running a normal weekday. 268 of 310 beds are filled. The NICU on floor 2 has 12 infants, 3 of them on ventilators. The ICU on floor 3 has 19 patients, 6 of them ventilated. Labor and delivery on floor 4 is steady, and medical-surgical units 6 West and 7 West are at their usual census. On the ground floor, the emergency department is busy with ordinary weekday volume, and imaging and the main pharmacy are working through their morning queues. Hurricane Quinlan, a Category 3 storm, is in the western Gulf, and it is the main topic in the hallways. Under EOP Annex F, the 120-person ride-out roster is posted, and Facilities is filling the generator fuel tanks toward 96 hours. No evacuation order covers Zone B. Hospital Incident Commander COO Dennis Falcone and CNO Dr. Marguerite Delacroix are in the command center (ext. 4520). Night shift is handing over to day shift on every unit, and the Nueces County OEM daily call is starting.

- Hospital census and units (NICU floor 2, ICU floor 3, L&D floor 4, 6 West and 7 West): GREEN: 268/310 beds filled; NICU 12 (3 ventilated), ICU 19 (6 ventilated); normal operations
- Emergency power and generators (central plant second-floor mezzanine), red outlets and elevators: GREEN: on utility power; generators tested and ready; fuel tanks filling toward 96 hours; all elevators in service
- Ventilators, transport monitors, infusion pumps and portable oxygen: GREEN: all units report equipment in service and charged; bulk oxygen supply normal
- Transfer center (ext. 4700) and regional transport: GREEN: transfer center staffed; Coastal Bend Regional Advisory Council bed board active; no transfers pending
- Ride-out staffing and nurse-to-patient ratios: GREEN: 120 ride-out staff on the roster; ratios at plan targets (ICU 1:2, NICU 1:3 with 1:1 for ventilated infants, medical-surgical 1:6); Staffing Coordinator Rosalind Aguirre, RN, at ext. 4410
- Communications: hospital phones, Wi-Fi and radio channel 3: GREEN: all systems up; radio check on channel 3 completed overnight

**The Anomaly:** **06:05 AM, command center (ext. 4520), Nueces County OEM daily call**

The routine 06:00 county call is under way, and National Hurricane Center Advisory 14 for Quinlan has just arrived in the command center inbox. When the roll call reaches hospitals, the OEM duty officer says: "Mariner Bay, are you sheltering or evacuating? We need your posture for the state situation report." The line stays open while the duty officer waits for an answer.

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep every patient safe, keep critical care and the rest of the hospital running, and work out and settle Mariner Bay's storm posture before the clearance-time deadlines in EOP Annex F run out. You must balance acting fast against confirming your information: confirm beds, transport, staff and the forecast before you commit. Every patient, family and staff member must hear one consistent message.

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every phone call, radio call and page with "This is an exercise." A real patient, family member or staff member may overhear talk of evacuation or storm surge, and this keeps that from causing real panic in the hospital.
- If a real emergency happens, such as a real patient deteriorating, a real fire alarm or a real injury, say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." The exercise stops immediately so everyone can respond to it.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to skip ahead. When that happens, assume the hours in between went as planned and work from the new clock, including the new distance to H.
- Treat every printed inject as real intelligence and act on it: advisories, emails, bed boards, phone scripts, social media posts, logs and reports.

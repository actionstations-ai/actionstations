# Hot Wash Guide
## Clearance Time

### Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the storm response. Go around the room one team at a time (command, nursing and critical care, transfer center, PIO, facilities), and write each item on the whiteboard under "Up" or "Down" before you move to the discussion questions.

**Key Discussion Questions for Clearance Time:**
- **The Clock:** At 07:00 Tuesday, the forecast shift moved H to 12:00 Wednesday. That moved your horizontal evacuation deadline from 06:00 Wednesday to midnight Tuesday, and you lost six hours you thought you had. When did you stop trusting the original Hurricane Plan 1 timeline? Did you call the Nueces County OEM and NWS Corpus Christi before you decided, or after? Zone B had not been ordered out, and you had only 5 critical care ambulances and one helicopter. What fact, call or pre-set trigger would have let you make and document the shelter-or-evacuate decision sooner? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Bays:** At I-14, the 6 West charge nurse told you she had 19 usable spaces. The intensivist then gave you the call on 3-07, who is on three pressors, and 3-14, who is proned, and asked for an answer before the 06:30 elevator slot. Did command understand the clinical risk of unproning a patient in a hallway? Did the clinicians understand the floor 3 problem: an isolated floor at 1:3 once the ground floor flooded? Who actually made that call? How did you keep 3-11 and 3-18 separate as NUR-EM-07 6 requires, and still fit the NICU move into 90 minutes and the ICU move into 3 hours on two generator-powered elevators? _(Objective: Operational Coordination)_
- **The Handoff:** In the middle of Vertical Evacuation Phase 1, the infusion pump battery failed and the ventilated NICU infant's early warning score kept rising. How long did it take to get a physician to the bedside, and what slowed it down? In the 7 West handoff, did you treat the structured read-back as real evidence and catch the missing allergy and the pending culture? Or was it a box you ticked to clear the elevator? Who started the medication-delay disclosure with the family, and was that inside 60 minutes? _(Objective: Patient Safety and Clinical Escalation)_
- **The Post:** At I-09, the Bay Tribune had a 21:00 deadline. At I-19, Coastal Bend 6 News was going live at 21:10. Both cited posts saying all the babies were going to Houston and that you were 14 nurses short. What did you tell the public before you knew the true numbers? Were the CNO, the Transfer Center Liaison and the PIO all working from the same count of patients in house, patients transferred, staff on site and H, as Hurricane Plan 5 requires? How long did it take to clear and issue a correction? Did those same numbers go into the status report the RAC wanted by 22:00 at I-21? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **Trust:** At I-04b, Dana asked to leave by noon to get her children out of Zone A. Luis, who is on the ride-out list, said his wife was 8 months pregnant in Flour Bluff. Everyone in the 'Storm Alert' group thread could see how you answered. What did you decide for each of them, and who decided it? How did your answer change ICU staffing once H moved forward six hours? Did the NICU and ICU rosters still keep a single-competency nurse in place, so that no off-unit nurse was ever left alone with a ventilated patient? _(Objective: Operational Coordination)_

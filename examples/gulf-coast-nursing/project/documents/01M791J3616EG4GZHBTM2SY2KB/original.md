# Mariner Bay Medical Center — Hurricane Plan and Vertical Evacuation Annex

Emergency Operations Plan, Annex F · Owner: Chief Nursing Officer and Director of Emergency Management · Revised May 2026

## 1. Facility and setting

Mariner Bay Medical Center is a 310-bed acute care hospital on Ocean Drive in Corpus Christi, Nueces County, Texas, about 1.4 miles from Corpus Christi Bay. It has a main tower of eight floors and a three-story ambulatory wing. The hospital is in Hurricane Evacuation Zone B of the Coastal Bend. Its lowest occupied floor (ground) has the emergency department (ED), imaging and the main pharmacy; the 100-year storm surge map places up to 4 feet of water on the ground floor in a major hurricane from the southeast. The generators and main electrical gear are on the second floor mezzanine of the central plant, which is above the surge line; fuel storage covers 96 hours at full load.

Critical care areas sit low in the tower: the 16-bassinet Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) on floor 2, the 24-bed ICU on floor 3 (including 6 patients on ventilators on a typical day), and labor and delivery on floor 4. Floors 6 and 7 contain medical-surgical units 6 West and 7 West (28 beds each), which in a hurricane are the designated receiving areas for vertical evacuation.

## 2. Decision to shelter in place or evacuate horizontally

The Hospital Incident Commander (the Administrator on Call, moving to the Chief Operating Officer, Dennis Falcone, on activation) decides about 72 hours before expected tropical-storm-force winds. The decision uses: the National Hurricane Center forecast cone and wind speed probabilities, the Nueces County Judge's evacuation order, the state's guidance on hospital evacuation by zone, and an estimate of clearance time. For this hospital the planning clearance time to move all patients out of the area, including loading critical patients on ambulance and air transport, is 12 hours (720 minutes). Tropical-storm-force winds (34 knots) stop ambulance and ground transport on the causeways and bridges, so the plan sets the final horizontal evacuation order no later than 12 hours before the forecast arrival of 34-knot winds. After that point the hospital shelters in place and moves critical patients vertically.

Horizontal evacuation is requested through the Texas Hospital Association's regional healthcare coordination (the Regional Advisory Council in the Coastal Bend) and the receiving hospitals, which in this plan are in San Antonio and Victoria. A transfer center at Mariner Bay (ext. 4700) arranges each patient's transfer, the level of care needed, the mode (ground, critical care ground, air) and the receiving physician.

## 3. Vertical evacuation procedure

If the hospital shelters in place and a major surge is forecast, or the ground floor floods, critical care units move up:

1. The Incident Commander orders "Vertical Evacuation, Phase 1" when surge or wind forecasts require, and no later than 6 hours before 34-knot winds arrive.
2. NICU moves first from floor 2 to the designated area on 7 West (16 bassinets, with transport incubators, 4 ventilators and wall gas). ICU moves next from floor 3 to 6 West (24 beds with all monitors on portable telemetry).
3. Patients move in priority order using the Evacuation Priority List (section 4). The target is 90 minutes for the NICU and 3 hours for the ICU; each move needs a physician or advanced practice provider order and a receiving nurse accepted.
4. Elevators: two of the six elevators are on generator power; they are held for patient movement only and are staffed by security. If power fails, patients move by evacuation sled and stair chair; each stairwell team has four people and the transport of a ventilated patient requires a respiratory therapist.
5. Each unit keeps a patient tracking board with location, acuity, equipment and family contact. The House Supervisor has the master board in the command center on floor 1 of the tower (the Boardroom is on floor 5, ext. 4520, and is used if the ground floor floods).

## 4. Evacuation priority list

The default order in which patients move vertically, when time or staff are short, is:

1. patients who rely on electricity or oxygen that the receiving area cannot supply at the original location (ventilated adults, unstable NICU infants on respiratory support);
2. ICU patients on continuous infusions or hemodynamic monitoring;
3. NICU infants who are stable on incubator care, moved together with the mother when possible;
4. ambulatory and stable inpatients.

The Charge Nurse and the attending provider may change the order by exception and record why. Staff may not move a patient out of order to suit a family request without the House Supervisor's approval.

## 5. Staffing

Ride-out teams of 120 clinical staff are scheduled in 12-hour shifts for 72 hours; they report to the hospital 24 hours before landfall and have lodging on the sixth floor of the ambulatory wing. Nursing ratios during the storm are targeted at ICU 1:2, NICU 1:3 (1:1 for infants on ventilators), medical-surgical 1:6. The Staffing Coordinator (Rosalind Aguirre, RN, ext. 4410) holds the roster, requests a contract staffing agency (Gulf Nurse Staffing, a vendor) for relief after the storm, and tells the House Supervisor of any shortage in advance. An escalation of more than 3 nurses short on a unit goes to the CNO within 15 minutes.

## 7. Communication

Staff are alerted by text group "Storm Alert", overhead page and radio. The hospital radio channel 2 is for command, channel 3 for nursing. Satellite phones are in the command center and each critical care unit has a handheld radio. Families are told, before landfall, that visiting is closed; the PIO posts updates on the hospital website and with the Nueces County Emergency Management Office. Outside contacts: NWS Corpus Christi/Ironcladsville, the Nueces County Office of Emergency Management, the State of Texas hospital coordination system, receiving hospitals and the Coastal Bend Regional Advisory Council. Telephone numbers in the exercise are 361-555-01xx.

## 8. After the storm

When winds drop below 34 knots for 2 consecutive hours, the Facilities Director inspects the building, the CNO decides the return of units to their original floors, and the House Supervisor reconciles patient tracking and staffing. Disclosure of any patient harm event to families is done by the attending provider and the unit manager within 60 minutes of the harm being recognized, according to the hospital's disclosure policy, and recorded.

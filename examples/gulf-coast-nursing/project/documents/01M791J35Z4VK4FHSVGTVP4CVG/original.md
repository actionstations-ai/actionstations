# Mariner Bay Medical Center — NICU and ICU Evacuation and Handoff SOP (NUR-EM-07)

Owner: Chief Nursing Officer · Applies to NICU (floor 2), ICU (floor 3) and the receiving units 7 West and 6 West

## 1. Readiness

At hurricane watch the NICU and ICU managers verify: a count of patients, equipment and ventilators; transport incubators and their batteries (6 on hand), 24 portable monitors with batteries charged for 4 hours, 8 transport ventilators, oxygen cylinders (30 E-size and 6 H-size on the NICU), evacuation sleds under each bed on floor 2 and 3, flashlights, markers for each patient's body (to tag with the plan), and the unit's paper downtime forms. At hurricane warning each unit prints a medication administration record (MAR) and a handoff sheet for every patient.

## 2. Moving a patient: the six steps

1. **Order and acceptance.** The physician or advanced practice provider writes a transfer order; the receiving charge nurse accepts the patient by name and bed.
2. **Safety checks.** Two nurses verify identification band, allergies, code status, isolation status and the current infusions. For infants, the identification bands (mother and baby) and the security tag are checked before the infant leaves the unit.
3. **Prepare equipment.** The respiratory therapist confirms oxygen and ventilator settings and battery time; the nurse confirms infusion pumps are on battery and drug supply is enough for 6 hours.
4. **Move.** Teams of two to four; one person watches the patient, one the equipment, one the route. If using stairs, a stair chair or sled team of four and a nurse go together. Time of departure is written on the tracking board.
5. **Handoff.** A structured handoff (SBAR: situation, background, assessment, recommendation) is given at the bedside to the receiving nurse, who reads back; the sending nurse gives the MAR and notes any drip that has to be titrated.
6. **Confirm.** The receiving nurse confirms arrival on the tracking board and reports to the House Supervisor within 10 minutes. The sending unit logs the patient as "arrived."

A handoff is complete only when the receiving nurse has read back the diagnosis, code status, allergies, current infusions, next medication due and any pending test.

## 3. Priority and sequence

The NICU's first transport priority is infants on ventilators or CPAP and those with umbilical lines, then others by acuity. In the ICU, ventilated patients on three or more infusions move first, then patients on one or two infusions, then spontaneously breathing patients. If surge water is already in the building, or there is a power failure, all patients are moved in the order set by the Medical Director for Critical Care (Dr. Chaudhary) and the CNO; the House Supervisor records the order, the reasons and the times.

## 4. Early warning and rapid response

Patients keep their early warning score every 4 hours (every hour in the NICU for infants on respiratory support). The nurse calls a rapid response for an early warning score of 7 or more, a single critical vital sign, or concern from staff or family. During vertical evacuation the rapid response team is staffed by the intensivist and the respiratory therapist on duty. The target time from first abnormal early warning score to physician at bedside is 15 minutes.

## 5. Escalation of staffing shortages

If a unit expects to fall below a safe ratio (ICU 1:2, NICU 1:3, medical-surgical 1:6), the charge nurse tells the House Supervisor at once. The House Supervisor tells the Staffing Coordinator, who reassigns nurses from units with spare capacity, brings ride-out staff, then calls the staffing agency. Shortages that last more than 2 hours are told to the CNO and the Hospital Command Center.

## 6. Isolation and infection control

Patients on contact or droplet isolation are moved in isolation gowns with a dedicated route and receiving room. Two patients with the same organism may be cohorted on 6 West. The infection preventionist (Hailey Brandt, RN, ext. 4455) approves cohorts. During a storm the hospital does not admit infants on contact isolation to the NICU holding area.

## 7. Receiving hospital transfers

If a patient must leave the building after all (for example if the building is damaged), the Transfer Center Liaison arranges the receiving hospital, level of care and transport mode (critical care ground, helicopter). The nurse calls in the report on the transfer line (ext. 4700) before departure and gives the receiving nurse the MAR and the isolation status. The center sends a record of who was transferred where to the command center.

## 8. Disclosure and documentation

Any event that harms a patient is disclosed by the attending provider and the unit manager to the patient or family within 60 minutes of recognition, using the hospital's disclosure policy, and recorded. Nurses document patient condition at the start and end of every move. During a storm the hospital's EHR (a cloud-hosted system) stays up if the network does; if it does not, paper is used.

## 9. Family support

Families of NICU infants are offered a place to stay at the Mariner Bay Family House on floor 8 of the tower, and told each time a baby is moved. A family liaison nurse (Naomi Villareal, RN) calls parents who have left the hospital as soon as an infant has been moved.

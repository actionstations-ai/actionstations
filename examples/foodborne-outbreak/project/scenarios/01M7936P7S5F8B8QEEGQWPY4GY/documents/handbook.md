### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for the Marlow County Health Department (MCHD) and its partner at Marlow Regional Medical Center (MRMC). It runs for 4 hours of real time, starting at a simulated 08:40 on Monday, October 12, 2026. No one is deployed, no specimens move and no real notices go out. Players talk through what they would do, decide and write down. You and the SimCell make the outside world push back.

The injects simulate a mass-gathering foodborne outbreak. About 412 people ate at the Marlow Heritage Days Community Supper at the Fenwick Fairgrounds Exhibition Hall on Saturday, October 10 (service 17:00 to 19:30), and many are now sick. The cause is later identified as Salmonella from a point source. It likely traces to the Kessler produce vendor, which also supplied grocers in Garrison and Pell counties. The phases run as follows:
- **Phase 1, The First Call (0:00–1:00):** the infection preventionist's report of 11 ED patients, a stale voicemail on the duty phone and the first media call.
- **Phase 2, Hotline Overflow (1:00–2:00):** a broken shared line list, a red-herring potluck, an epidemic curve built by report date, a 14-versus-17 count conflict and a Facebook post naming the caterer.
- **Phase 3, The Caterer Call (2:00–3:00):** the one decision of the exercise, made with an attack-rate table that cannot yet separate the coleslaw from the pasta salad. The choice is a voluntary hold, an embargo or a closure order for Prairie Table Catering before Tuesday's 11:30 senior meal.
- **Phase 4, One Count, Wider Net (3:00–4:00):** matching public, clinician and school products, Spanish-language access and a state call about matching isolates in two neighboring counties.

Afternoon and evening events are compressed into Phase 4: the stool culture grows Salmonella at 15:20, and the list reaches about 90 cases by evening.

**What evaluators should watch for:**
- **Case counting.** Cases are counted by onset date and hour, never by report date. Every count names its tier and its as-of time.
- **Case definition.** The definition has three tiers and is logged with each revision (MOISOP 2).
- **Decision log.** Each decision is recorded with the time, the person, the facts known, the option chosen and the reason (MOISOP 8).
- **Outbreak declaration.** The Level 3 declaration states its basis (EOP 4).
- **State notification.** The state is told by phone the same day, on suspicion (County Profile 6).
- **The caterer.** No food vehicle is named publicly until the analysis supports it, and the caterer is still controlled before Tuesday.
- **One count.** MCHD, MRMC and the school district publish the same figure (MCHD-COM-03 5).
- **Kessler traceback.** It goes through Iowa HHS to FDA, and MCHD does not call the vendor.

**Objectives:**
- **Case Definition and Epidemic Curve:** Demonstrate the ability to adopt a three-tier (confirmed, probable, suspect) case definition approved by the Director within 8 hours of the 08:40 hospital report, and build the Heritage Days supper epidemic curve from onset date and hour, not report date, using the coded line list.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to choose between a documented voluntary hold, an embargo signed by the Director or a closure order for Prairie Table Catering before Tuesday's senior meal, while the attack-rate table still cannot separate the coleslaw from the pasta salad and the chicken thighs.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to issue a holding statement, a clinician health alert, an exposure notice and an approved school letter that carry one count with an as-of time matching the MCHD line list, that settle the gap between the hospital's 14 cases and MCHD's 17, and that answer the Facebook post naming the caterer without naming an unproven food vehicle.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply the Level 3 outbreak declaration criteria, specimen and courier procedures and escalation steps in the MCHD plans: declaring and recording the outbreak, authorizing a special State Hygienic Laboratory courier for Cary-Blair stool kits, and deciding with the state epidemiologist whether to request CDC Epi-Aid once isolates match in two neighboring counties.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Separate team tables (islands) set up like the MCHD departmental operations center (DOC) on the second floor of the Marlow County Services Building. The main screen and the whiteboard are at the front.
- **Front-center island, Team A (Public Health Director):** Sits nearest the whiteboard and the decision log. The Director signs off on everything, so everyone else must come to this table.
- **Front-left island, Team B (Epidemiology Lead):** Put the single master line list laptop here. Only one keyboard edits the list; this reinforces the I-07/I-07b lesson.
- **Front-right island, Team C (Environmental Health Supervisor):** Keep the Prairie Table file, invoice KPV-10-0931 and the chain-of-custody forms here.
- **Mid-left island, Team D (Nurse Manager):** Keep the interviewer roster, the Cary-Blair kit count and the code-to-name key here, in a sealed envelope that only the Nurse Manager opens.
- **Side island, Team F (Hospital Infection Preventionist):** Set slightly apart and labeled "MRMC". The infection preventionist is a partner, not MCHD staff, and reaches MCHD by "phone" or by walking over.
- **Rear table, Team E (Public Information Officer):** Put this table at the back of the room, away from the Epidemiology table. The PIO has to walk over to get each count and as-of time. This forces them to verify numbers before release, and it makes the gap between the hotline, the line list and the hospital figure obvious. Do not let the PIO shout across the room for numbers.
- **SimCell / controller table:** Set this at a side wall, with the inject deck, phone cards and a clock showing simulated time. Evaluators sit behind each island with their evaluation guides.

**Equipment needs**
- Projector and screen for main-screen injects: the Facebook post, the TV slip, the curves and the attack-rate table.
- Whiteboard with a pre-drawn ICS-201 (situation summary, objectives, organization, resources). Leave a column for "Count / tier / as-of time".
- Printed inject deck in MSEL order. Mark it EXERCISE MATERIAL and include spares of I-10 and I-13 for every table.
- Optional: simulated phones or radios (labeled cards will do) so players "call" the SimCell rather than talk across the room.
- Clock or slide showing the simulated time, plus the time-jump cards (I-07, I-12, I-16).

**Materials for players:** MCHD outbreak plan (EOP) and MOISOP excerpts, MCHD-COM-03, a blank decision log, a blank three-tier case definition form, an onset-curve grid in 6-hour bins, a blank holding statement and HAN template, a chain-of-custody form, and name tents.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**The SimCell role**
You play everyone in the world outside the room. If a student says "I call the state epi", you answer as the Iowa HHS duty epidemiologist. If they say "I call the sheriff", you answer as the deputy. If they say "I call Prairie Table", you are Delphine Okonkwo, and she is angry. Pick up the phone card, use the character's voice and answer only what was asked. Never step out of character to coach. If players call someone who is not scripted, play that person in their own interest: busy, protective of their organization, and asking for what they are owed.

**Do not make it easy**
Real partners do not hand over cooperation for free. For example, when the Environmental Health Supervisor asks Prairie Table to cancel Tuesday's senior meal (I-13b), Delphine Okonkwo does not agree. She offers to pull the Kessler items and nothing more, and says "Put it in writing." She gives way only when the Director produces a documented voluntary hold or a signed order with stated grounds. In the same way, the state epidemiologist (I-06) will not log the event until players give a case definition, a count by tier with an as-of time, and the earliest and latest onset. If a player hands a partner a report-date curve or a count without a tier, the partner asks "As of when? Which tier?" until they get a real answer.

**Managing time**
Hold to the phase clock. At each time jump, stand, wait for silence and read:
> "PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down. We are jumping forward one hour. It is now 10:40 on Monday, October 12. [Read the I-12 card.] Whatever you decided before the pause has been carried out. Whatever you did not decide has not happened. RESUME."

Use the same format for I-07 (09:40) and I-16 (11:40). If a table stalls on one issue for more than 10 minutes, use a short jump: "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. The hotline has taken 40 more calls and the radio newscast has aired. RESUME." For compressed time in Phase 4, say plainly: "This result is arriving at 15:20, compressed time."

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "we'd just text the duty phone", answer:
> "Noted. Write it on the parking lot sheet for the hot wash. For today, the system works the way the card says: the duty phone has no text, the line list is one shared spreadsheet, and the courier runs Tuesday through Friday. How do you work with it?"

Do not argue the point, and do not let it eat the clock. These gaps are deliberate improvement-plan items.

**Delivering injects**
- Do not explain an inject. Hand it over and say "This just came in", or point and say "This is on the main screen."
- Deliver phone injects by voice, in character, with the number the call came from.
- Do not point out the trap in an inject (the report-date curve in I-10, the Campylobacter voicemail in I-02, the potluck in I-08). Players must find it.

**Exercise conventions**
- Every product, email and call starts or ends with "This is an exercise." Any document that leaves the room must be marked EXERCISE MATERIAL.
- If a real emergency or a real-world issue comes up (a medical problem, a real outbreak call, a fire alarm), anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." All play stops at once until the lead controller says "RESUME EXERCISE."

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** 3-Up, 3-Down: ask the room for three things that went well and three that broke or failed during the exercise. Go around the room one team at a time (Epi, Environmental Health, Communications, Leadership and the hospital/school players) and write every item on the whiteboard in two columns. No debating until each team has had a turn.

**Key Discussion Questions for Side Dish:**
- **The Curve:** At 1:40 you got the 'Cases by Day' chart (I-10), plotted by report date with the caption 'Ongoing exposure?' The onset column for the same 17 cases already showed a single peak on Sunday morning, with the last onset at Mon 03:00. When did someone in the room stop trusting that chart? Did anyone catch it before it shaped a decision or a message? What would have made onset date and hour the default for every curve you build? _(Objective: Case Definition and Epidemic Curve)_
- **The Line List:** At 1:06 the interviewer turned up at the door (I-07b) with a conflicted copy of the sheet: two HD26-015 records with different onset times, first names typed into the notes column, and two more interviewers asking for edit rights. Did you handle the line list as investigation evidence, with one master, a single owner for case IDs and coded identifiers, or as a shared spreadsheet anyone could fix? How did you settle the duplicate IDs? And did your three-tier case definition get Director approval within 8 hours, so every interviewer was counting the same way? _(Objective: Case Definition and Epidemic Curve)_
- **The Call:** At the 10:45 huddle the attack-rate table (I-13) showed coleslaw OR 15.5 and pasta salad OR 11.5. Dev's note said they couldn't be separated and that chicken from Prairie Table's own kitchen had a cold-holding finding from 06/2026. Then the owner called (I-13b): he'd pull the Kessler items but refused to cancel Tuesday's senior meal, and said 'put it in writing.' Did you choose a documented voluntary hold, a Director-signed embargo or a closure? What evidence were you waiting on? Did you collect the leftover salads and the raw thighs from the same case lot? And with senior meal prep starting at 18:00, what would have let you decide faster? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **One Count:** At 2:34 two emails landed (I-14b). The hospital planned to post '14 patients' at noon against the 17 you'd given the radio, and the superintendent planned to send a 13:00 letter blaming 'bad coleslaw' for 'dozens of students,' in English only. Did you reach the hospital communications manager and the district before their deadlines? How did you explain the 14-versus-17 gap with an as-of time? How did you answer the Facebook post naming the caterer without naming a food vehicle that wasn't proven? What did you tell the public before you knew, and did each product meet its MCHD-COM-03 Section 3 target time? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **Escalation:** Over these four hours you had a growing case count, a stool culture at 15:20 showing Salmonella and a vendor who also sold to two other communities. ### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Epidemiology and outbreak investigation**: Outbreak investigation, surveillance and response for exercises: the terms and sequence field epidemiologists use, the products they issue (case reports, questionnaires, exposure notices, clinician alerts, letters, guidance), the decisions they make and the clocks they are measured on. Includes epidemic curves, line lists, attack-rate tables, contact networks, laboratory and dispensing boards, wastewater series, and simulated outbreaks. Stays at public health response decisions; it carries no pathogen handling detail.
  - Data sources: cdc-nwss, cdc-nndss
  - Reference documents: Principles of Epidemiology in Public Health Practice, Third Edition (CDC); PHEP Exercise Framework Supplemental Guidance (CDC, April 2024); Public Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Capabilities (CDC, 2018)
- **Media environment**: The news cycle and social feeds around an incident: wire alerts, articles and broadcast segments, social posts whose reach and sentiment change over time, reporter calls and press conference questions, and verification tasks with provenance clues. Simulated spread and sentiment are teaching models, not predictions. All outlets, handles and people are fictional.
  - Data sources: gdelt
  - Reference documents: FCC report on the Hawaii false alert (2018): a correction case study

# Player Briefing Packet
## Side Dish — Supper Outbreak, Line List & One Count

Location: Marlow County Health Department, second floor of the Marlow County Services Building, Fenwick, Marlow County, Iowa · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 08:40 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Overcast, 52°F, light wind, no weather concerns. A typical mid-October Monday in Fenwick, Iowa.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is Monday, October 12, 2026, at the Marlow County Health Department (MCHD) on the second floor of the Marlow County Services Building in Fenwick. The office is busy in the usual Monday way after the Marlow Heritage Days weekend. Staff are clearing weekend paperwork, returning calls and picking up the week's routine food establishment inspections. The surveillance unit finished the 08:00 check of the Iowa state disease surveillance queue and found no cluster flag. The next scheduled check is at 15:00. Over the weekend only the Epidemiology Lead and one nurse were on call, and nobody has checked the after-hours duty phone since Friday. All staff are in today: the Director, the Epidemiology Lead with backup analyst Dev Kulkarni, Environmental Health, the nursing staff, the Public Information Officer and administrative staff. The county has no laboratory of its own. Marlow Regional Medical Center (MRMC) runs bacterial cultures and a GI PCR panel and sends positives on to the State Hygienic Laboratory. At MRMC, infection preventionist Sunita Reddy, RN, CIC, is starting her week. The outbreak hotline (555-0150, six lines) opened at 07:00 and has taken two routine calls. MCHD has no open investigations.

- MCHD epidemiology program (surveillance, investigation, nursing, environmental health): GREEN: fully staffed, no open investigations
- Iowa state disease surveillance queue (electronic lab reports): GREEN: 08:00 check complete, no cluster flag; next check at 15:00
- MCHD outbreak hotline 555-0150 (six lines, two staff per shift): GREEN: open since 07:00, two routine calls
- Line list spreadsheet, notice templates and document library on the MCHD file server: GREEN: available; templates pre-approved by the Director apart from the figures
- Lab path: MRMC culture and GI PCR panel, State Hygienic Laboratory courier and specimen cold chain: GREEN: normal; courier runs Tuesday through Friday
- Health alert distribution (fax and e-mail list of 212 clinicians, urgent care clinics, long-term care facilities, school nurses) and staff text tree: GREEN: tested and current

**The Anomaly:** **08:42, Monday. MRMC Emergency Department, at shift change.**

The ED night charge nurse stops Sunita Reddy, RN, CIC (Infection Preventionist, MRMC) at the nurses' station on the way out and hands her a tracking-board printout. "Sunita, take a look at this before I leave. It was a busy night for stomach bugs. Since 20:00 last night we've seen 11 adults and teens with vomiting, watery diarrhea and fever. Two are still in obs on IV fluids, and one of them is 81 and looks dry. Nobody's admitted yet. We sent stools on three of them for the GI panel and culture."

The printout lists the visits by time of arrival. Nobody has called anyone else yet.

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep MCHD's routine public health services and its public communication running accurately while you investigate and resolve the increase in gastrointestinal illness reported from the MRMC Emergency Department. You will have to balance speed against verification and continuity against containment. Act early to protect the public, but never release a number, name a cause or name a business until the evidence and the Director support it.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: Public Health Director (Command)**— Public Health Director | You own command of the MCHD response: the decision to declare an outbreak, approval of the case definition and of every public notice, the signature on any embargo or closure order, and briefings to the Board of Health, the county administrator and the press. | **Declare on Evidence: before you announce anything, record the basis for the declaration, the count with its tier and as-of time, and the time you made the declaration.** **One Voice: the Board of Health, the school district and the hospital receive exactly the number the PIO publishes, never an informal or off-the-record estimate.** **Escalate Deliberately: you decide on a special State Hygienic Laboratory courier outside the Tuesday-to-Friday run and on any request for state or federal help, so set your triggers early and don't wait for every result.** |
| **Team B: Epidemiology (Surveillance and Analysis)**— Epidemiology Lead | You own the case definition, the coded line list (the single source of every count), the epidemic curve, hypothesis-testing analysis, and contact with the state epidemiologist at Iowa HHS. | **Single Count: every number that leaves this room comes from the line list and carries a tier (confirmed, probable or suspect) and an as-of time.** **Onset, Not Report: build the epidemic curve from symptom onset time (6-hour bins for the first 3 days, per MOISOP 6) and reject any chart ordered by report or visit date.** **Table Before Name: don't let anyone name a source until an attack-rate and odds-ratio table supports it, and brief backup analyst Dev Kulkarni before the line list outgrows one person.** |
| **Team C: Environmental Health**— Environmental Health Supervisor | You own food establishment inspection, operator and vendor records, recommendations for a voluntary hold, embargo or closure, and the collection of any food samples under chain of custody. | **Control Now: control measures do not wait for the final analysis, so request voluntary holds when the evidence warrants and log each request and the operator's reply time.** **Chain of Custody: no sample goes to the courier without a signed custody form and a cold chain record.** **Recommend, Don't Order: embargo or closure goes to the Director for signature, never out on your own authority.** |
| **Team D: Nursing (Interviews and Specimens)**— Nurse Manager | You own the interviewer pool, distribution of Cary-Blair stool kits, exclusion counseling for food handlers, child care workers and health care workers, and the only key that links case codes to names. | **Guard the Key: names stay in your key file and never go into the shared line list, an e-mail or a briefing.** **Interview by Priority: interview hospitalized cases, food handlers, health care workers and students first with your six interviewers, and re-plan if mutual aid nurses from Garrison and Pell counties arrive.** **Report the Unreachable: log every person you cannot reach and send the list to Epidemiology rather than dropping them quietly.** |
| **Team E: Public Information**— Public Information Officer | You own holding statements, exposure notices, the hotline script, school and employer letters, social media, Spanish translation and rumor control. | **Match the Line List: every figure you publish carries the Epidemiology tier and as-of time, and the hospital and school versions must match it.** **Don't Guess: answer media and rumors with verified facts and approved text, and never confirm or deny a source or name a business without the Director's authorization.** **Plan for Translation: start the Spanish version as soon as the English text is locked, because translation takes about four hours, and send nothing out without the Director's approval.** |
| **Team F: Hospital Infection Prevention (MRMC)**— Infection Preventionist, Marlow Regional Medical Center (Hospital Infection Preventionist) | You own MRMC's case reporting to MCHD, stool and blood culture results, enteric precautions, staff exposure assessment, and keeping the hospital's public figures consistent with MCHD's. | **Report Into One List: send every ED patient who fits the case definition to MCHD by code so the hospital count and the line list stay the same.** **Hold the Hospital Number: the hospital publishes no case total of its own until it matches the MCHD count and its as-of time.** **Results Fast: phone MCHD the moment a stool or blood culture is positive, forward isolates to the State Hygienic Laboratory, and check ill staff before they return to patient care.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every phone call, voicemail, text or simulated radio message with "This is an exercise," so that no one outside this room, such as a clinician, school, caterer or reporter, mistakes a practice outbreak for a real one.
- If a real emergency happens during play, anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD" and the exercise stops immediately, because real people and real public health duties always come first.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to skip ahead, so reset your clock, counts and as-of times to the new time and carry on from there.
- Treat printed injects (tracking-board printouts, voicemails, phone slips, social media posts, lab reports and logs) as real intelligence, because the investigation depends on how you verify, record and act on them.

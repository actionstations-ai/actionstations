# Hot Wash Guide
## Side Dish

### Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** 3-Up, 3-Down: ask the room for three things that went well and three that broke or failed during the exercise. Go around the room one team at a time (Epi, Environmental Health, Communications, Leadership and the hospital/school players) and write every item on the whiteboard in two columns. No debating until each team has had a turn.

**Key Discussion Questions for Side Dish:**
- **The Curve:** At 1:40 you got the 'Cases by Day' chart (I-10), plotted by report date with the caption 'Ongoing exposure?' The onset column for the same 17 cases already showed a single peak on Sunday morning, with the last onset at Mon 03:00. When did someone in the room stop trusting that chart? Did anyone catch it before it shaped a decision or a message? What would have made onset date and hour the default for every curve you build? _(Objective: Case Definition and Epidemic Curve)_
- **The Line List:** At 1:06 the interviewer turned up at the door (I-07b) with a conflicted copy of the sheet: two HD26-015 records with different onset times, first names typed into the notes column, and two more interviewers asking for edit rights. Did you handle the line list as investigation evidence, with one master, a single owner for case IDs and coded identifiers, or as a shared spreadsheet anyone could fix? How did you settle the duplicate IDs? And did your three-tier case definition get Director approval within 8 hours, so every interviewer was counting the same way? _(Objective: Case Definition and Epidemic Curve)_
- **The Call:** At the 10:45 huddle the attack-rate table (I-13) showed coleslaw OR 15.5 and pasta salad OR 11.5. Dev's note said they couldn't be separated and that chicken from Prairie Table's own kitchen had a cold-holding finding from 06/2026. Then the owner called (I-13b): he'd pull the Kessler items but refused to cancel Tuesday's senior meal, and said 'put it in writing.' Did you choose a documented voluntary hold, a Director-signed embargo or a closure? What evidence were you waiting on? Did you collect the leftover salads and the raw thighs from the same case lot? And with senior meal prep starting at 18:00, what would have let you decide faster? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **One Count:** At 2:34 two emails landed (I-14b). The hospital planned to post '14 patients' at noon against the 17 you'd given the radio, and the superintendent planned to send a 13:00 letter blaming 'bad coleslaw' for 'dozens of students,' in English only. Did you reach the hospital communications manager and the district before their deadlines? How did you explain the 14-versus-17 gap with an as-of time? How did you answer the Facebook post naming the caterer without naming a food vehicle that wasn't proven? What did you tell the public before you knew, and did each product meet its MCHD-COM-03 Section 3 target time? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **Escalation:** Over these four hours you had a growing case count, a stool culture at 15:20 showing Salmonella and a vendor who also sold to two other communities. When did you formally declare and record a Level 3 outbreak? When did you authorize the special State Hygienic Laboratory courier for the Cary-Blair kits? At what point did you call the state epidemiologist about a possible CDC Epi-Aid once isolates matched in neighboring counties? Did the plan's escalation steps drive those calls, or did events? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_

# MCHD-EPI-02 — Outbreak Investigation Standard Operating Procedure

Marlow County Health Department · Epidemiology Program · Effective September 2026

## 1. Principle

An investigation moves through the usual steps: confirm the outbreak, define cases, find cases, describe by time, place and person, form a hypothesis, test it, collect specimens and food samples, control, communicate, and report. MCHD runs several steps at once; control does not wait for the analysis to finish.

## 2. Case definition

The Epidemiology Lead writes the definition in three tiers and revises it as facts arrive. A definition change is logged with the new text, the effective time and the reason.

- **Confirmed:** a person with diarrhea (three or more loose stools in 24 hours) or vomiting, with onset on or after the first exposure date, who attended the exposure event, and has a laboratory-confirmed pathogen from a stool specimen.
- **Probable:** a person with illness meeting the clinical criteria who attended the event, with no laboratory confirmation, or an epidemiologically linked person with a confirmed case.
- **Suspect:** a person with one symptom (for example abdominal cramps or fever) and attendance, or a person with illness who cannot say whether they attended.

Time is always the onset date and hour of illness, never the date the report came in. The exposure window is computed backward from onset using the incubation period of the suspected agent: for Salmonella usually 6 hours to 6 days, most often 12 to 72 hours; for norovirus 12 to 48 hours; for Clostridium perfringens 6 to 24 hours; for STEC 1 to 10 days. The working agent is stated on the definition and changed when the laboratory result arrives.

## 3. Finding cases

- Ask the event organizer for the ticket list, sign-in sheets or the attendee roster, and the caterer for the menu and the serving times.
- Ask the hospital, urgent care clinics and school nurses to report anyone who fits the definition. MCHD sends a clinician health alert by fax and e-mail the same day the definition is adopted.
- Publish a short public notice and open the hotline.
- Ask the event organizer to send a message to every attendee with the questionnaire link. Attendees without e-mail are phoned.

## 4. Interviews and the questionnaire

The standard questionnaire has demographics, event attendance, every dish served (with a yes, no or unsure for each), time of eating, symptoms with onset date and hour, duration, care sought, a specimen question and household members who are also ill. Interviewers are nurses, sanitarians and trained volunteers. Target: first-case interview within 4 hours of the report; 60 percent of known cases within 24 hours. Each interviewer works from the questionnaire script and never discusses a suspected food with the person being interviewed, to avoid bias.

For a cohort (everyone at the event is known), call a sample of well people as well as the ill, so food-specific attack rates can be computed. For an event with a list of 400 or more, MCHD calls every ill person and a random sample of 100 well attendees.

## 5. The line list

The line list is a shared spreadsheet owned by the Epidemiology Lead. Columns: case code, status tier, age, sex, onset date and hour, symptoms, hospitalized (yes/no), specimen collected, laboratory result, items eaten, source of report. No names. Every edit is dated. Counts in any notice come from the line list as of a stated time, and the notice says the time.

## 6. Describe and analyze

- **Epidemic curve.** Cases by onset in 6-hour bins for the first 3 days, then in days. The shape tells whether the exposure was a single meal (one peak within one incubation period) or continuing.
- **Attack rate table.** For each food item, ill and total among those who ate it and those who did not, relative risk and odds ratio. The item with the highest odds ratio and a plausible mechanism is the lead hypothesis. A plausible companion dish (served together with the real vehicle) will often appear nearly as strong; check them with stratified analysis before the Director names a vehicle publicly.
- **Spot map.** Schematic, by where the person lives and their seat or table at the event.

## 7. Specimens and laboratory

Stool specimens go in Cary-Blair medium (kits kept in the MCHD refrigerator, shelf B) and are couriered to the State Hygienic Laboratory on the Tuesday through Friday run or by special courier when the Director authorizes it; laboratory priority is set by the Epidemiology Lead through the state epidemiologist. Turnaround target for a culture-confirmed enteric result is 48 to 72 hours. Isolates are sequenced by the laboratory and compared in the national database. Food samples are collected by Environmental Health with chain of custody forms from leftover food kept in the caterer's or hosts' freezer, never from the buffet in service.

## 8. Decision log

For each decision (adopt or change a case definition, declare an outbreak, request CDC Epi-Aid, issue an exposure notice, recommend an exclusion or closure, set laboratory priority, release data) the log has the time, the person, the facts known at that time, the option chosen and the reason. The log is not edited afterward; a correction is a new line.

## 9. Targets

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| First report to working case definition | 8 hours |
| First report to outbreak declaration | 12 hours once the criteria are met |
| First report to first control measure | 24 hours |
| First report to public notice | 24 hours |
| Ill attendees interviewed | 60 percent by 24 hours |
| Message numbers agree across MCHD, the hospital and the school | Exactly |

# Marlow County Health Department — Foodborne and Enteric Outbreak Response Plan

MCHD-EPI-01 · Marlow County Health Department, Iowa · Revised August 2026

## 1. Purpose and scope

This plan describes how the Marlow County Health Department (MCHD) detects, investigates and controls clusters of gastrointestinal illness that may share a common source: a meal, an event, a restaurant, a child care center or a water supply. It applies to every report received by MCHD from a clinician, a laboratory, a school, an employer or a resident. It does not replace the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services (Iowa HHS) reporting rules; the state rules govern whenever they differ from this plan.

Marlow County (population 38,600; county seat Fenwick) is a fictional county in central Iowa. MCHD has 31 staff and a budget that does not allow a standing outbreak team, so the plan relies on cross-assignment from nursing, environmental health and administration.

## 3. Detection

Reports reach MCHD through four routes: the Iowa disease surveillance system (laboratory reports of Salmonella, Shiga toxin-producing E. coli, Shigella, Campylobacter, Listeria and others), a phone call from an infection preventionist or clinician, a complaint to the environmental health line, and an absentee spike reported by a school nurse or an employer. A single report of a reportable enteric pathogen is investigated as a routine case. Two or more people with similar illness who ate at the same place or event are treated as a possible cluster, and the Epidemiology Lead is told the same day.

## 4. Levels of response

- **Level 1, routine.** Single cases. Interview within one working day, exclusion counseling for food handlers, child care and health care workers.
- **Level 2, cluster.** Two to nine linked cases, or any linked case in a high-risk setting. The Epidemiology Lead opens an investigation file, assigns an interviewer, and notifies the state epidemiologist by phone.
- **Level 3, outbreak.** Ten or more linked cases, any hospitalized or fatal linked case, a vehicle not yet identified with continuing exposure, or multi-jurisdiction spread. The Director declares an outbreak, activates the call-back list and informs the Board of Health chair and the county administrator.

The Director declares an outbreak when the number of cases exceeds what is expected for the place and time and the cases are linked by a common exposure, and records the basis, the count so far and the time of declaration.

## 5. First four hours

1. Log the first report: time received, who reported, what they said, callback number.
2. Epidemiology Lead drafts a working case definition with person, place, time and clinical criteria (see MCHD-EPI-02), and the Director approves it. Target: within 8 hours of the first report.
3. Start the line list. Every case gets a code, never a name, in the shared sheet; the key linking codes to names is kept by the Nurse Manager only.
4. Alert the receiving hospital infection preventionist, the urgent care clinics and the school nurses that the definition is in use, and ask for stool specimens from anyone who fits it.
5. Environmental Health pulls the permit and inspection history of every food business or event that could be the source and phones the operator. If the source is still serving, the Environmental Health Supervisor may place a voluntary hold on suspect food; an embargo or closure order requires the Director's signature.
6. Call the state epidemiologist on duty and the State Hygienic Laboratory courier line to arrange pickup of specimens and any food samples.

## 6. Escalation to the state and CDC

Iowa HHS is the point of contact for CDC. MCHD requests CDC Epi-Aid only through the state epidemiologist, and only when local and state capacity cannot do the interviews, the typing or the analysis in time, or when cases are in more than one state. A request names why capacity is not enough, the support needed (field team, laboratory typing, analytic support or communications support) and the time of the request. The State Hygienic Laboratory at the University of Iowa runs whole genome sequencing of isolates and uploads to the national network, so isolates must be forwarded within 2 working days of culture confirmation.

When the suspect food is meat, poultry or egg products from a federally inspected plant, the state epidemiologist informs USDA FSIS; for other foods, FDA. MCHD does not contact the plant or the supplier directly about a trace-back unless the state asks it to.

## 7. Control measures

- **Exclusion.** Workers in food handling, child care, health care and patient care who have Salmonella, Shigella, STEC or norovirus are excluded until released under the state rule: symptom-free for the required period and, for STEC, Shigella and Typhoid, two negative specimens.
- **Embargo or closure.** An operator whose food is implicated may be asked to stop service voluntarily; the Environmental Health Supervisor documents the request and the reply time.
- **Public notice.** When the vehicle is plausibly still available or when people who ate it need to seek care, MCHD issues an exposure notice naming the place, the dates and times, the symptoms and what to do.
- **School and employer letters.** Sent when students or staff attended the event. The letter carries the same numbers as the press notice.

## 8. Stand-down

An outbreak is closed when no new cases have onset after two maximum incubation periods past the last exposure, the source is controlled and the state concurs. The Epidemiology Lead writes the investigation report within 30 days, including the epidemic curve by onset date, the attack rates by food item and recommendations, and the Director presents it to the Board of Health.

## 9. Records

Keep the call log, the questionnaire forms, the line list with change history, the laboratory slips, every notice with its time of issue, and the decision log (who decided what, when and why). Records are retained for the period set by Iowa public health record rules.

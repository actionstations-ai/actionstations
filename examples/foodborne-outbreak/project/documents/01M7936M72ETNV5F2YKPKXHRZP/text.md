# Marlow County — Profile, Systems and Partners for Outbreak Response

Supporting description for MCHD-EPI-01 and MCHD-EPI-02 · Marlow County Health Department

## 1. The county

Marlow County is a fictional rural and small-city county in central Iowa with 38,600 residents. Fenwick (population 14,200) is the county seat; the other towns are Dunmore (3,100), Kessler (1,900) and Ostrander (820). Farm families, a regional food processor, a small state university branch campus and a hospital employ most people. Fall brings county fairs, harvest dinners and school homecoming events, which is when large community meals concentrate.

## 2. MCHD facilities and systems

- **Office:** MCHD, second floor of the Marlow County Services Building, Fenwick. Main line 555-0100; fax 555-0199; hotline for outbreaks 555-0150 (six lines, staffed 07:00 to 21:00).
- **Disease surveillance:** the Iowa state disease surveillance system takes electronic laboratory reports; MCHD staff check the queue at 08:00 and 15:00 each working day, and the Epidemiology Lead is paged for any cluster flag.
- **Line list and notices:** a shared spreadsheet and a document library on the MCHD file server; the notice templates (exposure notice, school letter, clinician health alert, hotline script) are pre-approved by the Director apart from the figures.
- **Health alert distribution:** fax and e-mail list of 212 clinicians, three urgent care clinics, two long-term care facilities and the school nurses; a text-message tree for staff.
- **Laboratory:** none in the county. Stool kits and the courier schedule are described in MCHD-EPI-02. Marlow Regional Medical Center (MRMC) runs bacterial cultures and a gastrointestinal PCR panel, and sends positives to the State Hygienic Laboratory.

## 3. Partners

**Marlow Regional Medical Center.** 62 beds, an emergency department that sees about 38 patients on an average day, and an infection prevention program led by Sunita Reddy, RN, CIC (555-0171). The infection preventionist reports clusters to MCHD and coordinates hospital statements so numbers agree with the health department's. The hospital communications manager is Kyle Brandt (555-0173).

**Fenwick Community School District.** Four schools, 2,350 students. The district nurse coordinator is Margo Teller, RN (555-0183); the superintendent is Dr. Anselm Voss. About 30 district staff and 140 students attended the event below; the district sends letters only after MCHD supplies the approved text.

**Heritage Days Committee.** A nonprofit that organizes the Marlow Heritage Days weekend each October. Its chair is Hollis Bagley (555-0191). The committee holds the ticket list for the community supper, the biggest event of the weekend, and cooperates with MCHD.

**Prairie Table Catering.** A licensed caterer in Fenwick (license held under the Iowa food establishment rules, inspected by the Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing under a contract with MCHD's Environmental Health) that cooked and served the supper for the committee with two subcontracted vendors for desserts and a salad. Owner: Delphine Okonkwo (555-0166).

**State and federal.** The state epidemiologist's duty line at Iowa HHS; the State Hygienic Laboratory at the University of Iowa; CDC (Epi-Aid through the state); the FDA and USDA FSIS (food traceback through the state); and the neighboring public health departments in Garrison and Pell counties, which share a mutual aid agreement under which each lends two nurses for interviews.

**The press.** Fenwick has a weekly newspaper and a small radio station; regional television visits when hospitalizations are reported. MCHD names no business until the Director authorizes it.

## 4. The event used in the exercise record

Marlow Heritage Days Community Supper, Saturday October 10, 2026, Fenwick Fairgrounds Exhibition Hall, with service from 17:00 to 19:30. About 412 people attended; the menu was roast pork loin, chicken thighs, mashed potatoes, a coleslaw made with a house dressing, a pasta salad, green beans, rolls, a fruit cobbler and a sheet cake. Prairie Table prepared the meats and sides; a Dunmore baker supplied the cake; a Kessler produce vendor supplied the coleslaw and pasta salad.

## 5. Staffing and surge

MCHD can field six interviewers on the first day (four nurses, the sanitarian staff of two), eight when the Garrison and Pell nurses join, and twelve when the Iowa HHS regional epidemiologist adds trained volunteers. The hotline needs two staff per shift. The Epidemiology Lead is the single point for the line list; a second analyst, Dev Kulkarni, MPH (555-0125), is trained as backup. Weekend coverage: Epidemiology Lead and one nurse on call; the Director is reachable at all times.

## 6. Reporting clocks

Reportable enteric diseases are reported by laboratories and clinicians to the state within the time set in the Iowa reporting rules; MCHD records the time it learns of each case. A foodborne outbreak is reportable to the state on suspicion, by phone, the same day; MCHD does not wait for laboratory confirmation. Health care workers and food handlers with a confirmed enteric pathogen are excluded as described in MCHD-EPI-01.

## 7. Known gaps

The after-hours duty line has one phone and no text capability. The case-sensitive spreadsheet breaks when two people edit at once, so only the Epidemiology Lead and the backup edit. The school letter template does not have a Spanish version; 11 percent of Fenwick students come from Spanish-speaking households, and Spanish translation takes four hours from the district office. These gaps are acknowledged in the 2026 after-action improvement plan.

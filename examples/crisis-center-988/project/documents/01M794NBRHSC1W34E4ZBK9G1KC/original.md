# Great Plains Crisis Network — Contact Routing and Backup Requests

GPCN-OPS-02 · Operations and Technology · Revision 3, 2026

## 1. Contact channels

GPCN receives three kinds of contact. Calls arrive from 988 through the telephony platform. Texts and chats arrive in the web-based contact platform, which queues them and shows a counselor a conversation. Each channel has its own queue and its own service level.

| Channel | Queue name | Who staffs it |
|---|---|---|
| Calls (in-state area codes 402, 531, 308) | NE-VOICE | Counselors, then supervisors |
| Text | NE-TEXT | Counselors and trained administrative staff |
| Chat | NE-CHAT | Counselors |

## 2. How 988 routes a call

A caller's area code decides the first center. If GPCN does not answer within a threshold set by the network administrator, the call overflows to a national backup route. During surge GPCN asks the network administrator to increase the overflow share, for example 40 percent of in-state calls routed to backup centers in other states. The network operations desk (the administrator's 24-hour line, 402-555-0180 in this exercise) confirms by telephone and by e-mail; the change usually takes 10 to 15 minutes to be active. The state 988 coordinator, Hannah Lindqvist at the Division of Behavioral Health (ext. 4-0120 on the state line), is told of every request within 30 minutes.

Texts and chats have separate backup arrangements. The state backup center, a neighboring state's center, can take a share of texts and chats through the platform's shared queue once its supervisors are given a login.

## 3. Queue monitoring

The Operations wall board shows, per queue: contacts waiting, the oldest wait in minutes, contacts answered within 120 seconds over the last 15 minutes, abandoned contacts, counselors logged in and counselors on contacts. Colors always carry words: OK, WATCH, SURGE, CRITICAL. The board refreshes every 60 seconds. When the board and the platform's own report disagree, the platform report is used and the Technology Manager is told.

Thresholds for the board:

- WATCH: oldest wait over 5 minutes or answer rate under 85 percent.
- SURGE: oldest wait over 10 minutes or answer rate under 80 percent for two intervals.
- CRITICAL: oldest wait over 20 minutes or answer rate under 70 percent for 30 minutes.

## 4. Requesting backup

1. The surge commander chooses the contact type and the share.
2. The commander records the request in the surge log: party, contact type, time requested, time support is to begin.
3. The commander calls the network operations desk. For texts and chats, they call the backup center's supervisor on the contact sheet.
4. The Technology Manager makes the platform changes and confirms by chat in the operations channel.
5. The commander tells the state coordinator and the Executive Director.
6. The commander sets a reminder to review the request every 30 minutes, and to ask for the backup to end when the board has been OK for an hour.

Backup centers answer in their own protocols. GPCN does not pass them records about a caller, and a backup center never calls back a GPCN caller except through the shared system.

## 5. Callbacks and follow-up

GPCN offers follow-up contacts to some people. During Level 2 and above, follow-up for non-imminent risk is paused; follow-up after an active rescue is never paused. A list of paused callbacks is kept so that nobody is lost, and is worked first when the board returns to OK.

## 6. Staffing moves

- Off-duty call-in list: 38 counselors; the workforce coordinator calls in the order of fairness rota, not by favorites.
- Overflow to supervisors: each supervisor takes calls while the other keeps the floor.
- Trained administrative staff: six people with the annual textline course; they take texts only.
- Remote counselors: 14 hold remote credentials; the Technology Manager activates a login in 5 minutes.
- Volunteers: GPCN does not use untrained volunteers on live contacts.

## 7. Failure modes

- **Platform lag.** Texts show as delivered late. The Technology Manager contacts the vendor's support line and posts the status.
- **Carrier problem.** Calls drop. The secondary carrier route is activated.
- **Backup center overloaded.** A national event may exhaust backups. The network administrator then rebalances, and GPCN tells its supervisors to expect their own volume back.

## 8. Records

The surge log and the board's exported series are kept for the after-action review. They record no caller information. Contacts are never described in the log other than by counts.

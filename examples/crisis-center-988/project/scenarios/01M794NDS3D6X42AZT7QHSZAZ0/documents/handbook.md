Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Great Plains Crisis Network — contact floor and executive command room · Duration: 4 Hours

### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise for the Great Plains Crisis Network (GPCN) leadership and surge team in Lincoln. Nobody touches a live system, and no real call, text or chat is handled. Players talk through what they would decide, who they would call and what they would write. You run the clock, deliver the injects and play every outside party. The exercise covers 4 hours of scenario time, from 13:00 to 17:00 on Thursday, 12 March 2026, in a single room session.

The incident falls in the Mass gathering / crowd incident category. A fictional fatal school-bus collision near Ellery Ridge pulls a whole region together. Families gather in the high school gym, a community vigil is being planned, and a crowd builds online. The injects show what that does to a 988 center. They include platform interval reports that run ahead of a lagging wall board, a call-in list that is not as ready as the tool says, "988 is down" rumors, a regional TV station asking for call counts and caller stories, backup centers that are themselves under strain, a state coordinator who wants written status, a school superintendent and a vigil organizer who want counselors on site, and counselors who reach their limit. **Phase 1, The Queue Moves First (0:00–1:00, 13:00–14:00):** texts climb, then calls, and players must trust the platform report over the board and declare Level 1 and Level 2. **Phase 2, Two Intervals Behind (1:00–2:00, 14:00–15:00):** players ask for out-of-state call routing and a state backup text share, publish the Level 2 post on time, and handle the first rumor and media pressure. **Phase 3, Fail Over or Hold (2:00–3:00, 15:00–16:00):** after Level 3, with backup centers stretched, the Technology Manager and Executive Director decide whether to fail the lagging text queue over to the secondary route. **Phase 4, Clearing the Queue (3:00–4:00, 16:00–17:00):** players wind the surge down in order, look after counselors, close notifications and hand off to the night shift.

Evaluators should watch for these things. First, do players act on the platform's own report when the board disagrees (GPCN-OPS-02 section 3)? Second, do they request backup before any queue has run 60 minutes over threshold, and log each request with party, contact type and start time? Third, do they meet the deadlines in the public messaging plan section 4: the post within 30 minutes of Level 2, the state and partner briefing within 90 minutes, and the media statement within 3 hours of Level 3? Fourth, do they refuse caller stories and correct rumors once, with the facts (public messaging plan section 5)? Fifth, do they keep untrained volunteers off live contacts (section 6), remember that Spanish-language contacts cannot be rerouted to backup, and keep the surge log to counts only (section 8)? Finally, do they notice, rotate and support counselors? Record the time each decision is made, not just whether it was made.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to declare Level 2 and Level 3 from the platform's own report rather than the lagging operations board, and to request out-of-state call routing and state backup for texts and chats before any queue has run 60 minutes over its threshold, with each request recorded in the surge log.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to spot the 15:30 text-queue delivery lag on the shared text and chat platform, decide whether and when to fail over, and confirm routing changes in the operations channel while backup centers are also under strain.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to post the Level 2 public message within 30 minutes, correct the 'lines are down' rumor once with facts and every way to reach help, refuse the regional TV station's request for caller stories, and brief the state 988 coordinator, all under safe messaging review.
- **Staff Wellbeing:** Demonstrate the ability to bring in counselors from the call-in list and the 14:00 evening shift while letting the counselor with ties to Ellery Ridge step off the queue and giving the counselor at 5 hours a break, keeping the floor staffed without wearing out the counselors on it.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Arrange the tables in a U-shape to stand in for the GPCN contact floor. The open end faces the projector screen, which acts as the operations wall board. The controller/SimCell table sits at the open end beside the screen, so you can hand over cards and take "phone calls" face to face.
- **Raised desk (head of the U):** Team C, Operations Shift Supervisor / Surge Commander, and Team E, Technology and Telephony Manager, sit side by side. They share the "supervisor desk" view of the platform screenshots, as in the real room.
- **Left arm of the U:** Team D, Workforce Coordinator, with the paper call-in binder prop and the workforce tool printout. Team B, Director of Clinical Operations, sits next to Team D, because the callback, surge script and staffing questions cross between them.
- **Right arm of the U (executive command line):** Team A, Executive Director. Leave one empty chair for "the state 988 coordinator on the line" so you can sit there when you play the coordinator.
- **Rear table, behind the U and facing away from the screen:** Team F, Communications Lead. This placement is deliberate. On the real day, communications is not on the floor and cannot read the board. Players have to walk over to the raised desk for current waits, level and backup status before they draft anything. This brings out stale numbers, missing safe messaging review and missed deadlines, which are exactly the gaps this scenario tests. Do not hand Team F the screen injects directly unless the inject is addressed to them.
- **Wellbeing corner:** Place two chairs near the exit for Dr. Anneliese Park and anyone who steps out. Keep this area outside the exercise. Nothing that happens there is evaluated.
- **Evaluator positions:** Put one evaluator behind the raised desk (Teams C/E) and one between the executive line and the rear communications table (Teams A/F), where they can see the walk-overs. A third evaluator floats between Teams B and D.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and screen for main-screen injects: the platform interval reports, the wall board stills, social posts and the news chyron. Each one must show EXERCISE.
- A whiteboard set up as the ICS-201, with columns for current level and time declared, backup requests (party, contact type, requested, live), staffing on floor, notification deadlines, and open decisions. A second board or flip chart serves as the surge log, with counts only.
- A printed inject deck in MSEL order, one card per inject, with the addressee printed on the card. Also bring spare blank "This just came in" cards for improvised calls.
- A wall clock or on-screen clock showing scenario time in 24-hour format, which you reset at each time jump.
- Optional: simulated phones or radios (two handsets or walkie-talkies) so players can "call" the SimCell. All on-screen helpline numbers are fictional 555 numbers.

**Materials for players:** name tents by team; printed role cards; GPCN-OPS-02 sections 3, 6, 7 and 8 (levels, staffing moves, failure modes, records); the public messaging plan sections 4 and 5; the outside parties list; a blank surge log sheet; blank message forms marked EXERCISE; pens.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**The SimCell role.** You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the network desk," you answer as the 988 network operations desk analyst. If they say "I e-mail the state coordinator," you are the coordinator reading it. You also play the state backup center supervisor, the text platform vendor, Platte Valley 8 News, the NEMA liaison, the superintendent, the State Patrol and anyone posting online. Answer in character, in that party's own interest, and in real time. If players do not call someone, that party does not learn anything. A party they never call may call them later, and less patiently.

**Do not make it easy.** Outside parties want things from GPCN, and they do not volunteer help. For example, when the Surge Commander asks for 40 percent of in-state calls to go to backup (I-07), the network desk analyst does not just say "done." The analyst asks for the state coordinator's confirmation, warns that the roster is busy, and at I-12 reports that only about 29 percent was actually placed. When the Technology Manager calls the text platform vendor at 15:30 (I-14), the vendor gives no fix time. Support asks for the account ID and a ticket number and will not say whether failing over is safe, because that decision belongs to GPCN. Players should feel the cost of every choice. Do not hint at the right answer. If they are stuck, ask in character: "What do you need from me?"

**Managing time.** Keep to the MSEL timings. If a team is still talking a decision through when the next inject is due, deliver the inject anyway. Pressure is part of the design. To move the clock, stand up, raise a hand and read: "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. It is now 14:30. Level 2 has been in place since 13:50. The evening shift is on the floor, the call share request is with the network desk, and the text queue has been over threshold for 70 minutes. Update your ICS-201 to 14:30. RESUME." Change the times and facts to fit the time jump card (I-05) and reset the wall clock. If the room falls far behind, compress Phase 4 rather than Phase 3. The fail-over decision (I-14) must happen.

**Handling artificialities.** If a student says "I wouldn't use that system," "we'd just look at the live dashboard," or "in real life the vendor would already know," reply: "Noted. Put it on the parking-lot sheet for the hot wash. For today, the system works as described on your card. What do you do next?" Do not argue about tools. If the point is a real gap in GPCN's setup, the evaluator records it as an observation.

**Delivering injects.** Do not explain. Say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." Hand the card to the named addressee only, or put it on the screen. Then step back. If a player asks "What does this mean?", answer only as the party who sent it, or say "That's what you have." Read phone calls aloud in character. Hand e-mails and texts over on paper. For team F at the rear table, walk the card over. Do not shout it across the room.

**Content and safety conventions.** The exercise includes fictional material about the deaths of young people. Never add method, place or timing detail, and never improvise a caller's story. If a player asks "What did the caller say?", answer: "You have counts only. The log records no caller information." Any public message players draft that shows a helpline number must use a fictional 555 number and be marked EXERCISE.

**"This is an exercise."** Every inject, call and message is opened or closed with "This is an exercise," and every printed or projected item shows EXERCISE. If players make a real call or send a real e-mail or post by mistake, stop them and fix it at once.

**"REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD."** If anyone has a real emergency, including a real 988 surge, a real system problem at GPCN, a medical problem in the room, or a participant in distress, say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD" and give the reason. All play stops immediately and real procedures take over. Resume only when the lead controller says so. If two or more people leave the room for wellbeing reasons, stop the exercise as the STARTEX card says.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well (3-Up) and three things that broke or failed (3-Down) during the surge. Go around the room by team (supervisor desk, technology, communications, workforce/wellbeing) and write each item on the whiteboard in two columns so the patterns are clear before anyone leaves.

**Key Discussion Questions for Two Intervals Behind:**
- **Trust:** At 13:20 (I-01), the wall board showed TEXTS OK, but the platform report already showed WATCH with a 6:00 oldest wait, and a counselor was telling you texts were stacking up. By 14:10 (I-06), Marcus said the board was two intervals behind and the text queue had been over threshold for 50 minutes. When did you stop trusting the board and switch to the platform report, as Coordination Plan section 3 requires? When did you declare Level 2 and Level 3? Was the backup request for texts and calls logged before any queue reached 60 minutes over threshold? If it wasn't, what would have helped you decide sooner? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Call:** At 15:30 (I-14), the engineer gave you five minutes to choose between failing texts over to a secondary route that had never been used live for texts and holding until the vendor update at 16:00, while texters waited where you couldn't see them and backup had no room left. Did operations understand the risk the engineer described, including texts sitting unseen or arriving twice? Did the technical team understand what holding would mean for someone at risk in the queue? What did you decide, how did you confirm the routing change in the operations channel, and how did you account for the texts that were in transit? _(Objective: Technical Analysis)_
- **The Rumor:** At 13:36 (I-03), a post saying '988 shut down' had 64 shares within 20 minutes, and staff asked 'Do we reply?' Had the Level 2 public message already gone out within 30 minutes, as Public Communications Plan section 4 requires? Did you correct the rumor once, with facts and every way to reach help, and did the correction pass safe messaging review? Then think about the regional TV request for caller stories and the briefing to the state 988 coordinator: what did you tell the public and your partners before you knew the actual queue status, and would that statement have needed a correction in the 14-day review? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The People:** At 13:30 (I-02), the workforce tool listed all 38 call-in names as 'available,' but 11 couldn't be reached, and the corrected numbers existed only in a paper binder. The evening shift lead was also offering to bring people in early. At 15:13 (I-12b), the superintendent asked for four or five counselors on-site that night and said he would tell parents you were coming. How did you staff the floor and still let the counselor with ties to Ellery Ridge step off the queue and give the counselor at 5 hours a break? Who did you call back about the high school request, and did you coordinate with the state disaster team running the vigil or simply let the superintendent's deadline decide for you? _(Objective: Staff Wellbeing)_
- **The Gap:** At 13:54 (I-04b), the operations channel asked which of the 12 scheduled callbacks pause at Level 2, including the 2 imminent-risk follow-ups. It also asked whether the 14:00 shift counts as call-in staff. Did you have a written answer, or did someone make it up on the spot? What should go into the after-action review within 7 days (Coordination Plan section 8) so the next supervisor doesn't face the same gap: time from trigger to action, which backup was used, and what counselors said helped? _(Objective: Decision Making)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Crisis line operations**: Crisis center expertise for 988 and other crisis lines: the daily contact report, network surge and backup notices, queue and staffing boards, supervisor escalation, de-identified contact summaries and the state coordinator briefing, with the rerouting rules and service measures. Works with the behavioral health safeguards toolkit.
  - Data sources: lifeline-988-gao
- **Behavioral health safeguards**: Rules and handouts for exercises that touch suicide, self-harm, abuse or the loss of a child: safe messaging for generated content, an EXERCISE banner on anything that shows a real helpline number, a content notice at the briefing, a facilitator wellbeing check with a stop-and-step-out rule, and psychological first aid. It runs under the crisis line, mass care and reunification toolkits and any other domain that needs it. It is an exercise aid, not clinical guidance.

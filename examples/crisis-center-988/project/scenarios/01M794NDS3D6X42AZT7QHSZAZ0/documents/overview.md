# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Two Intervals Behind** (988 Surge, Backup Routing & Safe Messaging)
Location: Great Plains Crisis Network — contact floor and executive command room · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Executive Director, Director of Clinical Operations, Operations Shift Supervisor (Surge Commander), Workforce Coordinator, Technology and Telephony Manager, and Communications Lead of the Great Plains Crisis Network. The SimCell and facilitator play the 988 network administrator, the state 988 coordinator, the backup centers, reporters, the school superintendent, the disaster behavioral health team and the wellbeing contact.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Deciding early when the queue moves faster than the dashboard, asking for backup in time, keeping public trust with safe messaging, and looking after the counselors who carry the surge.

**The Threat:** At 12:10 on Thursday, 12 March 2026, before STARTEX, the Nebraska State Patrol reported a fictional fatal collision between a school bus and a truck on a county road near the fictional town of Ellery Ridge, with students among those who died and were hurt. By 12:50 the news had spread on local media and social posts. From 13:00, contacts to GPCN's 988 queues rise sharply. Texts climb first, then calls, so the center moves from Level 1 to Level 3 under its surge levels. Meanwhile, a viral post falsely claims the lines are down, the operations board runs two 15-minute intervals behind the platform, and at 15:30 the hosted text-queue platform starts to lag.

**The Operational Impact:** Callers, texters and chatters from across the region wait longer and longer. At 13:20 text volume doubles and the oldest text waits 6:00 (Level 1). By 13:50 calls follow: the call answer rate is under 80 percent for two consecutive 15-minute intervals and the oldest wait is over 10:00 (Level 2). By 14:45 the answer rate is under 70 percent and the oldest text has waited 20:00 (Level 3). The 16 counselors on the floor (9 on calls, 7 on texts and chats) and the two shift supervisors see queues the operations board does not yet show, because the board looks better than the platform. Supervisors start answering calls, and the 6 trained administrative staff take texts after a refresher script. Non-imminent callbacks are paused. The Workforce Coordinator works through a call-in list of 38 names, 11 of which were unreachable at the last test, while the evening shift of 14 counselors arrives at 14:00. One counselor who grew up near Ellery Ridge asks to step off the queue, and another reaches 5 hours without a break. Requests for 40 percent of in-state calls to be routed to out-of-state centers, and for the state backup center to share texts, take 10 to 15 minutes to become active. The network administrator then warns that backup centers elsewhere are also stressed. At 15:30 texts start showing as delivered late, and the Technology Manager must decide whether to fail over. The executive command line opens. The Communications Lead handles the "lines are down" rumor and a regional TV station that wants call counts and caller stories, which GPCN refuses. The state 988 coordinator asks for status, a school superintendent asks for counselor visits that GPCN does not provide in person, and the disaster behavioral health team prepares a community vigil.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to declare Level 2 and Level 3 from the platform's own report rather than the lagging operations board, and to request out-of-state call routing and state backup for texts and chats before any queue has run 60 minutes over its threshold, with each request recorded in the surge log.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to spot the 15:30 text-queue delivery lag on the shared text and chat platform, decide whether and when to fail over, and confirm routing changes in the operations channel while backup centers are also under strain.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to post the Level 2 public message within 30 minutes, correct the 'lines are down' rumor once with facts and every way to reach help, refuse the regional TV station's request for caller stories, and brief the state 988 coordinator, all under safe messaging review.
- **Staff Wellbeing:** Demonstrate the ability to bring in counselors from the call-in list and the 14:00 evening shift while letting the counselor with ties to Ellery Ridge step off the queue and giving the counselor at 5 hours a break, keeping the floor staffed without wearing out the counselors on it.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Executive Director (ED):** Owns level declarations, the executive command line, the decision to escalate to the state, and is the only GPCN spokesperson to the media.
- **Director of Clinical Operations (DCO):** Manages clinical quality during the surge, including surge scripts, paused callbacks, high-risk consults and safe messaging review of every public statement.
- **Operations Shift Supervisor (Surge Commander) (SC):** Manages the contact floor as surge commander, setting queue priorities, overflow to supervisors and trained staff, and backup requests logged with party, contact type and start time.
- **Workforce Coordinator (WFC):** Owns the call-in list, schedules and the forecast in the workforce management tool and paper binder, aiming to get 6 extra counselors onto the floor within 90 minutes.
- **Technology Manager (Technology and Telephony) (TM):** Manages the telephony platform, the shared text and chat platform, the operations board and the backup routing changes, and owns the failover decision.
- **Communications Lead (COM):** Manages the GPCN website and social account, reporter requests, rumor control and partner and staff updates on the timelines in the public messaging plan.

After Level 3, with backup centers also stressed, the Technology Manager and Executive Director must decide whether and when to fail the lagging text queue over, knowing both choices cost contacts. They must keep the command structure and partners aligned while doing it.

Beats: Level 3 context (from 14:45): the answer rate is under 70 percent and the oldest text has waited 20:00, which is CRITICAL.

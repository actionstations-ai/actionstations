# Hot Wash Guide
## Two Intervals Behind

### Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well (3-Up) and three things that broke or failed (3-Down) during the surge. Go around the room by team (supervisor desk, technology, communications, workforce/wellbeing) and write each item on the whiteboard in two columns so the patterns are clear before anyone leaves.

**Key Discussion Questions for Two Intervals Behind:**
- **Trust:** At 13:20 (I-01), the wall board showed TEXTS OK, but the platform report already showed WATCH with a 6:00 oldest wait, and a counselor was telling you texts were stacking up. By 14:10 (I-06), Marcus said the board was two intervals behind and the text queue had been over threshold for 50 minutes. When did you stop trusting the board and switch to the platform report, as Coordination Plan section 3 requires? When did you declare Level 2 and Level 3? Was the backup request for texts and calls logged before any queue reached 60 minutes over threshold? If it wasn't, what would have helped you decide sooner? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Call:** At 15:30 (I-14), the engineer gave you five minutes to choose between failing texts over to a secondary route that had never been used live for texts and holding until the vendor update at 16:00, while texters waited where you couldn't see them and backup had no room left. Did operations understand the risk the engineer described, including texts sitting unseen or arriving twice? Did the technical team understand what holding would mean for someone at risk in the queue? What did you decide, how did you confirm the routing change in the operations channel, and how did you account for the texts that were in transit? _(Objective: Technical Analysis)_
- **The Rumor:** At 13:36 (I-03), a post saying '988 shut down' had 64 shares within 20 minutes, and staff asked 'Do we reply?' Had the Level 2 public message already gone out within 30 minutes, as Public Communications Plan section 4 requires? Did you correct the rumor once, with facts and every way to reach help, and did the correction pass safe messaging review? Then think about the regional TV request for caller stories and the briefing to the state 988 coordinator: what did you tell the public and your partners before you knew the actual queue status, and would that statement have needed a correction in the 14-day review? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The People:** At 13:30 (I-02), the workforce tool listed all 38 call-in names as 'available,' but 11 couldn't be reached, and the corrected numbers existed only in a paper binder. The evening shift lead was also offering to bring people in early. At 15:13 (I-12b), the superintendent asked for four or five counselors on-site that night and said he would tell parents you were coming. How did you staff the floor and still let the counselor with ties to Ellery Ridge step off the queue and give the counselor at 5 hours a break? Who did you call back about the high school request, and did you coordinate with the state disaster team running the vigil or simply let the superintendent's deadline decide for you? _(Objective: Staff Wellbeing)_
- **The Gap:** At 13:54 (I-04b), the operations channel asked which of the 12 scheduled callbacks pause at Level 2, including the 2 imminent-risk follow-ups. It also asked whether the 14:00 shift counts as call-in staff. Did you have a written answer, or did someone make it up on the spot? What should go into the after-action review within 7 days (Coordination Plan section 8) so the next supervisor doesn't face the same gap: time from trigger to action, which backup was used, and what counselors said helped? _(Objective: Decision Making)_

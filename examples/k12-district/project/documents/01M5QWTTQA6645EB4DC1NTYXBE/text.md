## SECTION 1: PURPOSE, SCOPE AND PLANNING BASIS

### 1.1 Purpose

This plan tells Halverson Unified School District (HUSD) staff what to do before, during and after an emergency that affects a school, a bus, a district facility or the systems the district runs on. It is built so that the first adult on scene can act without waiting for permission, and so that the district can tell families what is happening faster than rumor does.

### 1.2 Scope

The plan covers the fourteen schools, the District Service Center, the Transportation Center, the Nutrition Services Central Kitchen, district-sponsored activities on and off campus, and every student on a district bus. Each school keeps a site EOP built on this plan; site plans carry the building map with numbered exterior doors, the staff assignment list and the classroom go-kit inventory.

### 1.3 Planning Basis

The plan follows the six-step collaborative planning process and the basic-plan, functional-annex and threat- and hazard-specific-annex structure of the federal *Guide for Developing High-Quality School Emergency Operations Plans* (ED, HHS, DHS, DOJ, 2013), and Oregon requirements for school emergency plans and drills. It was developed with Halverson Police Department, Brandt County Sheriff's Office, Halverson Fire & Rescue, Brandt County Emergency Management and Brandt County Public Health, and was reviewed by the Oregon Department of Education school safety staff in 07/2026.

## SECTION 2: SITUATION OVERVIEW

HUSD serves about 9,100 students in kindergarten through grade 12 and employs 1,180 staff in the city of Halverson and the unincorporated east side of Brandt County, Oregon. 46 percent of students qualify for free or reduced-price meals; 14 percent have an IEP or a Section 504 plan; 19 percent of families list a home language other than English (Spanish, Russian, Vietnamese and Marshallese are the largest groups). The schools, their addresses, enrollments and buildings are listed in HUSD-INV-2026 Section 2; the fourteen schools are:

- Elementary (K–5): Alder Creek Elementary School, Birchwood Elementary School, Cascade View Elementary School, Fernhill Elementary School, Juniper Elementary School, Maple Grove Elementary School, Sandpiper Elementary School, Tillman Elementary School, Westbrook Elementary School.
- Middle (6–8): Halverson Middle School, Kestrel Ridge Middle School, North Fork Middle School.
- High (9–12): Halverson High School, Brandt Valley High School.

The district runs 24 regular bus routes and 6 special-education routes from the Transportation Center with 41 buses. The state assessment window for 2026–27 runs in spring; during the window, testing rooms depend on Chromebooks, Wi-Fi and the state testing platform, and an interruption invalidates sessions in progress.

## SECTION 4: CONCEPT OF OPERATIONS AND STANDARD RESPONSE ACTIONS

### 4.1 Standard Response Actions

HUSD uses five plain-language actions. Any staff member may call them; the principal or SRO announces them on the PA, by radio and in the panic-alert app (CENTEGIX badges at every school).

| Action | Means | Who typically calls it |
|---|---|---|
| HOLD | Stay in rooms, clear halls; business as usual inside | Principal (medical, hallway incident) |
| SECURE | Get inside, lock outside doors; teaching continues | Principal, SRO, HPD (threat in the neighborhood, police activity) |
| LOCKDOWN | Locks, lights, out of sight; no one opens the door | Anyone who sees a threat; principal, SRO, HPD |
| EVACUATE | Move to the named location | Principal, fire |
| SHELTER | Hazard and method (earthquake drop/cover/hold; hazmat seal room) | Principal, fire, county EM |

### 4.2 Incident Levels

| Level | Meaning | Example | EOC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One school, handled on site | Student medical emergency, short HOLD | Not activated |
| 2 | One school with outside responders, or a district system degraded | SECURE after a threat, SIS outage during a school day | Partial: IC, PIO, branch director |
| 3 | Several schools or a district-wide system, or a threat requiring closure | Ransomware, lockdown with police response, closure of a school | Full activation at the District Service Center, Room 210 |
| 4 | Community-wide emergency | Earthquake, regional fire | Full EOC; HUSD liaison at the Brandt County EOC |

### 4.3 Decisions Reserved to the Policy Group

1. Closing a school or the district, or early release.
2. Moving state assessment sessions or asking the Oregon Department of Education to reopen a testing window.
3. Any communication with an extortion actor, and any payment question (only with Legal Counsel, the risk pool insurer's breach coach and law enforcement).
4. Notices to families about exposure of student or staff records.

## SECTION 5: DIRECTION, CONTROL AND THE DISTRICT EOC

The district EOC is Room 210 of the District Service Center (1550 Larch Street, Halverson). Alternate EOC: the Transportation Center training room (4400 Industrial Way). During a cyber incident the EOC runs on paper ICS forms, the radio net and the cell phones on the EOC call list (Annex I); the district VoIP phones and Google Workspace may be unavailable. A printed copy of this plan, the contact list and the school rosters exported at 05:00 each school day (Section 6.3) is kept in the EOC go-box.

## SECTION 6: INFORMATION COLLECTION AND SHARING

### 6.1 Sources

Tips arrive through SafeOregon (the state tip line), the HUSD web form, building staff, SROs, Halverson Police and Brandt County 911, parents and the district social-media inboxes. Cyber alerts arrive from the managed detection service (Tallgrass MDR), MS-ISAC and K12 SIX advisories, and vendor notices.

### 6.2 FERPA

Student information may be disclosed to police, fire, health or mental-health partners without consent when knowledge of it is needed to protect the health or safety of the student or others (34 CFR 99.36). Each such disclosure is recorded in the student's record (Form J-6). FERPA has no breach-notice clock; notices for data exposure follow Oregon law and the CIRP.

### 6.3 Offline Rosters

At 05:00 each school day the SIS exports, for each school, the student roster with emergency contacts, authorized pick-up adults, medical alerts and bus assignments to an encrypted USB key in each principal's office safe and to the EOC go-box. During a cyber incident these exports are the source for accountability and reunification.

## SECTION 7: COMMUNICATIONS

Primary family notification is ParentSquare (text, email, voice and app) with templates in Annex C. District radio net: channel 1 District Command, channel 2 Transportation, channel 3 Facilities, one site channel per school. Media staging for incidents at Halverson High School is the Community Pool parking lot on Ninth Avenue; for other schools as named in the site EOP. Local media: Channel 6 and Channel 12 (Portland market stations), the Halverson Herald, and Brandt Valley Radio.

## SECTION 8: TRAINING AND EXERCISES

Drills per Oregon rules and the site EOP calendar (fire, earthquake, lockdown and bus evacuation). One district tabletop each year alternating physical and cyber scenarios; the 2026–27 tabletop is a cyber scenario during the state assessment window to meet the CIRP annual-exercise commitment. After-action reports follow HSEEP.

## SECTION 9: PLAN MAINTENANCE

The Director of School Safety reviews this plan every August and after any Level 3 or 4 incident. Contact lists are refreshed each semester.

## ANNEX C: COMMUNICATIONS AND WARNING (extract)

**C.1 First message within 30 minutes** of a SECURE, LOCKDOWN or district system outage that affects the school day: what happened in one sentence, what students are doing, what parents should do (usually "do not come to the school"), when the next update comes.

**C.2 Data-exposure messages** are drafted with Legal Counsel; they never guess at what was taken and never repeat an extortion actor's claims as fact.

**C.3 Translation:** Spanish, Russian, Vietnamese and Marshallese versions within two hours; ParentSquare auto-translation is a fallback only.

## ANNEX G: SECURITY AND THREAT ASSESSMENT (extract)

1. Any threat (post, message, email, call) goes the same hour to the principal and the SRO; if the threat is specific and imminent, call 911 and SECURE or LOCKDOWN.
2. The threat assessment team (administrator, counselor or psychologist, SRO) meets the same day, following the U.S. Secret Service NTAC model: identify, assess, manage.
3. Emailed or called threats against several schools at once are treated as possible hoaxes but are never ignored; HPD and the FBI Portland Field Office are told, and the Policy Group decides on operations with HPD.
4. Swatting: if 911 dispatches police for a reported shooting and staff report no threat, the school goes to LOCKDOWN anyway until police clear it; the PIO posts a holding statement within 15 minutes.

## ANNEX H: CYBER INCIDENT (summary)

The CIRP (HUSD-CIRP-2026) governs technical response. This annex sets the school-day consequences: paper attendance on Form J-2; nurses use the printed medical-alert list; buses run printed route sheets; state testing pauses on the CTO's word and the Assessment Coordinator notifies the Oregon Department of Education; phones fall back to the cell list; buildings keep HVAC in local mode.

# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Cold Storage** (EHR Storage Failure & Downtime Continuity)
Location: Lakemont Community Hospital, Duluth, St. Louis County, Minnesota · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Administrator on Call / Incident Commander, Clinical Systems Lead, Night House Supervisor / Chief Nursing Officer, Laboratory and Pharmacy Managers, Privacy and Security Officer, Public Information Officer. The SimCell plays the vendors, the HIE, the Minnesota Department of Health, St. Louis County ambulance dispatch, a neighboring hospital, the cyber insurer's breach coach, reporters and families.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Declaring the right downtime level fast when a security cause can't yet be ruled out, keeping patients safe on paper overnight, restoring in a safe order without a chart that looks complete but isn't, and speaking publicly about a failure that people are calling a hack.

**The Threat:** On Tuesday, February 9, 2027, at 02:10, before STARTEX, a storage controller failed at the primary data center and the Lakechart (EHR) database went down. There was no attacker. The IS Help Desk (ext. 5500) took the first calls at 02:14. At 02:25 on-call analyst Ananya Desai confirmed that both the EHR and the interface engine were down and that replication to the Hermantown secondary was 40 minutes behind. With the engine down, ADT, orders, results, medication administration, charges and the outbound public health and HIE feeds are queuing in their source systems and can only be held for up to 8 hours. At 03:30 the managed security provider reports an unrelated odd login on a service account. Until 06:15 it is unclear whether this is Level 3 or Level 4.

**The Operational Impact:** The exercise starts at 02:40 on a cold night. 98 inpatients are in house, 22 patients are in the ED and a woman is in active labor. Clinicians see spinning Lakechart screens. The read-only downtime viewer is 3 hours 20 minutes old, so every downtime report is stale by its as-of time. Charge nurses wait for the overhead page and the House Supervisor's call, and slow units must be chased to report when they went on procedures. Unit clerks and registration staff switch to paper. In the ED, two unidentified trauma patients are registered as ZZ-Trauma Tuesday 1 and 2, and on the medical-surgical unit two patients named Carlson may share one record (an overlay). Laboratory technologists keep the analyzers running, print results and phone them. A critical potassium of 6.9 and a positive blood culture each need a read-back and a log entry within 60 minutes. Pharmacy technologists face override pulls from the automated dispensing cabinets and a controlled-substance count that doesn't match. Biomedical technicians get an ICU request to isolate the pump and monitor network. EHR analysts and interface analysts wait 55 minutes for an EHR vendor callback that was promised in 30, and about 2 hours for the interface engine vendor's on-call engineer. At 06:00 they promote the standby engine and must decide, class by class, whether to replay, hold or discard 31,000 queued messages, including stale orders for discharged patients. The ED medical director asks to open the EHR before the lab interface has been validated. The exercise ends before the roughly 14:00 restore of the EHR; the hourly reconciliation counts and duplicate tracking by the HIM Director that follow are covered in the hot wash. Throughout, families ask what is happening at the bedside. Staff, a local TV reporter and a post in a community Facebook group say the hospital has been hacked, and the PIO has to keep every statement consistent with what is actually known.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to declare and later revise the Lakechart downtime level, choosing between Level 3 and Level 4 while the 03:30 service-account login is unexplained, and to decide within 2 hours, with the ED Medical Director, whether to divert elective admissions or ambulances.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to put every unit on paper downtime procedures within 60 minutes of the overhead page and to keep patients safe on paper, including phoning the critical potassium of 6.9 and the positive blood culture with read-back and a Critical Result Call Log entry within 60 minutes, and holding the ZZ-Trauma and Carlson identities apart.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to sequence the restore and decide, message class by message class, whether to replay, hold or discard the 31,000 queued messages under IS-14-C section 2 (ADT first, decisions logged), and to refuse to open Lakechart to clinicians before the lab results interface is validated.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to issue staff, family and media statements that are cleared by the Incident Commander and match what is known, including a holding statement that answers the 'hospital was hacked' claims from staff, a local TV reporter and a community Facebook post without confirming or ruling out a security cause before the Privacy and Security Officer decides.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Downtime Incident Commander (Administrator on Call) (IC):** Owns the downtime declaration and level, opens the Boardroom command center, makes the diversion decision with the ED Medical Director, and declares return to normal until handing command to the CMIO.
- **Director of Clinical Informatics (Clinical Systems Lead) (CSL):** Manages the technical recovery with the EHR and interface engine vendors, logs vendor case numbers, and proposes the restore order and the replay, hold or discard decision for each message class.
- **Nursing House Supervisor / Chief Nursing Officer (CNO):** Owns unit readiness and the Nursing paper workflow overnight, calls each charge nurse, chases units that have not reported, and keeps the running count of lost, delayed or duplicated orders and results.
- **Laboratory Manager and Pharmacy Operations Manager (LPM):** Owns the LIS, the critical result call list, the pharmacy system, the dispensing cabinet override, and the Downtime Medication Log, and reports the manual state of each within 30 minutes.
- **Privacy and Security Officer (PSEC):** Owns the decision on whether the outage is a security incident, which systems may reconnect, and the HIPAA four-factor breach risk assessment with its notification clocks and decision log.
- **Public Information Officer (Director of Communications) (PIO):** Owns the standard statement for patients and families, staff intranet updates, and media and social posts about the outage, all cleared with the Incident Commander.

# Lakemont Community Hospital — Downtime Procedures SOP (IS-14-A)

Paper workflows for nursing, laboratory, pharmacy, radiology and registration · Owner: Director of Clinical Informatics with the CNO

## 1. Starting downtime on a unit

When the overhead page or the House Supervisor's call announces downtime, the charge nurse:

1. Prints the census and last printed MAR from the downtime viewer if it is up; otherwise uses the binder copy.
2. Moves the unit's downtime cart to the nurses' station and tells the House Supervisor the time the unit is on procedures.
3. Stamps each downtime form with the patient's addressograph label, or handwrites name, date of birth and the medical record number (MRN) if no label is available. Paper-chart MRNs begin with "D-" followed by a 6-digit number from the downtime pad when a patient has no record number.
4. Assigns one nurse per shift as "paper runner" for orders, results and pharmacy requests.

## 2. Orders

- Providers write orders on the Downtime Order Set (white, three-part). Verbal orders are read back and signed within 24 hours.
- The unit clerk faxes or sends the pink copy by runner to the department; the yellow copy stays on the chart. Pneumatic tube is used for specimens only.
- Orders given during downtime are entered into the EHR after restore by the entering nurse or the clerk, one unit at a time, as described in the reconciliation SOP.

## 3. Laboratory

- The laboratory switches to manual requisitions (green). The analyzers continue to run; results print at the lab and are hand-delivered by runner or phoned.
- Critical values are called to the unit charge nurse or the covering provider within 60 minutes of verification, with read-back, and logged on the Critical Result Call Log (ext. 5410 to reach the critical value desk).
- Lab specimens carry two identifiers, the handwritten patient name and date of birth, plus the downtime accession number from the pad (format DT-yyyymmdd-nnn).
- Results are not released to the EHR until the LIS-EHR interface has been validated after restore. Held results are reprinted for the chart.

## 4. Pharmacy and medication administration

- The pharmacy verifies from the paper order. Automated dispensing cabinets are switched to override mode for all units except the ICU, where nurses use pharmacist-verified patient-specific bins. Override pulls require a second nurse's witness for controlled substances.
- The nurse documents administration on the printed MAR, with time, dose, route and initials. High-alert medications (insulin, heparin, opioids, magnesium) require an independent double check by a second nurse.
- The pharmacy keeps a Downtime Medication Log of every dispensed dose, to be reconciled against the electronic MAR after restore.
- Allergy information comes from the downtime viewer or the armband; if neither is available the nurse asks the patient or family and the pharmacist is called before the first dose.

## 5. Radiology

- Orders on paper; the technologist records the exam on the manual log. Images stay in PACS if PACS is up; if not, they are stored on the modality and sent after restore. Preliminary reads are phoned to the ordering provider and recorded.

## 6. Emergency department

- The ED uses a paper tracking board on the wall (magnetic whiteboard with 28 spaces). Triage is on the downtime triage form with an ESI level. Registration assigns an "ED-D" encounter number from the pad.
- The ED charge nurse reports a hourly count to the House Supervisor: patients in the department, boarding, and awaiting results.

## 7. Registration and billing

- Registration writes patient demographics on the downtime face sheet. Insurance verification is done by phone or the payer portal when available. Charges are captured on paper charge sheets and entered by Patient Financial Services after restore.

## 8. Patient identity

- Two identifiers at every contact. For unidentified patients, trauma names use the downtime convention "ZZ-Trauma" followed by the day of the week and a number (for example ZZ-Trauma Tuesday 1). These are linked to a real identity after restore by HIM using a merge request.
- The most frequent downtime harm is a mix-up of patient identities. If two charts are suspected of being the same person, or one chart of holding two people's data, the nurse calls HIM (ext. 5430) and both charts are frozen until the Record Integrity Specialist decides.

## 9. Devices and monitors

Bedside monitors, infusion pumps and ventilators continue to work standalone. Pump libraries and drug dose limits still apply on the pump. If a security cause is suspected, the Security Officer may order networked devices disconnected; the nurse then records vital signs and infusion rates by hand every 15 minutes in the ICU and every hour elsewhere.

## 10. Staffing and surge

At Level 3 or above the CNO adds one paper runner per unit at the start of each shift and activates the downtime float pool (eight nurses and four unit clerks from outpatient clinics and the education department). The nurse-to-patient ratio targets are not relaxed: medical-surgical 1:5 days and 1:6 nights; ICU 1:2.

## 11. Ending downtime

Units do not return to the EHR until the House Supervisor announces return to normal. After that the unit starts reconciliation: orders entered during downtime, MAR entries, results received on paper, vital signs, and any registration. Each item is entered and checked by a second person. The unit reports counts done and remaining to the command center at the start of each hour.

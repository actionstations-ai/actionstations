# ICM-GOV-01: Board Oversight, Delegation of Authority and Insurance

Ironcrest Machinery Corporation · Office of the Corporate Secretary · Revision 3

## 1. The board and its committees

The board of Ironcrest Machinery Corporation has nine directors, eight of them independent. Chair of the Board and Lead Independent Director: Eleanor Whitcomb. The CEO, Thomas Aldridge, is the only management director.

- **Audit Committee** (chair Dr. Samuel Obuya, a retired accounting professor, with the Audit Committee financial expert designation; members Daniel Reyes and Hannah Pruitt). Oversees financial reporting, disclosure controls, internal audit, the external auditor (a national firm, fictional Pemberton & Gray LLP) and the cybersecurity incident disclosure process.
- **Risk and Technology Committee** (chair Karen Lindqvist; members Robert Yang and Anne-Marie Dube). Oversees enterprise risk, cybersecurity strategy, business continuity and the quarterly CISO report.
- **Compensation and Governance Committees** are not involved in incident response except for succession.

The board meets six times a year and has a standing 30-minute cybersecurity session at two of the meetings. The CISO reports to the Risk and Technology Committee quarterly, and the Business Continuity Manager reports on the BIA and tests once a year. The board reviews a crisis tabletop exercise annually.

## 2. How the board learns of an incident

The General Counsel informs the Audit Committee Chair of any cybersecurity incident that the Disclosure Committee treats as possibly material within 24 hours, and at once on a determination of materiality. The Chair of the Board is told by the CEO of any Level 3 event. The board portal carries the briefing materials; the portal's weekend support number is (815) 555-0126. The board receives a first written briefing within 24 hours of a Level 3 declaration, a video meeting within 24 hours, and daily updates after that until the CEO ends the crisis. Each briefing states the facts known and not known, the decisions made and the decisions asked of the board. The minutes record what was reported, what directors asked, and the outcome.

## 3. Delegation of authority (extract)

| Decision | Authority | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Declare a crisis, activate the CMT | CEO (or COO) | None |
| Engage forensic firm and outside counsel | General Counsel | Up to 2 million dollars without further approval |
| Pay a ransom or an extortion demand | CEO with the Audit Committee Chair consulted, after sanctions screening, insurer and law enforcement notice | Over 5 million dollars: board approval |
| File Form 8-K | General Counsel and CEO (CFO certifies related periodic reports) | Draft reviewed by outside counsel |
| Contact the SEC, a state regulator or a law enforcement agency | General Counsel | The CEO is told first where time allows |
| Shut down a plant or a production line | COO | Over 24 hours: CEO |
| Invoke the business continuity plan and recovery order | CEO on the BCM's recommendation | Cost over 10 million dollars: CFO and CEO |
| Communicate with analysts and investors | IR VP with CFO | Only text that matches the filing |
| Employee lay-off or furlough during an event | CHRO with CEO | Board informed |
| Settle a customer claim | General Counsel | Over 2 million dollars: CEO; over 10 million: board |

## 4. Succession

The CEO's deputy is the COO, Walter Brandt. The CFO's deputy is the Chief Accounting Officer, Colleen Hayes. The General Counsel's deputy is Alan Ferreira. The board's resolution of last March allows the COO to act as CEO for up to 14 days if the CEO is incapacitated or unreachable for more than 4 hours in a Level 3 event, and the Chair of the Board must be told at once. A designated acting official may not authorize a ransom payment or sign a Form 8-K without the Chair of the Board's confirmation.

## 5. Insurance

Ironcrest's cyber policy with Blue Heron Mutual has a limit of 50 million dollars with a 2 million dollar retention. It covers forensic and legal costs, business interruption after an 8-hour waiting period, notification, and regulatory defense, and requires notice within 24 hours and the insurer's prior consent to retain forensic and legal vendors, and to any ransom payment. The CFO and the Deputy General Counsel hold the policy and the claims line. The company also carries property, business interruption and directors and officers liability policies; any disclosure of an incident is shared with the D&O carrier.

## 6. External auditor and the quarterly close

The external auditor, Pemberton & Gray LLP, is told by the Audit Committee Chair when an incident might affect internal control over financial reporting. A material incident during a quarter-end close is raised with the auditor at once. The Chief Accounting Officer assesses whether any incident affects the close timetable, accruals for incident costs, and a possible impairment. The current quarter ends on the last day of March; earnings are released the third week of April.

## 7. Customers, suppliers and lenders

The company's two largest customers, a tractor maker and a construction equipment maker (both fictional, Fieldmark Equipment and Granite Peak Machinery), account for 41 percent of sales; supply contracts require notice of a material supply interruption within 72 hours and provide for a penalty of 0.5 percent of the monthly order value per day of delay after 5 days. The revolving credit facility of 600 million dollars (agent bank: Prairie Lakes Bank, fictional) requires notice of a material adverse event; the CFO talks to the agent bank. Notices are prepared by the General Counsel.

## 8. Insider trading

When the Disclosure Committee is assessing a possibly material incident, the Corporate Secretary closes the trading window for directors and officers and the CMT, and tells the 10b5-1 plan administrator. The window opens only on the General Counsel's written notice, after the material facts have been disclosed.

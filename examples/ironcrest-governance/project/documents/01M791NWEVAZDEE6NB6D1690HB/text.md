# ICM-CMP-01: Crisis Management Plan

Ironcrest Machinery Corporation · Office of the Chief Executive · Rockford, Illinois · Revision 7

## 1. Purpose

Ironcrest Machinery Corporation builds agricultural and construction machinery transmissions and hydraulic drives. It has 9,400 employees, net sales of 3.1 billion dollars, a headquarters and Plant 1 in Rockford, Illinois, Plant 2 in Beloit, Wisconsin, Plant 3 in Dubuque, Iowa, and a parts distribution center in Joliet, Illinois. Its common stock is listed on a national exchange. This plan says who decides what when an event threatens the whole company: a cyber incident, a plant loss, a safety event, a supplier failure. It sits above the plant emergency plans and the IT incident response plan.

## 3. Activation levels

- **Level 1, Watch:** a single plant or function affected, restored within 24 hours. The COO and the function head manage; the Business Continuity Manager (BCM) logs it.
- **Level 2, Alert:** more than one site, or a critical function down beyond its recovery time objective, or any ransomware. The CEO is told within 30 minutes; the CMT meets within 2 hours; the General Counsel is told, and she decides on privilege and outside counsel.
- **Level 3, Crisis:** loss of ERP or manufacturing systems across plants, threat to life, a regulator, law enforcement or media inquiry, or any event that could be material to the company. The CMT meets within 60 minutes; the Audit Committee Chair is told within 4 hours; the full board is briefed within 24 hours.

The CEO or, in the CEO's absence, the COO declares the level. The BCM keeps a one-page decision log with time, decision, who decided, what was known and why. Decisions without these fields are not recorded as decisions.

## 5. Board escalation

The Chair of the Board is called by the CEO for any Level 3 event. The Audit Committee Chair receives a written note from the General Counsel through the board portal, with a phone call for anything involving a possible disclosure. The board portal is hosted by an outside provider; if it is unavailable the General Counsel sends a hard-copy summary by courier and phones each director. The full board meets by video within 24 hours of a Level 3 declaration, with a standing agenda: facts known and not known, decisions made, decisions needed, the disclosure position, and the next report time.

## 6. Ransom and payment decisions

The company policy is not to pay. If the CEO considers payment, the following must be done first and recorded: the General Counsel confirms sanctions screening of the recipient through outside counsel; the insurer and the broker are informed; the FBI and CISA are informed; the forensic firm gives its view of the likelihood that data or systems can be recovered without payment; the Audit Committee Chair is consulted; the board is told. The CEO decides within the limit of delegated authority in the Corporate Delegation of Authority (cash payments over 5 million dollars need board approval). The decision is documented with the facts known and the basis for it.

## 7. Communications in a crisis

Messages to employees, customers, dealers, suppliers, regulators, analysts and the press come from the fact sheet kept by the Chief Communications Officer. The same numbers and dates appear in the board briefing, the SEC filing, the press release and the employee message. No one makes a public statement outside the CMT. Plant managers refer inquiries to the press line (815) 555-0124.

## 8. Succession and delegation

The CEO, CFO and General Counsel each have a named deputy. If the CEO is unreachable for 4 hours in a Level 3 event, the COO acts under the board's standing resolution, and the Chair of the Board is told. Delegated authorities do not extend to ransom payment, filing an SEC report on the company's behalf or settling litigation.

## 9. After action

Within 10 working days of closing a crisis the BCM prepares a report to the Audit Committee: timeline, decisions with their documentation, recovery against RTO, and corrective actions with owners and dates.

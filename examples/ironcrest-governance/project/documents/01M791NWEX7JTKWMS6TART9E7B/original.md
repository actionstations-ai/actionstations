# ICM-LEG-03: Disclosure Controls for Cybersecurity Incidents

Ironcrest Machinery Corporation · Office of the General Counsel · Revision 4

## 1. Purpose

This procedure makes sure Ironcrest decides promptly whether a cybersecurity incident is material and, if so, files a Form 8-K Item 1.05 within four business days after the determination. It is part of the company's disclosure controls and procedures. It is an exercise aid, not legal advice.

## 2. Who reports to whom

Any employee who suspects an incident calls the Security Operations Center (SOC), (815) 555-0122. The SOC reports to the CISO, Anil Deshmukh. The CISO tells the General Counsel, Marguerite Daley, within one hour of any of the following: ransomware on any system; unauthorized access to systems that run manufacturing or hold personal or financial data; theft of intellectual property; an outside demand or claim. The General Counsel convenes the Disclosure Committee within four hours.

## 3. The Disclosure Committee

Chair: the General Counsel. Members: CFO Priya Raghavan, Chief Accounting Officer Colleen Hayes, CISO, CIO Joanna Ketterman, Chief Communications Officer Gabriel Soto, Investor Relations VP Neil Okamoto, and the Head of Internal Audit, Dennis Chu. Outside counsel (Harrow Fenn LLP) and the forensic firm (Blackwater Cyber, fictional) are on call. Minutes are kept by the Deputy General Counsel, Alan Ferreira, under privilege where counsel advises.

## 4. The materiality determination

The committee makes the determination without unreasonable delay after the company discovers the incident. It does not wait for the investigation to end, for the end of the quarter or for the insurer's view. The determination is made on facts known at the time and may be revisited. The committee records:

1. The determination: material, not material, or not yet determined (with the information the committee is waiting for and the time it will meet again).
2. The factors considered, quantitative and qualitative.
3. The facts known at the time.
4. The person who decided (the committee chair for the recommendation; the CEO and the CFO concur).
5. The basis.

Quantitative factors include: estimated cost of response, restoration and notification; lost production (a day of lost production at all plants is about 8.5 million dollars of sales, and the Rockford plant makes 38 percent of the company's transmissions); revenue at risk on customer contracts; and cost of any payment. Qualitative factors include: harm to the company's reputation and its relationships with the original equipment manufacturers and dealers who are its customers; regulatory exposure; likelihood of litigation; and whether safety or customer data is involved. Ironcrest's working planning threshold, used as a prompt and not as a rule, is 60 million dollars of expected pre-tax impact, or 2 percent of annual pre-tax income, but a smaller impact can be material for a qualitative reason.

## 5. Form 8-K

- **Item 1.05, Material Cybersecurity Incidents.** Describes the material aspects of the nature, scope and timing of the incident and the material or reasonably likely material impact on the company, including its financial condition and results of operations. Where information is not yet determined, the filing says so, and an 8-K/A is filed within four business days of the information becoming available.
- **Item 8.01, Other Events.** For an incident not yet determined material. The text does not claim materiality or describe impact as material. If the company later determines the incident material, Item 1.05 is filed within four business days of that determination.
- **Delay by the Attorney General.** The Attorney General may delay for national security or public safety. The General Counsel alone requests it through the FBI; a request does not stop the clock until a delay is granted. The four business days run from the end of any delay.
- **Filing.** Draft by the General Counsel; review by outside counsel, the CFO, the CAO and the Chief Communications Officer; approved by the CEO and the General Counsel; filed by the EDGAR filing agent (Lakeline Filing, fictional) before the 5:30 p.m. Eastern EDGAR cut-off. The Deputy General Counsel holds the EDGAR access codes and a backup code is in the safe.

## 6. Consistency check

Before any release the General Counsel and the Chief Communications Officer compare the board briefing, the Form 8-K, the press release, the employee message and the dealer letter. Dates, counts of plants and systems affected, the status of production and any dollar figure must match exactly or be marked as an estimate in all of them. A figure not on the fact sheet is not used.

## 7. Insurance and other notices

The CFO notifies the insurer through the broker within 24 hours; the policy requires prior notice before any ransom or vendor payment. Regulators that may need notice: the state attorney general where personal data of residents is involved; the Department of Defense if any controlled unclassified information is affected (the company supplies a drive component to a defense contractor); customers under contract terms (most require notice within 72 hours of a confirmed incident affecting them). The General Counsel records each recipient, the summary sent, who decided and the basis.

## 8. Board reporting

The Audit Committee Chair, Dr. Samuel Obuya, is told within 24 hours of any incident the committee treats as possibly material and at once on a determination of materiality. The full board receives a briefing at the next meeting or earlier. The board's oversight is recorded in the minutes: what the directors were told, when, and what they asked.

## 9. Trading restrictions

When a possibly material incident is being assessed, the Corporate Secretary closes the trading window for directors, officers and the CMT members, and tells the 10b5-1 plan administrator. Employees who know about the incident may not trade.

## 10. Records

The committee's one-page record, the minutes, the EDGAR confirmation, and every draft are kept for seven years.

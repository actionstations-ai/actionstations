# Prairie Ridge Health — Pharmacy Department, Supply Chain and Systems

Reference for PH-22 · Pharmacy Administration, ext. 3600

## 1. Department

- Central pharmacy on the first floor of the main building, open 24 hours; satellite pharmacies in the ICU/OR corridor (07:00–23:00) and the infusion center (07:00–17:00).
- Staff: 12 pharmacists (including 2 clinical specialists in critical care and infectious diseases), 18 pharmacy technicians, 2 purchasing technicians.
- Night pharmacist on duty (ext. 3601) and the on-call pharmacist (cell, 515-555-0142).
- Compounding: a USP 797 cleanroom suite (a buffer room and an ante-room) that makes about 40 sterile preparations per day, including total parenteral nutrition for 3 patients and IV admixtures.
- Pharmacy and Therapeutics (P&T) Committee meets monthly on the second Tuesday.

## 2. Inventory and systems

- The pharmacy system (inventory, order verification, labels) is connected to the EHR and to 22 automated dispensing cabinets on nursing units.
- The perpetual inventory shows on-hand quantities, par, usage and days on hand by product and location. The Purchasing Manager checks it at 07:00 daily.
- The pump library on the smart infusion pumps (240 pumps) contains the standard concentrations and is updated by Clinical Informatics.
- Barcode scanning at the bedside is used for nearly all doses.
- Reports: a daily shortage report by product and a weekly summary to the P&T Committee.

## 3. Supply chain

- **Primary wholesaler:** a large national distributor; deliveries Monday to Saturday at 05:30; emergency delivery in 4 hours for a fee. Its allocation desk (800-555-0111) sets weekly allocations of products in short supply based on a historic usage period.
- **Secondary wholesaler:** a regional distributor in Des Moines with a daily delivery at 11:00.
- **Direct manufacturer accounts** for the injectable products listed in the IV fluid SOP; the manufacturer customer service line is 800-555-0124.
- **Group purchasing organization (GPO):** a contract that prefers one IV fluid manufacturer, who holds about 60% of the market.
- **Neighboring hospitals:** Webster Valley Medical Center in Fort Dodge, North Iowa Memorial Hospital in Mason City, and the regional network of the Iowa Hospital Association, which can lend product after the Director of Pharmacy signs a loan agreement and a licensed wholesaler or the lending pharmacy's license covers the transfer.
- **Dispatch of loaned product:** by pharmacy courier in under 1 hour from Fort Dodge, 90 minutes from Mason City.
- **Stockpile:** the hospital keeps 7 days of IV fluids normally (days on hand target), held in 10 pallets in the stockroom and 2 on the dock; a typical stock is 9 days of 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9%, 11 days of lactated Ringer's and 14 days of 250 mL minibags.

## 5. Regulators and partners

- **FDA drug shortage staff:** receive notices; the hospital can email shortage reports. The FDA may allow temporary importation of product from another country with a different label; the importer's letter lists the lot, the label differences and a dear-doctor letter.
- **Iowa Board of Pharmacy:** inspects the pharmacy and approves compounding and loan rules.
- **Iowa Department of Public Health:** receives reports of any shortage that affects patient care across the region.
- **Poison control:** the regional poison center (800-222-1222) helps with an overdose from a mislabeled bag.
- **Medical staff:** about 90 physicians, who get shortage updates via the medical staff email list.

# Prairie Ridge Health — IV Fluid Conservation and Substitution SOP (PH-22-A)

Owner: Clinical Pharmacy Manager with the Medication Safety Officer and the CNO

## 1. Products covered

The hospital stocks the following, with normal use per day:

| Product | Presentation | Daily use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium chloride 0.9% | 1000 mL bags | 340 | Most used; carrier and hydration |
| Sodium chloride 0.9% | 250 mL and 100 mL minibags | 410 | Piggyback antibiotics, medication dilution |
| Lactated Ringer's | 1000 mL bags | 120 | OR, ED, obstetrics |
| Dextrose 5% in water | 1000 mL, 250 mL | 55 | Hypoglycemia, dilution |
| Dextrose 5% and sodium chloride 0.45% with potassium chloride 20 mEq | 1000 mL | 38 | Maintenance |
| Sterile water for injection and irrigation | Various | 30 | Compounding |
| Peritoneal dialysis and CRRT solutions | Various | 12 | Not part of this SOP; handled by Nephrology |

Storage: central pharmacy stockroom (10 pallets), the dock and satellite pharmacies at the ICU and OR, and par levels on every unit (typically a 2-day supply).

## 2. Conservation (Level 2 and 3)

1. Prefer oral or enteral hydration and medications when the patient can take them.
2. Use the smallest effective volume; switch from 1000 mL to 500 mL or 250 mL where available.
3. Stop routine "keep vein open" infusions; use saline locks and flushes with prefilled syringes.
4. Administer IV push medications at the labeled rate undiluted when the drug allows it (pharmacy keeps a list) instead of as a minibag piggyback; the Clinical Pharmacy Manager publishes the list and rates.
5. Use bolus rather than continuous infusion only when the order and monitoring allow.
6. In the OR, anesthesia uses balanced solutions only when needed and avoids bags above the case estimate; the anesthesia technician returns unopened bags.
7. Do not remove bags from a unit's par except by pharmacy.

## 3. Substitution

- Lactated Ringer's and sodium chloride 0.9% are substituted for each other only by an order from the prescriber, because they differ in electrolytes; the pharmacist checks hyperkalemia risk, kidney function and blood transfusion at the same line.
- Imported product or product from an alternate source may have a different label, volume, bag port and concentration. The pharmacist does not assume the dose; the label, volume and conversion note come from the shortage notice or the manufacturer. The pharmacy labels the bag with the Prairie Ridge standard label and sends an alert to nursing.
- Compounded product made in-house from sterile water and sodium chloride additive is allowed only under the compounding SOP and Iowa rules, and has beyond-use dating set by USP chapter 797; the Director of Pharmacy approves each batch.
- Every substitution is run through the checklist: product match, concentration, volume, line and pump compatibility, library entry on the infusion pump, look-alike risk, nurse education, order set update, and second pharmacist check. The checklist is kept for 5 years.

## 4. Pump library and order sets

The Medication Safety Officer asks Clinical Informatics to change the order set and the pump drug library within 12 hours of an approved substitution. Until then, pharmacists verify every order manually. The unit does not use a substitution that is not in the pump library.

## 5. Tracking

- The Purchasing Manager keeps the Shortage Board in the command center: product, days on hand, allocation percent, restrictions, substitutions, loans and expected delivery.
- Every 8 hours the team counts the patients affected: patients whose therapy was changed, delayed or canceled, by unit.
- Pharmacists review every dose changed for the previous 24 hours to detect error and report safe versus unsafe substitutions.

## 6. Dispensing cabinet and unit actions

Pharmacy sets cabinet maximum quantities for the product and removes it from override lists. The unit manager checks that no unit holds more than its reduced par. Charge nurses report any bag found out of place or damage to the pharmacy.

## 7. Infusion center

The infusion center manager (Carla Moreno) groups patients by whether treatment can safely wait (for example, hydration) or not (chemotherapy, biologics, iron). Appointments that can wait are moved by the physician's office after a call from the infusion center, using a script from the PIO.

## 8. Patient communication

A nurse or pharmacist explains changes in the patient's IV therapy, including why the solution changed and what to watch for. The call script for outpatients states: "Because of a national supply shortage of IV fluids, we are changing how some treatments are given. Your care team will call you about your appointment."

## 9. Safety events

An adverse drug event related to a substitution is reported at once to the Medication Safety Officer. A near miss is reported the same day. The Medication Safety Officer reviews all events weekly during a shortage and daily if any event is serious.

# Player Briefing Packet
## Forty Percent — IV Fluid Allocation & Substitution Safety

Location: Prairie Ridge Health, Fort Dodge, Iowa · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 07:00 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Clearing and cool in Fort Dodge after an overnight line of storms crossed the Midwest: 48°F, wind gusts dying down, no local power or road problems.

### 1. Situation Brief

It is Monday, October 5, 2026, at Prairie Ridge Health in Fort Dodge, Webster County, Iowa, and the hospital is running a normal busy Monday. 104 of 118 beds are occupied, 8 of 10 ICU beds are filled, and the emergency department saw 31 visits overnight. The operating rooms have 14 cases booked across all 5 rooms, and the infusion center opens at 08:00 with 22 appointments. In the central pharmacy, the day shift is coming on as the night shift hands over. The primary wholesaler's 05:30 delivery arrived on time and is on the dock. The storeroom technician is checking the central pharmacy stockroom, and the ICU and OR satellite pharmacies are restocking for the morning rush. IV fluid demand follows the usual weekday pattern, with sodium chloride 0.9% 1000 mL bags and minibags moving fastest, mostly to the ED, the medical-surgical units, the ICU and the ORs. The Drug Shortage Team has no active IV fluid shortages, and the command center in Conference Room C (ext. 3690) is not open.

- IV fluid stock and days on hand (central stockroom, dock, ICU and OR satellites, unit par levels): GREEN: 07:00 report shows normal days on hand; no active IV fluid shortages
- 22 automated dispensing cabinets (IV fluid pockets): GREEN: all 22 cabinets online and linked to the pharmacy system and EHR
- Smart infusion pump drug library (240 pumps): GREEN: current library loaded; routine quarterly update scheduled for Thursday 10/08, 02:00–04:00
- Wholesaler allocation, loans and alternate supply: GREEN: 05:30 primary wholesaler delivery received on schedule; no allocations in effect; no loans open
- Staff and medical staff messaging (one message): GREEN: no conservation or substitution notices active
- Public mood and social media: GREEN: no media inquiries; routine patient and family calls only

**The Anomaly:** **07:05 AM: Central pharmacy stockroom door. Status board printout plus a face-to-face report.**

The pharmacy system's perpetual inventory report for IV fluids printed at 07:00, using 14-day average daily usage:

- Sodium chloride 0.9% 1000 mL: 3,060 bags on hand, 340 used per day, 9.0 days on hand
- Lactated Ringer's 1000 mL: 744 bags on hand, 120 used per day, 6.2 days on hand
- Sodium chloride 0.9% 250 mL/100 mL minibags: 3,280 on hand, 410 used per day, 8.0 days on hand
- D5W 1000/250 mL: 12.4 days on hand
- Sterile water: 15.0 days on hand

Storeroom technician Tyler Bergstrom, CPhT, stops you at the stockroom door with the printout:

> "Morning. Before you sign off on that report, the NS pallets look light to me. The system says we're full, but the back two pallets on the left are only about half wrapped. And the 05:30 truck dropped two pallets on the dock that nobody's scanned in yet. Want me to count by lot?"

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep IV therapy safe and uninterrupted for every patient in the ED, inpatient units, ICU, ORs and infusion center while you investigate and resolve any IV fluid supply or inventory problem that comes up today. You will have to trade speed against verification and service against safety. Decisions have to be quick enough to keep care moving, but every number, source, product and message must be checked before it reaches a cabinet, a pump or the public.

### 2. Team Assignments & Standing Orders

| Team | Role | Standing Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Team A: Director of Pharmacy (Command)**— Director of Pharmacy (Drug Shortage Team Chair) | You own the overall pharmacy picture: the shortage level recommendation, any internal allocation, loan agreements, spending authority, and the single consolidated pharmacy briefing to the Incident Commander in Conference Room C (ext. 3690). | **Numbers First: base every level call and allocation on current days on hand (on-hand divided by usage per day) from a reconciled count, never on the 07:00 report alone.** **Sign Before Ship: no loaned or purchased product enters stock until a signed loan agreement or a verified licensed source covers it.** **One Voice Upward: give the Incident Commander one consolidated pharmacy update on a fixed rhythm, not separate updates from each department.** |
| **Team B: Purchasing and Inventory Manager (Supply)**— Pharmacy Purchasing and Inventory Manager | You own the perpetual inventory, cabinet par levels, and all contact with wholesalers and manufacturers. You also own the search for loans and alternate supply, and you make sure the counts the hospital reports are true. | **Count Twice: reconcile stockroom, dock, ICU and OR satellite and cabinet quantities by lot before you report days on hand.** **Know the Source: refuse any offer that cannot show a lot list and a licensed chain of custody, however tight stock gets.** **Flag Every Difference: send any product with a different label, bag volume or port to the Medication Safety Officer before put-away.** |
| **Team C: Clinical Pharmacy Manager / Medication Safety Officer (Clinical Safety)**— Clinical Pharmacy Manager / Medication Safety Officer | You own conservation guidance, activation of the IV Fluid Conservation SOP (PH-22-A), and the substitution safety checklist. You also review any medication error or near miss involving IV fluids. | **Checklist Before Cabinet: load no substitute product until concentration, volume, labeling, pump library, look-alike risk, nursing education and a second pharmacist check are all complete.** **Plan Text Only: give dose, rate and conversion guidance only from PH-22-A and the shortage plan, never as improvised advice on a unit.** **Stop and Review: after any substitution error, hold that product on all units until the cause is understood.** |
| **Team D: Chief Medical Officer and P&T Chair (Medical Staff)**— Chief Medical Officer and P&T Chair | You own emergency P&T approvals, notification of the medical staff, and clinical rulings on any restriction, including physician requests for exceptions and decisions about elective surgery. | **Same Numbers: medical staff emails, texts and huddle scripts carry exactly the figures the Chief Nursing Officer sends to units.** **Priority by Plan: settle physician demands against the clinical priority order and protected uses, not by who calls loudest.** **Deferral Is Clinical: support deferring elective surgery only when the plan's 3-day trigger is met and urgent, emergency, obstetric and cancer cases are protected.** |
| **Team E: Chief Nursing Officer (Nursing Units)**— Chief Nursing Officer | You own what nurses on every unit are told and taught. That covers notifying units, educating staff on any conservation or substitute product, and ending conflicting unit-level instructions. | **One Unit Message: units follow only the CNO notice; local fluid or flush instructions are withdrawn once it is published.** **Bedside Truth: if a bag, label or pump screen doesn't match the instruction, the nurse stops and calls pharmacy before infusing.** **Report Without Blame: near misses and errors are reported at once so other units can be warned the same shift.** |
| **Team F: Public Information Officer (Communications)**— Public Information Officer (Director of Communications) | You own everything said outside the clinical chain: holding statements, media responses, social media monitoring, and consistent messages to patients, families and Patient Relations. | **Truth over Reassurance: say what continues and what may be rescheduled; never say everything is fine if it isn't.** **No Blame by Name: never name a vendor or manufacturer as a cause unless the FDA or the manufacturer has done so publicly.** **All Calls to One Desk: route every media call from physicians, nurses and staff to ext. 3050, and answer false claims the same day with an Incident Commander–approved statement.** |

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every phone call, page, radio call or overhead message with "This is an exercise" so that no one in the central pharmacy, on the units or outside the hospital mistakes a scenario message for a real IV fluid emergency.
- If a real emergency happens during the exercise, such as a patient event, a real supply problem or a building issue, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD" and the exercise stops at once so that real patient care comes first.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to move the scenario forward; accept the new time and work from the situation as it then stands, so the exercise can cover a full shortage response in four hours.
- Treat every printed inject as real intelligence, including inventory reports, wholesaler emails, phone transcripts, product photos, labels and social media posts, because decisions made on them are the point of the exercise.

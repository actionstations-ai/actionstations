# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Forty Percent** (IV Fluid Allocation & Substitution Safety)
Location: Prairie Ridge Health, Fort Dodge, Iowa · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Director of Pharmacy, Purchasing and Inventory Manager, Clinical Pharmacy Manager / Medication Safety Officer, Chief Medical Officer, Chief Nursing Officer, Public Information Officer

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Making the Level 3 call from days on hand and allocation percent, rationing scarce IV fluids fairly by clinical priority, keeping substitutions safe when labels, bag volumes and the pump library don't match, and giving staff and the public one consistent message.

**The Threat:** After a regional storm, the manufacturer's main IV fluid plant has gone down (no attacker is involved). On Monday, October 5, 2026 at 07:40, the primary wholesaler's allocation desk tells Prairie Ridge Health that for the next 4 weeks it will receive 40 percent of its baseline-period usage of 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9%, lactated Ringer's and 250 mL minibags. At 09:30 the FDA lists the products as in shortage. The 07:00 morning report already showed only 9.0 days of 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9% (340 bags a day), 6.2 days of lactated Ringer's (120 a day) and 8 days of 250 mL minibags (410 a day). The cut runs through the pharmacy's perpetual inventory and 22 automated dispensing cabinets to the emergency department, operating rooms, ICU, medical-surgical units, obstetrics, the infusion center and the long-term care unit.

**The Operational Impact:** Within hours, days on hand fall at the central pharmacy and in the dispensing cabinets on nursing units. Nurses find cabinet pockets for 1000 mL bags and 250 mL minibags restricted, and they have to switch to oral routes, IV push in place of minibags and no keep-vein-open infusions. Two units send out conflicting instructions on flush volumes. Buyers must decide on a secondary supplier offering 400 cases at 3 times the price with no lot list. A 60-case loan from Webster Valley Medical Center arrives with a different label. An FDA-allowed imported product comes with a different bag volume and port, and the 240 smart infusion pumps don't have it in their library yet. On 6 West, an imported 500 mL bag is run at the wrong rate, causing an adverse event. Wednesday's OR schedule has 11 elective joint replacement and abdominal cases, and those patients may face a 48-hour deferral if 1000 mL sodium chloride falls below 3 days on hand. The infusion center is moving hydration appointments and has a patient who can't wait. A physician demands unrestricted fluids for sepsis boluses. A local TV reporter calls, and a Facebook post claims the hospital has run out of IV fluids and canceled surgeries. Patients and families are calling Patient Relations and the infusion center.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to calculate days on hand for 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9%, lactated Ringer's and 250 mL minibags under the wholesaler's 40 percent allocation, recommend Level 3 activation to the Incident Commander, and set an internal allocation by percent of normal use and clinical priority, as Drug Shortage plan section 5 describes.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply the IV Fluid Conservation SOP (PH-22-A) and the Medication Safety Officer's substitution checklist (concentration, volume, labeling, pump library, look-alike risk, nursing education, second pharmacist check) before the Webster Valley loan and the FDA-allowed imported 500 mL bags reach the 22 dispensing cabinets and the 240 smart pumps, and to respond to the 6 West rate error.
- **Resource Management:** Demonstrate the ability to decide whether to accept or refuse the secondary supplier's 400-case offer at 3 times the price with no lot list, to secure and document the loan agreement with neighboring hospitals, and to make the 48-hour elective surgery deferral decision for Wednesday's 11 cases against the 3-days-on-hand trigger.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to issue one message with the same numbers to nursing units, the roughly 90 physicians on the medical staff and the public, ending the conflicting flush-volume instructions and correcting the false 'run out of IV fluids' Facebook post the same day with a statement signed off by the Incident Commander.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Director of Pharmacy (Drug Shortage Team Chair) (DOP):** Owns the shortage level recommendation, the internal allocation by percent of normal use, loan agreements, spending authority and the briefing to the Incident Commander in Conference Room C.
- **Pharmacy Purchasing and Inventory Manager (PIM):** Owns wholesaler and manufacturer contact, allocation counts, the perpetual inventory, cabinet par levels and the search for loans and alternate suppliers.
- **Clinical Pharmacy Manager / Medication Safety Officer (CPM):** Owns the conservation notice, activation of the substitution protocol in PH-22-A, the substitution checklist and the review of the 6 West adverse event.
- **Chief Medical Officer and P&T Chair (CMO):** Owns emergency P&T approvals, medical staff notification and the clinical arbitration of restrictions, including physician demands for exceptions.
- **Chief Nursing Officer (CNO):** Owns unit notification, nursing education on conservation and substitutes, and ending conflicting unit-level instructions such as flush volumes.
- **Public Information Officer (Director of Communications) (PIO):** Owns the holding, surgery-deferral and media statements, social media monitoring, and consistent messaging to patients, families, Patient Relations and local media.

# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Forty Percent — IV Fluid Allocation & Substitution Safety

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Prairie Ridge Health, Fort Dodge, Iowa · Duration: 4 Hours

### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for the Prairie Ridge Health Drug Shortage Team and its hospital partners in Fort Dodge. It runs for 4 hours of exercise time, starting at 07:00 on Monday, October 5, 2026. Nothing is moved, ordered or administered. Players talk through the decisions, notifications and messages they would make, using their real plans: the Drug Shortage plan (sections 2, 4 and 5), the IV Fluid Conservation SOP PH-22-A, and the Clinical plan sections on media (section 5) and message consistency (section 6). Controllers deliver injects and play every outside party. Evaluators record what players decide, when they decide it and why.

The injects simulate an **infrastructure failure upstream of the hospital**. After an overnight storm, the manufacturer's main IV fluid plant goes offline. No attacker is involved. The primary wholesaler cuts Prairie Ridge to 40 percent of its baseline-period usage of 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9%, lactated Ringer's and 250 mL minibags, and the FDA lists those products as in shortage. The scenario has four phases:
- **Phase 1, The Forty Percent Call (0:00–1:00, Mon 07:00–08:00).** Players find the inventory error, take the allocation call and must give the Incident Commander a single Level 2 or Level 3 recommendation by 08:15.
- **Phase 2, Cabinets Running Dry (1:00–2:00, Mon 08:00–09:00).** The cut reaches the 22 dispensing cabinets. Two units issue conflicting flush memos, an ED attending demands unrestricted fluids, and a Facebook rumor and a TV call arrive.
- **Phase 3, Defer or Operate (2:00–3:00, Mon 09:00–10:00).** A lot goes into quarantine and days on hand drop to 4.9. Players must decide by 11:00 whether to defer Wednesday's 11 elective cases. They also face a gray-market offer, a loaned product that will not scan, and a chemotherapy patient who cannot wait.
- **Phase 4, Imported Bags, New Risk (3:00–4:00, Wed 10:00–11:00).** FDA-allowed imported 500 mL bags arrive before the pump library can hold them. A rate error on 6 West sends a patient to the ICU, and the Iowa Board of Pharmacy, the FDA and the Iowa Department of Public Health want their notifications.

Evaluators should watch for these behaviors:
- **Arithmetic.** Days on hand are recalculated every time on-hand stock, usage or allocation changes: 3,060 falls to 2,560 after the recount, then to 1,660 usable after the quarantine, against 340 bags a day.
- **Timing.** Players escalate before the information is complete.
- **One message.** The Medication Safety Officer's "single page" is used, and every message carries the same numbers.
- **Pressure.** Players refuse gray-market product and the clinicians demanding exceptions, and they treat the "60 percent" email as unconfirmed.
- **Substitutions.** The full substitution checklist runs before any loaned or imported product reaches a patient.
- **Public statements.** The PIO never names a vendor and meets the reporters' deadlines without overstating the situation.

Record times. Hesitation and conflicting instructions are findings, not failures.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to calculate days on hand for 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9%, lactated Ringer's and 250 mL minibags under the wholesaler's 40 percent allocation, recommend Level 3 activation to the Incident Commander, and set an internal allocation by percent of normal use and clinical priority, as Drug Shortage plan section 5 describes.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to apply the IV Fluid Conservation SOP (PH-22-A) and the Medication Safety Officer's substitution checklist (concentration, volume, labeling, pump library, look-alike risk, nursing education, second pharmacist check) before the Webster Valley loan and the FDA-allowed imported 500 mL bags reach the 22 dispensing cabinets and the 240 smart pumps, and to respond to the 6 West rate error.
- **Resource Management:** Demonstrate the ability to decide whether to accept or refuse the secondary supplier's 400-case offer at 3 times the price with no lot list, to secure and document the loan agreement with neighboring hospitals, and to make the 48-hour elective surgery deferral decision for Wednesday's 11 cases against the 3-days-on-hand trigger.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to issue one message with the same numbers to nursing units, the roughly 90 physicians on the medical staff and the public, ending the conflicting flush-volume instructions and correcting the false 'run out of IV fluids' Facebook post the same day with a statement signed off by the Incident Commander.

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Set up islands in a U-shape facing the main screen. The open end of the U is the pharmacy operations desk, where Teams A, B and C sit. Behind it, one long table represents the Hospital Command Center in Conference Room C (ext. 3690), where Teams D and E sit and where the Incident Commander (played by the Controller) speaks during the Phase 3 and 4 Drug Shortage Team meetings.
- **Table 1, Pharmacy operations desk (center):** Team A, Director of Pharmacy, sits next to Team B, Purchasing and Inventory Manager, so counts and spending decisions happen side by side.
- **Table 2, Clinical safety (left of the U):** Team C, Clinical Pharmacy Manager / Medication Safety Officer. Put the printed PH-22-A product table and a blank substitution checklist here.
- **Table 3, Command Center table (behind pharmacy):** Team D, Chief Medical Officer, and Team E, Chief Nursing Officer. This is where the physician and unit calls land.
- **Rear table, Public Information Officer:** Team F sits alone at the back of the room, away from the islands. The PIO is isolated on purpose. In the real hospital the PIO (ext. 3050) does not hear pharmacy's numbers unless someone sends them. The PIO must walk over, or be sent the single page, to get days on hand, the allocation percent and the next update time. That walk tests whether one consistent message actually reaches communications.
- **SimCell / Controller station:** Place it at the side of the room, near the screen, out of the players' line of sight. Evaluators sit behind or beside the team they are assigned to, with clear sight of the whiteboard.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and screen for main-screen injects: status boards, the daily shortage report, the OR projection and the Facebook posts
- A whiteboard or flip chart for the ICS-201 (Incident Briefing) and a running days-on-hand tracker
- A printed inject deck: one copy for the Controller, plus handouts for the I-08 memos, I-12 projection, I-13 email and I-16 importer letter
- Optional: simulated phones or radios, or a desk phone prop at each table, for SimCell calls
- A visible wall clock showing exercise time, plus time-jump cards

**Materials for players:** name tents by team; the Drug Shortage plan roles and response levels; the PH-22-A product table; a blank single-page message template; the ICS-201; notepads and calculators.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**The SimCell role**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the wholesaler," you answer as the allocation desk rep. If they say "I page the ED attending," you are the attending. If they say "I email the Iowa Board of Pharmacy," you are the inspector writing back. Stay in character, want what that character wants, and answer only what you are asked. Players get information by calling for it, not by asking the Controller.

**Do not make it easy**
Real partners have their own priorities, and you should too.
- **ED charge nurse, I-07.** She insists IT broke the cabinets and wants the pocket overridden. Do not confirm or deny an interface problem. Keep saying "I have three patients waiting, just unlock it" until a player explains the restriction and gives her the approved workaround.
- **Webster Valley buyer, I-14.** If players accept the loan without checking it, the buyer is happy. Nobody tells them the bags won't scan unless they ask the dock.
- **Secondary supplier rep, I-13.** He sends the lot list only after payment, and the offer always "expires at 11:00."

**Managing time**
At each TIME_JUMP (I-06, I-11, I-15), stand, raise a hand and read the script:
"PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down for a moment. We are jumping forward [60 minutes / two days]. It is now [time and day]. [Read the time-jump card.] Anything you said you would do has been done. Anything you did not decide is still open. RESUME."

If a discussion stalls for more than 5 minutes, have the Incident Commander's office call: "Dale Pruitt needs your number in ten minutes."

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "We'd never get that call," answer: "Noted, and the evaluator will capture it for the after-action review. For today, assume this is what you have in front of you. What do you do with it?" Do not argue the realism of the scenario.

**Delivering injects**
Do not explain. Say "This just came in," "This is on the main screen" or "Your phone is ringing." Hand over the paper, or read the call in character, then stop talking. Do not hint at the right answer, and do not tell players which inject is a red herring.

**Conventions**
- Every SimCell call, email and written product starts with "This is an exercise," or is marked [EXERCISE].
- If a real emergency or a real-world issue comes up, anyone may say "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." All play stops at once until the Controller restarts it. Resume with "RESUME EXERCISE."

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **Primary wholesaler allocation desk rep, also playing the manufacturer customer service rep it passes calls to (vendor / service provider)** — _Trigger: The Purchasing and Inventory Manager or Director of Pharmacy calls the allocation desk ((800-555-0111) to ask for more than 40 percent, an emergency shipment or a restart date, or calls about the 60 percent update email (I-18)._
  - Ask: ""What's your account number and the PO number for the order you're asking about? I can't look anything up without it.""
  - Ask: ""You're asking for an exception to the 40 percent. Send me your current days on hand and patient-care impact in writing, on letterhead, signed by the Director of Pharmacy. Every hospital in the state says it's an emergency.""
  - Ask: ""Our 4-hour emergency delivery only covers allocated quantities. Have you already ordered against this week's 952? If you have, there's nothing left for me to send.""
  - Ask: ""About the 60 percent: the email said MAY. Do you see a confirmed quantity on your open orders? No? Then the answer is still 40 percent.""
  - Concede only when: Once a written request arrives with days on hand and patient-care impact, the rep opens a case number and files it for the manufacturer's emergency allocation program (a few cases, no promised ship date).
  - Concede only when: If players ask, the rep confirms in writing that Thursday 05:30 carries 952 NS 1000 mL and that no increase is confirmed. That gives players a document to stop the push to put electives back on the schedule.
  - Concede only when: If players commit to standing orders at exactly the allocated amount, the rep agrees to call the Purchasing Manager directly when allocation review results come out.
- **Webster Valley Medical Center pharmacy buyer, Fort Dodge (neighboring hospital lending product)** — _Trigger: The Purchasing Manager calls 515-555-0164 to request a loan, or calls back about the 60 cases delivered to the dock (I-14)._
  - Ask: ""I emailed the loan form an hour ago. Until it's signed by someone with authority, those 720 bags are still on our books. Who's signing on your end, and when?""
  - Ask: ""We need one-for-one replacement in 30 days: same product, 1000 mL. If your Thursday truck is only 952 for the week, how are you paying us back?""
  - Ask: ""You want more? We're on allocation too. Tell me how many days on hand you really have before I go back to my director.""
  - Concede only when: Once the Director of Pharmacy signs the loan agreement, the buyer emails the lot and NDC detail for lot WV-88213 so pharmacy can build the product in its system and check it against the substitution checklist.
  - Concede only when: If players agree to repay with the same product or to a written repayment schedule, the buyer agrees to stretch repayment to 45 days.
- **Iowa Board of Pharmacy inspector (state regulator); if players contact them, the same Controller also plays FDA drug shortage staff and the Iowa Department of Public Health** — _Trigger: The inspector calls (515-555-0172) at I-19, or players contact the Board, the FDA's CDER shortage mailbox or the Iowa Department of Public Health about the imported product, the loan or the 6 West event._
  - Ask: ""Has the temporarily imported product been labeled for your staff? Who did the dose and label conversion, and is it documented?""
  - Ask: ""You told me there was a patient event. When did it happen, has it been reported internally, and has anyone told the FDA? Why am I hearing this from you at 10:50 when it happened at 10:15?""
  - Ask: ""Is any of the imported lot still on a unit shelf right now? How many bags, and where are the two missing cases?""
  - Ask: ""The Department of Public Health asked for your regional status by noon. Will the numbers you send them match what you've told me?""
  - Concede only when: If players give a written notification that states the lot, the label differences, the hold on every unit, the event and the corrective actions, the inspector accepts it as timely and schedules a follow-up rather than an on-site visit today.
  - Concede only when: If the hospital confirms imported bags will be released only after the checklist, a second pharmacist check, nurse education and the pump library build, the inspector agrees the import use can continue.
- **Ryan Teasdale, assignment desk, North Iowa NewsChannel (local TV); the same Controller may play the health reporter from the Fort Dodge daily newspaper** — _Trigger: The PIO calls back about the 08:38 or 09:16 media slip (I-09, I-12b), or answers the newspaper reporter's email (I-03b), or the 10:24 Facebook post gets the station tagged (I-17b)._
  - Ask: ""Simple question: did Prairie Ridge run out of IV fluids, yes or no? I have a woman on camera saying it did.""
  - Ask: ""Is her dad's knee replacement Wednesday cancelled? How many surgeries are you cancelling?""
  - Ask: ""Which company's plant went down? Everyone online is saying the name. Confirm it or we'll report what's out there.""
  - Ask: ""I need something by 15:00 to edit for the 17:00 package. Can someone go on camera, and who?""
  - Concede only when: If the PIO gives a statement before the deadline, the reporter runs the hospital's numbers and the line 'emergency, urgent surgery and obstetric care continue' next to the family's claim. The statement must say a national shortage, emergency, urgent surgery and obstetric care continue, some elective procedures may be rescheduled, who patients should call, and when the next update is.
  - Concede only when: If the PIO offers a named spokesperson and a time for the next update, the reporter drops the request to name the manufacturer for tonight's package.
- **Dr. Marcus Feld, ED attending physician** — _Trigger: The CMO or Director of Pharmacy returns the I-07b call, or players announce restrictions or an allocation that affects the ED._
  - Ask: ""I've got a septic patient in Bay 6 who needs a bolus. Who exactly do I call at 3 a.m. when the pocket is locked? Give me a name and an extension, not a policy.""
  - Ask: ""You say the ED is a priority. What percent of normal use am I getting? Why is that number different from what the charge nurse just told me?""
  - Ask: ""If you won't give me unrestricted fluids, put the restriction in writing with the CMO's name on it.""
  - Concede only when: If the CMO explains the clinical priority tier in writing, with a guaranteed override path for sepsis and resuscitation, the attending accepts the restriction on non-emergent uses. That includes no KVO and oral hydration where possible.
  - Concede only when: Once players send the single page with days on hand, the allocation percent and the next update time, the attending agrees to brief the ED physicians at the next huddle using those numbers.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the exercise. Go around the room one team at a time (pharmacy, nursing, OR and anesthesia, PIO, command) and write every item on the whiteboard in two columns before anyone discusses them.

**Key Discussion Questions for Forty Percent:**
- **Trust:** At I-01 the 07:00 perpetual inventory showed 9.0 days on hand for NS 1000 mL. At the stockroom door, Tyler Bergstrom told you the back pallets looked light and the dock pallets hadn't been scanned. At I-08 the lot recount dropped the figure to 2,560 bags and 7.5 days, and the dock pallets turned out to be D5W. When did you stop trusting the system figure? Which number did you use for your Level 3 recommendation and your percent-of-normal-use allocation? What would have made you order a physical count before you signed off on the morning report? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **The Call:** At I-12, Dr. Lucas Hendrickx and Denise Aldrich needed a yes or no by 11:00 on Wednesday's 11 elective cases. Their projection showed NS dropping to 2.96 days on hand by Wednesday morning. Did you decide against the 3-days-on-hand trigger, or against the pressure from patients who had arranged rides, rehab beds and family travel? Who signed the written deferral? What information would have let you decide before anesthesia had to ask? _(Objective: Resource Management)_
- **The Gap:** The 6 West rate error and the imported 500 mL bags tested whether a substitution can reach the 22 dispensing cabinets and 240 smart pumps before the pump library, labels and nursing education are ready. Did you run the Medication Safety Officer's checklist, including the second pharmacist check, before the Webster Valley loan and the imported product were released? When the rate error came in, did you treat it as evidence that the substitution process had failed, or as an incident ticket to close? Did pharmacy and the bedside nurses understand each other on bag volume and pump settings? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Offer:** You were offered 400 cases from a secondary supplier at three times the price, with no lot list. You were also negotiating a loan from neighboring hospitals. How did you decide whether to accept or refuse the 400 cases? Who owned that decision? Was the Webster Valley loan agreement documented before any product moved? When did you bring in the wholesaler, the neighboring hospitals and the Iowa Department of Public Health, which wanted your status by noon? Would earlier contact have changed your options? _(Objective: Resource Management)_
- **One Message:** By mid-morning, staff were working from conflicting instructions. Memo A (6 West) said no KVO and one prefilled flush syringe. Memo B (ICU) said to draw flushes from a bedside 250 mL bag and keep KVO running. At the same time, the public saw the 'RUN OUT of IV fluids' post at I-09 and the 'foreign IV bag' post at I-17b. North Iowa NewsChannel wanted someone on camera at noon, and patient relations was taking calls. What did you tell staff, physicians and the public before you had confirmed numbers? Did all three groups get the same days-on-hand figures? How long did it take to get a corrected statement signed off by the Incident Commander? _(Objective: Communication)_

# Player Briefing Packet
## Unmarked Line — Gas Strike, Trench Collapse & Adjacent Structures

Location: Stillwater Creek Interceptor project (trench stations 14+00 to 18+00 and the east staging yard), Larkspur Constructors, Inc., Dayton, Ohio · Exercise Duration: 4 Hours
Current Time: 09:45 AM (Exercise Time) · Weather: Overcast, 52°F, light wind from the west-southwest at about 8 mph; ground saturated after 0.6 inch of rain overnight.

### 1. Situation Brief

**EXERCISE — NOT A SAFETY EVALUATION OF ANY REAL STRUCTURE.** It is 09:45 on Wednesday, April 14, 2027, at the Stillwater Creek Interceptor Phase 2, east of downtown Dayton. This is a full production day and the site is busy. About 70 workers have signed the daily board at the trailer: Larkspur crews plus Pike & Marlow Paving, Hollis Electric and Cascade Dewatering. The pipe crew is laying 48-inch pipe in the 13-foot trench between stations 14+00 and 18+00. They work from the 10-by-20-foot trench box in Type C soil, and Excavator E-7 digs ahead of the box toward 16+00. Rain overnight has left the ground soft and the dewatering pumps are working hard. Deliveries are queued at the east gate (Ninth Street and the rail spur), and the west gate at Corliss Avenue and Ninth Street is handling foot traffic. Next to the alignment, the Harlan & Weir print shop, a four-unit apartment house, the Ninth Street Church hall preschool and the Little Acorn Learning play yard are open as usual. The crack gauges on the print shop wall are reporting to the trailer 2 monitoring display. At 06:45 the competent person signed form LC-F-22, clearing the trench for work with one note: "seepage, south wall, 16+00." Site Superintendent Marcus Hale and Safety Director Priya Venkataraman are on site. The Project Manager, the Project Executive and the Engineer of Record can be reached by phone.

- Trench 14+00 to 18+00 and protective system (trench box, two hydraulic shores, standby excavator at the east staging yard): GREEN: LC-F-22 signed at 06:45 with a seepage note at 16+00; box in use; spare shores, box and excavator staged
- Adjacent buildings: Harlan & Weir print shop, four-unit apartment house, Ninth Street Church hall preschool, Little Acorn Learning play yard: GREEN: occupied and operating normally
- Crack gauges CG-1 / CG-2 / CG-3 on trailer 2 monitoring display: GREEN: 0.03 in / 0.05 in / 0.09 in (alarm 0.12 in, action 0.25 in), logging every 15 minutes
- Dewatering pumps and vibration controls: GREEN: Cascade 12-inch pump running at the 15+20 sump; vibration monitors reporting
- Site radios (channels 1 and 2), emergency horn and daily sign-in board: GREEN: radios checked at the morning huddle; horn tested; about 70 workers signed in
- Gates, staffing and public line (west gate Corliss Ave & Ninth St, east gate Ninth St & rail spur; community hotline): GREEN: both gates staffed; superintendent and Safety Director on site; hotline quiet

**The Anomaly:** **09:55 AM — Radio call, channel 1, Cascade Dewatering pump technician to the Superintendent and the Competent Person**

> "Super, this is Cascade at the 15+20 sump. The 12-inch pump is running flat out and the sump level has come up 8 inches since seven o'clock. I can't keep up with what's coming through that south wall around 16+00. I'm asking for a second pump, but it's a 3-hour delivery."

Excavator E-7 is still digging ahead of the trench box toward 16+00.

**YOUR MISSION:** Keep the Stillwater Creek Interceptor running safely and on schedule while you investigate and resolve any problem in the trench, on the site or at the adjacent buildings. You have to weigh production against stopping work, quick decisions against confirmed data, and the needs of the job against the safety of workers and neighbors. Follow LC-EAP-01, the stop-work rules in LETPS 9 and the notification clocks in LPONRC 3.

### 3. Rules of Engagement

- Start and end every radio call, phone call and face-to-face report with "This is an exercise." The trailers, the crews on site and the neighbors must never mistake a play message for a real emergency.
- If a real emergency happens, anyone who sees it says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." The exercise stops at once so that real-world safety comes first.
- The facilitator may announce "Time Jump: it is now [time]" to move the exercise forward. When that happens, update your clocks, logs and notification deadlines to the new time.
- Treat every printed inject as real intelligence and act on it: radio calls, posts, logs, photos, dashboard readings and reports. Use it to make decisions as you would on a live job.

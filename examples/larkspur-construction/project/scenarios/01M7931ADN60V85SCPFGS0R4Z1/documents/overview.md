# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Unmarked Line** (Gas Strike, Trench Collapse & Adjacent Structures)
Location: Stillwater Creek Interceptor project (trench stations 14+00 to 18+00 and the east staging yard), Larkspur Constructors, Inc., Dayton, Ohio · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Site Superintendent, Safety Director, Project Manager, Project Executive (spokesperson), Competent Person (foreman) and the Engineer of Record (advisor to the team). The SimCell plays 911, Dayton Fire Rescue 2 and the battalion chief, Valley Gas, the OSHA area office, the building official, the owner's representative, the insurer, the media and neighbors.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Who stops work and who decides, a rescue the contractor must not attempt, protecting neighbors before responders arrive, and speaking to families and the public before the facts are complete.

**The Threat:** On a Wednesday in mid-April 2027, 0.6 inch of rain fell overnight. At 06:45 competent person Tomas Reyes inspected and cleared the 13-foot trench at stations 14+00 to 18+00 of the Stillwater Creek Interceptor. The trench uses a 10-by-20-foot trench box in Type C soil, and he noted seepage at 16+00. At 10:12 excavator E-7, run by operator Gordon Pruitt, tears a gas service line at station 16+00. The drawings show this line, but the field marks did not. Gas pours into the trench, and at 10:14 the 4-inch main upstream reads 55 psi. At 10:20 the south trench wall at 16+05 slumps and shifts the trench box. Pipe layer Luis Marquez is buried to the chest and a second worker climbs out injured. Vibration and dewatering continue to work on the Harlan & Weir print shop foundation 18 feet away. Crack gauge CG-3 passes its 0.12-inch alarm level at 10:24 and its 0.25-inch action level at 11:05.

**The Operational Impact:** All excavation stops. About 70 workers must move upwind and at least 300 feet from the gas. Engines within 100 feet shut down, and radios cannot be keyed in the gas area. This leaves foremen, laborers, ironworkers, crane operators and signal persons calling headcounts by voice while the buried pipe layer is out of reach. Larkspur crews must not dig into an unstable, gassed trench. The superintendent calls 911 with only cross streets to give, then waits for Dayton Fire Department Rescue 2 and a battalion chief to take command. He then hands them the shoring drawings, the soil classification record and the locate tickets. The shores, the spare trench box and the standby excavator in the east staging yard sit unused until the Incident Commander asks for them. Next door, the team must decide before the fire department arrives whether to evacuate the print shop (14 employees), the four-unit apartment house (about 9 residents), the Ninth Street Church hall preschool (22 children, 4 staff) and the Little Acorn Learning play yard. The church hall preschool's own muster point is in the gas standoff. Every 15 minutes the trailer dashboard shows CG-3 rising, against a print shop wall that already had a crack. When it reaches the action level, the building must be evacuated until the Engineer of Record and the Dayton building official decide. Only the building official can post the building INSPECTED, RESTRICTED USE or UNSAFE, which leaves owners and tenants waiting to learn if they can return. A local TV helicopter circles overhead. At 10:50 a bystander posts a video saying "sewer workers buried, gas everywhere," and calls start reaching the community hotline and the families. Extrication is at 12:10. The pipe layer is pronounced dead at the trauma center at 13:20, which starts the OSHA 8-hour fatality clock. The second worker's hospitalization has its own 24-hour clock, which starts when a supervisor first learned of it. The family must be told in person before any name is released.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to stop all excavation, move about 70 workers upwind and at least 300 feet from the gas, and decide before Dayton Fire arrives whether to evacuate the print shop, the apartment house, the church hall preschool and the Little Acorn play yard, including choosing a new muster point outside the gas standoff.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to follow LC-EAP-01 section 4 by keeping Larkspur crews out of the failed, gassed trench, handing the shoring drawings, soil classification record and locate tickets to the Rescue 2 Incident Commander, and committing the standby shores, spare trench box and excavator only when the Incident Commander asks for them on the Engineer of Record's advice.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to read crack gauge CG-3 at its 0.12-inch alarm and 0.25-inch action levels against the print shop foundation, notify the Dayton Division of Building Inspection under LPONRC section 3, and keep the print shop evacuated until the Engineer of Record and the building official decide, without Larkspur declaring any building INSPECTED, RESTRICTED USE or UNSAFE.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to run the LPONRC section 2 call sequence and the OSHA 8-hour fatality and 24-hour hospitalization clocks, tell the pipe layer's family in person before any name is released, and issue a holding statement through the Project Executive only, after the owner's representative is told, that answers the bystander video without guessing at the cause.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Site Superintendent (SUPT):** Manages the site from the gas strike until responders arrive: stops work, places the 911 and call-tree calls, orders neighbors out and serves as site liaison to the Rescue 2 Incident Commander.
- **Safety Director (SAFE):** Owns stop-work authority, the OSHA and building-official notifications, the incident log, scene preservation and the in-person family notification.
- **Project Manager (PM):** Manages the project-side response: owner notice, the written incident log kept by the PM's assistant, subcontractor coordination and the schedule and cost consequences of the stop.
- **Project Executive (spokesperson) (PE):** Owns the company response and is the only Larkspur voice to media, insurer and surety, speaking only after the owner's representative has been told.
- **Competent Person (foreman) (CP):** Manages the trench work area: inspects for hazards, removes workers from danger, takes the voice headcount and keeps people and vibration away from the failed trench.
- **Engineer of Record (advisor to the team) (EOR):** Manages structural advice to the team: the trench protective system, any shoring the Incident Commander requests, and the CG-3 readings on the print shop foundation.

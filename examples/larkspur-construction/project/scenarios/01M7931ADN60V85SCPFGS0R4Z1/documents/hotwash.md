# Hot Wash Guide
## Unmarked Line

### Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three that broke or failed during the response, with honest answers getting the same weight as the wins. Go around the room team by team (site supervision, safety, engineering, Project Executive and communications), write each item on the whiteboard in two columns, and don't debate anything until all six items are up.

**Key Discussion Questions for Unmarked Line:**
- **The Gap:** At I-01, before the strike, you had two warnings within 90 seconds. Cascade reported the sump up 8 inches and losing to the south wall at 16+00. Your field engineer then showed you a service tee on the Valley Gas drawing that wasn't marked on locate ticket 2711-4482, with E-7 about 10 feet away. LSUAP 4 already notes that the field marks didn't show the 16+00 service. When did you stop trusting the paint and start trusting the drawing? Who had the authority to stop E-7 at that moment, and did anyone use it? Did you treat the mismatch as a hazard that stops the work, or as a ticket problem to raise with the one-call center later? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **Who Stops Us:** At I-07b the laborer foreman asked for the standby excavator keys, and two laborers were pulling on gloves to go into a trench reading 18% LEL. Who stopped them, what exactly was said, and was it the superintendent, the competent person or the fire department? Under LC-EAP-01 section 4, Larkspur crews stay out of a failed, gassed trench. Did the room hold that line as a rule, or did it feel like a choice you were arguing about? What would have kept the crew from reaching that point, for example being told what Rescue 2 was doing and why? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Gauge:** CG-3 on the Harlan & Weir foundation passed its 0.12-inch alarm level at 10:24 and its 0.25-inch action level at 11:05. Walk us through what changed at each threshold. Who told the Engineer of Record and who called the Dayton Division of Building Inspection, and at what time? Under LETPS 7, all excavation and dewatering effects in the zone of influence are the issue, and the building stays evacuated until the EOR and the building official say otherwise. Did anyone on our side start to say the print shop was 'fine' or 'safe to re-enter'? Did the readings go into the incident log the way LPONRC 5 requires, with the data left unaltered? _(Objective: Technical Analysis)_
- **The Call:** At I-07 you faced a viral video claiming a 'clearly marked' gas line and several workers buried, 19 hotline calls including three possible relatives, and a Dayton Ledger reporter asking for names and the number dead by noon. What did you actually say to the public and to the relatives before you knew the facts? Who spoke: the Project Executive only, or others too? Was the owner's representative told first? Who was assigned to tell the pipe layer's family in person? When did the OSHA 8-hour and 24-hour clocks start, and who was tracking them? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The Utilities:** At I-12 the battalion chief asked for your spare box, your shores, crane C-2 and the crew to run it. The EOR would not approve tracking the crawler over the 12 kV Miami River Power duct bank at 17+20. That left two choices: wait about 45 minutes for the utility to de-energize, or take 35 to 40 minutes walking around by the west end. Meanwhile, water was rising about an inch every 5 minutes. When did you first call Miami River Power and Valley Gas: at the strike, or only when the crane route forced the question? Did you commit equipment only when the Incident Commander asked for it, based on the EOR's advice? What could you have prepared before 11:50, such as a crane route, a mat design or a de-energization request, so this decision took minutes instead of three-quarters of an hour? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_

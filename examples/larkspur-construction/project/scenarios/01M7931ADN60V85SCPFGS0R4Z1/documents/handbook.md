# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Unmarked Line — Gas Strike, Trench Collapse & Adjacent Structures

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Stillwater Creek Interceptor project (trench stations 14+00 to 18+00 and the east staging yard), Larkspur Constructors, Inc., Dayton, Ohio · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for Larkspur Constructors, Inc. on the Stillwater Creek Interceptor Phase 2. Players are in their real roles: Site Superintendent, Safety Director, Project Manager, Project Executive (spokesperson), Competent Person (foreman) and the Engineer of Record as an advisor. They talk through what they would decide, who they would call and what they would write down. Nobody moves equipment, contacts a real agency or evaluates a real structure. Every product in the inject deck is marked EXERCISE and is not a safety evaluation of any real structure.

The injects simulate an infrastructure failure on Wednesday, April 14, 2027. After an overnight rain, the excavator tears a gas service at station 16+00. The service is on the Valley Gas drawings but was not marked in the field. Then the south trench wall slumps and buries a pipe layer to the chest, and crack gauge CG-3 on the Harlan & Weir print shop climbs through its alarm and action levels. The 4 hours of play cover 09:45 to 13:45 on the exercise clock:
- **Phase 1, The Unmarked Line (0:00–1:00):** early warnings, the strike, the collapse, and the exercise's one neighbor-evacuation decision, made before Dayton Fire arrives.
- **Phase 2, Command Passes to Fire (1:00–2:00):** handover to the Rescue 2 battalion chief, holding resources until they are requested, and CG-3 reaching its action level.
- **Phase 3, Extrication and Families (2:00–3:00):** the crane-route decision, extrication, families at the gate, the press, and the second worker's in-patient admission.
- **Phase 4, Fatality and Restart Hold (3:00–4:00):** the fatality, the in-person family notification, OSHA and owner notices, and the pressure to restart at 18+00. ENDEX follows.

Time jumps open Phases 2, 3 and 4.

Evaluators should watch for these things:
- **Who stops work and who decides.** The competent person, superintendent or Safety Director stops work. The Engineer of Record decides structural adequacy. Only the building official posts a building (LPONRC 3).
- **No self-rescue.** Larkspur must not attempt a rescue in a failed, gassed trench (LC-EAP-01 section 4).
- **Neighbors before responders.** Watch whether the neighbors get out before Dayton Fire arrives.
- **The reporting clocks.** The OSHA hospitalization clock starts when the foreman learned of the admission at 11:25, not when the Safety Director hears of it. The 8-hour fatality clock starts at 13:24.
- **Records.** Watch whether the incident log and scene records are kept as found (LPONRC 5), including refusing to alter form LC-F-22.
- **Public communication.** Watch whether the team speaks to families and the public with empathy and accuracy before the facts are complete, without releasing a name before the family is told in person.

**Objectives:**
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to stop all excavation, move about 70 workers upwind and at least 300 feet from the gas, and decide before Dayton Fire arrives whether to evacuate the print shop, the apartment house, the church hall preschool and the Little Acorn play yard, including choosing a new muster point outside the gas standoff.
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to follow LC-EAP-01 section 4 by keeping Larkspur crews out of the failed, gassed trench, handing the shoring drawings, soil classification record and locate tickets to the Rescue 2 Incident Commander, and committing the standby shores, spare trench box and excavator only when the Incident Commander asks for them on the Engineer of Record's advice.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to read crack gauge CG-3 at its 0.12-inch alarm and 0.25-inch action levels against the print shop foundation, notify the Dayton Division of Building Inspection under LPONRC section 3, and keep the print shop evacuated until the Engineer of Record and the building official decide, without Larkspur declaring any building INSPECTED, RESTRICTED USE or UNSAFE.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to run the LPONRC section 2 call sequence and the OSHA 8-hour fatality and 24-hour hospitalization clocks, tell the pipe layer's family in person before any name is released, and issue a holding statement through the Project Executive only, after the owner's representative is told, that answers the bystander video without guessing at the cause.

**Plan sections exercised:**

- LETPS › 7. Monitoring of adjacent structures

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

Set the room as **islands**, not one table. The distance between islands is what makes players run messages, the way they would on site.

- **Island 1 – Site trailer 2 (monitoring station):** Team A (Site Superintendent), Team B (Safety Director) and Team E (Competent Person). Put a laptop or printed "trailer 2 display" here for the CG-3 readings, and hang a paper sign-in board for headcount play.
- **Island 2 – Project office / trailer 4:** Team C (Project Manager) and Team F (Engineer of Record, advisor). Seat the EOR so they must be phoned or walked to. They are not standing at the trench.
- **Island 3 – Ninth Street Command (west gate, Corliss Avenue and Ninth Street):** an empty table with a "COMMAND – Rescue 2 Battalion Chief" tent card. The SimCell plays the battalion chief here. From Phase 2 on, the superintendent or a named liaison must physically sit at this table to work with fire.
- **Rear table – Public information:** Team D (Project Executive, spokesperson) sits alone at the back of the room. **Why:** in the real event the spokesperson is not in the trailer where facts arrive. Putting this player at a distance forces them to walk over, ask, and confirm before speaking. That exposes whether the team actually feeds the spokesperson verified facts, and whether the owner's representative is told first. Do not let other players shout updates across the room.
- **SimCell / Controller station:** a front corner with the inject deck, a phone and a radio. It should have a clear sightline to all islands.
- **Evaluators:** one per island where possible, seated slightly behind the players with an evaluation guide and a clock synced to the exercise time.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and screen for main-screen injects: STARTEX and time-jump cards, CG-3 alarm displays, the social media posts, the Dayton Ledger article and the RESTRICTED USE placard.
- A whiteboard or flip chart set up as the ICS-201 (incident briefing: map sketch, objectives, resources, organization). Players keep it current.
- A printed inject deck in MSEL order, with a controller copy and one slip per inject for hand delivery.
- Optional: simulated radios (channel 1 general, channel 2 emergency) and two desk phones or mobiles for SimCell calls.
- A wall clock showing exercise time, plus printed time-jump cards.
- Paper for the incident log and call log, plus a "REAL WORLD" card at the controller station.

**Materials for players:** LC-EAP-01 (Project Emergency Action Plan), LPONRC (notifications and records), LETPS (trench protection), LSUAP (site utility and access plan), the route plan showing stations 14+00 to 18+00, locate ticket 2711-4482, a blank form LC-F-22, a blank ICS-201, an incident log sheet, and a contact list.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role: SimCell**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the deputy. On this exercise that means you are:
- the 911 dispatcher and the Rescue 2 battalion chief
- the Valley Gas dispatcher and crew foreman
- the Ohio811 damage desk
- the OSHA area office duty officer
- the Dayton Division of Building Inspection inspector
- the Stillwater Regional Sewer District owner's representative and PIO
- the Channel 9 assignment desk and the Dayton Ledger reporter
- the trauma center charge nurse and chaplain
- the neighbors, families, subcontractors and suppliers

Answer as that person would, in their interest, with only what they would know. If players call someone not in this handbook, improvise from that person's own interest and keep it short.

**Do not make it easy**
Real outside parties are busy, partly informed and protective of their own interests. Do not hand players the answer.

Example from this scenario: when the superintendent calls Valley Gas at the strike (I-02), the dispatcher does not say "a crew is on the way." The dispatcher asks:
- Is it a main or a service? What is the exact address and cross street?
- What is the locate ticket number?
- Have you called 911, and is anyone in the gas area?

Then the dispatcher gives a vague ETA ("about 35 minutes") that later slips (I-10). If the team never got a call reference or never followed up, that is the gap the evaluator records.

In the same way, when the laborer foreman demands the excavator keys (I-07b), do not back down after one "no." Push once more ("He's drowning, are you going to stand there?"). Make the competent person and superintendent hold the line under LC-EAP-01 section 4.

**Managing time**
Keep to the MSEL. If a discussion runs long, let it reach a decision or a clear "we don't know," then move on. At each time jump (I-06, I-11, I-16), stand, put the card on the main screen and read:

> "PAUSE EXERCISE. We are jumping forward 30 minutes. It is now [time on card]. Here is what has happened in that time. [Read the card.] Anything you said you would do before the pause has been done, unless I tell you otherwise. RESUME."

(The I-06, I-11 and I-16 cards each move the clock about an hour on the exercise timeline. Read the time printed on the card.)

If players are far behind, have an outside party force the issue. For example, the battalion chief asks, "Who is my Larkspur liaison?"

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "We don't do it that way":

> "Noted, and that is worth raising at the hot wash. Write it on your parking-lot sheet. For today, use what the plan says. If you would use something else, tell me what it is, who it reaches and how fast, and we'll play it that way."

Do not argue about whether the scenario is realistic. Write it down and continue.

**Delivering injects**
- Do not explain.
- Hand the slip over and say "This just came in," or point and say "This is on the main screen."
- For phone or radio injects, read them aloud in character to the named team only.
- Do not tell players which document or section applies, and do not hint at the "right" answer.
- If a player asks you what something means, answer only as the character would.

**Safety conventions**
- Every call you place or answer as SimCell starts with **"This is an exercise."** Every printed product carries "EXERCISE — NOT A SAFETY EVALUATION OF ANY REAL STRUCTURE."
- If a real emergency or real-world issue arises (a medical problem in the room, a real site incident, a real call), anyone says **"REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD"** and play stops at once. The controller handles the real issue. Resume only when the controller says so.
- Players must not call real agencies, utilities, media or the real 1-800-321-OSHA line during play.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three that broke or failed during the response, with honest answers getting the same weight as the wins. Go around the room team by team (site supervision, safety, engineering, Project Executive and communications), write each item on the whiteboard in two columns, and don't debate anything until all six items are up.

**Key Discussion Questions for Unmarked Line:**
- **The Gap:** At I-01, before the strike, you had two warnings within 90 seconds. Cascade reported the sump up 8 inches and losing to the south wall at 16+00. Your field engineer then showed you a service tee on the Valley Gas drawing that wasn't marked on locate ticket 2711-4482, with E-7 about 10 feet away. LSUAP 4 already notes that the field marks didn't show the 16+00 service. When did you stop trusting the paint and start trusting the drawing? Who had the authority to stop E-7 at that moment, and did anyone use it? Did you treat the mismatch as a hazard that stops the work, or as a ticket problem to raise with the one-call center later? _(Objective: Decision Making)_
- **Who Stops Us:** At I-07b the laborer foreman asked for the standby excavator keys, and two laborers were pulling on gloves to go into a trench reading 18% LEL. Who stopped them, what exactly was said, and was it the superintendent, the competent person or the fire department? Under LC-EAP-01 section 4, Larkspur crews stay out of a failed, gassed trench. Did the room hold that line as a rule, or did it feel like a choice you were arguing about? What would have kept the crew from reaching that point, for example being told what Rescue 2 was doing and why? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_
- **The Gauge:** CG-3 on the Harlan & Weir foundation passed its 0.12-inch alarm level at 10:24 and its 0.25-inch action level at 11:05. Walk us through what changed at each threshold. Who told the Engineer of Record and who called the Dayton Division of Building Inspection, and at what time? Under LETPS 7, all excavation and dewatering effects in the zone of influence are the issue, and the building stays evacuated until the EOR and the building official say otherwise. Did anyone on our side start to say the print shop was 'fine' or 'safe to re-enter'? Did the readings go into the incident log the way LPONRC 5 requires, with the data left unaltered? _(Objective: Technical Analysis)_
- **The Call:** At I-07 you faced a viral video claiming a 'clearly marked' gas line and several workers buried, 19 hotline calls including three possible relatives, and a Dayton Ledger reporter asking for names and the number dead by noon. What did you actually say to the public and to the relatives before you knew the facts? Who spoke: the Project Executive only, or others too? Was the owner's representative told first? Who was assigned to tell the pipe layer's family in person? When did the OSHA 8-hour and 24-hour clocks start, and who was tracking them? _(Objective: Communication)_
- **The Utilities:** At I-12 the battalion chief asked for your spare box, your shores, crane C-2 and the crew to run it. The EOR would not approve tracking the crawler over the 12 kV Miami River Power duct bank at 17+20. That left two choices: wait about 45 minutes for the utility to de-energize, or take 35 to 40 minutes walking around by the west end. Meanwhile, water was rising about an inch every 5 minutes. When did you first call Miami River Power and Valley Gas: at the strike, or only when the crane route forced the question? Did you commit equipment only when the Incident Commander asked for it, based on the EOR's advice? What could you have prepared before 11:50, such as a crane route, a mat design or a de-energization request, so this decision took minutes instead of three-quarters of an hour? _(Objective: Use of Doctrine)_

### Toolkits used

This exercise draws on field expertise from these toolkits. Their terms, product formats and realism rules shaped the injects.

- **Structures and building safety**: Site inspection records, incident and stop-work orders, OSHA report records, structural monitoring and post-disaster building evaluation: rapid and detailed evaluation forms, assignment logs and the INSPECTED, RESTRICTED USE and UNSAFE placards. For contractors, engineers, building departments and every organization that has a building, a trench or a crane in a hazard.
  - Reference documents: 29 CFR 1904.39: Reporting fatalities, hospitalizations, amputations and losses of an eye; 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P: Excavations; 29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC: Cranes and Derricks in Construction; A Guide to Scaffold Use in the Construction Industry (OSHA 3150); Trenching and Excavation Safety (OSHA 2226); FEMA P-2055, Post-disaster Building Safety Evaluation Guidance; NSPE Code of Ethics for Engineers; ASCE Code of Ethics
- **Media environment**: The news cycle and social feeds around an incident: wire alerts, articles and broadcast segments, social posts whose reach and sentiment change over time, reporter calls and press conference questions, and verification tasks with provenance clues. Simulated spread and sentiment are teaching models, not predictions. All outlets, handles and people are fictional.

  - Reference documents: FCC report on the Hawaii false alert (2018): a correction case study

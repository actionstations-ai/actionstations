# LC-SOP-07: Excavation, Trenching and Protective Systems

Larkspur Constructors, Inc. · Revision 5 · Approved by Priya Venkataraman, Safety Director, and Helen Brandvold, PE

## 1. Purpose

This procedure controls every excavation of 4 feet or more on Larkspur projects and every excavation, of any depth, in which a worker could be exposed to a cave-in. It paraphrases the OSHA excavation standard, 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P, and adds the project's own rules. Where this SOP is stricter than the standard, this SOP governs.

## 2. The competent person

Each shift, the foreman named in the Daily Excavation Plan acts as the competent person. On the Stillwater Creek Interceptor that is Tomas Reyes (alternate: Imani Duclos). The competent person is trained in soil classification and protective systems, and has authority to remove workers from an excavation and to stop work. The competent person inspects, in writing on form LC-F-22:

- before work starts each shift,
- after every rainstorm of 0.25 inch or more, after a thaw, or after any event that could increase hazards (a vibration source, a utility strike, a change in the water level),
- as the trench advances, at least every 50 feet.

If an inspection finds evidence of a possible cave-in, a failure of the protective system, hazardous atmosphere or other hazard, workers leave the excavation until precautions are in place.

## 3. Soil classification

The competent person classifies soil by a visual test and at least one manual test (pocket penetrometer, thumb test, dry strength test). On this project the geotechnical report found sandy clay fill over layers of saturated silty sand along stations 12+00 to 20+00, which the Engineer of Record has told us to treat as Type C, the least stable class. Water seeping into the trench downgrades the soil one class. Soil is reclassified after any rain.

## 4. Protective systems

Every excavation 5 feet deep or more has a protective system: sloping, benching, shoring or a shield (trench box). For Type C soil the maximum allowable slope is 1.5 horizontal to 1 vertical. On this project the trench is too close to buildings for sloping, so a trench box or hydraulic shoring is used throughout stations 10+00 to 25+00.

- The trench box is installed per the manufacturer's tabulated data, which stays on site at the trailer. The box is within 2 feet of the bottom of the trench. The box is never moved with a person inside.
- The Engineer of Record designs any system outside the tabulated data, any trench deeper than 20 feet, and any system next to a building, and signs the design before use.
- Spoil and equipment are kept at least 2 feet from the trench edge, and further for heavy equipment; the project rule is 4 feet for spoil piles and 10 feet for loaded trucks.

## 5. Access and egress

A ladder, ramp or stairway is within 25 feet of lateral travel for every worker in a trench 4 feet or deeper. Ladders extend 3 feet above the edge and are secured.

## 6. Underground utilities

Before any excavation:

1. The one-call center (Ohio811) is called at least 2 working days before digging; the ticket number and the response from each member utility are recorded on the Daily Excavation Plan.
2. Marks are checked against the utility drawings. The superintendent walks the route with the competent person.
3. Where a mark is within 18 inches of the planned excavation, the utility is exposed by hand tools or vacuum excavation. Machine digging does not start within 18 inches of a marked line.
4. If the work reaches a utility that was not marked, or is marked wrongly, digging stops, the line is protected, and the superintendent calls the utility and the one-call center.

## 7. Monitoring of adjacent structures

Where an excavation lies within the zone of influence of a building (a distance equal to twice the trench depth from the trench), the project installs instruments, in the monitoring plan approved by the Engineer of Record. On this project: crack gauges on the Harlan & Weir print shop foundation wall (CG-1, CG-2, CG-3) and survey prisms on the church hall and the apartment house, read automatically every 15 minutes and displayed on the monitoring dashboard in the trailer.

| Instrument | Alarm level | Action level |
|---|---|---|
| Crack gauges CG-1 to CG-3 (change in crack width) | 0.12 inch | 0.25 inch |
| Survey prisms, settlement | 0.25 inch | 0.50 inch |

At the alarm level the superintendent is notified and the Engineer of Record reviews. At the action level, all excavation within the zone of influence stops, workers leave the trench, and the building is evacuated until the Engineer of Record and the building official say otherwise. The data log is never altered or deleted.

## 8. Hazardous atmospheres

Excavations deeper than 4 feet are tested for oxygen and combustible gas where the area is known to have a hazard, including any time a gas smell is reported. A meter reading of 10 percent or more of the lower explosive limit removes everyone from the trench.

## 9. Stopping work

Anyone may stop work. The competent person, the superintendent and the Safety Director must stop it for any of these: a failed protective system, evidence of a cave-in, an alarm-level monitoring reading with no explanation, a utility strike, a hazardous atmosphere, water entering the trench faster than pumps can remove it. Work restarts only on the written release of the superintendent after the Safety Director's agreement, and after the Engineer of Record agrees where a protective system was involved.

## 10. Records

The Daily Excavation Plan, form LC-F-22, the soil classification, the one-call tickets and the monitoring log are kept for the life of the project plus 5 years. After any incident they are copied and sealed by the Safety Director.

# LC-EAP-01: Project Emergency Action Plan, Stillwater Creek Interceptor, Phase 2

Larkspur Constructors, Inc. · Dayton, Ohio · Project 26-114 · Revision 3 · Effective March 1, 2027

## 1. Purpose and scope

This plan tells every Larkspur employee and subcontractor on the Stillwater Creek Interceptor Phase 2 project what to do when someone is hurt, an excavation fails, a utility is struck, or an adjacent building is threatened. It applies to the pipe corridor (stations 0+00 to 38+00), the staging yard, the trailer compound and the three pump station shafts. The owner is the Stillwater Regional Sewer District. The work is the replacement of 3,800 feet of 36-inch sewer with 48-inch pipe, laid in an open trench 10 to 14 feet deep through a built-up neighborhood east of downtown Dayton.

Any person on the site may stop work they believe is unsafe. The competent person, the superintendent and the Safety Director may stop all work in an area and no one may restart it except the superintendent after the Safety Director agrees. The Engineer of Record alone decides whether a failed or damaged protective system is safe to reuse. Only the City of Dayton building official declares a neighboring building unsafe.

## 3. First five minutes

1. **Stop work** in the affected area by radio, channel 2: "All stop, [location]." Equipment is shut down and operators leave machines in neutral with buckets on the ground.
2. **Call 911** from the nearest supervisor's phone. Say: Larkspur Constructors, Stillwater Creek Interceptor, the station number or the cross street, what happened, the number of people trapped or hurt, and any gas smell. Give the site gate. The site has no street address that dispatch recognizes, so always give the cross streets: Corliss Avenue and Ninth Street (west gate) and Ninth Street and the rail spur (east gate).
3. **Account for people.** Foremen count their crews against the daily sign-in board at the trailer. The superintendent announces the count and who is unaccounted for.
4. **Control the area.** Place the exclusion zone (section 5) with barricades and a person at each gate to meet responders.
5. **Notify** the Project Manager and Safety Director by phone, then follow the notification list in LC-EAP-03.

## 4. Cave-in and trapped worker

Rescue of a person buried in an excavation is a technical rescue. Larkspur employees do not enter a failed trench to dig. The first people on site take only these actions: call 911; keep everyone out of and away from the trench edge; shut down equipment and keep vibration away (no trucks within 50 feet); lower a ladder to the person if the trench wall is stable; keep the person's face clear if a coworker can reach from outside the failed section. Rescuers from Dayton Fire Department technical rescue take command of the rescue. The superintendent becomes the site liaison to the Incident Commander and hands over the shoring drawings, the soil classification record and the utility locate tickets.

Shoring is added only on the Incident Commander's request and with the Engineer of Record's advice. Larkspur keeps two aluminum hydraulic shores and a 10-by-20-foot steel trench box on the site for this purpose, with an excavator and operator on standby at the east staging yard.

## 5. Exclusion zone

The default exclusion zone for a trench failure is the failed section plus 50 feet in each direction along the trench and 25 feet to each side. If a gas line has been struck it is the larger of that zone or the area decided by the fire department, and typically includes the adjacent buildings on the north side of the trench. Barricades and tape are in the yellow conex at the east yard.

## 6. Utility strike

A strike on a gas line, electric line, water main or sewer force main is an emergency. Stop work, move people upwind and away at least 300 feet from a suspected gas release, keep ignition sources off (engines running within 100 feet are shut down, no radios keyed in the gas area), call 911 and the utility's emergency number, and call the one-call center to open a damage report. See LC-EAP-02 and LC-EAP-04 for the locate records and the utility emergency contacts.

## 7. Adjacent buildings

Five occupied buildings lie within 60 feet of the trench: a two-story brick print shop (Harlan & Weir Printing), a four-unit apartment house, a church hall, a corner tavern and a day-care center. If a monitoring alarm, a utility strike or a collapse threatens them, the superintendent orders the occupants out and meets them at the muster point in the church parking lot. The superintendent asks the fire department to take over the evacuation as soon as units arrive. The building official, not Larkspur, declares any building unsafe.

## 8. Injury reports and clocks

Larkspur reports a work-related fatality to OSHA within 8 hours of learning of it, and an in-patient hospitalization, amputation or loss of an eye within 24 hours, by telephone to the OSHA area office or the national number, or through the OSHA online form. The Safety Director makes the report. The owner is notified by the Project Manager within one hour of any serious injury or any event that stops the critical path. The insurer's claims line is called by the Project Executive within 24 hours. The detail is in LC-EAP-03.

## 9. After the emergency

Nothing at the scene is cleaned up or moved before the Safety Director and the responders agree it can be, except to save a life. Photograph, record, and keep the shoring, the soil logs, the daily inspection forms, and the monitoring data exactly as they are. Interviews of employees by the OSHA compliance officer are scheduled by the Safety Director; subcontractors are told they may have their own counsel present.

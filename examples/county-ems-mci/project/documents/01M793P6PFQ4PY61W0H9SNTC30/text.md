# Larkfield County EMS: Mass Casualty Incident Plan

Larkfield County Emergency Medical Services (LCEMS) · Medical Director Dr. Samir Anand · Revision 8 · Effective September 2026

## 1. Purpose and scope

This plan guides LCEMS, the fire departments and the hospitals of Larkfield County, Wisconsin, in the first hours of a mass casualty incident (MCI): an event with more patients than the first-arriving units can treat with normal procedures. It sets the declaration, command structure, triage method, transport rules and communications. The plan is a county protocol under the authority of the LCEMS Medical Director.

## 2. Definitions and levels

An MCI is declared when 6 or more patients are reported, or when the number of patients exceeds the resources first dispatched.

| Level | Patients | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | 6 to 10 | Local plan: 4 additional ambulances, one supervisor |
| Level 2 | 11 to 30 | Local plan with regional notification: all county ALS units, mutual aid from the neighboring counties' EMS agencies, air medical, the Medical Communications (MEDCOM) position at the hospital |
| Level 3 | More than 30 | Regional plan; request state resources through the regional healthcare coalition and Wisconsin Emergency Management |

The first arriving ALS or BLS crew declares ("Larkfield, MCI declared, estimated 25 patients, Level 2") and records the declaring unit, the time and the estimated number. The dispatcher repeats the declaration on all channels and starts the incident on the unit status board.

## 3. Command structure

The first-arriving fire officer is Incident Commander (IC) until relieved. Under the IC, the Medical Group Supervisor (an EMS supervisor, usually Deputy Chief Luis Navarro or the on-duty shift supervisor) directs: Triage Unit Leader, Treatment Unit Leader, Transportation Unit Leader and Medical Communications Coordinator (the person speaking to hospitals, at the hospital's MEDCOM or on the radio). Staging is under the Staging Area Manager. Hazard and rescue (fire) direct extrication; EMS enters only when the scene is safe.

Command transfer happens by face-to-face or by radio with a briefing: situation, resources, hazards, objectives, the time of transfer and the units now under the new commander. Both commanders record the time.

## 4. Triage method

Larkfield County uses the START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment) algorithm for adults and JumpSTART for children in the field. The regional healthcare coalition is moving to SALT (Sort, Assess, Lifesaving interventions, Treatment/Transport), and the Medical Director may order SALT at a given incident; the IC and the Triage Unit Leader state the method on the radio so all crews apply the same one. Patients are tagged Immediate (red), Delayed (yellow), Minor (green), Expectant (gray) or Deceased (black). Each tag has a number, which is used for the patient list and transport log.

The target is to complete the first triage pass of all patients in 15 to 20 minutes after the first triage unit is on scene. The Triage Unit Leader reports complete: time and counts of Immediate, Delayed, Minor and Expectant patients. Counts are reported to the IC, who passes them to MEDCOM; the PIO uses only counts released by the IC.

## 5. Treatment and transport

Treatment areas are set up by color: Immediate (red) closest to the loading area, Delayed, Minor. Transportation assigns each patient a destination using the destination rules in SOP-MC-02 and records the tag number, priority, hospital, mode (ground ambulance, air ambulance, bus or other), time and the reason for this destination. Minor patients go by bus (Larkfield County Transit has four buses on call) to a minor injury site, not to the trauma center. The Transportation Unit Leader does not send the first patient to the nearest hospital by default; the destination follows the injury and the hospital's status.

## 6. Hospital notification and medical communications

The Medical Communications Coordinator calls Larkfield Regional Medical Center (LRMC) MEDCOM (920-555-0190, the dedicated MEDCOM radio channel MED 1) and reads the incident, the method, the counts and the expected arrivals. LRMC MEDCOM keeps the capacity board for the other hospitals (see SOP-MC-02) and tells the coordinator which hospitals are Open, Limited intake, Diversion or Closed. The coalition coordinator (Greg Wilmot, 920-555-0192) is notified for Level 2 and above.

## 7. Public information and reunification

The PIO (Sandra Kowal, LCEMS) or the county Public Information Officer opens a Joint Information Center within 45 minutes. The only casualty counts released are those approved by the IC and confirmed with MEDCOM; the PIO says the count may change. A reunification center is opened at Brandt Lake High School gymnasium (family members are not sent to the hospitals), staffed by the Red Cross and the Sheriff's victim services; a call-in line 920-555-0195 is posted. The Medical Examiner (Dr. Bernard Okoro) is notified at once when any patient is tagged deceased and controls the bodies.

## 8. After action

The IC calls a hot wash at the scene within 24 hours; LCEMS reviews the patient list, the destination fit and the times in the incident report; hospital counts, triage counts and public counts must agree and any discrepancy is explained in the after action report.

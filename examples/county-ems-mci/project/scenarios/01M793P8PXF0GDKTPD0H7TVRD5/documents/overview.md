# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Open on Paper** (Hospital Capacity Board & MCI Patient Flow)
Location: Highway 21 and County Road K intersection near Brandt Lake, Larkfield County (with Larkfield Regional Medical Center (LRMC) MEDCOM, Brandt Lake, and the Brandt Lake High School reunification center) · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Incident Commander / Fire Battalion Chief; Medical Group Supervisor with Triage and Transportation Unit Leaders; Dispatch Supervisor; Hospital Medical Communications (MEDCOM) Nurse; Healthcare Coalition Coordinator; Public Information Officer. The SimCell plays the other hospitals' MEDCOM nurses, air medical crews, neighboring EMS agencies, State Patrol, Medical Examiner, Red Cross, the bus and fuel companies, families and reporters.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Trust in hospital status data when the capacity board and a key scanner fail. Getting patients to the right hospital under hard bed limits. Keeping command, accountability and the casualty count on the radio and in public statements the same across all three.

**The Threat:** Before STARTEX, St. Brendan's Community Hospital's CT scanner failed, so the hospital went to Limited intake. LRMC was already at 21 of 24 ED beds because of a respiratory surge. At 17:42 on Tuesday 20 October 2026, at dusk in steady rain, a Tri-County Coach Lines charter bus (31 passengers and a driver) and a fuel tanker collide near the Highway 21 and County Road K intersection, and a small car is also involved. The bus ends up on its side against the ditch, the tanker leaks diesel, about 28 patients are reported and seven are trapped. During the MCI, the hospital capacity board (the shared web page that LRMC MEDCOM keeps) shows one hospital as open to arrivals when it is actually on Diversion. This breaks the destination rules, MEDCOM's status reports, transport decisions, hospital surge planning and the public casualty count that all rely on the board.

**The Operational Impact:** At the scene, firefighters and company officers extricate seven trapped patients from a bus lying on its side. Hazmat technicians dike a diesel leak in the rain while EMS waits in the cold zone until fire declares the scene safe. Triage crews have to state on the radio whether they are using START/JumpSTART or SALT, which the Medical Director may order. They should finish the triage pass in 15 to 20 minutes and report counts of Immediate, Delayed, Minor and Expectant patients. At 18:20 the bus shifts during extrication and a hurt firefighter calls a mayday. The channel is cleared, the RIT is committed and a PAR follows, while patient care continues under a second command position. In the treatment areas, Immediate patients wait because LRMC can take only 3 in the first 30 minutes. Others need routing to St. Brendan's, where the CT is down and intake is limited, or by Air 7 to Lakeshore for head and burn injuries. The Transportation Unit Leader cannot trust the board: one hospital shown as open is actually on Diversion, so an ambulance may be sent to the wrong door. Minor patients wait for Larkfield County Transit buses to the Brandt Lake High School minor injury site. Walking wounded leave in private cars and arrive unannounced at Pinecrest Memorial, which has only 5 ED beds. Telecommunicators handle LARK DISP 1, LARK CMD 2, LARK TAC 3 and MED 1 while mutual aid ambulances from two neighboring counties check in. Social media reports 40 casualties against command's 28. Families call hospitals that will not give out names, and the reunification center line and the PIO face pressure to state one count that matches.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Use of Doctrine:** Demonstrate the ability to assign and log a destination for every tagged patient under SOP-MC-02 4 (no more than 3 Immediate patients to LRMC in the first 30 minutes, no more than one-third of Delayed patients to any one hospital, Air 7 to Lakeshore for head and burn injuries, and Minor patients by bus to Brandt Lake High School), even when St. Brendan's has Limited intake because its CT is down.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to spot that the LRMC MEDCOM capacity board shows a hospital as open when it is on Diversion, to confirm each hospital's real status by voice on MED 1, and to stop or redirect any ambulance already sent to the wrong door.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to keep command, the mayday and the personnel accountability report on LARK CMD 2 and LARK TAC 3 when the bus shifts at 18:20, while the Medical Group keeps triage, treatment and transport going under a second command position and dispatch logs every change in CAD.
- **Communication:** Demonstrate the ability to release one casualty count, approved by the IC and confirmed with MEDCOM, through a Joint Information Center within 45 minutes, against social media reports of 40 casualties, and to send families to the Brandt Lake High School reunification center and call-in line instead of the hospitals.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Incident Commander (Battalion 1, Fire Battalion Chief) (IC):** Owns overall strategy at the Highway 21 and County Road K crash: takes command from the first-arriving company officer with a full briefing, declares the MCI, decides when the scene is safe for EMS, runs the mayday and the personnel accountability report, and approves every casualty count released.
- **Medical Group Supervisor (Triage and Transportation Leaders) (MED):** Manages triage, treatment, transportation and medical communications at the scene (usually Deputy Chief Luis Navarro or the on-duty shift supervisor), including the triage method, the counts in each category and every destination decision.
- **Dispatch Supervisor (Telecommunicator) (DISP):** Manages LARK DISP 1, LARK CMD 2, LARK TAC 3 and MED 1, assigns units, announces and records command transfers in CAD, and handles mutual aid check-in and the call load across the rest of the county.
- **LRMC MEDCOM Nurse (Hospital Medical Communications Coordinator) (MCOM):** Manages the hospital capacity board and the hospital side of MED 1: gets and confirms each hospital's status, accepts or refuses offered patients, and reports the counts of arriving patients.
- **Larkfield Healthcare Coalition Coordinator (HCC):** Manages the regional view of beds, Diversion, surge plans, inpatient transfers and patient tracking across LRMC, St. Brendan's, Pinecrest and Lakeshore.
- **Public Information Officer (LCEMS PIO Sandra Kowal) (PIO):** Owns the public message, the Joint Information Center, the released casualty count and family reunification guidance through the Brandt Lake High School gymnasium and the call-in line.

## Phase 3: Board Off, Voice On (Hour 2:00 – 3:00)

Goal: Tests the one high-consequence call: whether to stop using the capacity board and run all destinations by voice roll call on MED 1, or keep using the degraded board with spot checks. Also tests running the manual workaround and the friction with partners that it brings.

Beats: DECISION POINT (19:35): MEDCOM, the Coalition Coordinator and the Medical Group Supervisor must decide. One option is to declare the capacity board unreliable and switch every destination to a voice roll call on MED 1 every 15 minutes. That slows transports, ties up MED 1 and MEDCOM staff, and patients still on scene wait longer. The other option is to keep dispatching off the board and spot-check by phone, which risks another wrong-door transport for a patient who is already deteriorating.; The manual workaround needs people: a second MEDCOM nurse and a scribe for the paper status board, a hospital liaison at each ED, and the Coalition Coordinator calling hospitals for bed counts. Dispatch posts the hospital statuses on the unit status board, and the Transportation Unit Leader keeps a paper transport log (time, tag, priority, unit, ground/air, destination, reason).; Partner friction: St. Brendan's (Limited intake, CT down) refuses a head-injury patient. Air 7 (SimCell) has one helicopter and a 25-minute turnaround to Lakeshore in rain and low ceilings. LRMC is near its 8-Delayed-in-the-first-hour limit and asks the coalition to transfer inpatients to Lakeshore under SOP-MC-02 5.; Minor patients still wait for Larkfield County Transit buses to the Brandt Lake High School minor injury site. The Red Cross reports the reunification line is overloaded with families calling about passengers whose names the hospitals will not give out.; Media pressure with a deadline (20:00): the regional TV station (SimCell) will air the '40 casualties' figure on the 20:00 broadcast unless the JIC gives one confirmed count. The count must match the tag count, patient list and MEDCOM's hospital arrival totals..

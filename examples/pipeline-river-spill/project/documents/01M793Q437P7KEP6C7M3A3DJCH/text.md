# Sterling Bluff Water Department: River Intake Protection and Source Contamination Plan

Sterling Bluff Water Department · Director Odell Prescott · Revision 4

## 1. The utility

Sterling Bluff Water Department serves 41,000 people in Sterling Bluff, Kansas, and wholesale customers in two neighboring towns. The treatment plant (capacity 14 million gallons a day, average demand 8.2) draws raw water from the Kansas River at an intake structure at river mile 34 below the Ironbark crossing, 3 miles upstream of the plant by pipe. It also has two backup wells (0.9 million gallons a day together), 11 million gallons of treated water in storage (about 32 hours at average demand) and an emergency connection to the Prairie Bend Rural Water District (maximum 1.5 million gallons a day). Plant Superintendent is Lin Zhao. Customer service and public communications are led by Renata Joss. The 24-hour operations number is 785-555-0171.

## 2. Contamination threat procedure

When the utility is told that oil has been released upstream, the plant superintendent and the Director do the following:

1. Record the time of the notification, the caller, the product, the volume estimate, the river mile of the release and the estimated arrival time at the intake (a window, not a point).
2. Start sampling at the raw water intake every hour (visual check for sheen, odor, total organic carbon and a petroleum hydrocarbons field screen).
3. Check the status of storage and the wells and compute the hours of supply without the river: the formula is storage plus wells divided by demand.
4. Decide on closing the intake: the Director may close the intake gate when the trajectory shows the oil within 6 hours or a sheen is seen at the intake. The intake is closed by the utility, not by the responsible party. An intake gate takes 20 minutes to close and the line to the plant 40 minutes to drain.
5. Arrange an alternate supply: the wells run at full output; the connection to the rural water district is opened; a request for a boil water or an ask for conservation is considered.
6. Notify the state's drinking water program (the Kansas Department of Health and Environment Bureau of Water) and the county health department.
7. Brief the Mayor and the City Manager.

## 3. Hours of supply

At average demand of 8.2 million gallons a day, treated storage of 11 million gallons and wells of 0.9 gives about 32 hours with the wells off and about 36 hours with the wells running; adding the connection with the rural district (1.5 million gallons a day) extends it to about 45 hours. A conservation request that reduces demand by 25 percent extends it to about 70 hours. The plan assumes the intake can stay closed for 48 hours before supply becomes critical.

## 4. Decision timing

Close the intake before the leading edge of the oil arrives. The closure must be complete (gate closed and line drained) at least 1 hour before the earliest arrival in the trajectory window. After the oil has passed, the Director reopens the intake only on the recommendation of the Environmental Unit, the state drinking water program and the plant's test results (no sheen, no hydrocarbons above the plant's action level, and the plant's operators' assessment of taste and odor).

## 5. Public communication

The utility, not the responsible party, tells customers what to do. Messages are cleared in the Joint Information Center, and state: the intake is closed, the supply is from storage, wells and the connection, what is requested (reduce outdoor use), how long supply lasts, and where to call. The utility never says the water is "safe" or "unsafe" without the state's concurrence. For vulnerable customers (a dialysis center, the Sterling Bluff Medical Center and two nursing homes) the Water Department calls the facilities directly.

## 6. Coordination with Prairie Bend Rural Water District

The rural water district (Manager Carl Dunning, 785-555-0173) has an intake at river mile 11, 6,200 customers and 1.1 million gallons of storage. Its intake would be reached about 5 hours after oil enters the river; it has no wells. If the district closes its intake it must buy water from Sterling Bluff, and the emergency connection may then be needed to flow the other way; the two utilities agree who has priority on each call.

## 7. Roles of other agencies

The Kansas Department of Health and Environment advises on drinking water rules and the monitoring needed. EPA Region 7 (FOSC) provides spill technical support. The county health department communicates health advice. The Joint Information Center coordinates messages.

## 8. After the event

The utility keeps a log of all contacts, decisions and sample results; it provides a record for the Unified Command and the state; and it reviews the procedure within 30 days.

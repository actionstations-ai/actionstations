# Master Scenario Events List (MSEL)

**Ditch to Intake** (Prairie Line River Crossing Release & Water Intakes)
Location: Ironbark Pipeline Control Center (Tulsa) and the Mill Ridge Station incident command post, Prairie Line Kansas River crossing, Prairie Bend County, Kansas · Duration: 4 Hours
Target Audience: Qualified Individual / Incident Commander (Ironbark), Control Center Shift Supervisor, Environmental Unit Leader, Public Information Officer / Joint Information Center, OSRO Duty Manager (Prairie Spill Response Cooperative), Water Department Director (Sterling Bluff). The SimCell plays the NRC, EPA Region 7, the Kansas agencies, USFWS, county emergency management, the rural water district, PHMSA, reporters and residents.

## Scenario Overview

**Theme:** Who calls whom, and when; one consistent volume range; protecting the river and the water intakes against the time oil takes to arrive; and making clear who decides what, since closures and countermeasures are not Ironbark's decisions.

**The Threat:** On Tuesday 8 June 2027 the Prairie Line fails at the Prairie Bend segment Kansas River crossing (milepost 214.3). There is no attacker, and the cause is unknown and must not be guessed. At 05:52 the Ironbark Pipeline Control Center in Tulsa sees a 46 psi pressure drop in 2 minutes and a 1.8 percent flow imbalance. At 06:02 a leak cannot be ruled out. The line is shut down at 06:04, and the block valves at Mill Ridge Station (milepost 213.1) and Sandhill Valve (milepost 215.6) are closed by 06:09. The SCADA volume balance puts the release at 2,700 to 3,500 barrels of medium sour crude, with 3,100 barrels as the planning figure. This is an estimate, not confirmed, and it is not a worst case discharge. At about 06:40 the crude reaches the Kansas River through a drainage ditch. The river is running about 8,500 cfs at about 2 knots and carries the oil toward the Mill Ridge boat ramp, the piping plover nesting sandbars at miles 6 to 9, the Prairie Bend Rural Water District intake at mile 11, the Cottonwood Slough mussel bed at mile 22 and the Sterling Bluff Water Department intake at mile 34.

**The Operational Impact:** Shift supervisor Nadia Kowalczyk has a shut-in line with an unconfirmed loss. As person in charge, the supervisor has to report to the National Response Center within 15 minutes without waiting for the Qualified Individual. Meanwhile a fisherman has already called county 911, so the county, reporters and residents hear about the spill before Ironbark's report. QI Teresa Holloway has to activate Prairie Spill Response Cooperative, whose Duty Manager Alicia Brandt reports that the first boom can be at the nearest ramp about 3.5 hours after notification. The cooperative has 9,500 feet of river boom spread across its depots and only a few boats. At a 2-knot current the boom must be angled to deflect oil, not set square across the flow. The QI also has to reach KDHE, county emergency management and the intake operators in the plan's order. A call to a utility's general line does not count until the operator is reached. The IMT escalates to Level 2, with Gordon Iwata as Incident Commander and Cole Whitfield leading the Environmental Unit, and forms a Unified Command with the FOSC from EPA Region 7 and the KDHE state OSC at Mill Ridge Station. The trajectory, given as time windows with the model run time, shows oil at the rural water district intake in about 4.8 hours. The district has no wells and only storage, so its manager must decide whether to close the intake and buy water from Sterling Bluff. Oil reaches the Sterling Bluff intake in about 14.8 hours. Director Odell Prescott has about 34 hours of supply without the river and must close the intake himself, with closure finished an hour before the earliest arrival. Sterling Bluff also has to call the dialysis center, the medical center and the nursing homes directly. USFWS directs any work near the nesting sandbars. The state closes recreational fishing and the Mill Ridge ramp. Any request for in-situ burning or dispersant goes to the FOSC because it is not preauthorized on this river. The JIC has to issue one volume range, never say the water is safe, and open a claims line while phones fill with calls from the press, residents and fishers.

**Exercise Objectives:**
- **Notification and Activation:** Demonstrate the ability to make the National Response Center report within 15 minutes of the 06:02 'leak cannot be ruled out' decision, then reach the QI, KDHE, county emergency management, Prairie Spill Response Cooperative and the intake operators (not general lines) in the order set by the Ironbark Response Plan, and log each contact with its time.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to produce and update a river trajectory, with arrival windows and the model run time, for the Mill Ridge ramp, the plover sandbars at miles 6 to 9, the mile 11 rural water district intake, Cottonwood Slough and the mile 34 Sterling Bluff intake, and to use it to rank protection priorities on the ICS 232.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to decide where to put 9,500 feet of river boom and the few available boats in a 2-knot current, using angled deflection booming that is in place before landfall and keeping clear of the plover nests until USFWS is consulted, and to route any burn or dispersant request to the FOSC because neither is preauthorized on this river.
- **Communication and Authority:** Demonstrate the ability to issue Joint Information Center messages, cleared by Ironbark, EPA, KDHE and county PIOs, that use one barrel range with its SCADA basis, leave intake closures and drinking water status to the utilities and the state, and open a claims line while the county 911 call has the story out first.

## Roles & Responsibilities (For Students)

- **Qualified Individual / Incident Commander (Ironbark) (QI):** Owns plan activation, the IMT activation level, funding commitments up to $5 million, the OSRO contract and Ironbark's seat in the Unified Command at Mill Ridge Station.
- **Control Center Shift Supervisor (CCSS):** Owns line shutdown, valve isolation, SCADA and computational pipeline monitoring data, and the first report as the person in charge.
- **Environmental Unit Leader (ENVL):** Owns the trajectory, the ICS 232 Resources at Risk ranking, the sampling plan and the coordination needed with the trustee agencies.
- **Public Information Officer (Joint Information Center) (PIO):** Owns Ironbark's messages through the Joint Information Center, the media and resident inquiries, and the claims line.
- **OSRO Duty Manager (Prairie Spill Response Cooperative) (OSRO):** Manages the cooperative's responders, boats, vacuum trucks and river boom from the Topeka, Wichita and Kansas City sites against the Tier 1 and Tier 2 commitments.
- **Director (Sterling Bluff Water Department) (SBWD):** Owns the decisions to close and reopen the Sterling Bluff intake, supply management for 41,000 customers, and direct notice to critical-care customers.

## Phase 3: Close the Intake? (Hour 2:00 – 3:00)

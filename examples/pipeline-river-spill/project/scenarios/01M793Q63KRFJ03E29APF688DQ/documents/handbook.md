# Controller / Evaluator (C/E) Handbook
## Ditch to Intake — Prairie Line River Crossing Release & Water Intakes

Exercise Date: [Date] · Location: Ironbark Pipeline Control Center (Tulsa) and the Mill Ridge Station incident command post, Prairie Line Kansas River crossing, Prairie Bend County, Kansas · Duration: 4 Hours


<!-- section:overview -->
### 1. Exercise Overview & Objectives

This is a discussion-based Tabletop Exercise (TTX) for Ironbark Pipeline Company's Incident Management Team, the Control Center in Tulsa, Prairie Spill Response Cooperative and the Sterling Bluff Water Department. It runs for four hours of exercise time and starts at 05:45 CDT on Tuesday 8 June 2027. No equipment moves and no real calls go out. Players talk through what they would decide, who they would call and what they would put in writing. You run the room and play everyone outside it.

The injects simulate an infrastructure failure: the Prairie Line fails at the Kansas River crossing at milepost 214.3. The cause is unknown and stays unknown. The SCADA volume balance gives a release of 2,700–3,500 bbl, with 3,100 bbl as the planning figure. This is not a worst case discharge. The oil reaches the river through a drainage ditch and moves downstream at about 2 knots toward the Mill Ridge ramp, the piping plover sandbars, the Prairie Bend Rural Water District intake at mile 11, Cottonwood Slough and the Sterling Bluff intake at mile 34.

The exercise has four phases:
- **Phase 1, Pressure Drop at 214.3 (0:00–1:00):** detection, shutdown and the person-in-charge report to the NRC within 15 minutes.
- **Phase 2, Oil on the Kaw (1:00–2:00):** OSRO activation, notifications in the order SNIMTA 2 sets, and the first trajectory. A red herring and the press compete for attention.
- **Phase 3, Close the Intake? (2:00–3:00):** Unified Command forms. Sterling Bluff decides when to close its intake. Limited boom is placed. The USFWS is consulted and countermeasure requests go to the FOSC.
- **Phase 4, Reopening the River (3:00–4:00):** the clock jumps to Wednesday morning. Players deal with failed boom, pressure to reopen the intake, the wildlife report, the PHMSA and NRC updates, and the after-action note.

Three time jumps (at 1:00, 2:00 and 3:00) move the clock forward.

Evaluators should watch the notification log more than anything else. A clerk or a voicemail is not a notification under SNIMTA 2. Also watch for:
- Whether the team uses one volume range in one unit, with its basis, everywhere.
- Whether anyone guesses at a cause.
- Whether Ironbark stays in its lane as the responsible party. Under the Ironbark response plan 5, intake, fishery and ramp closures belong to the utilities and the state. Burns and dispersants belong to the FOSC.
- Whether boom is angled, not set square to a 2-knot current.
- Whether anyone says or implies "the water is safe."

**Objectives:**
- **Notification and Activation:** Demonstrate the ability to make the National Response Center report within 15 minutes of the 06:02 'leak cannot be ruled out' decision, then reach the QI, KDHE, county emergency management, Prairie Spill Response Cooperative and the intake operators (not general lines) in the order set by the Ironbark Response Plan, and log each contact with its time.
- **Technical Analysis:** Demonstrate the ability to produce and update a river trajectory, with arrival windows and the model run time, for the Mill Ridge ramp, the plover sandbars at miles 6 to 9, the mile 11 rural water district intake, Cottonwood Slough and the mile 34 Sterling Bluff intake, and to use it to rank protection priorities on the ICS 232.
- **Decision Making:** Demonstrate the ability to decide where to put 9,500 feet of river boom and the few available boats in a 2-knot current, using angled deflection booming that is in place before landfall and keeping clear of the plover nests until USFWS is consulted, and to route any burn or dispersant request to the FOSC because neither is preauthorized on this river.
- **Communication and Authority:** Demonstrate the ability to issue Joint Information Center messages, cleared by Ironbark, EPA, KDHE and county PIOs, that use one barrel range with its SCADA basis, leave intake closures and drinking water status to the utilities and the state, and open a claims line while the county 911 call has the story out first.

- Ironbark Pipeline Company: Respo… › 6. Sensitive areas downstream

### 2. Room Setup & Logistics

- **Layout:** Use islands (or a broken U-shape), so each group has to make a deliberate move to talk to another.
- **Table 1, Pipeline Control Center (Tulsa), front left:** Team B, the Control Center Shift Supervisor. Give them a printed SCADA alarm sheet and the notification log form. This table is "remote" from the command post. They talk to the others by phone only.
- **Table 2, Incident Command Post / Unified Command (Mill Ridge Station), center:** Team A (QI / Incident Commander), Team C (Environmental Unit Leader) and Team E (Prairie Spill OSRO Duty Manager). In Phase 3, leave two empty chairs labeled "EPA Region 7 FOSC" and "KDHE State OSC". You sit in them when the Unified Command forms (I-12).
- **Table 3, Sterling Bluff Water Department, front right:** Team F, the Director. Seat this table apart from the Ironbark tables on purpose. The intake decision belongs to the utility, not Ironbark. The physical distance makes Ironbark go through its Liaison instead of deciding for the utility.
- **Table 4, Joint Information Center, rear of the room:** Team D, the PIO. Put this table at the back, away from the Unified Command. The PIO has to get up and walk over for the volume range, the trajectory and the closure status. This copies the real lag between the command post and the JIC, and shows whether the PIO waits for cleared facts or makes up numbers under deadline.
- **SimCell table, side wall:** Seat yourself here with the inject deck, phones or radios, and a clock showing exercise time.
- **Evaluators:** Seat one at each island, with the evaluation guide and a copy of the notification log.
- **Main screen:** Put it where every table can see it.

**Equipment needs**
- A projector and main screen for main-screen injects: SCADA alarms, social posts, trajectory products TRJ-01 and TRJ-02, the boom plan and the time-jump cards.
- A whiteboard or easel pad at Table 2 for the ICS-201 (situation map, resources, actions). Add a second panel for the ICS 202 objectives and the ICS 232 resources-at-risk ranking.
- A printed inject deck in MSEL order, one copy for the controller and one per evaluator.
- A large wall clock or timer showing exercise time.
- Optional: simulated radios or desk phones for Tables 1–4 and the SimCell, so calls go to a named person and get logged.

**Materials for players:** Situation manual, river map with mileposts and river miles 0–34, Ironbark response plan 6 sensitive-areas table, SNIMTA 2 first-hour table, blank notification log, blank ICS 201/202/232/213 forms, the 555-01xx contact sheet, pens and sticky notes.

### 3. Rules of Play (Instructor Guide)

**Your role: the SimCell**
You play everyone else in the world. If a student says "I call the sheriff," you answer as the deputy. If they say "I call the NRC," you are the NRC duty officer. The same goes for Prairie Bend Rural Water District, the USFWS biologist, the Prairie 6 News reporter, the EPA Region 7 FOSC, the KDHE engineer, PHMSA and the Mayor's office. Answer only the person they reached:
- If they call a general line, you are the clerk or the voicemail, not the manager.
- Ask the caller for their name and the time, as a real duty officer would. Write the call on your SimCell log so evaluators can compare it with the players' notification log.

**Do not make it easy**
Each outside party protects its own interests and gives only what the players earn. Here is an example from this scenario. In I-06, the Prairie Spill Duty Manager says the first boom reaches the Mill Ridge ramp at about 10:20. If the IC says "Get it there by 08:00," the answer is: "The trucks are on the road. Physics doesn't take orders. Where do you want the first 1,200 feet, and are you calling Tier 2?" Do not speed up the boom, add boats, or let a utility manager pick up a call made to the front desk. Some other cases:
- If the PIO asks the NRC or the county for "the number," they get "What did you report?"
- If someone suggests restarting the line "to check the balance" (I-07), you are the PHMSA inspector who will ask about it later.

**Managing time**
Read the time jumps at 1:00, 2:00 and 3:00 word for word, standing, with the card on the main screen:
"PAUSE EXERCISE. Pens down for a moment. We are jumping forward [60 minutes / to 08:45 Wednesday]. It is now [time] CDT. Here is what has happened in that time: [read the card]. Anything you said you would do before the pause has been done as you described it. Anything you did not say has not been done. RESUME."

That last line is important. If nobody named the KDHE call before the jump, the notification log still shows it missing after the jump.

If a table stalls for more than 3 minutes, push it forward with a phone call in character. Do not step out of character to coach.

**Handling artificialities**
When a student says "I wouldn't use that system" or "We don't have that form," answer: "Understood. For today, treat it as the tool you would use. What would you put in it, and who gets it?" Then move on. Do not argue about the scenario's realism during play. Write the point down for the hot wash.

When a student asks for data the scenario does not give (sample results, a cause), answer: "That isn't available yet. What do you do without it?"

**Delivering injects**
Do not explain or interpret. Say "This just came in." or "This is on the main screen." Hand the printed card to the addressed role only. If the inject is a call, pick up the phone and speak in character. Deliver noise injects (I-01b, I-14b, I-19b) with the same weight as core injects. Players have to sort them out themselves.

**Conventions**
- Every player communication that could be mistaken for real starts and ends with "This is an exercise."
- If a real emergency or safety issue happens (illness, fire alarm, a real spill call), anyone says "REAL WORLD, REAL WORLD." All play stops at once. The exercise resumes only when the controller announces "RESUME EXERCISE."
- Phones used for real calls must never dial the real NRC line 800-424-8802 or real agencies. All exercise numbers are 555-01xx.

**SimCell Scripts (play these when the trigger happens — do not make it easy):**

- **National Response Center duty officer (report 1398274)** — _Trigger: The Control Center Shift Supervisor (or anyone at Ironbark) calls the NRC to report the release, or the NRC callback at I-03 or I-20 is answered._
  - Ask: ""Are you the person in charge of the facility? Give me your name, the time you shut in, and the quantity. A range is fine, but tell me what it's based on, and use barrels or gallons, not both.""
  - Ask: ""The cause field says unknown. Was this third-party damage or corrosion? EPA Region 7 and KDHE are asking. You're telling me you don't know? Then I'm writing 'unknown', and don't phone me a guess later.""
  - Ask: ""Has oil reached the water? Which water body, and what river mile? I need a latitude and longitude or a milepost, not 'near the ramp.'""
  - Ask: ""Just so you know, I'm not sending anyone and I'm not approving anything. If you need boom, call your OSRO. Have you called them?""
  - Concede only when: Gives the report number 1398274 and the time of the report once the person in charge has reported quantity, location, the material and that oil is on the water.
  - Concede only when: Accepts a range with its basis (for example '2,700–3,500 bbl, SCADA volume balance, unverified') and confirms the NRC will relay it to EPA Region 7 and KDHE.
  - Concede only when: Records the update for the Wednesday follow-up (I-20) only if the revised range VB-0621 comes with its basis and the cause stays 'unknown' rather than a guess.
- **Prairie Bend Rural Water District Manager Carl Dunning (the utility, which depends on the Sterling Bluff interconnect and serves Prairie Haven nursing home)** — _Trigger: An Ironbark player or the Sterling Bluff Director reaches the manager directly at (785) 555-0173. A call to the general office line 555-0147 reaches only the clerk (I-09). The manager calls back at I-13b._
  - Ask: ""You left a note with my clerk at 07:14 and your log says I was notified? I'm the operator. When did oil hit the river, and what's my arrival window, with your model run time? Not 'a few hours.'""
  - Ask: ""I've got no wells and 1.1 million gallons. If I close at 09:00 I'm buying 0.5 MGD from Sterling Bluff through an interconnect that's set up to flow the other way. Who's paying for that, and where's it in writing? Your liaison said 'we'll cover it.' That isn't a document.""
  - Ask: ""Prairie Haven nursing home is already calling me. Is Ironbark's JIC going to tell my customers anything about my water? Because that's my call and KDHE's, not yours.""
  - Ask: ""Sterling Bluff, which comes first on the interconnect today, my 6,200 or your dialysis center? Sterling Bluff plan 6 says we agree on each call. So let's agree.""
  - Concede only when: Gives a firm intake closure time (09:00) and the storage figure, but only after a named Ironbark person gives him the trajectory product with arrival windows and model run time and logs him as reached.
  - Concede only when: Agrees to a joint interconnect priority with the Sterling Bluff Director once the Director commits a flow rate and start time.
  - Concede only when: Sends the district's own customer notice through the JIC only after Ironbark puts its cost commitment in writing (signed by the QI) and agrees the district alone speaks about its water status.
- **EPA Region 7 Federal On-Scene Coordinator (seated with the KDHE state On-Scene Coordinator at Mill Ridge Station)** — _Trigger: The QI confirms and joins Unified Command (I-12), or anyone asks for approval of in-situ burning, dispersant, or a chemical sorbent such as the BioSheen Eraser (I-14, I-19b)._
  - Ask: ""Draft ICS 202 objectives and an ICS 232 in 30 minutes. What's your number one resource at risk and why? Plovers, the mile 11 intake or Sterling Bluff? Show me the ranking, not a list.""
  - Ask: ""Your field lead wants to light off the ditch pool. Burning isn't preauthorized on this river. Give me a written request: the volume, how weathered the oil is, wind direction, distance to the road and the homes, the smoke plume toward people, and who's monitoring. Without that, the answer is no.""
  - Ask: ""Is the Sterling Bluff operator holding TRJ-02, or did someone leave a voicemail at the front desk? KDHE's drinking water bureau wants the name and the time.""
  - Ask: ""Someone in your shop is saying 'the water is safe.' Who cleared that? Ironbark doesn't close or open intakes and doesn't certify drinking water.""
  - Concede only when: Signs the ICS 202 objectives once they include public health and water intakes, the ranked sensitive areas, angled deflection boom and wildlife under USFWS and KDWP direction.
  - Concede only when: Decides on a countermeasure only after a complete written request, and gives the decision with its conditions and the time. With no RRT preauthorization, the default is denial or 'pending RRT concurrence.'
  - Concede only when: Has the KDHE OSC confirm drinking-water coordination once the EU shows a named operator-level contact and time for both utilities.
- **US Fish and Wildlife Service, Kansas field office biologist** — _Trigger: A crew is sent toward the plover sandbars at miles 6–9 (I-14 Crew 3, I-19 skid steer), the Environmental Unit calls as a trustee, or the wildlife report goes out (I-19)._
  - Ask: ""Who authorized a crew on the bars at mile 7? There are active piping plover nests on at least two of them. Nobody sets a stake or drives a skid steer there until I've talked to your Environmental Unit. Who is that, by name?""
  - Ask: ""Square boom staked across the bar heads? At 2 knots it won't hold oil, and you'll trample nests setting it. Show me a deflection plan that keeps people off the bars.""
  - Ask: ""Your wildlife report counts the oiled plover adult under both live and rehab. Which is it? I need oiled live, oiled dead, hazed, in rehab and released, each counted once.""
  - Ask: ""Who did the hazing? If it wasn't authorized personnel under our direction, I need to know now.""
  - Concede only when: Approves manual removal only on bars 2 and 3, with a USFWS escort, once the EU asks for consultation before the work and names a crew lead.
  - Concede only when: Accepts a corrected wildlife status report (no double count) with times and locations, and confirms it is going to KDWP.
  - Concede only when: Agrees to deflection boom placed downstream of the bars (for example mile 9.5) in place of work on the bars themselves.

  - Ask: ""The bait shop owners say nobody's told them who pays. Is there a claims number, or do they call the station?""
  - Concede only when: Corrects the on-air figure to the JIC's single range (for example 2,700–3,500 bbl, an estimate from the SCADA volume balance) if a spokesperson gives it, with its basis, before the deadline.
  - Concede only when: Runs the claims line 555-0131 and the utilities' own water-status contacts if the PIO gives them in a cleared statement.
  - Concede only when: Takes 'the cause is under investigation' with no follow-up rumor only if the PIO also gives a firm time for the next briefing and keeps it.

### 5. Hot Wash Guide (Debrief)

**The "3-Up, 3-Down" Method:** Ask the room for three things that went well and three things that broke or failed during the exercise. Go around the room one team at a time (Control Center, Ironbark command, spill co-op, agencies, utilities, PIOs). Write every answer on the whiteboard in two columns so the room can see the patterns before the facilitator moves to the discussion questions.

**Key Discussion Questions for Ditch to Intake:**
- **The Call:** At I-02 the CPM analyst told you at 06:02 that a leak could not be ruled out, but the location estimate covered MP 209 to MP 219 and nobody in the field could see anything. When did you treat that moment as a reportable release and start the 15-minute clock for the National Response Center report? Did the shutdown decision wait for a confirmation that SCADA could never give you? What would have let the console and the QI decide faster? _(Objective: Notification and Activation)_
- **The Gap:** At I-09 the first calls to the Prairie Bend RWD and Sterling Bluff WD reached a clerk and a front-desk voicemail, not an operator. One call went to a clerk who left a note on a desk, and the other went to a front-desk voicemail. The Sterling Bluff superintendent learned about the oil from a county page. Who did you actually reach? Why did the log count a voicemail as a notification? What direct contacts for the intake operators should be in the plan before the next event? _(Objective: Notification and Activation)_
- **The Clock:** At I-13 and I-13b both utilities made closure decisions from your trajectory. The RWD closed at 09:00 against a 10:50 earliest arrival. Sterling Bluff had a latest gate start of 18:45 against a 20:45 arrival. How old was the model run each of them was using, and did they know when it would be updated? Did your ICS 232 priorities match the order the oil would reach the plover sandbars, the mile 11 intake, Cottonwood Slough and Sterling Bluff? _(Objective: Technical Analysis)_
- **Who Decides:** At I-13 the Ironbark liaison stood in the Sterling Bluff lobby and emailed that Ironbark 'recommends immediate closure.' Then at I-13b the RWD asked to reverse the interconnect, although the Sterling Bluff storage figure assumed water flowing the other way, and asked Ironbark to put payment in writing. Who in the room thought they owned the intake decisions? Did anyone notice the interconnect conflict before the two utilities committed to it? Who took on the call to the Prairie Haven nursing home, and who should make it? _(Objective: Communication and Authority)_
- **Trust:** At I-04b and I-10 the public saw '10,000 barrels' and '100,000 gallons' before they heard anything from you. A reporter wanted gallons and a cause on camera by 7:40, and a mother called about her newborn's tap water. What was the first cleared JIC message you released, and when did it go out? Did it give one barrel range with its SCADA basis, without guessing at a cause? Did it send drinking-water questions to the utilities and KDHE, and did it open a claims line? Which of the four PIO clearances slowed you down most? _(Objective: Communication and Authority)_

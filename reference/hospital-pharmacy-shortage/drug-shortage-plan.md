# Prairie Ridge Health — Drug Shortage Management Plan

Pharmacy and Therapeutics Policy PH-22 · Owner: Director of Pharmacy · Approved by the Pharmacy and Therapeutics (P&T) Committee, January 2026

## 1. Purpose

Prairie Ridge Health is a 118-bed community hospital in Fort Dodge, Webster County, Iowa, with an emergency department, a 10-bed intensive care unit (ICU), surgical services with 5 operating rooms, an infusion center and a long-term care unit. This plan sets how the hospital detects, classifies and responds to shortages of medications, with special attention to intravenous (IV) fluids, which touch nearly every patient. It follows the American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) shortage guidance and the FDA drug shortage database.

## 3. Shortage levels

| Level | Definition | Authority | Typical action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Watch | Supplier backorder or FDA listing; on-hand supply at least 30 days | Purchasing Manager | Increase par, alternate supplier, watch |
| 2 Active | On-hand supply 8 to 29 days, or allocation below 80% of usage | Drug Shortage Team | Conservation measures, restrictions, P&T notification |
| 3 Critical | On-hand supply 7 days or less, or allocation below 50%, or drug has no safe alternative | Incident Commander with the CMO | Hospital command center, allocation by clinical priority, therapeutic substitution protocol, notification to the medical staff, possible procedure deferral |

The Purchasing Manager calculates days on hand each morning from the pharmacy system: on-hand units divided by the average daily usage of the last 14 days.

## 4. Information sources

- the FDA Drug Shortage Database and the FDA's shortage notices for IV fluids;
- the wholesaler's allocation notices (the primary wholesaler's allocation desk and the secondary wholesaler);
- manufacturer customer service and direct-purchase accounts;
- ASHP shortage resources and the Iowa Hospital Association's shortage alerts;
- the Iowa Board of Pharmacy for rules on compounding and imports;
- peer hospitals in the Iowa regional pharmacy network, for loans.

The Drug Shortage Team does not buy from gray market sellers. An offer of product from an unfamiliar vendor is checked for licensure by the Purchasing Manager and the Director of Pharmacy; imported products labeled for another country are used only under an FDA-announced temporary importation, with dose and label conversion by the Clinical Pharmacy Manager.

## 5. Response by level

**Level 2.** Purchasing calls both wholesalers and manufacturers; the Clinical Pharmacy Manager issues a conservation notice (use oral or enteral routes when possible, use smaller bag sizes, minimize flush volumes, avoid carrier fluids when not needed); the P&T Chair informs medical staff.

**Level 3.**
1. The Incident Commander opens the command center (Conference Room C, ext. 3690) and the team meets within 2 hours, then every 8 hours.
2. The Drug Shortage Team sets an **allocation** by percent of normal usage for each product and presentation, and by clinical priority (section 6).
3. The Clinical Pharmacy Manager activates the substitution protocol (IV Fluid Conservation SOP, PH-22-A) and the Medication Safety Officer runs the substitution checklist: concentration, volume, labeling, pump library, look-alike risk, nursing education, and a second pharmacist check.
4. The CNO notifies units and the CMO notifies the medical staff by email, text and huddle script, using one message with the same numbers.
5. The Anesthesia Medical Director and the OR Manager review elective surgery volume; if the days on hand of 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9% fall below 3, the Incident Commander may defer elective cases for 48 hours.
6. The Purchasing Manager seeks loans from neighboring hospitals (Webster Valley Medical Center in Fort Dodge may lend product; North Iowa Memorial Hospital in Mason City is farther), alternate suppliers and the manufacturer's emergency allocation program.
7. The PIO prepares a statement for patients and media if procedures are deferred.

## 6. Priority for allocation

When supply is allocated, the order of priority is: (1) patients in shock, resuscitation, or on IV medications with no oral alternative, including pediatric and dialysis patients; (2) the ICU and the emergency department; (3) operating rooms for urgent and emergency procedures; (4) inpatient units; (5) the infusion center for medications that cannot be given later; (6) elective procedures and outpatient hydration. The Chief Medical Officer may change this order and records why.

## 7. Communication rules

- The same numbers go to pharmacy, nursing, physicians and the public: days on hand, allocation percent, what is restricted, when the next update is.
- Staff are told what to do instead, not only what is unavailable.
- Patients are told about changes to their treatment by their care team.
- Conflicting statements are corrected by the Medication Safety Officer within 2 hours.

## 8. Notification and reporting

- The Director of Pharmacy reports a shortage that affects patient care to the FDA Drug Shortage Staff by the CDER shortage email, and to the Iowa Board of Pharmacy when an emergency import or compounding is used.
- P&T Committee receives a written report within 7 days of any Level 3 declaration.
- The hospital reports any adverse event related to a substitution through the medication event system, and to the FDA MedWatch and ISMP when the event is serious.

## 9. Return to normal

The shortage is closed when days on hand exceeds 30 and the wholesaler allocation is lifted for 14 days. The Medication Safety Officer reviews every substitution event and the team produces an after-action report within 30 days.

# Prairie Ridge Health — Clinical Impact, Surgical Services and Communication During a Shortage

Companion to PH-22 · Owners: Chief Medical Officer, Chief Nursing Officer, Director of Pharmacy

## 1. Who is hurt first

When 1000 mL sodium chloride 0.9% and lactated Ringer's run short, the hospital sees problems in this order: (1) the emergency department and sepsis care, where 30 mL/kg boluses of fluid are standard; (2) operating rooms, where anesthesia uses balanced fluids for every case; (3) medication dilution and flushes, which use 100 mL and 250 mL minibags; (4) hydration for oncology and infusion patients; (5) obstetrics, for labor and postpartum hemorrhage; (6) the long-term care unit.

The ED Medical Director (Dr. Marcus Teller) and the Anesthesia Medical Director (Dr. Lucas Hendrickx) are asked at Level 3 to give the Drug Shortage Team a daily list of the clinical uses that cannot wait. Sepsis bundles, trauma resuscitation, burn care, diabetic ketoacidosis and hemorrhage are protected uses and are not restricted.

## 2. Surgical services

- Five operating rooms run 36 to 44 cases per day on weekdays; typical use is 80 bags of lactated Ringer's and 60 of sodium chloride 0.9%.
- At Level 3 the OR Manager (Denise Aldrich) and the Anesthesia Medical Director review the schedule each evening for the next day. Elective cases that need large fluid volumes (joint replacement, abdominal surgery) are the first candidates for a 48-hour delay; urgent and emergency cases, cesarean births and cancer surgery proceed.
- Anesthesia may use 500 mL bags and blood warmer lines only as appropriate; surgeons are told in advance of any change.
- If days on hand fall below 3, the Incident Commander and the CMO decide whether to defer elective surgery; they inform surgeons, the surgery schedulers and patients.

## 3. Medication dilution

About 410 minibags per day carry antibiotics and other drugs. When minibags run short pharmacy:
- moves eligible antibiotics to IV push or to oral therapy by a therapeutic interchange approved by P&T;
- prepares syringes in the cleanroom;
- uses sterile water or dextrose only where the drug label allows;
- keeps a record of every change for the Medication Safety Officer.

## 4. Patients and families

Patients who are affected hear it from their nurse or physician. A patient whose treatment is moved receives a call from the infusion center the same day. Complaints go to Patient Relations (ext. 3200). Patients are never charged more because of a substitution.

## 5. Media and public

The PIO (Ellen Sorensen) prepares three statements before any deferral: a holding statement, a statement on deferred surgeries, and a statement for local media if patients or families call reporters. The statement says: that the hospital is affected by a national shortage of IV fluids; that emergency care, urgent surgery and obstetric care continue; that some elective procedures may be rescheduled; who patients call; and when the next update is. It never names a vendor as a cause unless the FDA or the manufacturer has done so publicly. The PIO answers all media, local television, the Fort Dodge daily newspaper and radio; physicians and nurses refer all calls to her (ext. 3050).

Social media: the PIO monitors the hospital's page and the community groups; a false claim ("the hospital has run out of IV fluids") is corrected with a statement the same day, signed off by the Incident Commander.

## 6. Message consistency

Pharmacy, nursing, physicians and the public affairs office must state the same numbers: the hospital's days on hand, the allocation percent, what is restricted and the next update time. The Medication Safety Officer owns the "single page" that lists these, updated after each Drug Shortage Team meeting; staff are told to use only that page. If two units give different instructions the Medication Safety Officer calls both unit managers and issues a correction within 2 hours.

## 7. Supply options and their risks

| Option | Benefit | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Loan from a neighboring hospital | Same labeled product | The lender may be short as well; paperwork; time to transport |
| Alternate supplier at a higher price | Fast | Price gouging; check licensure; may be gray market |
| Imported product (FDA temporary importation) | Large volume | Different label, volume and port; staff education; pump library |
| In-house compounding | Control | Cleanroom capacity; beyond-use dating; Iowa rules |
| Manufacturer emergency allocation | Sound | Smaller quantity |
| Reduced use | Free | Clinical risk if applied wrongly |

The Director of Pharmacy never accepts product with no lot number or from an unlicensed seller.

## 8. Metrics tracked

At each 8-hour meeting the Drug Shortage Team records: days on hand by product, allocation percent against normal usage, number of patients affected (therapy changed, delayed or canceled), substitutions approved and the percent that passed the safety checklist, adverse events, and messages issued with the same numbers.

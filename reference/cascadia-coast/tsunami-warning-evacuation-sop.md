# SOP-TSU-01: Tsunami Warning and Evacuation

Marlow County Emergency Management · Marlow County Communications · Revision 3

## 1. Purpose

This procedure tells the 911 telecommunicator, the Warning Coordinator and the Sheriff's duty supervisor how to warn the public of a tsunami and how to order an evacuation. It carries out Annex J of the Emergency Operations Plan.

## 2. Tsunami messages the county receives

The National Tsunami Warning Center in Palmer, Alaska issues four message levels for the Oregon coast: Tsunami Warning, Tsunami Advisory, Tsunami Watch and Tsunami Information Statement. They arrive over the NOAA Weather Wire receiver at Position 2, the National Weather Service Portland forecast office relay, NOAA Weather Radio and the EAS receiver. The message gives the earthquake's magnitude and location, the areas covered and estimated arrival times by location.

- **Local source.** For an earthquake felt strongly on the coast, the first message may arrive 5 to 8 minutes after the event and the first wave 15 to 25 minutes after. Nobody waits for the message.
- **Distant source.** For an earthquake in Alaska or Japan, the county has hours. A Tsunami Warning means evacuate the zones named; an Advisory means stay out of the water, off beaches and out of harbors; a Watch means be ready.
- The county never upgrades, downgrades or cancels an NTWC message. It adds local instruction.

## 3. Sirens

The county has 18 tsunami sirens: 11 in Port Alder, 4 in Kellerton and Neskin Point, 3 at the state park and the Alder River bar. They are activated from the encoder at Position 2 and from the backup encoder at the EOC. The tone is a three-minute wail, repeated every 10 minutes. Sirens run on batteries for 72 hours. A siren that fails the monthly test (first Wednesday at 12:00) or the 15-minute polling check is logged and sent to Public Works (ext. 220). Sirens are not an all-clear device; no siren sounds the end of the warning.

## 4. Evacuation zones

The county uses the state's five tsunami evacuation zone sizes (Small, Medium, Large, Extra Large, Extra Extra Large) as shown on the tsunami evacuation brochures. For a local Cascadia source the orders go to XL and XXL, which covers all of Port Alder's waterfront and the Alder River flats. Assembly areas are above the XXL line: Spruce Hill (primary, for the school and downtown), Kellerton Ridge, and Neskin Point Hill. The Neskin Point Community Center tower is a vertical evacuation refuge for 450 people at 38 feet; it is used only by people who cannot reach high ground in time.

| Zone | Where | Walking time to assembly area |
|---|---|---|
| Port Alder waterfront | Harbor, Front Street to 4th Avenue | 12 to 18 minutes |
| Alder River flats | Cedar Court assisted living, mobile home park | 20 to 30 minutes |
| Neskin Point | Beach cottages, campground | 8 to 14 minutes |
| State park campground | 60 sites | 15 minutes |

## 5. Who orders what

| Action | Who | By when |
|---|---|---|
| Sound sirens | Telecommunicator on Position 2, on the first strong shaking report or the first NTWC warning | Immediately |
| MarlowAlert and county WEA (the county sends its own WEA with the evacuation instruction) | Warning Coordinator | Within 5 minutes of the message |
| Evacuation order for XL and XXL | EOC Command (Director or Deputy) on the advice of the Warning Coordinator | Immediately for a local source |
| Close Highway 101 and Port Alder streets to inbound traffic | Sheriff | Within 10 minutes |
| Port action: vessels to deep water or hold in port | Harbor Master at the Port of Port Alder with the Coast Guard | If more than 60 minutes before arrival, move to deep water; otherwise leave the vessel |
| Facilities in the zone | Hospital, school and care home incident commanders | Start on shaking |

## 6. Messages and consistency

Every message states the NTWC message type, the zone names, the time the message was issued, and one instruction: go to high ground now on foot. Text messages are under 160 characters; WEA is within 360 characters and sent in English and Spanish. Messages never say "all clear" and never give a wave height as a certainty. After the first wave the message says: "More waves are expected. Stay out of the zone until Marlow County says it is safe."

The PIO keeps a message log. The Warning Coordinator checks that the county message agrees with the NTWC message on message level, area and times, and corrects any disagreement before the next broadcast.

## 7. Distant-source procedure

For a Watch or Advisory, MCEM notifies the Harbor Master, the State Parks campground host, the school district and the hospital and holds a conference call with OEM. For a Warning, the order is the same as for a local source, but the Sheriff has time to do door-to-door notification in the Alder River flats and the Warning Coordinator sets a return time only after NTWC cancels the warning and the Harbor Master reports the harbor safe.

## 8. Ending the warning

The NTWC cancels the warning. The Warning Coordinator, the Sheriff and the Harbor Master confirm waves are no longer observed (gauge readings at the Port Alder tide gauge below 0.3 meters of amplitude for two hours). The EOC Command then issues the return order and the PIO announces it through every channel at the same time.

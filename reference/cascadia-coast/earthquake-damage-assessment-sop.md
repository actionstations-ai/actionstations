# SOP-EQ-02: Early Warning Actions, Damage Assessment and Lifelines

Marlow County Emergency Management · Marlow County Building Department · Revision 2

## 1. Purpose

This procedure sets out the automated actions that county facilities take on an earthquake early warning alert, the way buildings are inspected and placarded afterward, how routes are closed, and how lifeline status is reported to the EOC.

## 2. Earthquake early warning (ShakeAlert) at county facilities

The ShakeAlert system detects the first waves of an earthquake and sends an alert through partners (phone alerts, apps and direct feeds). Near the epicenter there may be no warning; at a distance there may be seconds to a minute. The county treats the alert as a reason to take simple, fast protective actions at sites that have an automated or manual action defined:

| Site | Action | Done by |
|---|---|---|
| Port Alder Fire District stations 1 and 2, Kellerton Station 3 | Open bay doors, sound station alert | Station automation |
| Marlow Coast Community Hospital | Send elevators to the nearest floor and open doors; alert surgery and the nursery | Facilities supervisor |
| Port Alder Elementary School | Bell and public address: drop, cover, hold on | Front office |
| Cape Marlow PUD Substation 2 | Alarm only; no automatic switching | Control room |
| Marlow & Pacific Short Line (freight rail to the port) | Slow or stop trains on the Alder River trestle | Dispatcher |
| Cape Marlow Natural Gas (distribution) | No automatic valve closure; crews survey after the event | Gas control |

Each site logs the time the alert arrived, the action and the time it was complete in the early warning action log (a form kept by the Warning Coordinator). An alert for a magnitude 5 earthquake at a distant source is not a reason to evacuate. The county does not promise warning time everywhere; a person next to the epicenter gets none.

## 3. Rapid damage picture

Within 30 minutes of a large earthquake the EOC reads, as estimates: the USGS earthquake event summary and magnitude; the ShakeMap shaking intensities; the PAGER loss estimate; and the ground failure summary. Each version of the product is dated, and decisions say which version they used. The first numbers will change: the first magnitude estimate is often lower than the final one. The Duty Seismologist (a USGS Pacific Northwest advisor) is contacted through the state RCC.

Field reports (fire, Sheriff, Public Works) are kept separate from modeled estimates in the situation report; the county never publishes a modeled loss as an observed one.

## 4. Building safety assessment

The Building Official (Ines Calloway) directs inspectors and volunteer engineers from the state's Structural Engineers Association. Assessments follow the ATC-20 rapid evaluation procedure (by name; the placards are the standard ones):

- **INSPECTED** (green): no apparent hazard; people may re-enter.
- **RESTRICTED USE** (yellow): hazards exist; entry limited as written on the placard.
- **UNSAFE** (red): not to be entered except by the building department's authorization.

Priority: the hospital and the 911 center, the schools used as shelters (Marlow Coast High School), the Fire District stations, the EOC, then the downtown masonry on Front Street (about 40 unreinforced masonry buildings), then houses. A green placard does not protect against a damaging aftershock; each placard is dated and the inspector's name written on it. Search teams use the placard categories and reports of collapse to set priorities, and reassess after any aftershock of magnitude 6 or more.

## 5. Routes

Public Works (Wes Hartnett, ext. 220) reports status on each route as Open, Restricted or Closed with the basis. Closure authority: Public Works for county roads, ODOT District 2 for state highways; the Sheriff controls access. Bridge closures follow a visual inspection by a bridge engineer from ODOT. Routes are reopened only after inspection. The Highway 101 Alder River bridge (built 1936, un-retrofitted) is closed after any earthquake that produced shaking of intensity VII or more at Port Alder. The county gives partners one list of closed routes, updated every hour on the hour.

## 6. Lifelines

| Lifeline | Owner | Report |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity (about 14,500 meters) | Cape Marlow PUD | Customers out, substations energized, feeders restored |
| Water | Port Alder Water District; Kellerton water system | Tank levels, line breaks, boil-water status |
| Natural gas (about 3,100 customers) | Cape Marlow Natural Gas | Leaks, shutoffs |
| Telephone and cell | two carriers | Towers on battery, fiber cuts |
| Wastewater | Port Alder sanitary district | Lift stations in the zone |

The lifeline status board is updated at the top of every hour. Restoration is announced in stages (for example, "feeder 3 energized, serving Kellerton ridge"); nobody announces "power restored" because one utility's status is good. The state estimate of 72 hours for first restoration is not repeated to the public as a promise.

## 7. Aftershocks

After a large earthquake the USGS issues an aftershock forecast. The county announces it exactly: the probability of an aftershock of at least a given magnitude within a stated period, as a range, and says that nobody can say aftershocks have ended. The PIO, the Building Official and the shelter manager all use the same numbers from the same forecast issuance and say when it was issued.

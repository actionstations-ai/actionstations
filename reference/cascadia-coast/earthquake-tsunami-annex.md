# Marlow County Emergency Operations Plan, Annex J: Earthquake and Tsunami

Marlow County Emergency Management (MCEM) · Revision 4 · Effective January 2027

## 1. Purpose and scope

This annex sets out how Marlow County prepares for and responds to a Cascadia Subduction Zone earthquake and the local tsunami that follows it. It also covers a large crustal earthquake without a tsunami and a distant tsunami arriving hours after a source far away. It applies to the unincorporated county, the cities of Port Alder (county seat, population 6,100) and Kellerton (2,300), the Neskin Point community and the Cape Marlow State Park campground. The county's population is about 27,500; roughly 9,800 residents and, in April and in summer, up to 4,000 visitors are inside the tsunami evacuation zone.

## 2. Situation

Marlow County sits on the Oregon coast. The Cascadia Subduction Zone lies about 80 km west of Port Alder. A magnitude 9 rupture would produce shaking for four minutes or longer, ground failure along the Alder River estuary, damage to bridges and unreinforced masonry, loss of power, cell service and most of the county's road network, and a local tsunami. The first wave reaches the open coast about 15 to 20 minutes after the shaking starts and the first wave at the Port Alder harbor in 20 to 25 minutes. Waves continue for eight hours or more and the largest wave is often not the first. The state's modeled worst case (the "XXL" evacuation zone) puts water above 10 meters at the Port Alder waterfront and up to the foot of Spruce Hill.

For planning, the county assumes that external help will not arrive for at least 72 hours, that Highway 101 and Highway 6 are both cut, and that county communications run on backup systems.

## 5. Concept of operations

**Phase 1, the first 30 minutes (shaking and the first wave).** People are expected to protect themselves during shaking (drop, cover, hold on) and then, without waiting for a message, walk to high ground or a vertical refuge. The county does not rely on an alert for this. The message to the public is: long or strong shaking near the coast is the tsunami warning; go to high ground on foot. The Tsunami and Warning Coordinator (or whoever is on duty at the EOC) confirms the National Tsunami Warning Center (NTWC) message and sounds the sirens (SOP-TSU-01, section 3).

**Phase 2, hours 1 to 12.** The county keeps the evacuation in place until the NTWC cancels the warning and the Warning Coordinator and the Sheriff confirm waves have stopped. A single return time is given to all partners. Search and rescue begins in zones declared safe by the Fire District. Aftershocks are expected; the USGS aftershock forecast is read to the public in ranges, with the same numbers in every message.

**Phase 3, days 1 to 3.** Damage assessment, building placards, route clearing and a lifelines restoration schedule. The county requests state resources through Oregon Emergency Management (OEM) and the Regional Coordination Center.

## 6. Decisions the EOC Command must make and by when

1. **Activate the EOC at Full Activation** on the first strong shaking report; no confirmation from the NTWC is required.
2. **Confirm the tsunami zones to evacuate.** For a local source the whole XL and XXL zones are ordered at once; the zone name is used in every message.
3. **Hospital and school evacuation.** Marlow Coast Community Hospital moves ambulatory patients uphill on foot and non-ambulatory patients by vehicle to the Kellerton Fire Station 3 apron in the first 20 minutes, then to the EOC medical branch.
4. **Close the Highway 101 Alder River bridge** at the first confirmed damage report, and the Highway 6 grade in the Neskin landslide area after any report of cracking.
5. **Declare a local emergency** within four hours and request a state declaration.
6. **Message the aftershock forecast.** The first aftershock forecast is released by the USGS within about an hour of a large earthquake. The county states probability ranges and says that a green placard is not a guarantee against later aftershocks.

## 7. Communications

The county uses MarlowAlert (text, voice and email; about 9,600 subscribers), the Wireless Emergency Alert system through IPAWS, 18 tsunami sirens (see SOP-TSU-01), the county website and a radio simulcast on two local stations. Cell service is expected to fail within an hour; amateur radio operators (the Marlow County ARES group) staff the EOC and the shelters. All messages carry the same magnitude, the same time of the event and the same zone names.

## 8. Training and exercises

MCEM holds an annual tsunami drill in October (siren test and a walking drill at Port Alder Elementary School) and a tabletop each spring. The county takes part in the state's Cascadia Rising-style regional exercise every four years.

# Marlow County: Facilities and Systems

Reference sheet for exercise players · Marlow County Emergency Management · 2027

## 1. County facts

Marlow County, Oregon: 27,500 residents, 640 square miles on the central coast, Port Alder as county seat. Principal industries are fishing and seafood processing, timber, tourism and a small hospital. Tourism peaks from June to September; April spring break puts several thousand visitors in the zone.

## 2. Emergency operations center

The EOC, 410 Hilltop Road, Kellerton, has 24 workstations, a backup generator with 5 days of fuel, a satellite phone (541-555-0125), an amateur radio station, two Starlink terminals and a conference bridge. Position list: Command (Gwen Halvorsen), Operations (Tomas Reyes), Planning, Logistics, Public Information (Dale Whitcomb), Warning (Priya Natarajan), Liaison (the state and the Red Cross). The county uses WebEOC for status boards: Tsunami Status, Lifelines, Shelters, Road Status and the Resource Board.

## 3. Marlow County Communications

Primary 911 center: 118 Front Street, Port Alder, inside the XXL zone. Backup center at the EOC with four consoles. Radio: a county VHF system with 9 repeaters; three repeaters run on propane generators and the other six on batteries for 8 hours. Computer-aided dispatch: a commercial CAD hosted at the primary center and mirrored to the EOC every 5 minutes. NOAA Weather Wire and the EAS receiver are at Position 2. The ShakeAlert feed enters the center through a delivery partner and rings an audible alarm. In the first minutes after a large earthquake the center takes about 1,200 calls in 15 minutes, nearly all for shaking.

## 4. Warning systems

- 18 tsunami sirens on batteries and solar (see SOP-TSU-01).
- MarlowAlert (text, voice, email), about 9,600 subscribers; voice calls take 14 minutes for the whole county.
- Wireless Emergency Alerts through IPAWS; Marlow County MCEM is an alerting authority and writes WEA in English and Spanish (about 11 percent of the population speaks Spanish at home).
- Radio simulcast on two local stations (the exercise calls them "Marlow Coast Radio 1" and "Marlow Coast Radio 2") and the county Facebook page.

## 6. Utilities

Cape Marlow People's Utility District serves 14,500 meters from three substations (Substation 1 at Port Alder, in the zone; Substation 2 on Kellerton Ridge; Substation 3 at Neskin Point). Port Alder Water District has two storage tanks (1.2 million gallons each), one on Spruce Hill. Cape Marlow Natural Gas serves 3,100 customers through a distribution main under the Alder River bridge.

## 7. Transportation

Highway 101 runs north-south through Port Alder and crosses the Alder River on the 1936 bridge; Highway 6 runs east to the valley through the Neskin grade, which has a history of landslides. The Marlow Coast Regional Airport (a 3,000-foot runway, no tower) is on a ridge outside the zone and is the planned helicopter landing zone. The Marlow & Pacific Short Line runs freight over a trestle to the port, two trains a week.

## 8. Outside parties

The National Tsunami Warning Center; the USGS National Earthquake Information Center and the Pacific Northwest Seismic Network; the National Weather Service forecast office in Portland; Oregon Emergency Management and its Regional Coordination Center; the Oregon Department of Geology and Mineral Industries (hazard maps); ODOT District 2; the Coast Guard; neighboring Tillamook and Lincoln counties (mutual aid); and the American Red Cross Oregon region.

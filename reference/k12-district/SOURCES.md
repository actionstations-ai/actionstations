# Sources — public guidance the Halverson documents paraphrase

The Halverson Unified School District, its schools, people, vendors' local contacts, numbers, hostnames and procedures in this folder are simulated for training use; no real district is depicted. Real products (PowerSchool, Google Workspace, Clever, ParentSquare, Tyler Munis, Transfinder, Raptor, CENTEGIX, GoGuardian, Zonar, Microsoft Defender, Veeam), federal and state agencies, MS-ISAC and K12 SIX keep their names. The shape of the documents and the obligations they describe follow the public guidance below. Nothing was copied; the plans paraphrase what these sources require or recommend, in the way a mid-size district's own plans would.

## Emergency operations planning

- U.S. Departments of Education, HHS, Homeland Security and Justice, *Guide for Developing High-Quality School Emergency Operations Plans* (June 2013) — six-step planning process; basic plan, functional annexes (evacuation, lockdown, shelter-in-place, accounting for all persons, communications and warning, family reunification, continuity, recovery, security) and threat- and hazard-specific annexes. https://www.schoolsafety.gov/resource/guide-developing-high-quality-school-emergency-operations-plans — file: https://www.ed.gov/media/document/rems-guide-developing-high-quality-emergency-operations-plans-k-12-schools-2013-113150.pdf
- CISA, *K-12 School Security Guide, 3rd Edition* and its companion for school business officials. https://www.cisa.gov/resources/k-12-school-security-guide-3rd-edition
- FERPA health-or-safety emergency exception, 34 CFR 99.36, and the record of disclosure, 34 CFR 99.32. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-A/part-99

## Threat assessment and threats to schools

- U.S. Secret Service National Threat Assessment Center, *Enhancing School Safety Using a Threat Assessment Model* (July 2018). https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/18_0711_USSS_NTAC-Enhancing-School-Safety-Guide.pdf
- U.S. Secret Service NTAC, *Protecting America's Schools* (2019) and *Averting Targeted School Violence* (2021). https://www.secretservice.gov/protection/ntac
- CISA, *K-12 Anonymized Threat Response Guidance* (2024) and *Social Media Threat Guidance for School Staff and Authorities* (2023); CISA and USSS, *K-12 Bystander Reporting Toolkit*. https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/k-12-anonymized-threat-response-guidance — https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/k-12-bystander-reporting-toolkit

## K-12 cybersecurity

- CISA, *Partnering to Safeguard K-12 Organizations from Cybersecurity Threats* (January 2023) with *Key Findings and Recommendations* and *Implement the Most Impactful Security Measures* (June 2023): MFA, patch management, tested backups, reduced exposure, an exercised incident response plan, training; join MS-ISAC and K12 SIX. https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/protecting-our-future-partnering-safeguard-k-12-organizations-cybersecurity-threats
- CISA Tabletop Exercise Package, *K-12 Schools (cyber)* situation manual. https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/cybersecurity-scenarios
- CIRCIA: the April 2024 proposed rule named local education agencies with 1,000 or more students as covered entities; the final rule was not published when these plans were written, so the plans do not rely on it.

## Incidents and advisories the scenario is modeled on

- #StopRansomware: Vice Society, AA22-249A (September 2022) — a group that disproportionately targeted the education sector, stealing data before encrypting and threatening publication. https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa22-249a
- #StopRansomware: Rhysida, AA23-319A (November 2023) — double extortion against education, healthcare, manufacturing and government targets. https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa23-319a
- FBI public warnings on swatting and hoax threats against schools (2023–2025), as summarized in the domain research; no specific incident is depicted.

## SECTION 1: PURPOSE, AUTHORITY AND PLAN BASIS

### 1.1 Purpose

This plan tells the people of the Corbin Falls Works how to recognize, classify and respond to an accidental release of chlorine or another hazardous material, how to protect workers and the fence-line community, and whom to notify, in what order and by when. Life safety comes first, then stopping the release, then the environment and property.

### 1.2 Authority and plan basis

The Works holds more than the threshold quantity of chlorine (CAS 7782-50-5) in a process and is subject to the Risk Management Program rule, 40 CFR Part 68, at Program 3. The Works is a **responding facility**: the Emergency Response Team (ERT) responds to releases with its own people and equipment, supplemented by the Corbin Falls Fire Department and the Teague County Hazardous Materials Team. This plan meets 40 CFR 68.95 and is also the site's Emergency Action Plan (29 CFR 1910.38) and HAZWOPER emergency response plan (29 CFR 1910.120(q)). Process Safety Management (29 CFR 1910.119) applies to the chlorine process; the emergency planning element of PSM is met by this plan.

This plan was coordinated with the Teague County Local Emergency Planning Committee (LEPC) on 10/14/2025 (annual coordination, 68.93) and is reflected in the Teague County Hazardous Materials Emergency Plan, Facility Annex 7.

## SECTION 2: FACILITY SUMMARY

The Corbin Falls Works (2200 River Terminal Road, Corbin Falls, Teague County, West Virginia) is a chlor-alkali plant on 140 acres on the south bank of the Teague River. It makes chlorine, caustic soda (sodium hydroxide, CAS 1310-73-2), hydrogen (CAS 1333-74-0) and sodium hypochlorite (CAS 7681-52-9). The process units, tank storage, loading positions and control systems are described in TVCC-CFW-FPD-2026; in short:

| Unit | Name | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 100 | Cell Room | Membrane electrolyzers; wet chlorine gas and hydrogen headers |
| Unit 200 | Chlorine Processing | Drying towers C-201 and C-202, compressors, liquefier, receiver V-210 |
| Unit 300 | Chlorine Storage and Loading | Storage tanks TK-31, TK-32, TK-33 (90 tons each), Rail Spot 1 to Rail Spot 4, ton-container filling at Dock 1 |
| Unit 400 | Caustic Evaporation | 50% caustic storage TK-41, TK-42; Truck Spot 1 |
| Unit 500 | Hypochlorite | Reactor R-501, product tanks TK-51, TK-52; Loading Rack 1 |
| Unit 600 | Utilities and Emergency Scrubber | Scrubber C-650, hydrogen vent stack, cooling water, power |

About 210 people work at the Works; at night the plant runs with one shift of 14: a Shift Supervisor, two board operators in the Central Control Room (CCR), eight outside operators, a security officer at Gate 1 and two ERT-qualified maintenance technicians.

Neighbors: the Riverside Addition (about 380 homes) lies east of Gate 3 across River Terminal Road; Corbin Falls Elementary School (about 410 pupils, 0.8 miles east) and the Teague River Senior Apartments (0.9 miles north-east) are inside the Alternative 1 endpoint distance. The Corbin Valley Railroad (CVRR) main line runs along the south fence.

## SECTION 4: EMERGENCY CLASSIFICATION AND ACTIVATION

| Class | Meaning | Examples | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alert | An abnormal condition that the shift is handling; no release beyond the unit | Gas detector at 1 ppm in a building, small leak isolated at once | Shift Supervisor notifies the Emergency Coordinator; log only |
| Site Emergency | A release or fire that needs the ERT or affects more than one unit; no effect expected beyond the fence | Chlorine leak in the TK building captured by C-650; hydrogen fire in Unit 100 | Site alarm, muster of affected units, ERT response; notify Teague County 911 as information |
| General Emergency | A release that leaves or may leave the site, or any event with injuries needing hospital care | Chlorine release at a rail spot with a visible cloud moving off site; fence-line detector above 1 ppm | Site alarm and full muster, sirens S-1 to S-6, request for shelter-in-place from Teague County OEM, notifications in Section 5 at once |

Any employee who sees or smells a release calls the CCR on extension 2222 or radio channel OPS-1 and says "chlorine release" and the location. The board operator sounds the site alarm without waiting for permission if a fence-line detector (AT-901 to AT-906) reads above 1 ppm.

Muster points: **Muster Point A** (Administration Building lobby, sealable), **Muster Point B** (North Gate guardhouse), **Muster Point C** (River Dock warehouse). Muster Point B is used when the wind blows from the south or west toward the Administration Building. Site gates: **Gate 1** (main, staffed 24 hours), **Gate 2** (rail gate, CVRR), **Gate 3** (River Terminal Road, emergency vehicles).

Each caller records the time, the person reached, the report number and what was said on Form ERP-5 (Notification Record). The EHS Manager decides whether a release reached the reportable quantity; **when in doubt, call the NRC** and correct the estimate later. An estimate is given in pounds with the basis (duration, hole size, railcar weight change).

### 5.2 What the first call says

"This is the Teague Valley Chemical Corbin Falls Works, 2200 River Terminal Road. We have a chlorine release at [location] since [time]. Wind is from [direction] at [speed]. We are sounding the community sirens and request shelter-in-place for [zone]. Our Incident Commander is [name] at [number]. Send fire and hazmat to Gate 3."

### 5.3 After the event

- Written follow-up to the SERC and the LEPC as the state requires (EPCRA §304(c)).
- RMP five-year accident history: an accidental release of chlorine from a covered process that caused on-site deaths, injuries or significant property damage, or known off-site deaths, injuries, evacuations, sheltering in place, property damage or environmental damage, is entered in the accident history and the RMP is corrected within six months (68.42, 68.195).
- Incident investigation under PSM begins within 48 hours.

## SECTION 6: CHLORINE RELEASE RESPONSE

### 6.1 First actions (board operator and Shift Supervisor)

1. Confirm by at least two of: fixed detectors, CCTV, outside operator report, railcar scale weight change.
2. Trip ESD-310 (rail spots) or ESD-320 (tank farm) from the hard-wired ESD pushbuttons on the CCR console (they act on the Triconex SIS, not the DCS). Confirm valve position **in the field** (limit switches can be wrong).
3. Start the emergency scrubber C-650 (SIF-650 starts it on building detectors).
4. Sound the site alarm; make the first call (Section 5.2); sirens if General Emergency.
5. Stop loading on all rail spots; stop the chlorine compressors only on the Operations Section Chief's order (the cell room trips with them; Section 6.3).

### 6.2 Entry and stopping the leak (ERT)

Entry only in pairs with SCBA and Level A suits, with a backup team ready. On a rail tank car, the ERT uses the Chlorine Institute Emergency Kit C. Manual isolation valves HV-311 to HV-314 at each rail spot can be closed by hand when the remote-operated valves do not close. Water curtains WC-1 to WC-4 knock down vapor at the rail spots; **never spray water on a leaking chlorine container** (it makes the leak worse).

### 6.3 The cell-room decision

Unit 100 makes chlorine continuously. If chlorine cannot go to liquefaction or storage, the cells must be tripped (SIF-110) or the gas sent to the scrubber, which can absorb the full cell-room output for about 40 minutes with fresh caustic. The Operations Section Chief decides, with the Incident Commander, whether to trip the cell room (a full restart takes 18 to 24 hours and the membranes can be damaged).

## SECTION 7: PROTECTIVE ACTIONS FOR THE COMMUNITY

The Works does not order protective actions off site; it **recommends** them to the Teague County Office of Emergency Management (OEM), which decides and issues them. Default recommendation for a chlorine release that leaves the site: **shelter in place** (close windows, turn off heating and air conditioning, stay inside, upper floors) for the downwind sectors to 1.5 miles; evacuation is recommended only for a long-lasting release when the cloud has passed a sector or when the county decides.

The EHS Manager runs a plume model (EPA/NOAA ALOHA) with the source estimate and data from the site weather station WX-1 (wind at 10 m on the Administration Building) and gives the Incident Commander a threat-zone map within 20 minutes. Sectors (planning grid used with the county):

| Sector | Area | Sensitive sites |
|---|---|---|
| E-1 | Riverside Addition | about 380 homes |
| E-2 | Corbin Falls east of Route 9 | Corbin Falls Elementary School, Teague River Senior Apartments |
| N-1 | Teague River and the north bank | River Road Marina, CVRR yard |
| S-1 | South of the CVRR main line | Corbin Falls Industrial Park |
| W-1 | West of the plant | Route 9 truck stop |

**The school.** The Teague County Schools transportation office and the principal of Corbin Falls Elementary are on the CAER-Text list. During school hours the school shelters in place on the county's message; the Works never calls the school to evacuate. Before school hours (06:30–07:45), the county decides whether to delay opening and bus routes through E-1 and E-2.

## SECTION 8: COMMUNITY AND MEDIA COMMUNICATION

- **Sirens S-1 to S-6** (steady tone, 3 minutes) mean "go inside and listen". The siren panel is in the CCR; S-3 and S-5 also have a voice message.
- **CAER-Text** (text and voice message to 1,140 registered neighbors) is sent by the Teague County OEM duty officer on the Works' request; the Works has a backup login on the PIO laptop (TVCC-PIO-LT1).
- **Community line** 304-555-0190 carries a recorded message updated every 30 minutes during an event.
- **Media:** all statements come from the PIO, approved by the Incident Commander; the first holding statement within 30 minutes of a General Emergency. Never speculate on the cause or on health effects; refer health questions to the Teague County Health Department. Local outlets: Channel 7 (WCFT), the Corbin Falls Courier, and the Riverside Addition neighborhood Facebook group, which the PIO monitors.
- Holding statement template: Annex B, Form ERP-8.

## SECTION 9: MEDICAL TREATMENT AND DECONTAMINATION

Exposed workers are moved upwind, given oxygen by the ERT medic and decontaminated with water at the Muster Point A decontamination station. Corbin Falls Fire Department EMS transports to Teague Valley Medical Center, which is told "chlorine inhalation" before arrival. Medical surveillance of exposed staff follows the Works' occupational health procedure.

## SECTION 10: FIRE, HYDROGEN AND CAUSTIC EVENTS

Hydrogen fires in Unit 100 are allowed to burn while the supply is isolated (SIF-120). Caustic spills at Truck Spot 1 or TK-41/TK-42 are contained in the dikes and neutralized; caustic is not an RMP substance but large releases to the river are reported to the NRC and WV DEP.

## SECTION 11: CONTROL-SYSTEM AND CYBER EVENTS

The control systems are described in TVCC-CFW-FPD-2026, Section 6, and the cyber response in TVCC-CFW-OTSEC-2026. In an emergency the rules here come first:

1. **Field over screen.** When the DCS or the SIS display disagrees with what an outside operator sees, act on the field report.
2. **Do not reset the SIS** (Triconex) after an unexplained trip or a failure to trip until the OT Engineer has checked the key switch position, the logic and the controller diagnostics, and the Incident Commander agrees.
3. Any unexplained change of a setpoint, a valve state or SIS logic is reported to the OT Engineer at once, and remote access (vendor VPN, TVC-JMP-01) is disabled on the OT Engineer's or the Incident Commander's order.
4. If the cause may be deliberate, the FSO calls the FBI and the OT Engineer calls CISA; evidence (engineering workstation, logs) is preserved before anything is rebuilt.
5. Manual operation of Unit 300 uses the field hand valves and the procedure OP-300-11; the railcar scales (WT-301 to WT-304) have local readouts.

## SECTION 12: MUTUAL AID, CONTRACTORS AND EQUIPMENT

## SECTION 13: EXERCISES, TRAINING AND PLAN MAINTENANCE

### 13.1 RMP exercise program (40 CFR 68.96)

| Exercise | Requirement | Status at the Works |
|---|---|---|
| Notification exercise | Every year (68.96(a)); tests the call list, the sirens request and CAER-Text | Last held 11/18/2025; 3 numbers corrected |
| Tabletop exercise | Simulated accidental release of a regulated substance; first one before **21 December 2026**, then at least every three years (68.96(b)(2)) | Planned for the fourth quarter of 2026 with the LEPC, Teague County OEM, CFD and the school district |
| Field exercise | First one before 15 March 2027, then at least every ten years unless local responders agree in writing that this is impractical (68.96(b)(1)) | Planned with TVIMA for February 2027 |

EPA proposed changes to the 2024 RMP amendments in February 2026; the EHS Manager confirms the current dates with Corporate EHS before each exercise is scheduled.

### 13.2 Training

ERT members: HAZWOPER technician level, annual refresher, monthly drills. All employees: annual Emergency Action Plan training, chlorine awareness. Board operators: SIS and ESD procedures yearly.

### 13.3 Plan maintenance

This plan is reviewed every year, after every General Emergency and after every exercise. Contacts are checked at each notification exercise.

## SECTION 14: RECOVERY AND RESTART

Restart of Unit 300 or Unit 100 after a General Emergency requires: the Incident Commander's termination of the emergency; a pre-startup safety review (PSSR) for any changed equipment or logic; the OT Engineer's written validation of SIS logic against the approved logic file on the offline archive; Plant Manager approval; and notice to Teague County OEM and the community line before restart.

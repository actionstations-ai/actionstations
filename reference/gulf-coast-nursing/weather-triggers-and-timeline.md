# Mariner Bay Medical Center — Hurricane Triggers, Timeline and Command Cycle

Appendix to Annex F · Owner: Director of Emergency Management (Elena Sandoval, ext. 4500)

## 1. Why a timeline

The hospital works from a time-based timeline anchored on the National Hurricane Center's forecast arrival of 34-knot (tropical-storm-force) winds at Corpus Christi, called "H" in this plan. Ground transport and ambulance transfers stop at about H, because the bridges and causeways close and the road to San Antonio floods at the low points. Clearance for the whole hospital is 12 hours, so the final horizontal evacuation decision is H minus 12 hours.

| Time | Trigger and action |
|---|---|
| H − 120 h | Hurricane watch or the hospital sits in the forecast cone: Emergency Management activates the planning team; supplies are checked (food, water, pharmacy, linen, fuel). |
| H − 96 h | Staffing Coordinator confirms ride-out rosters; Facilities fills fuel tanks (96-hour capacity). |
| H − 72 h | Incident Commander makes the shelter-or-evacuate decision with the CNO, the Medical Director, the Facilities Director and the Nueces County OEM; if evacuating, requests start for high-risk patients (infants on ventilators, ICU patients on life support). |
| H − 48 h | Hurricane warning: elective admissions stop; discharge of all who can safely go; patient tracking board started; transfer requests continue. |
| H − 36 h | Visiting closed; ride-out teams report. |
| H − 24 h | Last non-critical transfers; closing windows, moving cars to the upper level of the parking garage, relocating records and equipment from the ground floor. |
| H − 12 h | Final horizontal evacuation order deadline. After this the hospital shelters in place. |
| H − 6 h | Vertical Evacuation Phase 1 begins if surge or wind forecasts require it (NICU to 7 West, ICU to 6 West). |
| H | Tropical-storm-force winds arrive; windows are covered; the ED operates from the second floor if the ground floor floods. |
| After | When winds are below 34 knots for 2 hours, inspections and the return of units. |

## 2. Thresholds the Incident Commander watches

- The NHC forecast intensity at landfall and the track's distance to Corpus Christi: a major hurricane (Category 3 or higher) within 50 miles triggers the vertical evacuation phase.
- The forecast storm surge height from the National Hurricane Center's surge product; any forecast depth above 3 feet at the hospital address triggers moving the NICU and ICU upward, and emptying the ground floor.
- The county evacuation order for Zone B.
- Staffing: fewer than 90% of the ride-out nurses present at H − 24 h.
- Fuel and water: less than 72 hours of either.
- Wind: when sustained winds exceed 74 mph, the ambulatory wing and the upper floors' windows are protected, and no patient movement occurs near windows.

## 3. Command cycle

- Every 6 hours during the storm the Incident Commander runs an operational period briefing at 06:00, 12:00, 18:00, 00:00 in the command center (ext. 4520).
- Each unit sends a status report to the House Supervisor on the hour: census, acuity, staffing, supply status and equipment problems.
- Facilities reports every 2 hours on generators, fuel, water pressure and roof or window problems.
- The PIO issues a public statement every 6 hours and when there is a major change.

## 4. Information sources

- National Hurricane Center advisories at 5 a.m., 11 a.m., 5 p.m. and 11 p.m. Central, with intermediate advisories every 3 hours within watch areas, and its storm surge and wind speed probability graphics.
- NWS Corpus Christi forecast discussions and local warnings (hurricane, storm surge, flash flood, tornado).
- The Nueces County Office of Emergency Management daily call.
- The Texas Division of Emergency Management situation reports.
- The Coastal Bend Regional Advisory Council hospital bed availability.

The hospital does not give its own forecast; the Emergency Management Director interprets official forecasts for hospital decisions, and the PIO repeats only official information.

## 5. Consistency of messages

The PIO, the CNO and the Transfer Center Liaison use the same numbers (patients in house, patients transferred, staff on site, expected time of H). The PIO clears statements with the Incident Commander; nurses refer family and media calls to the PIO. If NHC changes the forecast, the Emergency Management Director updates H in the command center within 30 minutes and every unit is told by radio and text.

## 6. Scenario hooks

For exercises the plan assumes the most difficult case: a forecast change that shortens the horizontal evacuation window, a bridge closure that traps ambulances, lost power in the ground floor, the failure of one elevator, an NICU infant whose condition worsens during a move, and a staffing shortfall on the night of landfall.

# CCEM-LOG-04 — Depopulation Support, Disposal Sites and Logistics

Calder County Emergency Management · Operations Section · Effective March 2026

## 1. What the county provides

The state and APHIS decide the depopulation method and the crews. The county provides what the response needs on the ground: roads, water, power, security, equipment and a place for the carcasses. This document fixes who does what so that no barn waits for something the county was expected to deliver.

## 2. Planning figures

A commercial turkey barn at the Larkin Ridge Turkey Complex holds about 15,500 toms; the complex has four barns, 62,000 birds, about 1,200 tons of carcass weight. The plan goal is to depopulate every barn on an infected premises within 24 hours of the presumptive result. With limited crews, the state sequences the premises with the most potential for spread first. Foam or carbon dioxide methods are decided by the incident management team; the county records the choice and the start and end times for each barn.

## 3. Disposal options

| Option | Capacity | Conditions | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-farm composting | Per barn about 3 days to build windrows | Premises needs space and soil approval; state approval | Producer with the Board |
| On-farm burial | Per site, one barn | Depth to groundwater, distance to wells and water bodies | Minnesota Pollution Control Agency approval |
| Calder County Regional Landfill (Larkin Road) | 120 tons per day accepted for this response, 24 hours if the state approves | Lined cell, leachate collection, trucks sealed and covered, no stops | Rhys Tolliver, 555-0158 |
| Pell County Landfill | 90 tons per day | Mutual aid agreement and Minnesota Pollution Control Agency permission | Pell County |
| Rendering | By truck | High-risk vehicle; cleaning and disinfection at exit | Rendering company contracted by the state |
| Incineration | Out-of-county | Longest transport | State contract |

The Disposal Branch chooses the site with the Board and the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency, and tracks the waiting time of each load. The planning rule: no load waits more than 12 hours at a site; if a site's queue exceeds 12 hours of work, the next load goes to another site.

## 4. Roads and haul routes

Haul routes avoid the control area roads that serve other turkey premises, and the Larkin school bus route. The Highway Engineer posts the routes at the checkpoints and closes any road for the movement of the first load. Each truck is sealed, covered and leak-proof, tracked by the dispatcher by radio, and cleaned and disinfected at the site exit and again at the landfill's wash station. County trucks are never used to haul carcasses.

## 5. Facilities

- **EOC:** Calder County Public Safety Building, Larkin, 410 Elm Street (fictional address), the Training Room with 22 seats; backup in the Highway Department shop, which has a generator.
- **Staging area:** the Calder County Fairgrounds, west lot, for PPE, vehicles and crews; the fair board has agreed to host. Hand wash and shower trailers from the state arrive within 12 hours.
- **Hotel and meals:** a block of 40 rooms at two Larkin motels under the lodging agreement; meals from the Larkin Lutheran Church kitchen at 05:30, 12:00 and 18:30.
- **Water:** a 4,000-gallon tanker from Larkin Fire District; a municipal hydrant at the staging area.
- **Power:** two county generators (30 kW) for the barn lighting and fans during depopulation if the premises power fails.

## 6. Personnel

CCEM provides one site liaison per infected premises, a safety officer from the Larkin Fire District, a Sheriff's deputy for security, and a County Public Health nurse at the staging area for symptom checks. Rotations: crews work shifts of at most 10 hours; the 12-hour night shift is not allowed. The state's incident management team provides the Depopulation Group Supervisor.

## 7. Records

For each barn: the time the order was given, the time the method started, the time the barn was declared complete (the Board records the "depopulation complete" time), the weight sent to each disposal site, and the truck count. The PIO takes the "barns depopulated" count from this list at a stated time.

## 8. Welfare and mental health

Producers who lose their flocks need contact the same day. The Extension Educator and Calder County Human Services arrange a call; Minnesota's farm and rural helpline is given in each notice. Crews' fatigue and stress are watched by the safety officer.

## 9. Cleaning and disinfection

After depopulation and disposal, the premises undergoes cleaning and disinfection, supervised by APHIS; the county provides water, wastewater handling and road access for the contractor. The Board certifies completion.

## 10. Escalation

If disposal capacity falls short (queue more than 12 hours), if a checkpoint is unmanned for more than 2 hours, or if any responder is injured, the Deputy Director calls the Director, who calls the state EOC. The Director also reviews, at each 16:00 planning meeting, whether to request a county emergency declaration.

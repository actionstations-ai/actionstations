# EMGT 4320 Session Run Sheet and Rotation Plan

Marlowe State University · Emergency Operations Lab, Hargrove Hall 120 · Spring 2027 · Instructor and teaching assistant document · Course document 5

## 1. Purpose

This is the working sheet for Dr. Imani Castellan and Joel Brandvold on exercise days. It covers the room, the clock, the rotation of students through seats, how evidence is recorded, how accommodations are applied, how the hot wash is run and how a make-up version is handled. It is not shared with students; the syllabus and the rubric handout are.

## 2. The room and the equipment

Hargrove Hall 120 seats 28 at six round tables and a back-of-room control table. Each seat team sits at one table and has a laptop on the course network, a printed copy of the plan documents, a landline-style phone that rings extension numbers on the control table (the SimCell), and a whiteboard. The Student Evaluators sit at the control table with the teaching assistant. The projector shows the exercise clock and the current situation summary. Rooms 122 and 123 are held as quiet spaces for students who need breaks.

The SimCell, run by Dr. Castellan and one guest, plays everyone outside the room: the National Weather Service Charleston forecast office, Marlowe Police and Fire, the Ohio Emergency Management Agency, the Red Cross chapter, reporters from Channel 9 and the Marlowe Daily Ledger, parents, and the university president's office.

## 3. Session timeline (170 minutes)

| Clock | Activity |
|---|---|
| 14:00 | Seat teams are seated; instructor reads the no-fault rule and the objectives |
| 14:10 | Briefing and situation summary; STARTEX called by the instructor |
| 14:15 to 15:25 | Phase 1 play: forecast and watch through the evacuation order (exercise time 05:30 to 09:30) |
| 15:25 | Time jump and 10-minute break |
| 15:35 to 16:20 | Phase 2 play: flooding, shelter and the first rumors (exercise time 09:30 to 13:00) |
| 16:20 | ENDEX |
| 16:20 to 16:35 | Hot wash: what happened, what did the plan say, what would you change |
| 16:35 to 16:50 | Reflection prompts on the laptops; collect printed sheets |

Within each phase the speaker for a seat changes after 20 minutes of exercise play, on a signal from the teaching assistant. The speaker changes are listed in section 5 so the evidence record can attribute a decision or message to the right student.

## 4. Evidence capture protocol

- The teaching assistant records evaluator observations on the form, each with the student or seat, the criterion, a level and a short note, within 2 minutes of seeing the behavior. The note quotes the words or describes the act; it does not describe the student.
- Student Evaluators record the observations for the seats assigned to them. Each Student Evaluator is assigned two seats and a third seat for backup. They record at least four observations per seat per phase.
- Decisions and messages are recorded by the exercise tool with the seat. The instructor links each one to the criterion afterward.
- Hot wash contributions are tallied with one tap per contribution per student; the teaching assistant adds a note of three to six words. The hot wash audio is transcribed without names attached.
- Nothing about an accommodation is recorded in the evidence. The note field is for behavior.

After the session the instructor reviews the suggested levels, changes any that disagree with the evidence, adds a comment, and releases the score report to each student within 7 days.

## 5. Rotation plan, Section 01 (24 students)

Seats: IC (Incident Commander), PIO, CPD (Chief of Campus Police), DoS (Dean of Students), FAC (Facilities), EVAL (Student Evaluator). Teams of four per seat. Students are in six teams of four, called A to F, and move one seat each session so that no team repeats a seat until all others are tried; the fifth seat in the sequence is the one the team does not play (the "bye seat"), and which seat that is changes by team.

| Team | Practice (Week 2) | Exercise 1 (Week 5) | Exercise 2 (Week 8) | Exercise 3 (Week 11) | Exercise 4 (Week 14) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | IC | PIO | CPD | DoS | EVAL |
| B | PIO | CPD | DoS | FAC | IC |
| C | CPD | DoS | FAC | EVAL | PIO |
| D | DoS | FAC | EVAL | IC | CPD |
| E | FAC | EVAL | IC | PIO | DoS |
| F | EVAL | IC | PIO | CPD | FAC |

In Exercise 3 (the flood) the seats are therefore: Team A is DoS, Team B is FAC, Team C is EVAL, Team D is IC, Team E is PIO and Team F is CPD. Section 02 has 22 students in six teams (four teams of four and two teams of three) and follows the same table; the two smaller teams are on the PIO and the FAC seats in Exercise 3 and share the speaker role in rounds of 15 minutes.

A student who is moved between teams for a documented reason keeps the seat for the session and the rotation plan is edited by hand; the instructor records the change.

## 6. Accommodations on the day

The instructor sees the list of accommodations when the session is set up and applies them without announcing them.

- **Extended time:** the student's individual prompts (for example the reflection and any written message) carry the extended time; the shared exercise clock does not change. The student's team picks the speaker for a time-critical decision so the team is not delayed, and the student keeps their share of writing.
- **Written instead of spoken:** the seat's messages are typed; the student's decisions are recorded as written.
- **Screen-reader packet or large print:** the plan documents are given in the format on the accommodation letter before the session.
- **Breaks:** the student leaves for Room 122 or 123 at any time; the team continues and the student's seat speaker role passes to the next in the team.
- **Quieter seat:** a student may take the Student Evaluator seat with an equivalent rubric on the session where their team is on a speaking seat.

## 7. Make-ups

A student who misses a session takes a make-up version within 10 days. The make-up uses the same objectives, outcomes and rubrics with different facts: a different creek or river name, different stage numbers and times, different people and sites, and a different sequence of rumors. The student plays the seat of the missed session, with two classmates volunteering as teammates and the instructor or teaching assistant as the SimCell. The make-up is a different scenario in the same project.

## 8. Practice run

The Week 2 practice run is flagged ungraded: evidence is collected, each student gets a report and nothing goes to the gradebook or the dashboard. The practice scenario is a one-hour power outage in Riverside Hall.

## 9. Grading routine and gradebook export

1. Within 48 hours the instructor and the teaching assistant check every criterion has evidence.
2. The instructor confirms or changes each suggested level, with a comment where the instructor differs.
3. Team scores go to every team member unless the instructor sets an individual score from evidence.
4. At the end of the term the instructor exports the gradebook CSV for each section, reviews the preview (students without a score, practice excluded, missing identifiers) and uploads the file to the university's learning management system. Only scores leave the project.

## 10. Participation and reliability checks

After each session the dashboard shows each student's share of decisions, messages and hot wash contributions against an even share. A student below half the even share for two sessions gets an email from the instructor inviting them to office hours; the figure is a prompt for a conversation and never enters the grade unless the instructor links a contribution to a criterion. Where a Student Evaluator and the teaching assistant rated the same student on the same criterion, the dashboard shows agreement; with about 24 students per section the numbers are unstable, so the instructor reads them as hints and reviews the criteria with the lowest agreement before the next session.

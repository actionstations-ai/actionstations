# Sources — public guidance the EMGT 4320 course documents paraphrase

Marlowe State University, the town of Marlowe, Sugar Camp Run, its gauge stages, every person, office, building, phone extension, newspaper and television station named in this folder, and the course EMGT 4320 are simulated for training use. The real places and agencies named (Athens County and the Hocking River in Ohio, the National Weather Service Charleston, West Virginia, forecast office, the Ohio River Forecast Center, the USGS, the Ohio Emergency Management Agency, the American Red Cross) are real. The documents are original text that paraphrases what the public sources below describe, the way a university's own plans and syllabus would. No student names or records appear anywhere in the folder.

- FEMA, Homeland Security Exercise and Evaluation Program (HSEEP), including the principle that exercises are low-stress, no-fault learning environments, and the exercise roles of evaluators and the simulation cell. Public domain.
- FEMA, National Incident Management System (NIMS) and the Incident Command System, activation and span of control. Public domain.
- FEMA Emergency Management Institute, independent study courses on exercise design and the Emergency Operations Center (IS-120, IS-130, IS-775). Public domain.
- U.S. Department of Education, The Handbook for Campus Safety and Security Reporting (Clery Act) and 34 CFR 668.46 on emergency notification and timely warning. Public domain.
- U.S. Department of Education, Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), 34 CFR Part 99, including the health or safety emergency exception, and the Student Privacy Policy Office guidance on campus emergencies. Public domain.
- National Weather Service, flash flood watch and warning products and the "Turn Around, Don't Drown" public messages; NWS Ohio River Forecast Center river forecast products. Public domain.
- FEMA, Shelter Field Guide and the Red Cross shelter standards for sheltering people with access and functional needs (general practice described, nothing copied).
- Association of American Colleges and Universities, VALUE rubrics are named only as a model of rubric design; their text is not used (non-commercial license).
- Bloom's taxonomy of learning levels (named only).

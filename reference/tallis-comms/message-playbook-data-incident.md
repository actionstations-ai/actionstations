# TLC-COM-02: Message Playbook for a Customer Data Incident

Tallis Home Products · Corporate Communications · Revision 3

## 1. Use of this playbook

This playbook holds the templates and the standing message map for an incident involving customer personal data held in the web store, the loyalty program or the customer care system. The VP Corporate Communications fills the blanks from the fact sheet. Square brackets are filled; nothing in them is guessed.

## 2. The message map

**Core message (the one thing):** Tallis learned [time] that [what happened]; we are investigating with outside experts; we will tell affected customers directly; here is what customers should do now.

**Supporting message 1, what we know:** the facts on the fact sheet, with times. Example kinds: when we detected the activity, which system, what kind of data categories the system holds, that payment card numbers are tokenized and not stored by Tallis.

**Supporting message 2, what we do not know yet:** how many accounts, how the access happened, whether data left the system. We say when we expect to know.

**Supporting message 3, what we are doing and what customers should do:** the web store vendor's access was suspended at [time]; Tallis engaged a forensic firm and outside counsel; we notified law enforcement; customers should change their Tallis password and any password reused elsewhere, and watch for emails that claim to be from us. Tallis will never ask for a password or card number by email.

## 3. Holding statement template

> **[Company] statement on [subject]** · [Date, time, time zone]
> Earlier today Tallis Home Products identified [unusual activity / a claim] involving [system]. We have [action taken: suspended access, engaged outside experts, informed law enforcement]. We do not yet know [what is not known]. Customers do not need to take action beyond [action]. We will update this page by [time]. Questions from customers: [care line and email]. Reporters: press@tallishome.example, (937) 555-0141.

Rules: three to six sentences, no adjectives about the attacker, no promise that "no data was taken", no reference to the number of accounts unless on the fact sheet.

## 4. Hard questions and the answers

1. **"Hackers say they have 1.9 million of your customers. Is that true?"** "We are aware of the claim and are investigating it. We have not confirmed it. We will tell customers directly if their information was involved."
2. **"Were credit card numbers stolen?"** "Tallis does not store full card numbers. Card data is held as tokens by our payment processor. We are checking whether anything else was reached."
3. **"Why did it take [hours] to tell customers?"** "We told the public within [time] of confirming the activity, and customers will be told as soon as we know whose information was involved."
4. **"Did you pay a ransom?"** "This is not a ransom matter" or "We do not discuss ransom matters" as the facts and Legal require. Nothing is invented.
5. **"Is your stock price falling because of this?"** "We do not comment on the stock. Investor questions go to Investor Relations."
6. **"Whose fault was this, your vendor's or yours?"** "We are focused on investigating and protecting customers. We will share findings when the investigation is complete."

## 5. Rumor and false claim protocol

1. Social listening flags a claim that appears on three or more accounts, or on one account with more than 5,000 followers, or in a reporter's question.
2. Social Media Manager records the claim word for word, the time and the account on the listening report, and does not reply.
3. Writer compares the claim with the fact sheet: true, false, unknown. Unknown stays "we are investigating".
4. If false and harmful (for example "your refund site is down, enter your card here" or "all passwords were stored in plain text"), COMMS approves a short factual reply on the company accounts and the newsroom page, and the Social Media Manager requests platform action for impersonation or phishing, with the URL and a screenshot.
5. Phishing that copies Tallis is reported to the platform, the registrar and the hosting provider, and to the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center by Security.

## 6. Corrections

A correction names what was wrong and what is right, where and when the earlier statement appeared, and is posted in the same place with the same prominence, dated. Example: "A statement posted at 09:40 said the affected system held no payment information. The web store account system holds the last four digits of the card used. Full card numbers are not stored by Tallis."

## 7. Message consistency

Three documents must agree at all times on the same fact: the public statement, the Form 8-K (or Item 8.01 text) and the customer email. The fact sheet is the single source. The VP Corporate Communications checks these items before any release: the date and time of discovery, the system, the data categories, the number of affected accounts (or "under investigation"), the steps taken and the phone number for customers. If any of them differs between two documents, nothing goes out until the difference is fixed.

## 8. Customer Care script (summary)

Agents answer from the macro approved by COMMS and Legal. They say: "We are investigating; we have no confirmation that your account is involved; here is what you can do." They do not guess, do not forward screenshots, and escalate reporters and lawyers to (937) 555-0141. The queue number on a normal Tuesday is under 40 calls in the first hour. If the queue exceeds 300 waiting calls, Customer Care sends the web banner message and moves 40 agents from the order desk.

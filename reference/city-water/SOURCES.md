# Sources — public guidance the Millbrook documents paraphrase

The City of Millbrook, its Department of Water & Sewer, and every person, vendor, number, hostname and procedure in this folder are simulated for training use; no real utility is depicted. The shape of the documents and the obligations they describe follow the public guidance below. Nothing was copied; the plans paraphrase what these sources require or recommend, in the way a mid-size utility's own plans would.

## Statute and regulation

- America's Water Infrastructure Act of 2018 §2013 / Safe Drinking Water Act §1433 — risk and resilience assessments and emergency response plans for community water systems serving more than 3,300 people; the four ERP elements (resilience strategies including physical security and cybersecurity; plans and procedures and equipment for responding to malevolent acts and natural hazards; mitigation actions; detection strategies); five-year review and recertification; coordination with local emergency planning committees. EPA overview: https://www.epa.gov/waterresilience/americas-water-infrastructure-act-risk-assessments-and-emergency-response-plans — EPA ERP template instructions: https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2019-07/documents/190712-awia_erp_template_instructions_kab_508c_v6.pdf — certification deadlines and recertification: https://www.epa.gov/waterresilience/certification-statements-risk-and-resilience-assessment-or-emergency-response-plan
- 40 CFR 141.202 — Tier 1 public notification: notice as soon as practical and within 24 hours; consultation with the primacy agency within 24 hours; the situations that require Tier 1 notice (waterborne disease outbreak or other waterborne emergency, E. coli, nitrate, turbidity treatment failures, chemical/intentional contamination as the primacy agency determines). https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/141.202 and https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-141/subpart-Q
- EPA Public Notification Handbook and Region 8 boil-water advisory templates (used only for the required elements of a notice and the kinds of advisories; the Millbrook templates are written independently): https://www.epa.gov/node/15257 and https://epa.gov/region8-waterops/ground-water-rule-boil-water-advisory-public-notification-template

## Water-sector cybersecurity guidance

- CISA / EPA / FBI, *Top Cyber Actions for Securing Water Systems* (February 2024; updated with AWWA, WaterISAC and MS-ISAC resources): reduce internet exposure of OT, assess regularly, change default passwords, inventory OT/IT assets, develop and exercise incident response and recovery plans, back up OT/IT, reduce exposure to known vulnerabilities, train. https://cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2024/02/21/cisa-epa-and-fbi-release-top-cyber-actions-securing-water-systems
- CISA / FBI / EPA, *Water and Wastewater Sector Incident Response Guide* (January 2024): the four incident-response stages and the federal roles and reporting paths at each. https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-01/wws-sector_incident-response-guide.pdf and https://cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2024/01/18/incident-response-guide-wws-sector
- EPA cybersecurity planning page (Cybersecurity Incident Action Checklist, asset-inventory guidance, Water Cybersecurity Assessment Tool): https://www.epa.gov/waterresilience/cybersecurity-planning
- WaterISAC, *12 Cybersecurity Fundamentals for Water and Wastewater Utilities* (consolidated May 2025; aligned with CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals): https://www.waterisac.org/spotlight/waterisacs-12-cybersecurity-fundamentals-water-and-wastewater-utilities-release and https://www.waterisac.org/tools
- AWWA, *Water Sector Cybersecurity Risk Management Guidance* and the Cybersecurity Assessment Tool (small-system and full options; mapped to the AWIA cybersecurity provision): https://www.awwa.org/resource/cybersecurity-guidance/

## Incidents the scenarios are modeled on

- Oldsmar, Florida water treatment plant, February 2021 — an intruder used remote-desktop software on a plant computer to raise the sodium hydroxide setpoint from about 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm; an operator saw the cursor move and the setpoint change and reversed it; remote access was then disabled; officials said treatment redundancies would have caught the change. Reporting: https://www.theregister.com/2021/02/09/florida_water_hacked/ and https://www.darkreading.com/attacks-breaches/hacker-raised-chemical-settings-at-water-treatment-plant-to-dangerous-levels/d/d-id/1340095 (The Millbrook "ScreenBridge on MBW-HMI-02" hook and the caustic setpoint limits in Appendix A follow this case.)
- Joint advisory AA23-335A, *IRGC-Affiliated Cyber Actors Exploit PLCs in Multiple Sectors, Including U.S. Water and Wastewater Systems Facilities* (December 1, 2023) — internet-exposed PLC/HMI units with default credentials defaced at multiple U.S. water facilities, including a booster station; mitigations: change default passwords, remove the devices from the internet, MFA for remote access, backups of logic and configuration. https://cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa23-335a (The Millbrook cellular-only RTU-05 and its modem web page follow this case.)

## SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

This plan tells the City of Millbrook how to inform customers, critical facilities, partner agencies and the news media during any event that affects the safety, pressure or availability of drinking water. It supports Section 9 (Public Notification and Advisories) and Section 10 (Public Information) of the Emergency Response Plan (MDWS-ERP-2026). The ERP decides *whether* an advisory is issued; this plan governs *how* the decision is communicated, by whom, in what order and in what words.

### 1.2 Scope

Covers: boil water advisories, do-not-drink and do-not-use notices, precautionary advisories, outage and pressure notices, planned-work notices during an emergency, media relations, social-media monitoring and rumor control, customer-service surge, and the rescission of each notice. Also covers the public side of a cyber incident at the Department of Water & Sewer (DWS), including the business systems (billing, customer portal, AMI) and the control system.

### 1.3 Principles

- Public health language goes out on time even when the cause is not yet known. The clock in Section 5 runs from the moment the Director decides, not from when the cause is understood.
- One voice: every public statement is issued or approved by the PIO. Operators, crews and customer-service agents refer questions to the PIO and never speculate about cause.
- Everything we say must be matched by what crews are doing on the street. Door hangers, MillbrookAlert and the outage map describe the same boundaries.
- Translations are part of the notice, not an afterthought. Spanish and Nepali versions go out with, or within one hour of, the English notice.

## SECTION 4: ADVISORY TYPES AND MANDATORY CONTENT

### 4.1 Types

| Type | When used (ERP Section 9 decides) | What the public is told to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Boil Water Advisory** | Loss of pressure below 20 psi in an area; a main break that depressurizes an area; interruption of a treatment process (filtration, disinfection) where water may have been delivered untreated; microbial sample result that requires public notice | Bring water to a rolling boil for one minute before drinking, cooking, making ice, brushing teeth or washing produce; bottled water is an alternative; bathing and laundry are allowed |
| **Do Not Drink** | Chemical contamination or chemical overfeed (for example a sodium hydroxide dose far above range), an unknown contaminant, or a credible intentional-contamination threat where boiling does not help | Do not drink, cook with, make ice from or give pets the tap water even if boiled; use bottled water; showering is permitted unless the notice says otherwise |
| **Do Not Use** | A contaminant that is harmful on skin contact or by inhalation, or a situation where any use could spread contamination | No use of tap water for any purpose until further notice; the City provides bottled water and tanker points |
| **Precautionary Advisory** | At the Director's discretion after consulting DDAGW and the Health Commissioner, when tampering is suspected or confirmed at a storage facility, when telemetry cannot confirm conditions, or when sampling is pending and the Director prefers to warn early | Same instructions as the matching advisory type, with the word "precautionary" and a plain statement that no contamination has been confirmed |

### 4.2 Mandatory Content

Every advisory, whatever the channel, carries the following elements in plain language, in this order. The federal public-notification rule and Ohio EPA's rules require each of them; the PIO's templates in Section 10 satisfy them.

1. **What happened** — the event in one sentence, with the date and time it began.
2. **Who is affected** — the Zone(s) and street boundaries from the map of record (MDWS-MAP-2026), and a link to the outage map polygon.
3. **What to do** — the action for the advisory type (Section 4.1), including what is still safe.
4. **Health effects** — who is most at risk and what symptoms to watch for, in the plain wording the Water Quality Manager maintains for each type (Section 10 carries the approved wording).
5. **What the City is doing** — repairs, sampling, flushing, alternate supply.
6. **When it will end** — the earliest possible lifting time and the condition for lifting (for a boil water advisory, two consecutive rounds of satisfactory bacteriological samples collected 24 hours apart, so at least 48 to 72 hours).
7. **Contact** — DWS Customer Service (555) 031-2222, the website, and the statement that the notice is being issued by the City of Millbrook Department of Water & Sewer, public water system OH-CARVER-0418.
8. **Share it** — a request that landlords, employers, schools and building managers pass the notice to everyone they serve.

Multilingual: the Spanish and Nepali versions are issued as complete notices, not summaries. Social posts carry a one-line Spanish and Nepali lead with a link.

### 4.3 What an Advisory Never Says

- Attribution of a cyber incident to any group or country.
- Which control-system components, hosts or vendors were affected.
- Any statement that the water "is safe" before the Water Quality Manager has results that say so.
- A lifting time that depends on an event outside the City's control (a vendor, a lab courier) without saying so.

## SECTION 5: TIMELINES AND SEQUENCE

### 5.1 Targets

| Step | Target from the Director's decision | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Critical-customer phone calls complete | 60 minutes (hospital and Carver Kidney Center within 15 minutes) | Customer Service Lead and Deputy PIO |
| Ohio EPA DDAGW consulted (if not already) and the County Health Commissioner told what is going out | Before public release; never later than 24 hours from learning of the situation | Water Quality Manager |
| MillbrookAlert sent to the affected polygon | 90 minutes | Deputy PIO |
| Website, outage map, social accounts | 2 hours | Deputy PIO |
| Channel 4 crawl requested, Millbrook Courier emailed | 2 hours | PIO |
| Door hangers started where required | 3 hours | Distribution Superintendent |
| Spanish and Nepali notices issued | Same as English or within 1 hour | Deputy PIO |
| Outside limit for any Tier 1 situation | 24 hours from learning of it | Director |
| Repeat notice | Every 12 hours while the advisory stands; and whenever the area or instructions change | Deputy PIO |
| Rescission | Within 2 hours of the Water Quality Manager confirming results, through every channel used for the advisory | PIO |

### 5.2 Sequence

1. The Director records the decision on Form ERP-2 (Advisory Decision Record) with the Water Quality Manager; the PIO is on that call.
2. The PIO selects the Section 10 template and fills in the boundaries from GIS with the Distribution Superintendent.
3. Critical customers are called (Section 6). Harvest Ridge Foods is called before any public release under a Do Not Drink.
4. MillbrookAlert, website, outage map, social, media, in that order, within the targets above.
5. The Deputy PIO opens the media log and the rumor log (Section 8) and reports to the PIO every 2 hours.
6. Customer Service opens the surge line (Section 9).
7. Repeat and update as the situation changes; rescind through every channel used.

## SECTION 7: MEDIA RELATIONS

### 7.1 Spokespersons

The PIO and the Director are the spokespersons. The Water Quality Manager answers health and sampling questions when present. The Mayor and City Manager may speak at a press conference after a PIO briefing. No other employee speaks to the media; crews on the street say "the City's communications office will have that information" and give the PIO's number.

### 7.2 Holding Statements

A holding statement goes to Channel 4 and the Millbrook Courier within 30 minutes of any media inquiry about an unplanned event, even before facts are confirmed. It states what is known, what is being done, and when the next update will come. Section 10.5 holds the cyber-incident version.

### 7.3 Press Conferences

Held in the City Hall council chamber (seating 60, podium feed) with the JIC when the County EOC is open. First briefing within 4 hours of a Do Not Drink or a Do Not Use; daily at 11:00 thereafter. The PIO prepares a one-page fact sheet, the current map, and the Annex B message map for each speaker.

### 7.4 Cyber Incidents

- Do not confirm or deny that an intrusion occurred until the SCADA / IT Security Manager and the City IT Director agree on the facts and the FBI/CISA liaison (when engaged) has been consulted on what may be released.
- Never name affected systems, vendors, hosts or the attack method. Say "a technology issue affecting [billing / the control system]" and what the public effect is.
- Water-quality statements come only from the Water Quality Manager's results. "Our operators are running the plant manually and testing water quality every two hours" is approved wording when true.
- Questions about ransom demands are answered with "the City is working with law enforcement and will not discuss that."

### 7.5 Media Log

The Deputy PIO logs every inquiry (time, outlet, reporter, question, answer given, by whom) on the shared media log. The log is reviewed at the hot wash.

## SECTION 8: SOCIAL MEDIA AND RUMOR CONTROL

### 8.1 Monitoring

From the first notice until rescission, the Deputy PIO monitors the City accounts, the Nextdoor neighborhoods, and the community groups "Millbrook Moms", "Hilltop Neighbors" and "Millbrook Rants & Raves" (Facebook) at least every 30 minutes, and logs posts that reach more than 50 interactions or that make a factual claim about water safety.

### 8.2 Response Matrix

| Rumor or claim | Typical form | Response | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| "The water is poisoned" | Facebook post, often with a photo of discolored water | Reply with the current advisory, what the lab is testing, and the Customer Service number; never argue; escalate to the PIO if a news outlet picks it up | Deputy PIO |
| "The plant was hacked" | X post, Nextdoor | Approved line only: "The City is aware of posts about a possible technology issue. Our treatment plant is operating and water quality is being tested on schedule. We will share confirmed information here." Nothing more until Section 7.4 clears it | Deputy PIO after PIO check |
| "The hospital is on bottled water" | Nextdoor, group chats | Confirm only with the hospital's own public statement; otherwise "Millbrook General Hospital has its own emergency water plan and is in direct contact with us" | Deputy PIO |
| "The advisory is for the whole city" (when it is one Zone) | Any | Post the map; use street boundaries, not Zone numbers, in replies | Deputy PIO |
| "The water smells like a pool" | Nextdoor, calls | Explain chlorine residual plainly if the Water Quality Manager confirms a residual change; invite the customer to call so the location is logged (complaint clusters are a detection input, ERP Section 6) | Customer Service / Deputy PIO |
| "The City knew for hours and said nothing" | Comments | State the time the condition was confirmed and the time the first notice went out; do not debate | PIO |
| Fake "City of Millbrook" account or forged notice | Any platform | Report to the platform; post from the verified accounts that the notice is not ours; notify Millbrook Police and the City IT Director | PIO |

### 8.3 Rules

- Replies carry facts and the next-update time, never speculation about cause or blame.
- Screenshots of HMI screens, maps with facility addresses, or crew photos showing equipment labels are not posted.
- Posts by employees on personal accounts about the event are a conduct matter for the Director; the PIO asks for removal once.

## SECTION 9: CUSTOMER SERVICE SURGE

### 9.1 Experience

During the 2023 Hilltop Transmission Main break, DWS Customer Service received 2,400 calls in 6 hours against a normal 180 per day; the phone system's queue limit (30) was reached within 20 minutes and callers heard a busy signal. The 2025 upgrade raised the queue to 120 and added an overflow recording.

### 9.2 Surge Procedure

1. The Customer Service Lead activates the overflow recording (current advisory, boundaries, website, next update time) within 15 minutes of the notice.
2. Six additional agents are drawn from the Utility Billing and Permits counters; they use the Section 10.7 call script and a one-page fact sheet. Agents log every call's address so the Control Room can see complaint clusters by street (ERP Section 6).
3. If the Pinnacle Utility Billing system or the customer portal is unavailable (for example during a business-network outage), agents record contacts on paper Form CS-2 and the website carries a banner saying the portal is unavailable and that no late fees will be charged.
4. Calls reporting illness are transferred to Carver County Public Health; calls reporting tampering, open hatches or suspicious persons at a tank or station are transferred immediately to the Control Room and Millbrook Police.
5. The Customer Service Lead reports call volume and the top three question types to the PIO every 2 hours.

>

>

>
> **Health information.** Microbes that can enter a depressurized system may cause diarrhea, cramps, nausea, headaches or other symptoms, and can be a particular risk for infants, young children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems. >
> **What the City is doing.** Crews are [repairing the main / restoring pressure / restoring treatment]. We are flushing lines and collecting water samples. The advisory stays in place until two rounds of samples, taken 24 hours apart, show the water meets standards. We expect that to take at least [48–72] hours.

> **Please share this notice** with others who use this water, especially in apartments, businesses, schools and care facilities.

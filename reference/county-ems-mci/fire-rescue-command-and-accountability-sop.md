# Brandt Lake Fire Department: Technical Rescue, Command Transfer and Accountability

Brandt Lake Fire Department · Chief Ron Szymanski · SOP 4.2 · Revision 6

## 1. Purpose

This SOP covers vehicle extrication in a multi-vehicle or bus crash, the transfer of incident command, the accountability of crews at the scene and the response to a firefighter in distress (a mayday). It is used with the LCEMS Mass Casualty Incident Plan.

## 2. Resources

Brandt Lake Fire Department has 3 stations (Station 1 on Main Street, Station 2 at Pell Harbor Road, Station 3 on Highway 21), 6 engines, 2 trucks, 2 heavy rescue units (R1 with hydraulic spreaders and cutters, struts and cribbing; R2 with air bags) and a battalion chief on duty at all times (Battalion Chief Tom Aldrich, call sign Battalion 1). A hazmat team of 8 trained to the operations level is on call at Station 1. Fire staffing on a weekday evening is 14 career and 8 on-call.

## 3. Incident command and transfer

The first arriving company officer takes command and gives a size-up: location, what is involved, hazards, actions and requests. Command is transferred to Battalion 1 on his arrival with a face-to-face or radio briefing covering the situation, hazards, resources assigned and their tasks, the objectives and the time. The outgoing commander moves to Operations or Rescue Group. The dispatcher announces the transfer with the time and records it in CAD. Radio traffic for a large crash uses three channels: dispatch (LARK DISP 1), the command channel (LARK CMD 2) and the tactical channel (LARK TAC 3), with the medical channel MED 1 reserved for hospital communications.

## 4. Extrication of a large vehicle

- Size up, stabilize (chocks, cribbing, struts) and control hazards: fuel leaks, batteries, airbags, power lines. A bus that is on its side or against a ditch needs struts before anyone cuts.
- Tell the Medical Group which patients are trapped, how many and the time needed to free each. Patients who can be freed in 10 minutes are tagged by triage and prioritized.
- Rescue crews cut away the roof or side window of a bus as directed; no one climbs into a damaged bus without a second person and radio contact.
- A fuel spill is contained (diking, absorbent) and the storm drain covered; spills over 25 gallons are reported to the state environmental agency.

## 5. Accountability

Every responder on scene has a tag on the company officer's board. The Accountability Officer (a captain assigned by Command) keeps a list of every company, who is in the hot zone and what time they entered. A personnel accountability report (PAR) is taken every 20 minutes and after any change in conditions, a collapse, a secondary event or a mayday. When a PAR is requested, each company reports "Engine 2 PAR, 4 of 4". A company that does not answer is treated as missing.

## 6. Mayday

A firefighter in distress says "Mayday, mayday, mayday" with name, unit, location and problem. The dispatcher clears the channel and announces "Emergency traffic" and the commander orders a PAR and activates the Rapid Intervention Team (RIT, a dedicated two-person crew staged at every working incident). After a mayday the commander transfers command or names a Safety Officer to run the rescue and keeps the original incident under a second command position. The Accountability Officer logs the time of the mayday, the time the RIT was committed and the time the firefighter was removed.

## 6.1 Rehab

On an extended scene, a rehabilitation area (EMS monitors firefighters' vital signs) is set up away from the treatment area. A firefighter injured during extrication is treated by a separate EMS crew, not counted among the incident's patients unless the Medical Group reports them so, and the count of patients is stated in both ways in the report.

## 7. Traffic and the scene

The Sheriff's deputies and the state patrol close Highway 21 and set up a detour on County Road K. The helicopter landing zone is at the Highway 21 and County Road K intersection, 600 feet from the crash, with a landing zone officer, and is cleared of wires and vehicles. The Public Works department supplies lights and sand.

## 8. After the incident

The Chief holds a critical incident stress debriefing within 72 hours. The department reviews radio recordings and times for: command transfer, PAR, extrication times and dispatch. The department's report is part of the county's after action report.

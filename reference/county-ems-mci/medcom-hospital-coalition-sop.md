# SOP-MC-02: Medical Communications, Destinations and the Hospital Capacity Board

Larkfield Healthcare Coalition · LCEMS · Revision 5

## 1. Purpose

This procedure tells the MEDCOM nurse, the Medical Communications Coordinator in the field and the hospitals how to match patients to hospitals during an MCI.

## 2. Hospitals and capabilities

| Hospital | Beds | Capabilities | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Larkfield Regional Medical Center (LRMC), Brandt Lake | 140 | Level II trauma center, 3 trauma bays, 4 ORs, ICU 12 beds, pediatric ED | MEDCOM site; air medical helipad on the roof |
| St. Brendan's Community Hospital, Pell Harbor | 45 | Level IV trauma, ED 8 beds, CT, no neurosurgery | 19 miles from Brandt Lake |
| Pinecrest Memorial Hospital, Wausau Road | 25 | Critical access, ED 5 beds | 28 miles; minor injuries only |
| Lakeshore University Medical Center | 520 | Level I trauma, burn center, pediatric trauma, neurosurgery | 52 miles; 22 minutes by air; helicopter only for Immediate patients |

The coalition coordinator is Greg Wilmot. Each hospital has a Medical Communications contact on call and posts its status in the capacity board (a shared web page that LRMC MEDCOM keeps) at the start of every MCI and every 30 minutes.

## 3. Status categories

A hospital's status is one of: **Open** (accepting any patient), **Limited intake** (accepting only the categories it lists), **Diversion** (not accepting arrivals unless an unstable patient cannot go elsewhere) and **Closed** (no arrivals, for example for a facility emergency). The nursing supervisor changes the status and tells MEDCOM, who updates the board and notes the time and reason.

## 4. Destination rules

1. **Immediate (red) patients** go to the closest appropriate trauma center. In Larkfield County this is LRMC for adults and children, up to its trauma bay count: no more than 3 Immediate patients to LRMC in the first 30 minutes. Further Immediate patients go to St. Brendan's if the injury is not one that needs neurosurgery, or to Lakeshore by helicopter. Head injuries with neurological deficit, burns over 20 percent and penetrating chest injuries are routed to Lakeshore when the air medical crew accepts.
2. **Delayed (yellow) patients** are distributed so that no hospital receives more than one-third of the total in 30 minutes and none above its ED bed count. LRMC receives at most 8 Delayed in the first hour.
3. **Minor (green) patients** go by bus to the Brandt Lake High School minor injury site (a team of 4 paramedics and the Red Cross) and are released or sent by shuttle to St. Brendan's or Pinecrest.
4. **Expectant (gray) patients** are held in the treatment area with comfort care and reassessed; the Medical Director may change the category when resources arrive.
5. Self-presenting patients (walking wounded who leave by car) are expected at the nearest hospital. Every hospital tags and triages arrivals at the door and reports the count to MEDCOM every 15 minutes.

A destination decision is logged: tag number, priority, hospital, mode and reason. A destination is a poor fit when a patient goes to a hospital that cannot treat the injury or that is on Diversion; the coalition's after action review scores the share of patients taken to an appropriate hospital.

## 5. Hospital surge actions

Each hospital implements its surge plan when MEDCOM calls an MCI at Level 2: discharge stable inpatients, clear the ED, open the surge ward, call in staff, activate the Hospital Incident Command System. LRMC's surge capacity is 25 additional ED patients in two hours; St. Brendan's 10; Pinecrest 6. The coalition arranges beds and the transfer of inpatients from LRMC to Lakeshore. Blood: LRMC has 40 units O-negative on the day shift; a request for more goes to the regional blood center.

## 6. Reporting counts

MEDCOM maintains a patient list by tag number with: destination, arrival time, and disposition. Counts are reconciled every 30 minutes with the field Transportation Unit Leader. Differences are resolved by name or tag. Walking wounded who left on their own are counted as "self-transport, unknown destination" until a hospital reports them.

## 7. Hospital communication with the public and families

Hospitals do not release patient names or conditions by phone; they refer family members to the reunification center line 920-555-0195. The hospital PIO tells the JIC the hospital's count, never the field's.

## 8. Coalition role

The coalition coordinator opens a coalition call at Level 2, asks the hospitals for status and capacity, and notifies the state healthcare coalition and Wisconsin Emergency Management for Level 3. The coordinator checks that the count the coalition gives the state equals the count in the field report and the hospital reports, or says why not.

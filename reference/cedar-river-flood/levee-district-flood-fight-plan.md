# Levee and Drainage District No. 3: Flood Fight Plan

Tallis County, Iowa · Board Chair Margo Lund · Superintendent Bill Hoeksema · Revision 7

## 1. The district and its works

The district was organized in 1951 under Iowa law to protect the south side of Cedar Ford. It maintains 3.8 miles of earthen levee on the right bank of the Cedar River, two pump stations (Pump Station 1 at Mill Street with three 36-inch pumps, Pump Station 2 at Hollow Creek with two 24-inch pumps), a drainage ditch system and one gated outlet at Hollow Creek. The levee protects 340 homes, the Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant, Tallis Elementary School and about 40 businesses on Front Street south. The design flood is 25.5 feet at CFDI4 and the crest of the levee is at stage 26.0 feet at the gauge. The district office and shop are at 14 Levee Road. Phone 319-555-0152; the after-hours line is 319-555-0153.

## 3. Stage triggers

| Stage at CFDI4 | Action |
|---|---|
| 13.0 ft | Superintendent notifies the county and starts daily inspections; open the shop fill station |
| 17.0 ft | Patrol twice a day; close pump station gates as needed; confirm 40,000 bags and two pumps are ready |
| 20.0 ft | 24-hour patrol with a two-person team on foot every 2 hours; the Superintendent is on site |
| 22.0 ft | Place sandbag rings around known sand boil areas; closure of Levee Road to the public; ask for an Army Corps of Engineers technical assistance visit |
| 24.0 ft | Continuous presence at the Hollow Creek low spot (a section 1.1 feet lower than the rest); Levee Road closed; notify the Coordinator of the 18-hour evacuation clock for the leveed area |
| 25.5 ft | Design flood reached; freeboard 0.5 ft to the crest |

The district uses the county's decision aid: when the forecast crest is at or above 23.0 ft, the Superintendent tells the Coordinator the district's judgement of the levee's condition at the next briefing.

## 4. Patrol and what to look for

Patrol teams of two use radios (the county VHF channel 4) and carry a field report form. They report: seepage (clear or muddy water on the landside toe), sand boils (a cone of sand with water bubbling), slumps and cracks, animal burrows, loss of freeboard, and overtopping. A boil with muddy water, or a slump, is an emergency report to the Superintendent and the Coordinator at once. A sand boil with clear water is ringed with sandbags to a height that stops the flow without sealing it. The patrol log records the time, the location by station number (0+00 at Mill Street) and the stage.

## 5. Sandbags and pumps

The district's supply is 18,000 filled bags in the shop yard and the county's 22,000 in the county shop. The plan needs 80,000 bags, 12 truckloads of sand, six 6-inch pumps and 2,000 feet of hose. Resources are ordered through the county Logistics position. Volunteers fill bags at the shop between 07:00 and 19:00 and are directed by the Patrol Captain. The district does not accept volunteers on the levee.

## 6. Pump station operation

Pump Station 1 moves the district's interior drainage when the river is above 15 ft and the flap gate at the outlet closes. At 17 ft the Assistant Superintendent switches to diesel backup if the PUD feeder is lost. Pump Station 1 has fuel for 36 hours. Fuel orders go to the county Logistics position at least 12 hours before the tank falls below one half.

## 7. Communications with the county

The Superintendent calls the EOC at 06:00 and 18:00 with a status. For any distress report he calls the Coordinator directly at 319-555-0140. The Superintendent never states in public that the levee is "safe"; the district reports the levee's measured condition and the stage and says it will tell the county at once if conditions change.

## 8. After the crest

After the crest the river falls slowly: expect the levee to stay saturated for weeks. Patrols continue until the stage is below 15 ft. The district inspects the whole levee with the Army Corps of Engineers before the county permits reentry to the leveed area.

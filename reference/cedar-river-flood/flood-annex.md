# Tallis County Emergency Operations Plan, Annex F: River Flooding

Tallis County Emergency Management Agency (TCEMA) · Revision 5 · Effective March 2027

## 1. Purpose and scope

This annex describes how Tallis County, Iowa prepares for and responds to flooding of the Cedar River. It applies to the unincorporated county, the city of Cedar Ford (county seat, population 11,400), the town of Ferris Crossing (1,150) and the Levee and Drainage District No. 3. Flash flooding and ice jams are covered in Annex F-2.

## 2. Situation

The Cedar River enters Tallis County from the north and flows southeast through Cedar Ford before leaving the county. The county's forecast point is **Cedar River at Cedar Ford (NWS identifier CFDI4, a fictional forecast point for this plan)**, a gauge on the Route 38 bridge piers read by the US Geological Survey and used by the National Weather Service River Forecast Center. The plan's categories are based on the county's flood impact studies:

| Stage at Cedar Ford | Category | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 13.0 ft | Action stage | TCEMA monitors; the Levee District starts patrols |
| 15.0 ft | Flood stage (Minor) | Riverside Park and River Road south of Mill Street flood; basements on Water Street |
| 17.0 ft | | Mill Street underpass closes; Front Street basements |
| 19.0 ft | Moderate | Cedar Hollow neighborhood (about 140 homes) floods; Route 38 bottoms closed; wastewater plant access road under water |
| 22.5 ft | | Seepage and interior ponding reach the Cedar Ford Wastewater Treatment Plant yard if the pump stations fall behind |
| 23.0 ft | Major | Levee seepage expected; 340 homes behind the levee at risk if it fails |
| 25.5 ft | | Levee design flood; overtopping begins above the crest at 26.0 ft |

Stage is in feet above gauge datum and is not elevation above sea level. A forecast has an issuance time and a forecast crest with a time. Forecasts are revised as rain falls upstream; the county uses the latest issuance, says which issuance, and says what changed.

## 5. Levels of activation

| Level | Trigger | Staff |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring | Forecast crest at or above action stage, or a flood watch | TCEMA staff and the Levee Superintendent by phone |
| Partial activation | Forecast crest at or above flood stage (15.0 ft), or any flood warning for CFDI4 | EOC Command, Operations, PIO, Public Works liaison, Sheriff liaison |
| Full activation | Forecast crest at or above moderate (19.0 ft), or any report of levee distress | All EOC positions and the Levee District |

The Coordinator decides the level and records the gauge reading, the forecast issuance or the report used.

## 6. Decisions and lead times

1. **Evacuation of Cedar Hollow.** The area floods at 19.0 ft. The clearance time is 12 hours (140 homes, 22 households with medical needs, a 36-bed assisted living facility, Hollow Court Care Center, which needs 12 hours and eight vans). The evacuation advisory (voluntary) is issued when the forecast crest is at or above 19.0 ft and more than 36 hours out; the order (mandatory) is issued at least 18 hours before the stage is expected to reach 19.0 ft, so that clearance finishes 6 hours before the water.
2. **Road closures.** Public Works closes a road before it floods, on a stated stage: River Road at 15.0 ft, the Mill Street underpass at 17.0 ft, Route 38 bottoms at 19.0 ft (Iowa DOT closes Route 38; the county places barriers).
3. **Sandbags and pumps.** The county and the Levee District hold 40,000 filled sandbags; the flood fight plan needs 80,000 and six 6-inch pumps. Orders go to Iowa HSEMD at least 24 hours before action stage at the point of need.
4. **Shelters.** Cedar Ford High School opens when an order is issued; the Red Cross and the school district decide staffing. Cedar Ford's pet shelter opens with it.
5. **Moving assets.** Cooperatives and farms move grain, equipment and livestock at least 24 hours before water; the plant moves the portable chemicals and generators above 24.0 ft.
6. **Request for state help.** Disaster declaration request when the forecast crest is at or above 23.0 ft or when the levee shows distress.

## 7. Public messages

Public messages use the National Weather Service flood categories (Action, Minor, Moderate, Major) and the forecast crest with its time. They never state a stage as minor, moderate or major unless the CFDI4 category table says so. When a forecast is revised the PIO says "The forecast crest has been revised" and gives the old and new numbers and the new time. The county uses TallisAlert (about 8,700 subscribers, text and voice), the county website and Facebook page, the local radio station, and door-to-door contact by the Sheriff and fire department in the evacuation area. A Wireless Emergency Alert is used for a mandatory evacuation order; it is not used for a river stage advisory.

## 8. Exercises

TCEMA exercises this annex each March with the Levee District, the wastewater plant and the school district.

# Great Plains Crisis Network — Public Communications and Responder Wellbeing

GPCN-COM-03 · Communications · Revision 2, 2026

## 1. Purpose

After a community tragedy more people look for help, and the way a crisis center speaks in public affects both who calls and how safe the conversation is. This plan sets GPCN's rules for communicating during a surge and for caring for its own staff and for disaster responders.

## 2. Who speaks

The Executive Director, Dr. Wilhelmina Strand, is the spokesperson. The Communications Lead, Jerome Bell, writes and posts. The Director of Clinical Operations, Marcus Obuya, reviews every statement for safe messaging. Counselors and supervisors do not speak to the media.

## 3. Safe messaging rules

Public statements follow the safe messaging recommendations for suicide and for reporting on death:

- Never describe a method, a location or the details of any death.
- Do not call a death a solution or an inevitable outcome, and do not describe it as a "successful" act.
- Say that help is available, and give 988 by call, text and chat. Say that many people get through a crisis with support.
- Describe the surge in plain terms: a rise in contacts, longer waits and what the center is doing.
- Do not give caller numbers that point to a particular person or family.
- Do not speculate about a cause.

## 4. Timing and channels

- **Within 30 minutes of Level 2:** a short post on the GPCN website and social account: "Many people are reaching out today. If you are in crisis, call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org. Waits may be longer than usual. You matter and help is there."
- **Within 90 minutes of a news event that raises volume:** a briefing note to the state 988 coordinator and a message to partner organizations, school districts and local officials who will refer people.
- **Within 3 hours of Level 3:** a media statement saying that backup centers are helping and that every contact is answered.
- **Daily during an extended surge:** an update to staff at the start of each shift.

All exercise statements are marked as exercise material when displayed in the app.

## 5. Media and social media

Requests from reporters go to Jerome Bell at 402-555-0160 or press@gpcn.example. GPCN asks reporters to follow the safe messaging guidance and offers a short note. A reporter who asks for caller stories gets a plain refusal. Rumors, for example that the line is "down" or that no one is answering, are answered once with the facts and the multiple ways to reach help.

## 6. Community partners

GPCN shares the same statement with the school district superintendents, the county emergency managers, the Nebraska Division of Behavioral Health's disaster behavioral health team and faith leaders who ask. GPCN does not run a community vigil; the disaster behavioral health team does, and GPCN counselors are not deployed in person.

## 7. Wellbeing of counselors

Surges after a tragedy are heavy. Counselors may be from the affected community. The rules:

- A supervisor checks each counselor at the start of the shift. Anyone who knows the people affected may ask for a different queue or leave without explanation.
- A break within 3 hours, overtime capped at 12 hours, and a quiet room.
- A group check-in at the end of every surge shift, led by a clinical coach.
- Dr. Anneliese Park, the contracted employee support contact, is available on 402-555-0170, and the employee assistance program is open to families.
- Supervisors watch for sudden quiet, visible distress, or stories that go beyond the shift, and talk to the person privately.

## 8. Exercise safeguards

Any exercise based on this plan includes fictional material about people in distress and about the death of young people. The facilitator tells the room before the start that the material is fictional, that no method detail is used, and that anyone may step out at any time. A wellbeing contact is named and present. The exercise stops if two or more people leave. At the hot wash each person is asked to name one thing that helped them stay steady.

## 9. After action

Within 14 days Communications reports which statements were issued, when and whether any needed correction. The Director of Clinical Operations checks them against safe messaging guidance and adds changes to the plan.

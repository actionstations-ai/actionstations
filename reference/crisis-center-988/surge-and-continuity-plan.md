# Great Plains Crisis Network — Contact Surge and Continuity Plan

GPCN-OPS-01 · Great Plains Crisis Network (GPCN), 988 Lifeline center for the State of Nebraska · Lincoln · Revision 5, April 2026

## 1. Purpose

GPCN is the state's primary 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline center. It answers calls, texts and chats from Nebraska area codes, and from the rest of the country when other centers overflow to it. This plan sets out how GPCN recognizes a surge, what it does at each stage and who decides. It deals with operations, not clinical content. How a crisis counselor supports a person is in the clinical manual and is not repeated here.

GPCN is a nonprofit funded by the State of Nebraska Division of Behavioral Health and by federal grants. It employs 74 people: 52 crisis counselors, 9 shift supervisors, 4 clinical coaches, 3 workforce coordinators and 6 administrative and technology staff.

## 4. Surge levels and triggers

**Level 1, watch.** The queue for any contact type exceeds 5 minutes of wait at the oldest contact, or the forecast tool shows volume above 130 percent of the same hour last week. Action: supervisor checks breaks, pauses non-urgent callbacks and tells the Director.

**Level 2, activate.** Answer rate falls under 80 percent for two consecutive 15-minute intervals, or the oldest waiting contact exceeds 10 minutes. Actions, in this order:

1. Overflow to supervisors and trained administrative staff (supervisors answer calls; the 6 trained staff take texts after a refresher script).
2. Pause outbound work and follow-up callbacks for non-imminent risk.
3. Call in off-duty counselors using the on-call list (target: 6 counselors on the floor within 90 minutes).
4. Shift to the surge scripts for opening and closing, reviewed by clinical coaches.

**Level 3, request backup.** Answer rate under 70 percent for 30 minutes, or the oldest waiting contact exceeds 20 minutes. Actions:

1. Ask the 988 network administrator to route overflow calls to backup centers in other states, by telephone to the network operations desk; requests are confirmed by the state 988 coordinator.
2. Ask the state backup center and another state center to take texts and chats.
3. Open the executive command line with the Executive Director, the Director of Clinical Operations, the Communications Lead and the Technology Manager.

The target is that backup is requested before a queue has run 60 minutes over its threshold. Requests are recorded with the party, the contact type and the time the support is to begin.

**Level 4, protect the service.** Telephony or platform failure, evacuation of the floor, or loss of more than half the staff on shift. See section 7.

## 5. Safe handling during surge

Surge scripts may be shorter; they never skip a risk assessment. Contacts at imminent risk are answered ahead of others by the routing rules (see GPCN-OPS-02). Nobody is told that they must wait for help in a way that implies they are not important; the recorded greeting during surge says that the wait may be longer than usual and gives the texting option. Public statements never describe methods or the circumstances of any death.

## 6. Counselor wellbeing

During a surge the shift supervisor ensures each counselor has a break within 3 hours and that anyone who has taken a hard contact has a debrief before the next one if they ask for it. Overtime is capped at 12 hours per person. After any surge above Level 2 the wellbeing contact runs a group check-in within 48 hours.

## 7. Continuity

If the primary telephony platform fails, Odalys Ferreira's team switches the numbers to the secondary carrier route (target within 20 minutes) and the 988 network administrator is told. If the floor must be evacuated, calls route to the backup centers and counselors work from home with the secure remote client, 14 of whom hold standing remote credentials. The Executive Director declares each level change and the log records it.

## 8. After the surge

Within 7 days the Director of Clinical Operations holds an after-action review. It reports the answer rate by contact type and hour, the time from trigger to action, which backup was used and the number of overtime hours, plus what counselors said helped.

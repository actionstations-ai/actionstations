# SOP-IMT-01: Notifications and Incident Management Team Activation

Ironbark Pipeline Company · Emergency Response · Revision 9

## 1. Purpose

This SOP gives the order of notifications after a pipeline release into water and the steps to activate the Ironbark Incident Management Team (IMT). It is used by the Control Center shift supervisor, the Qualified Individual and the IMT.

## 2. Who does what in the first hour

| Minute | Action | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Possible leak signal; shutdown if not ruled out in 10 minutes; close block valves | Control Center shift supervisor |
| Within 15 | Report to the National Response Center (the NRC is a 24-hour reporting line; a call is a report and not a request for help) | Shift supervisor (person in charge) |
| Within 15 | Call the Qualified Individual | Shift supervisor |
| Within 30 | QI activates the oil spill removal organization (OSRO), Prairie Spill Response Cooperative | QI |
| Within 30 | State notification: Kansas Department of Health and Environment 24-hour spill line and the Kansas Division of Emergency Management | QI or Environmental Unit Leader |
| Within 30 | Downstream water intakes: Prairie Bend Rural Water District (mile 11) and Sterling Bluff Water Department (mile 34) | Environmental Unit Leader |
| Within 45 | County emergency management (Prairie Bend County) | Liaison |
| Within 60 | EPA Region 7 duty officer (the FOSC) is contacted by the NRC; the QI confirms and joins Unified Command | QI |
| Within 60 | Natural resource trustees: US Fish and Wildlife Service, Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks | Environmental Unit Leader |

Each notification is logged: who was notified, the time, the person speaking and what was said. A notification made to a party that has no responsibility for the resource (for example, calling a utility's general line) does not count until the utility's operator is reached.

## 3. What to say

A notification states: who is calling and the call-back number; the facility and the location (milepost, river mile and latitude and longitude); the product (medium sour crude); the estimated volume released and its basis (for example, "SCADA volume balance 2,900 to 3,300 barrels; estimate, not confirmed"); the time of detection and the time of shutdown; whether oil has reached water; weather and river conditions; the response under way. Estimates are given as ranges with the basis. The caller does not guess about cause.

## 4. IMT activation

The QI declares the IMT activation level: Level 1 (a regional team of 12), Level 2 (a national team of 40 with Ironbark corporate staff), Level 3 (an international or third-party contract team). The QI activates Level 2 for any release to water over 1,000 barrels. The IMT's first objectives (ICS 202) are the safety of the public and responders, stopping the source, protecting the water intakes, and containing and recovering oil.

The IMT's main products in the first operational period: an ICS 202 Incident Objectives; an ICS 204 Assignment List for each task force; an ICS 232 Resources at Risk summary; an ICS 233 Open Action Tracker; an ICS 234 Work Analysis Matrix; the initial trajectory from the Environmental Unit; an aerial observation report; and a shoreline survey report.

## 5. Unified Command and Joint Information Center

The Unified Command (EPA FOSC, the state OSC and Ironbark's IC) meets at the incident command post (the Mill Ridge Station) at the first operational period and every 6 hours. The meeting agenda is: objectives, resource status, trajectory, safety, protection priorities, and the plan for the next period. A Joint Information Center (JIC) is opened by the PIOs of Ironbark, EPA, KDHE and the county. All public messages are cleared in the JIC, with the same volume estimate, the same basis and the same wording on the water intakes. Ironbark's PIO does not say that the water is safe to drink; that is the decision of the water utilities and the state.

## 6. Countermeasures

Dispersants, other chemical agents and in-situ burning are requested by the Unified Command and authorized only by the FOSC with the concurrence of the Regional Response Team's state and federal members where the Area Contingency Plan does not preauthorize them. On the Kansas River none of these is preauthorized. Mechanical recovery with boom and skimmers is the first strategy.

## 7. Claims

A claims line opens within 24 hours for anyone who has a loss: Ironbark's number 888-555-0148. The responsible party pays legitimate claims and the Joint Information Center says how to file.

## 8. After the incident

The IMT's Planning Section assembles the PREP record entries if the event is an exercise; for a real event, the incident file is kept for the regulatory investigation.

# Ironbark Pipeline Company: Response Plan for the Prairie Bend Segment

Ironbark Pipeline Company · Pipeline Integrity and Emergency Response · Revision 11 · Effective February 2027

## 1. The system

Ironbark Pipeline Company operates the Prairie Line, a 12.75-inch crude oil pipeline of 286 miles from the Hollis Gathering Terminal in Oklahoma to the Wabash Junction Terminal in Missouri. The Prairie Bend segment (mileposts 196 to 231) runs through Prairie Bend County, Kansas, and crosses the Kansas River at milepost 214.3 by a horizontal directional drill 38 feet below the river bed. Block valves are at milepost 213.1 (Mill Ridge Station, north bank, remotely operated) and milepost 215.6 (Sandhill Valve, south bank, remotely operated). The pipeline carries about 31,000 barrels a day of medium sour crude; the line pressure at the crossing is about 880 psi. The Pipeline Control Center in Tulsa monitors the line with SCADA and computational pipeline monitoring.

## 2. Worst case discharge

The worst case discharge (WCD) for the Prairie Bend segment is 8,400 barrels (1,336 cubic meters): the volume from a full-bore rupture at the river crossing, with 12 minutes to shut down and close the valves and drain-down between the valves. Volume figures in this plan are in barrels; the exercise should not mix barrels, gallons and cubic meters in one document. The medium case used for annual exercises is 3,100 barrels.

## 4. Release detection and first actions

1. A leak alarm in the Control Center (a pressure drop of 40 psi in 2 minutes with a flow imbalance of more than 1.5 percent) is checked against SCADA trends. The controller may not restart the line for 10 minutes after a possible leak signal.
2. If a release cannot be ruled out within 10 minutes, the shift supervisor shuts down the pump stations and closes the block valves remotely. This is done before the cause is known.
3. The shift supervisor, who is the person in charge for the first report, calls the National Response Center (NRC) immediately after confirming a release, and does not wait for the Qualified Individual. The supervisor records the time of the call and the NRC report number.
4. The supervisor then calls the Qualified Individual, who calls the OSRO and the state. The call-out order is in SOP-IMT-01.
5. A field responder (Mill Ridge) arrives in 40 minutes and reports the source and the size.

## 5. Response organization

The IMT is an ICS organization: Incident Commander, Safety, Liaison, Public Information, Operations, Planning (including the Environmental Unit), Logistics, and Finance. In a spill that crosses the river, Ironbark forms a Unified Command with the Federal On-Scene Coordinator (FOSC), which in inland waters is the US Environmental Protection Agency Region 7, and the state On-Scene Coordinator from the Kansas Department of Health and Environment. The Unified Command approves objectives in the ICS 202 and sets the next operational period. Ironbark is the responsible party: it pays for the response and cleanup, and it does not close water intakes, fisheries or boat ramps. Those closures are decisions of the water utilities and the state.

## 7. Notifications

The call-out list is in SOP-IMT-01. In addition, Ironbark notifies the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) as required by regulation, and the state pipeline safety office.

## 8. Training and exercises

Ironbark holds the annual QI notification exercise in March, an IMT tabletop in the spring and an equipment deployment exercise with the OSRO in the autumn. The exercises are recorded in the National Preparedness for Response Exercise Program (PREP) record, which names the core components exercised, the plan and the scenario, and whether the exercise was a worst case discharge. The next worst case discharge exercise is due within three years of the last.

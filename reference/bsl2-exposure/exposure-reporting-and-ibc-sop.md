# Halvorsen State University — Exposure Reporting and IBC Referral

HSU-EHS-SOP-12 · Office of Environmental Health and Safety · Revision 4, January 2026

## 1. When to use this SOP

Use it when anyone in an HSU laboratory may have been exposed to a biological material, whether through a needlestick or sharps injury, a splash to the eyes or mouth, contact with broken skin, an animal bite or scratch, a spill outside a cabinet or a failure of a cabinet or centrifuge containment. Use it also when a worker reports symptoms that could be related to their work. If you are not sure, call. The BSO decides whether it is an exposure.

## 2. What the PI sends the BSO

The first report is a telephone call to the BSO (ext. 5-0142), followed within 24 hours by the Potential Exposure Report in the EHS portal. The call and the form carry the same facts:

- date, time and room, and who was present;
- the material by its approved protocol number and risk group, not by technique;
- the route of possible exposure and what was done in the first minutes;
- whether the worker has been seen by a clinician;
- whether the container, tube or surface is still in place and whether anything has been disposed of.

The PI does not need to decide whether the event is reportable. That is the BSO's job with the IBC chair.

## 3. Conflicting guidance

Different sources give different advice in the first hours, and the SOP expects this. Typical conflicts:

- The approved protocol says "no post-exposure prophylaxis needed", while the Occupational Health physician wants to evaluate.
- The sponsor's award terms ask for notice within 72 hours, while HSU policy says 7 days.
- A funder's program officer says the event does not need NIH reporting, while the IBC chair thinks it does.
- A manufacturer's safety data sheet and the BMBL describe different practices for the same material.

Rules for resolving conflicts:

1. The worker's health comes first. The clinic physician's advice on evaluation is followed over the protocol until the physician says otherwise.
2. The shortest applicable deadline governs. If the sponsor's terms say 72 hours, 72 hours is the clock for the sponsor.
3. The IBC chair decides whether the event is reportable under the NIH Guidelines. A program officer's opinion is recorded but does not decide it. The BSO writes the question and the answer in the incident file.
4. A federal regulation overrides HSU policy. HSU policy overrides a laboratory protocol.

## 4. The IBC question

Under the NIH Guidelines, the IBC reports to the NIH Office of Science Policy (OSP) any significant problem, violation of the Guidelines, or significant research-related accident or illness. The practical test used by the IBC chair is:

- Does the work fall under the Guidelines (it uses recombinant or synthetic nucleic acid molecules)?
- Did a person have a possible exposure, or was there a loss of containment, or a breach of the approved protocol?
- Would a reasonable biosafety professional call it significant?

If the answer to all three is yes, the IBC chair directs the BSO to prepare the report and the chair signs it. Initial notice may be by e-mail to the OSP, and the written report follows within 30 days. If the work is not covered by the Guidelines, the BSO still records the event and tells the IBC at its next meeting. If the event is uncertain, the chair errs toward reporting and says so in the report.

## 5. Notifications and timing

- BSO: PI call at once, form within 24 hours.
- Occupational Health Clinic: BSO calls Dr. Mbeki within 30 minutes of receiving a report that involves a person.
- IBC chair: BSO within 3 calendar days, and the same day if a worker has been admitted to a hospital or the event might need an NIH report.
- Sponsor: notice from the Director of Research Compliance within 7 days of discovery or earlier if terms require. The notice states facts only.
- Iowa Department of Health and Human Services, local public health: the BSO calls if there is a possible risk to people outside the laboratory.
- The insurer: Research Compliance notifies Risk Management within 5 business days if there has been a medical claim.

## 6. Worker support

The worker is never required to continue the experiment. The PI offers alternate duties during the evaluation. The worker's medical information stays in the clinic's records; the BSO sees only what the physician releases, such as fitness for duty.

## 7. Closing the file

The file closes when the clinic has completed follow-up, all reports are filed, and the corrective actions are complete. The BSO reports to the IBC at its next meeting with the timeline, including any missed target, so that the committee sees where the clock ran short.

# Halvorsen State University — Communications and Sponsor Relations During a Biosafety Event

HSU-COMM-07 · University Communications and Office of Research Compliance · Revision 2, 2026

## 1. Who speaks

University Communications, led by Renata Delgado, is the only voice for HSU on a laboratory incident. The Institutional Official, Dr. Ingrid Solheim, approves any statement that mentions a worker's health or a regulator. PIs, laboratory staff and students send every media request to ext. 5-0200 or to communications@hsu.example. The student newspaper, local television and the regional wire service often call the department head first, so department heads are briefed twice a year.

## 2. Timing

- **Within 1 hour of an event that could draw attention:** the BSO gives Communications a three-line fact sheet: what happened, whether anyone is injured and whether the public is at risk.
- **Within 3 hours:** an internal note to the college dean and the laboratories in the affected building. A holding statement is ready.
- **Within 24 hours:** if media or social media interest continues, a short public statement is posted on the HSU news page.
- **Within 72 hours:** the IO briefs the Board of Regents' research committee chair by telephone.

## 3. What to say and not say

Say: what the university knows, what it is doing and when it will update. Name the HSU office responsible.

Do not say: the worker's name, department or age; the name of the organism, unless the IBC chair and the IO decide that it is needed for public health; the cause of the event before the review; or a promise that nobody is at risk. Never release information that tells a reader how to do anything with a biological material.

Example holding statement, marked as exercise material: "HSU is aware of an incident in a research laboratory on campus on [date]. A staff member was evaluated as a precaution. Campus safety experts are reviewing the event and there is no known risk to the public. HSU will share more when it has confirmed facts."

## 4. Rumors and social media

Communications monitors the HSU hashtag, the student forum and two campus group pages. A post that gives wrong facts is answered once, with the facts, from the official account. The BSO is asked within one hour whether a rumor is true. A claim of a "lab leak" gets a plain answer: what happened, what the risk is and who is looking at it.

## 5. Sponsors and program officers

The Director of Research Compliance, Joel Whitcomb, is the single contact for sponsors. Typical sponsors are a federal health agency, a federal science agency, the Iowa Corn Growers Board (a fictional commodity group, domain cornboard.example) and an industry partner, Prairie Biologics Inc. (fictional). Each award has its own incident-notice term, and the Compliance office keeps the terms in a table in the EHS portal. Where a term is shorter than the HSU 7-day target, the shorter one wins.

A program officer may offer informal advice, such as "that does not need a report." The advice is recorded. It does not decide whether the IBC reports to the NIH.

## 6. Staff and students

The Dean of the College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, Dr. Ruth Halloran, writes to the building's staff the same day. The note tells people that the BSO is available, that the Employee Assistance Program is open at ext. 5-0250, and that laboratories remain open unless the BSO orders stop-work in a room.

A graduate student who is the exposed worker is protected by the HSU non-retaliation policy. Their advisor is told what the student wants shared and no more.

## 7. After the event

Within 14 days Communications and EHS write a one-page account of what was said and when, for the incident file. The IBC chair reads it at the next meeting. Any statement found to be wrong is corrected in public.

## SECTION 1: PURPOSE, SCOPE AND AUTHORITY

This plan lets ORRMC protect patients, staff and visitors and keep essential services running through any emergency that disrupts normal operations: severe weather, power or water loss, fire, mass casualty events, infectious disease surge, civil disturbance, workplace violence and cyber incidents. It covers the entire campus (Tower A, Tower B, Pavilion C, the Surgical Pavilion and the Central Plant) and the Outpatient Dialysis Center. It uses the Hospital Incident Command System (HICS) and the National Incident Management System (NIMS).

The Administrator on Call (AOC) may activate the plan on his or her own authority. The Chief Executive Officer ratifies the activation as soon as practical. During activation, command decisions made under this plan take precedence over normal approval chains.

## SECTION 3: HAZARD VULNERABILITY ANALYSIS

The Emergency Management Committee updates the Hazard Vulnerability Analysis (HVA) every year with the regional healthcare coalition and the Lucas County Emergency Management Agency. The 2026 analysis ranks these hazards highest by combined probability and impact:

1. Cyber incident and loss of the EHR (ransomware, data theft, medical device compromise)
2. Utility interruption: loss of municipal water or a water quality advisory, loss of power, loss of medical gas
3. Severe weather: winter storm, thunderstorm and tornado, extreme heat, flooding on the Maumee River
4. Mass casualty incident (highway, rail, industrial or violent event) and surge from a neighbouring facility's failure
5. Infectious disease outbreak and surge in respiratory illness
6. Fire or internal evacuation
7. Workplace violence and civil disturbance

The patient population includes a large adult medical and surgical population, a busy emergency department and trauma service, a labor and delivery and neonatal unit, dialysis-dependent patients, behavioral health patients, pediatric patients, and patients dependent on ventilators and infusions.

## SECTION 4: CONCEPT OF OPERATIONS AND ACTIVATION LEVELS

| Level | Meaning | Examples | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 3: Watch | An event may affect operations; normal command structure handles it | Severe weather watch; single-system outage under four hours | AOC and department leaders informed; no HCC |
| Level 2: Partial activation | Part of the hospital is affected; some HICS positions are filled | Planned downtime longer than four hours; water advisory; ED surge | AOC activates selected HICS positions; HCC opens; situation report every four hours |
| Level 1: Full activation | The hospital's ability to deliver care is threatened | Ransomware with EHR loss; evacuation; mass casualty incident; loss of power or water | All HICS sections staffed; HCC open around the clock; operational periods of four hours; diversion and evacuation decisions considered; Lucas County EMA and the Ohio Department of Health notified |

The AOC convenes the first HCC meeting within 30 minutes of activation. The Incident Action Plan (IAP) for each operational period lists objectives, assignments and the communication plan.

## SECTION 5: HICS ORGANIZATION

The HCC (Room A-114; alternate in the Pavilion C conference room C-130) runs the HICS structure:

- **Command:** Incident Commander (the AOC), Public Information Officer (PIO), Safety Officer, Liaison Officer, Medical-Technical Specialists (infection prevention, information security, clinical engineering, ethics).
- **Operations Section:** Medical Care Branch (inpatient, ED, surgical, dialysis), Infrastructure Branch (power, water, gases, building automation, IT infrastructure), Security Branch, HazMat Branch, Business Continuity Branch.
- **Planning Section:** Situation Unit, Resources Unit, Documentation Unit, Demobilization Unit.
- **Logistics Section:** Service Branch (IT and communications, food, transport), Support Branch (supplies, staff care).
- **Finance/Administration Section:** Time, Procurement, Compensation/Claims and Cost Units.

Job action sheets for each position are in the HCC binder. Roles are filled by position, not by name; the on-call roster lists the primary and two alternates.

## SECTION 6: CONTINUITY OF OPERATIONS AND ESSENTIAL FUNCTIONS

Essential functions, ranked by recovery priority: (1) life-sustaining care and patient monitoring (ICUs, ED, OR, dialysis, labor and delivery, neonatal); (2) medication ordering, verification and administration; (3) laboratory, blood bank and imaging; (4) access to the patient record, either live or through the downtime viewer; (5) communications (nurse call, phones, paging); (6) patient registration, tracking and bed management; (7) supply chain; (8) revenue cycle.

Each department keeps a business continuity plan with its downtime forms, a printed 72-hour patient census and medication list refreshed every 15 minutes onto the BCA-VIEW01 viewer, and a downtime cart. The EHR Downtime Procedures Annex governs documentation. The Cyber Incident Response Plan governs technical response.

## SECTION 7: PATIENT TRACKING AND CARE CONTINUITY

During activation the Planning Section's Situation Unit keeps a patient tracking board (EHR when available; paper census sheet by unit when not). Each unit charge nurse reports census, acuity and available beds at the top of every hour at Level 1. A wristband and a downtime medical record number (the "DT-" series) identify patients registered during a downtime. Transfers out, evacuations and discharges are logged on the Patient Movement Log (kept in the downtime cart).

## SECTION 8: STAFFING, SUPPLIES AND UTILITIES

- **Staffing:** the nursing supervisor activates the call-in tree; Human Resources runs the staff care unit (childcare, rest areas, meals). Credentialed volunteers are used through the regional volunteer registry; others are not given clinical duties.
- **Subsistence:** 96 hours of food supplies, 72 hours of pharmaceuticals on the shelf and the ADC override stock, and a medical-surgical stock of seven days at normal use. Potable water storage of 40,000 gallons; bottled water and a water-hauling contract for the dialysis loop.
- **Power:** three generators carry the whole campus; fuel for 96 hours; load tested monthly and under full-building load annually; lighting, fire detection and suppression are on the emergency system.
- **Waste and sewage:** the campus continues to discharge to the city sewer; if that fails, waste is held under the infection prevention plan and the Central Plant has temporary holding capacity.

## SECTION 9: EVACUATION, SHELTER-IN-PLACE AND SURGE

Evacuation, shelter-in-place and surge procedures are in the Patient Surge and Evacuation Annex (ORRMC-SE-ANX-2026). Horizontal evacuation (to the next fire compartment on the same floor) is the first choice; vertical evacuation by stairs and elevators with the evacuation sleds is the second; full-campus evacuation requires the Incident Commander's approval and notice to Toledo Fire and Rescue, Lucas County EMS and the Lucas County EMA.

## SECTION 10: COMMUNICATIONS

Primary means: the hospital telephone system and the secure messaging service. Alternates: overhead paging, runners, the two-way radio system (channels assigned to Security, Facilities, Clinical Engineering and the HCC), satellite phones in the HCC and with the AOC, and amateur radio volunteers. During a cyber incident email and the messaging service may not be trusted: use telephone, radio and runners until Information Security says otherwise.

Information to the public is released only by the PIO. The PIO keeps a media log and prepares holding statements, one for each hazard in the HVA, approved in advance by the CEO and the Chief Privacy Officer. Patient-specific information is released only as the Chief Privacy Officer allows.

## SECTION 11: COMMUNITY COORDINATION

ORRMC reports status to the Lucas County Emergency Management Agency, the regional healthcare coalition and the Ohio Department of Health through the state's healthcare resource reporting channels. It coordinates diversion and patient distribution with Lucas County EMS and the other hospitals, requests mutual aid through the coalition and the Ohio Hospital Association, and coordinates infectious disease issues with the Toledo-Lucas County Health Department. For water issues it speaks with the City of Toledo Department of Public Utilities. For cyber incidents it contacts the FBI Cleveland Field Office, CISA and H-ISAC through the Information Security Officer.

## SECTION 12: TRAINING, TESTING AND PLAN MAINTENANCE

Every new employee receives emergency preparedness orientation within 30 days. Annual refresher training uses HICS position training for HCC members. The hospital tests twice a year: one community-based full-scale exercise with the regional coalition (or the activation for a real event), and one additional exercise, which in alternate years is a facilitated tabletop. Every exercise ends with a hot wash and an after action report with corrective actions tracked by the Emergency Management Committee. The plan, the HVA and the communication plan are reviewed at least every two years and after any activation.

# Sawmill County Emergency Operations Plan, Annex W: Wildland-Urban Interface Fire

Sawmill County Office of Emergency Management (SCOEM) · Sawmill County Sheriff's Office · Revision 6 · Effective March 2027

## 1. Purpose and scope

This annex sets out how Sawmill County, Colorado prepares for and responds to a wildfire that threatens homes, roads and people in the foothills west of the Front Range. It covers evacuation by zone, sheltering, traffic control, animal evacuation, coordination with the fire agencies and with Foothills Electric Cooperative, and smoke. It applies to the whole county; the highest-risk areas are the Pine Gulch and Kestrel Springs communities in the western foothills.

## 2. Situation

The county has 24,000 residents, about 5,900 of them in the foothills communities of Pine Gulch (1,900 residents) and Kestrel Springs (1,400) and the scattered homes along County Road 68 (Gulch Road). Most of the foothills have one road out. Fuels are ponderosa pine, mixed conifer and dry grass. The worst conditions occur on days with a Red Flag Warning: downslope winds from the west of 35 mph with gusts to 60 mph, relative humidity below 15 percent and dry fuels. On such days a fire can move 1 to 2 miles an hour and throw embers more than a mile ahead.

## 4. Evacuation zones and status terms

The county is divided into 41 evacuation zones named by community and number: PG-01 to PG-14 (Pine Gulch), KS-01 to KS-09 (Kestrel Springs), CR-01 to CR-18 (County Road 68 and the rural west). Zones are published on the county's zone map and in the Sawmill Ready app. A zone has one status at a time:

| Status | Meaning | Who orders |
|---|---|---|
| Evacuation Warning (GET READY) | Danger is possible; prepare to leave, people with medical or access needs and large animals should leave now | Incident Commander or Sheriff |
| Evacuation Order (GO NOW) | Danger is imminent; leave immediately | Sheriff or Incident Commander |
| Shelter in Place | Evacuation is more dangerous than staying; go to the safest room or a refuge | Sheriff on the Incident Commander's advice |
| Cleared / Repopulated | Zone safe to return | Incident Commander and Sheriff together |

An order is issued so that the zone is empty before the fire arrives: the latest time to give the order is the fire's projected arrival time less the clearance time of the zone. Clearance time for each zone is in the zone table (examples: PG-03 Gulch Village, 55 minutes; KS-04 with Stonebridge Assisted Living, 120 minutes; CR-07, 40 minutes). The county issues the order 30 minutes earlier than the latest time when it can.

## 5. Decisions the EOC makes

1. Activate the EOC at Level 2 on a Red Flag Warning for the foothills, Level 1 on a fire that threatens structures.
2. Set zone status, with the basis (projected arrival, Incident Commander request, field sweep report, public or media report, utility notice).
3. Order shelter-in-place for zones with no safe egress (the Highway 305 canyon; Stonebridge if the road is lost).
4. Close or control roads: close, control (residents out, responders in), contraflow, reopen.
5. Open shelters: Pine Gulch High School (capacity 500, clean air), Mountain View Fairgrounds (large animals, 120 stalls), Kestrel Springs Recreation Center (80).
6. Request resources: engines, strike teams, hand crews, helicopters, air tankers, law enforcement, buses and ambulances, mutual aid.
7. Request a state declaration and a federal Fire Management Assistance Grant declaration when the fire threatens a major disaster.
8. Public statements about smoke and air quality.

## 6. Public alerts

The county sends alerts by the Sawmill Ready app, text and voice call through the mass notification system ("SawmillAlert", about 17,000 contacts), Wireless Emergency Alerts through IPAWS for orders, the Emergency Alert System and the county website and Facebook page. A WEA goes out for an Evacuation Order and uses plain words: zone, what to do, route. Messages for Evacuation Warning use the app and SawmillAlert, not WEA, unless the Sheriff orders it. Every message names the zone, the status and the time; the PIO checks that the fire agency, the Sheriff, the utility, the school district and the hospital use the same zone statuses before release.

## 7. Smoke

The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment issues smoke advisories. The county uses the Air Quality Index (AQI) categories. At AQI 151 or higher (Unhealthy), the school district and the hospital act under their plans (indoor recess, filtered air, closing); the county opens a clean air shelter at the Recreation Center.

## 8. After the fire

Repopulation requires: the fire contained or the zone no longer at risk, the roads open, utilities safe and a safe check by the Sheriff and fire. Burned areas face debris flow and flash flood risk for years; the county works with the National Weather Service on burn-scar flash flood messages.

# HRI Mozambique: Supply Chain and Logistics Plan

Harbourlight Relief International · Logistics Manager Joaquim Tembe · Revision 5

## 1. Purpose

This plan describes HRI's supply chain in Mozambique and how the office manages stock, procurement, shipments and customs in an emergency.

## 2. Facilities

- **Beira warehouse:** 2,400 square meters in the port logistics zone (Rua do Porto 18), 0.8 kilometers from the port gate. Phone +258 84 555 0140. Capacity: 1,600 pallet positions. Prepositioned stock for 15,000 people: 3,000 shelter kits (tarpaulins, rope, tools), 2,800 jerrycans, 4,000 hygiene kits, water treatment tablets for 60 days for 20,000 people, 12 portable water treatment units, 400 latrine slabs, 6,000 mosquito nets and 200 oral rehydration kits.
- **Dondo sub-warehouse:** 600 square meters, 30 kilometers northwest of Beira on the EN6 road.
- **Fleet:** 9 four-wheel-drive vehicles, 3 trucks (7 tons each), 2 water tankers (10,000 liters).
- **Hubs:** HRI's regional hub in Nairobi (for tents and larger items), the humanitarian depot at Dubai (for emergency kits with 5 to 7 days air freight), and the national stock of the Logistics Cluster at Beira airport.

## 3. Pipeline

The pipeline is the flow of supply from source to the beneficiary. For each item the Logistics Manager tracks: stock on hand, stock in transit (with the expected arrival date), orders placed (with the supplier lead time), average weekly consumption, and the planned distribution. The stock balance is: opening stock plus receipts less issues equals closing stock; the closing stock of each warehouse is reconciled with the physical count weekly and after any distribution. A pipeline break is the date on which an item's available stock falls to zero when the future consumption and the arrivals are projected. When a break is projected to occur within 30 days the Logistics Manager tells the Emergency Coordinator, who decides whether to reroute a shipment, place an emergency order, request a customs waiver or ask the Logistics Cluster for transport.

## 4. Procurement

Local procurement within Mozambique is preferred for items on the local market (tarpaulin substitutes, fuel, food) to support the market, using three quotations for purchases over US$ 5,000; the Emergency Coordinator may waive quotations for a stated emergency up to US$ 50,000 for 30 days. International orders go through HRI's Nairobi hub or Dubai. A purchase request needs: the item, quantity, delivery date, budget line, and the approver (Country Director above US$ 50,000).

## 5. Customs and import

Humanitarian shipments to Mozambique enter through Beira port or Beira airport. HRI's customs agent (a fictional local firm, Sofala Freight and Clearing) files the import declaration; the government can grant a duty waiver for humanitarian goods if requested through INGD and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in the form of a letter with the packing list. A typical clearance takes 3 to 5 working days; in an emergency a waiver request can bring that to 24 to 48 hours but only with the INGD letter. A container held at the port by customs earns demurrage per day; shipping lines charge from day 5. The Logistics Manager sends a customs hold to the Country Director and the Logistics Cluster on the same day it is received.

## 6. Transport and access

The main road is EN6 (Beira to Chimoio) and EN1 north-south. In flood conditions, the EN6 crossing at Dondo and the road to Buzi district are cut for 2 to 7 days; boats and helicopters are used for access. The Logistics Cluster arranges common transport: the UN humanitarian air service, trucks and boats; HRI submits its transport requests with weight and volume on the cluster's request form. Convoys of more than two vehicles are not run without a movement request signed by the Security Focal Point.

## 7. Distribution

Distribution at a site follows: community committee lists, registration, an agreed distribution plan with the committee, crowd management with committee volunteers, women's separate line if the committee requests it, and a distribution report with: items by type, people reached by sex and age group, the stock balance, and any feedback received. The distribution report goes to the Information Management Officer within 24 hours.

## 8. Reporting stock

The warehouse report is the daily stock report from Beira and Dondo. Stock reported to the cluster must match the report: the "available" stock excludes quarantined or damaged items and stock already allocated to a confirmed distribution.

# HRI Mozambique: Staff Safety and Security Plan (decision-level summary)

Harbourlight Relief International · Security Focal Point Rui Cossa · Reviewed with the Regional Security Adviser · Revision 4

## 1. Purpose

This plan sets out how HRI's Mozambique office makes decisions about staff safety in a disaster response. It describes who decides and what is shared; it does not contain tactical procedures. HRI follows the principle that no program is worth a life.

## 2. Responsibility

The Country Director is responsible for staff security in Mozambique. The Security Focal Point (Rui Cossa, +258 84 555 0105) advises the Country Director and runs the security risk assessment (SRA), the incident reports and the security alerts. The Regional Security Adviser in Nairobi supports. The HQ Crisis Management Team (CMT) takes over when an incident involves a staff member detained, missing or seriously injured, or when evacuation is considered.

## 3. Security levels and decisions

HRI uses four threat levels: 1 (low), 2 (moderate), 3 (high, serious threat), 4 (critical). The Country Director sets the level for each area on the advice of the Security Focal Point after the SRA. For each area, the plan sets standing decisions:

- **Level 1 to 2:** normal movements with a movement request and check-in; staff share location with the office.
- **Level 3:** movements only with the approval of the Country Director; essential staff only; hibernation or relocation options are prepared; evacuation planning is started; the Regional Director is told.
- **Level 4:** evacuation of non-essential international staff and a decision on national staff support; the CMT leads.

A new sign of a threat (for example, a report of an armed group on a route, a landslide, a flood warning or a breakdown of order near a distribution site) triggers: stop movements in that area; share a security alert with the UN security officer and the NGO safety network; notify the families of staff affected; and decide the level. The first two actions are taken at once, before the facts are confirmed.

## 4. Natural disaster hazards

In a cyclone response the main risks are flooding, collapsed buildings, road accidents, disease and crowd pressure at distributions. The plan requires: a safety briefing at each site; two vehicles for field travel with radio and satellite phone; no night travel; first aid kits in each vehicle; staff accounted for every 24 hours; and a rest rotation of 7 days on and 2 days off after the first two weeks.

## 5. Movement authorization

All field movements need a movement request: destination, time, route, vehicle, staff on board, communications and the return time. The Security Focal Point approves in the first 72 hours. A movement into an area at Level 3 is approved by the Country Director.

## 6. Incident reporting

Every incident (a vehicle accident, a stolen item, harassment, an arrest or detention, a threat) is reported within 1 hour to the Security Focal Point and, for a serious incident, to the Country Director, and written in the incident report within 24 hours. The report has: date, time, place, what happened, who was involved, actions taken, status, and follow-up. Names of staff are not shared outside HRI in a security alert; the alert says "an HRI vehicle".

## 7. Saving Lives Together

HRI shares security information with the UN Department of Safety and Security and the NGO safety platform under the Saving Lives Together framework. HRI keeps its own responsibility for the security of its staff; being in the framework does not transfer that responsibility to the UN.

## 8. Staff detention or missing staff

If a staff member is detained or missing, the Country Director calls the CMT chair at once and the HQ CMT meets. The CMT's decisions are at the level of: who leads, who talks to the family (a trained family liaison), the contact with the authorities and the embassy, and the messages to media. HRI does not discuss tactics and makes no public statements about the incident beyond those approved by the CMT.

## 9. Wellbeing

After a critical incident or a prolonged emergency staff are offered a confidential counseling session and a rest period. The Country Director arranges a debrief with the Regional Director within 10 days of the end of the response.

# SOP-COORD-01: Humanitarian Country Team, Clusters, 3W and the Gap List

HRI Mozambique · Information Management and Coordination · Revision 3

## 1. Purpose

This SOP explains how HRI takes part in the coordination system in a disaster response, how it reports who does what where, and how it handles coverage gaps.

## 2. The coordination system

The United Nations Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) leads the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), which brings together UN agencies, INGOs and the Red Cross movement. The cluster approach groups agencies by sector under a lead agency: for example, health, water sanitation and hygiene (WASH), shelter, food security, protection, logistics, nutrition, camp coordination and camp management. Cluster activation is requested by the HC and approved by the Emergency Relief Coordinator in New York; it is strategic and time-limited. Where the government leads the response, clusters work as sector groups alongside government counterparts; in refugee settings the refugee agency leads. HRI is a member of the WASH, health and shelter clusters and is the co-lead of the WASH sub-group in Zambezia province. The UN's Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) provides the coordination support and publishes situation reports.

HRI's role at the HCT is as a member of the NGO platform, which has two seats; HRI's Country Director is the alternate.

## 3. Meetings and attendance

HCT meeting: daily in the first week at 08:30 (online and in Beira). WASH, health and shelter cluster meetings: daily at 11:00, 13:00 and 15:00 in the first week. Inter-cluster coordination: 16:00. The Information Management Officer attends the Information Management Working Group.

## 4. The 3W matrix

The 3W records which organization does which activity where. HRI's entries use the cluster's common template:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Organization | Harbourlight Relief International |
| Cluster and activity | WASH: water trucking; hygiene kit distribution |
| Location | Province, district, administrative post, site |
| People reached or targeted | number and whether reached or planned |
| Start date and end date | |
| Funding status | funded, partly funded, unfunded |
| Focal point | name and phone |

The Information Management Officer submits HRI's 3W entries to each cluster's information manager by 17:00 each day in the first week and corrects any entry within 24 hours of finding an error. Totals in HRI's reports must match its 3W entries. Counts of people reached are never summed across sectors without saying that the same person may be counted more than once.

## 5. Needs and response summary

OCHA and the clusters publish the number of people in need, the people targeted and the people reached by sector. HRI's reports use the cluster's figures and label HRI's own numbers as its own. When two sources disagree, HRI states both and the basis (for example, a government figure of 412,000 affected and an OCHA figure of 385,000 with an earlier cut-off time).

## 6. The gap list

A cluster keeps a list of gaps: a sector and an area where need is not matched by response (for example, "WASH in Buzi district, no partner"). A gap has: a description, the area, the cluster, the time identified, the partner assigned (when there is one), the date by which it should be covered, and the status (open, assigned, covered). The cluster coordinator assigns a gap to a partner within 12 hours of identifying it and the target is to have it covered within 48 hours. HRI picks up a gap only after checking its stock, staff, access and funding, and tells the cluster coordinator in writing what it can cover. A gap that the cluster cannot close in 48 hours is escalated to the HCT, which decides whether to ask for surge support, a new partner or a change in the response plan. HRI confirms a gap covered only when the first distribution or service has happened and the cluster has verified it.

## 7. Flash updates

An HRI flash update has: a header (title, date and time, the issuing office, version number); a situation paragraph with the source and time of each number; the needs by sector; HRI's response (people reached, items distributed, staff on site); gaps and constraints (access, supplies, funds); the next steps; and a contact. It is released to HQ and to the NGO platform. The numbers in the flash update must match the 3W, the situation message to HQ and the OCHA situation report or say why not. A flash update never includes a staff member's name in a security incident or the location of a staff move.

## 8. Funding and appeals

The HCT launches a flash appeal within the first week; the amount and sectors are agreed at the HCT. HRI's request is included only for activities that appear in its 3W and costing. The amount stated for the appeal is the total requested; the sum of sector requests must equal the total.

## 9. Saving Lives Together

HRI takes part in the UN security coordination under the Saving Lives Together framework: it shares security information with the UN Department of Safety and Security, which in turn shares what it knows. Participation does not make the UN responsible for the security of HRI staff. The responsibility is HRI's.

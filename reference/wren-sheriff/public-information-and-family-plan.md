# WCSO-PIO-02: Public Information, Joint Information Center and Family Liaison

Wren County Sheriff's Office · Revision 3

## 1. Purpose

This plan tells the Public Information Officer (PIO), the Victim and Family Liaison and the command staff what to say, to whom, in what order, and how agencies speak with one voice during a critical incident. The Office speaks in accordance with the Sheriff's priorities: the safety of the person at risk, the integrity of the case, accurate information for the public, and respect for families.

## 3. Principles

1. Tell the family first, then the partner agencies, then the public.
2. Say what is known, what is not known and what is being done. Never state a fact that is not confirmed. Never speculate about motive, suspect or victim.
3. One message across agencies. Times, names, descriptions and phone numbers must match in every agency's text.
4. Correct errors fast and in public, naming what was wrong and what is right.
5. No name or photo of a child under 18 is released unless the case requires it (an AMBER Alert or an Endangered Missing Person advisory), and with the family's agreement wherever possible.
6. Do not describe tactics, locations of resources or the progress of any operational plan.

## 4. First hour

Within 30 minutes of a critical incident being declared, the PIO issues a holding statement: what happened (in one sentence), what the Sheriff's Office is doing, where the public should avoid, and where updates will be posted. The statement is sent to local media, posted to the Office's channels and shared with partner PIOs. If the facts are not yet confirmed the statement says so. A short form of the statement is also read to the 911 center for callers.

## 5. The joint information center (JIC)

A JIC is opened when two or more agencies are in unified command or the incident draws regional media. It is opened at the Public Safety Building training room, 400 Cedar Street, or virtually by shared channel if the building is not available. Each agency sends a PIO. The JIC lead is the Sheriff's Office PIO unless the incident is led by another agency. The JIC's schedule is: a message agreement at the opening, a media update every 60 minutes at a fixed time, a message review after every unified command briefing, and a release log that records every product with the time, the approvers and the agencies that received it. The members are Wren County Sheriff's Office, Alder Falls PD (PIO Dana Kowalczyk, extension 5530), Fire District 1 (PIO Captain Ruben Salazar), Wren County Emergency Management, and Oregon State Police (Public Information Office). The Oregon State Police own the AMBER Alert text.

## 6. Message structure

Each statement has: what we know; what we do not know; what we are doing; what we are asking the public to do; where the next update will be. The PIO keeps a message map with three key messages and prepares answers to expected questions (for example "is the child in danger?", "do you have a suspect?", "why was an alert not issued sooner?"). The answers to these questions do not reveal case detail, use the same words in each agency, and do not blame anyone.

## 7. Social media and rumor

The PIO or back-up runs a monitoring sheet on the main social platforms, the community Facebook groups and local news sites, noting posts that are spreading, what they claim and whether the claim is verified, false or unknown. A rumor that could cause harm, panic or a search in the wrong place is answered in public within 30 minutes. The PIO does not argue with posters. Posts that show a person's name as a suspect are reported to the platform and answered with a short statement that no one has been named. A list of the Office's channels is given to the media so that fake accounts are easier to spot.

## 8. Media

Reporters are directed to the JIC; deputies refer all questions to the PIO. The media staging area is the Public Safety Building front lot. Press conferences are held at fixed times and the Sheriff or Incident Commander speaks; the PIO moderates. No on-camera interview is given by the family without the Liaison present. A reporter who has published a false statement is contacted by the PIO with the correct fact.

## 9. Family liaison

The Liaison explains the AMBER Alert and its effect to the family before it goes out, including the surge of calls, the attention of media and social media, and the fact that photos will be shared widely. The Liaison provides: a private room away from the command post; a direct line to the Investigations Sergeant; the support of the victim advocate; and an agreed plan for notification, including how and by whom the family will be told if the child is found. The family is told that anything they post to social media may be amplified and may affect the case. The Liaison arranges relief and meals for the family.

## 10. When the child is found

The family is told first, in person if possible. The Incident Commander confirms the facts with the Investigations Sergeant. The PIO issues a closing statement through all channels that thanks the public, asks that the alert and related posts stop being shared, and does not describe the child's condition or the circumstances beyond what the family has agreed to. OSP cancels the AMBER Alert. A hot wash is held within 7 days.

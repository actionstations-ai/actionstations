# WCSO-OP-04: Critical Incident Command and Mutual Aid

Wren County Sheriff's Office · Revision 5 · Approved by Sheriff Rosalind Teague

## 1. Purpose and scope

This plan sets out who commands, who joins unified command, how outside help is requested and how the Sheriff's Office keeps the public, families and partner agencies informed during a critical incident. A critical incident is any event that needs more than the on-duty patrol shift to manage: a missing or abducted child, a serious crime in progress at a public gathering, a large search, a major collision or a disaster. The plan is about coordination and decisions. Tactical methods are not described in this plan and are not written down in any exercise product.

The Sheriff's Office serves Wren County, Oregon (population 41,000, county seat Alder Falls). The county has no sheriff's contract city except Pine Landing (population 2,300). The Alder Falls Police Department (APD) polices the city of Alder Falls (population 18,500) and is the Office's closest partner.

The first deputy to arrive assumes command and says so on the radio on the dispatch talkgroup WREN-1. Command passes to the Patrol Lieutenant as soon as the Lieutenant arrives or takes over by phone, and passes up again if the incident outgrows the shift. Every change of command is announced on the talkgroup and logged in the computer-aided dispatch (CAD) incident chronology with the time.

## 3. Establishing the command post

The Incident Commander names a command post location, at building level (for example "Fairgrounds Exhibit Hall B office"), within 20 minutes of assuming command for any incident expected to last more than two hours. The Communications Center logs the location and the time. The command post is staffed by the Incident Commander, a scribe, a logistics officer and a liaison for each agency in unified command. The Sheriff's Office mobile command trailer (unit CP-1) is stored at the Alder Falls substation and needs 45 minutes to be on site anywhere in the county.

## 4. Unified command

Unified command is formed when another agency has legal authority or major resources in the incident. For a missing child the usual members are the Sheriff's Office, APD (if the child's last known location is inside the city), the Oregon State Police (OSP), Wren County Fire District 1 (for search support, medical and staging) and, when a federal offense is suspected, the FBI Portland Field Office. Target: unified command formed within 30 minutes of the first arriving unit. The formal record is a one-page unified command briefing listing the agencies, their commander and phone, the objectives and the next briefing time. Objectives are written in plain words (for example "find the child", "preserve scene and evidence", "keep families and the public accurately informed") and are reviewed every 60 minutes.

## 5. Mutual aid and state help

Requests are made by the Incident Commander through the Communications Center, in this order:

1. Alder Falls Police Department, by phone to the APD watch commander, extension 5501 on the shared line.
2. Deschutes County Sheriff's Office and Jefferson County Sheriff's Office, by the neighboring-county mutual aid agreement, for deputies and a second search coordinator.
3. Oregon State Police, Area Command, for troopers, aircraft requests and the AMBER Alert Plan.
4. Oregon Emergency Management (the state duty officer) when the incident needs resources from outside law enforcement: additional search teams, a mobile command vehicle or shelter support.
5. FBI Portland Field Office, through the OSP or by direct call from the Sheriff, for a suspected federal offense or kidnapping across state lines.

A request names what is needed, how many, where they report and the channel they should use. Aid that arrives is checked in at staging by the logistics officer, who keeps the roster. Staging for a county incident outside Alder Falls is the Wren County Fairgrounds parking lot unless the incident is there; the backup is the Alder Falls High School north lot.

## 6. Interoperable communications

The primary dispatch talkgroup is WREN-1; the incident gets a tactical talkgroup, WREN-T3 by default, assigned by the Communications Supervisor. APD, Fire District 1 and the Sheriff's Office share the interoperability talkgroup WC-INTEROP. OSP uses the state interoperability channel and a patch is made from WC-INTEROP at the request of the Incident Commander. If the radio system fails, the Communications Center switches to the backup site at the Pine Landing substation and deputies move to the county simplex channels; the Communications Supervisor tells the command post of the change within five minutes.

## 7. Family and public communication responsibilities

The Victim and Family Liaison is the single point of contact for the family and is assigned by the Incident Commander as soon as a child is reported missing in a way that suggests abduction. The family is told of events by the Liaison or the Investigations Sergeant before they are told by the media. The Public Information Officer does not release the name of the child or family before the Investigations Sergeant and the family agree, except in an AMBER Alert, in which case the name goes out only as the alert criteria require. No product describes the suspect beyond the descriptive information needed for the public to recognise a vehicle or person.

## 8. Scene, evidence and the investigation

The Investigations Sergeant sets the scene boundary, names the scene log keeper and assigns the evidence custodian. Evidence goes to the Wren County Sheriff's Office property room on Cedar Street with a chain of custody form. Any scene taken over by OSP or the FBI is handed over with a written transfer of custody, logged with the time. Case detail is need-to-know; the PIO sees only what the Investigations Sergeant releases.

## 9. Staffing and shifts

On a normal Saturday the Office has 5 deputies and one sergeant on patrol, one deputy at the jail intake and the Communications Center has 4 telecommunicators. When a critical incident exceeds the first four hours, the Undersheriff calls in off-duty deputies using the callout list and sets 12-hour shifts. The Logistics Officer plans meals, relief and the volunteer search teams, who are released after 10 hours.

## 10. Review

After every critical incident the Undersheriff holds a hot wash within 7 days and an after-action report within 30 days, using HSEEP format.

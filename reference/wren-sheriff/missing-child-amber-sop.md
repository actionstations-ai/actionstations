# WCSO-SOP-12: Missing and Endangered Children, and AMBER Alert Requests

Wren County Sheriff's Office · Wren County Communications (911) · Revision 4

## 1. Purpose

This procedure tells the Communications Center, the first deputy, the Incident Commander and the Investigations Sergeant how to respond to a report of a missing child, how to decide whether an AMBER Alert is justified, and how to request it. It supports Policy 6.4 and the Oregon State Police AMBER Alert Plan. It covers decisions and notifications. It does not cover tactics.

## 2. Every missing child report is taken

There is no waiting period. A report of a missing child under 18 is entered as a priority call by the telecommunicator, who asks and records: the child's name, age and a description including clothing; where and when the child was last seen and by whom; who the child was with; whether the caller believes the child was taken, and why; any vehicle or person description; medical needs or medication; and the caller's name and number. The telecommunicator dispatches the nearest deputy, notifies the Communications Supervisor and flags the CAD incident "missing child". The first deputy confirms the facts, and calls for a supervisor.

Within 30 minutes of the first report, the Communications Supervisor makes sure the child is entered in the Oregon Law Enforcement Data System (LEDS) and the national crime information database (NCIC) as a missing person. If the facts suggest an abduction, the entry carries the Child Abduction flag. The entry is made by a telecommunicator with a second person reading it back; the time is logged.

## 3. Early actions at the command level

The supervisor, working with the Incident Commander, does the following without waiting for a decision on the AMBER Alert:

- assigns an Incident Commander and a Victim and Family Liaison;
- opens a CAD incident chronology and a decision log;
- requests the Wren County Search and Rescue coordinator and Fire District 1 for ground search support if the child may be on foot or lost;
- notifies Alder Falls PD if the last known location is inside the city and the Oregon State Police Area Command for awareness;
- contacts the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) at 1-800-555-0120, which can support with a case manager and poster;
- asks the Investigations Sergeant to start the case.

## 4. The five AMBER criteria

The Oregon State Police AMBER Alert Plan follows the national guidance published by the Department of Justice. An alert is requested only if all five are met:

1. Law enforcement has a reasonable belief that an abduction has occurred.
2. Law enforcement believes the child is in imminent danger of serious bodily injury or death.
3. There is enough descriptive information about the child, the abductor or the vehicle that a broadcast would help the public to find the child.
4. The abduction victim is 17 years old or younger.
5. The child's name and the details have been entered into the national database with the Child Abduction flag.

A missing child who may have wandered off, or a custody dispute with no belief of danger, does not meet criterion 1 or 2. Those cases receive the same search effort but not an AMBER Alert; other tools such as a local media release, a Wireless Emergency Alert limited to a search area, or the "Endangered Missing Person" advisory are considered. The decision is made by the Incident Commander with the Sheriff or Undersheriff on the phone and recorded on the AMBER worksheet with the time, each criterion's yes or no, and the reason. If criteria are only partly met the Commander lists what information is missing and who will get it. The worksheet is reviewed every 30 minutes until the alert is issued or the child is found.

## 5. Requesting the alert

When all five criteria are met, the Incident Commander tells the Communications Supervisor to request the alert. The supervisor calls the OSP AMBER Alert coordinator through the OSP Northern Communications Center at 555-0142 and sends the worksheet by email. The Sheriff's Office does not issue the alert itself. OSP checks the criteria and, if satisfied, activates the alert. The Sheriff's Office target is a decision within 60 minutes of the first report whenever the facts known at that time meet the criteria, and the alert goes out within 15 minutes of the decision.

OSP sends the alert to broadcasters through the Emergency Alert System, to cellular phones as a Wireless Emergency Alert in the area OSP and the Sheriff's Office agree, to the roadside electronic signs through the Oregon Department of Transportation, and to the public through social media. The alert text is under 360 characters. The Sheriff's Office tip line (541-555-0190) and the 911 non-emergency line are the numbers on the alert. The alert text is agreed between the Incident Commander, the Investigations Sergeant and the PIO. It carries only what helps the public recognise the child, the vehicle or the person: no information about the family, the investigation or any theory.

## 6. After the alert is issued

- The tip line is staffed by three call-takers in the Communications Center conference room. Every tip is logged on a tip form with a time, the caller, the content and a priority (1 = urgent, may locate the child; 2 = possible; 3 = not useful). Priority 1 tips go to the Investigations Sergeant in under 5 minutes. A telecommunicator reads the priority 1 list back at each briefing.
- The Incident Commander decides whether to expand the alert area when new information suggests the child has moved. Expanding goes through OSP.
- Neighboring states are told by OSP. The Sheriff's Office informs Deschutes and Jefferson County sheriffs directly.
- The PIO publishes the same text on the Sheriff's Office social media pages and on the website, and posts updates only when the Investigations Sergeant approves.

## 7. Cancelling the alert

The alert is cancelled only by OSP on the Incident Commander's request, when the child is found or the criteria are no longer met. The PIO posts a closing message that thanks the public and asks them to stop sharing the alert. Social media posts of the original alert are updated, not deleted, and a correction is added to any post that carries wrong information.

## 8. Family contact

The Victim and Family Liaison meets the family in a private place, explains what the Office is doing and what will be asked of them, and gives them a direct phone number. The Liaison tells the family about the alert before it is issued and about important developments before the public learns them. The family is not asked to make public statements, but is offered the choice to do so. A family media appearance is supported by the PIO and the Liaison together and only with the Investigations Sergeant's agreement.

## 9. Records

The worksheet, the decision log, the tip forms and the CAD chronology are kept as part of the case file. The time of the first report, the time of each decision and the time the alert went out are entered in the after-action report for the incident, which compares them with the targets above.

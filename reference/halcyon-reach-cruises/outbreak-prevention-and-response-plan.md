# HR-HSP-01: Outbreak Prevention and Response Plan (Acute Gastroenteritis)

Halcyon Reach Cruises · Galveston, Texas · Fleet document, applies to MV Halcyon Dawn and MV Halcyon Tide · Revision 9 · Effective November 1, 2026

## 1. Purpose

This plan sets how Halcyon Reach ships prevent, detect and respond to acute gastroenteritis (AGE), most often norovirus, and how the shoreside team supports the ship. It is written to meet the Vessel Sanitation Program's outbreak prevention and response plan expectations and is reviewed every year by the Director of Public Health and Vessel Sanitation.

## 2. Definitions the fleet uses

- **Reportable AGE case:** a passenger or crew member who reports to the medical center, or is identified by a cabin steward or supervisor, with diarrhea (three or more loose stools in 24 hours, or more than is normal for that person) or with vomiting plus at least one other symptom (loose stool, abdominal cramps, headache, muscle aches or a temperature of 38 C / 100.4 F or more). Nausea alone is not a case. A crew member who became ill up to 3 days before boarding is counted.
- **Cumulative cases:** every case since embarkation of the current voyage, counted once. Counts are persons, not visits. Percentages are always cumulative cases divided by the number of persons on board at embarkation, separately for passengers and for crew.
- **Outbreak:** 3 percent or more of passengers or of crew reporting ill, or an unusual illness pattern even below 3 percent.
- **Action levels:** 1 percent (stage 1 enhanced cleaning), 2 percent (stage 2: special report, isolation measures, cancellation of self-service food), 3 percent (stage 3: outbreak, shoreside crisis team stood up).

## 3. Prevention

Hand hygiene stations are staffed at every dining venue and the gangway. Self-service buffets are served by crew at all times. Crew with symptoms are isolated 48 hours after symptoms stop (72 for food handlers) and do not return to work earlier. The health screening questionnaire at embarkation turns away anyone who answers that they have had vomiting or diarrhea in the last 48 hours; the Guest Services desk offers a refund or a rebooking.

## 4. Detection and daily review

The Chief Medical Officer or the doctor on duty keeps the medical log (see HR-HSP-02), updated at 06:00, 12:00, 18:00 and 24:00. The Environmental Officer reviews the log with the Hotel Director every day at 10:00. The count and percentage are sent to the shoreside Public Health office at 07:00 ship time each day, even at zero.

## 5. Stages of response

| Trigger | Required actions | Who |
|---|---|---|
| First reportable case | Isolate in the cabin until symptom-free for 24 hours; cabin disinfection with chlorine solution (1,000 ppm); stool kit if the doctor decides | Medical center, housekeeping |
| 1 percent of passengers or crew | Enhanced cleaning of public areas every 2 hours; written notices to guests; mask availability; no shared serving utensils | Hotel Director, Environmental Officer |
| 2 percent of passengers or of crew | Special report to the VSP with a phone call; notify the quarantine station if arriving in the US within 15 days; isolate all cases 48 hours after symptoms stop; close self-service stations; cancel group activities in confined spaces (kids club shared craft, the show lounge matinee) at the Captain's discretion; start the daily shoreside call at 08:00 ship time | Chief Medical Officer, Master, shoreside |
| 3 percent | Second special report; shoreside crisis team stood up; guest letter issued; outbreak announcement to the whole ship; consider an itinerary change or an early return only on the Fleet Operations Vice President's decision; sampling by the VSP at arrival | Master, VP Fleet Operations |

## 6. Isolation and cleaning

Sick guests are isolated in their own cabins; the cabin door receives a sign reading "Medical Service Required: Do Not Enter" and the steward does not enter without gloves and gown. Meals are delivered in disposable containers. Guests traveling with the sick person are not required to isolate unless they develop symptoms, but are asked to avoid public areas for 24 hours. Crew housing has 12 designated isolation cabins (deck 2, forward). If the 2 percent crew level is reached, the Chief Housekeeper and Chief Medical Officer plan more isolation cabins from the crew cabin list and report the number.

At the turnaround, an outbreak voyage gets a full terminal clean: all cabins and public spaces disinfected with chlorine at 1,000 ppm, bedding and soft furnishings laundered at 60 C or discarded, handrails and door handles every hour while guests are on board, and a documented walk-through by the Environmental Officer before the ship may board the next voyage.

## 7. Boarding decision for the next voyage

At the turnaround in Galveston the Master, the Public Health director and the VP Fleet Operations decide whether the ship boards on time. The decision weighs the 72-hour trend of cases, whether the last cases onset within the past 48 hours, the status of cabin cleaning, the VSP's inspection findings and advice, and the quarantine station's directions. Options: board on time with a clean attestation; delay boarding in blocks of 2 hours with a hold announcement for guests; cancel the voyage; shorten it. A delay is announced to booked guests, to travel partners and to the terminal operator as soon as it is decided, with the first guest message no later than 4 hours before the planned boarding time.

## 8. Reporting to authorities

See HR-HSP-02 for the clocks. The shoreside Public Health director confirms every report. The Public Information team may not give counts to the media that differ from the counts reported to the CDC.

## 9. Debrief

A hot wash is held within 72 hours of arrival; a written report is filed within 14 days, with corrective actions in the fleet's action tracker.

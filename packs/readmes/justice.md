# Law enforcement, corrections and courts

Domain pack and toolkit for the people who command, communicate and keep operating when something goes wrong in public safety and justice: police and sheriff's offices, jails and prisons, and courts.

Included with Action Stations and enabled by default. Turn it off on the Packs screen if you never exercise these fields; projects already on the domain keep working.

## What is in the pack

- **Domain profile `justice`:** roles for a municipal police department, a sheriff's office with a jail, campus or transit police, a correctional facility and a trial court or statewide judiciary, with their systems and outside parties, and seven threat families: active assailant response and unified command, barricaded subject and crisis negotiation, missing child and AMBER Alert decision, civil unrest, corrections disturbance or medical emergency, evidence and chain of custody failure, and justice service degradation (court ransomware, courthouse evacuation, jail power loss, dispatch failure).
- **Toolkit `justice`:** terms, product conventions and realism rules, the outside parties and advisor roles these fields bring in, and the guardrail checks described below. Any organization can turn the toolkit on: a school district, a campus, a venue or a transit agency does not need the justice domain to exercise unified command.

## Coordination level only

Action Stations exercises command, communication and continuity. The toolkit does not teach tactics. Generated text covers who commands, which agencies are at the table, what families, the public and the bar are told, and what keeps running. It never describes entry or breaching methods, weapon handling, tactical positioning, force options, ways to defeat security or detention measures, attack planning, real floor plans or security weaknesses. Radio traffic and logs use unit names, building-level locations and statuses. Public messages do not name or describe the person responsible. A negotiation log records contact times, decision requests and welfare at the level of "agreed to speak", with no technique and no dialogue. Every person, department and case number is fictional.

The same rule is checked when the scenario is checked: a phrase that slips into tactical or security detail is named in a warning on the inject, and the model is asked to rewrite it. Ordinary after-action words can occasionally be caught; a reviewer can clear them.

## Reference points

- Unified command is the planning norm for multi-agency violence responses; the Justice Department's review of the Robb Elementary response (January 2024) found that no one established command while officers from several agencies arrived.
- The AMBER Alert program recommends five criteria, including that the child is entered in the national crime database before the alert; states set their own.

Facts were checked against the AMBER Alert program site and public summaries of the cited reviews on 2026-11-05. Check the current documents before relying on a definition. Action Stations is not a records or evidence system.

# Space weather and satellite operations

Domain pack and toolkit for space weather: severe geomagnetic storms, solar radiation storms, radio blackouts, GNSS and timing loss, satellite anomalies, and the failure of the information that warns about them.

Included with Action Stations and enabled by default. Turn it off on the Packs screen if you never exercise space weather; projects already on the domain keep working.

## What is in the pack

- **Domain profile `space_weather`:** a grid control room (reliability coordinator and transmission operator), a satellite operations center and a GNSS timing and network operations team, with their roles, systems, outside parties and seven threat families (severe geomagnetic storm, solar radiation storm, radio blackout, combined event, GNSS and timing loss, satellite anomaly, and loss of or conflict in the information service).
- **Toolkit `space_weather`:** terms, product conventions and realism rules for the Space Weather Prediction Center watches, warnings and alerts and the G, S and R scales, plus the outside parties and advisor roles space weather brings in. Any organization can turn the toolkit on: a utility, a telecom carrier, an airline, a farm cooperative or a port does not need the space weather domain.

## Things the toolkit knows

- G levels map to Kp (G1 is Kp 5 up to G5 at Kp 9), R levels to X-ray class and S levels to proton flux.
- A watch is elevated risk with uncertain timing, a warning is an event that is occurring, imminent or likely, and an alert says a threshold has been crossed.
- Scale levels describe potential effects, impacts are regional, and the arrival time of a coronal mass ejection is uncertain by hours.

Facts were checked against the NOAA Space Weather Scales and live Space Weather Prediction Center products on 2026-11-04. Check current products before relying on a threshold. Every person, utility and satellite in a scenario is fictional.

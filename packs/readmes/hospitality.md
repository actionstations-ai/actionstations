# Hospitality, tourism and cruise

Domain pack and toolkits for the organizations that look after guests away from home: a cruise line (ship and shoreside), a hotel or resort, and a destination tourism board.

Included with Action Stations and enabled by default. Turn it off on the Packs screen if you never exercise these settings; projects already on the domain keep working.

## What is in the pack

- **Domain profile `hospitality`:** roles for a ship's command, medical and hotel teams, the shoreside team, a hotel rooms division and a tourism board, with their systems, outside parties and six threat families: gastroenteritis outbreak at sea or in a property, hurricane affecting a resort or itinerary, guest death or serious injury, cyber attack on reservations and keycards, security incident in a lobby, and fire in a hotel tower.
- **Toolkit `outbreak_at_sea`:** the cumulative gastroenteritis report with its 24-hour, 4-hour and 2 and 3 percent report points, the outbreak response checklist, guest and passenger letters, the port health notification and the boarding decision memo for the next voyage.

- **Toolkit `guest_emergency`:** muster and accounting report, man overboard checklist, alleged crime report, floor evacuation status against the in-house list, hurricane guest departure plan, guest death or serious injury notification checklist, hotline script and a property management system outage board. The guest letter is shared with the outbreak toolkit.

## Things the toolkit knows

- A ship bound for a US port reports cumulative cases of acute gastroenteritis 24 to 36 hours before arrival and again at least 4 hours before if the count changed.
- Within 15 days of a US port, a special report goes to the CDC when cases reach 2 percent of passengers or of crew, and again at 3 percent. Three percent is an outbreak.
- Ill passengers stay in their cabins until 24 hours after symptoms end; ill food employees stay off duty for 48 hours.
- A cruise line telephones the nearest FBI field office as soon as possible after a homicide, suspicious death, missing US national, kidnapping, serious assault, certain sexual offenses or theft over $10,000, then files a written report on the Department of Transportation website (46 U.S.C. 3507, read 2026-11-05).
- The CDC may ask a line to tell port authorities, delay boarding the next voyage and arrange cleaning in the terminal.

Facts were checked against 42 CFR 71.21 and the CDC Vessel Sanitation Program 2025 Environmental Public Health Standards on 2026-11-05. Check the current documents before relying on a number. Every ship, line, hotel and guest in the templates is fictional; real ports are fine.

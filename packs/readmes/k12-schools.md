# K-12 schools

Domain pack for school districts, charter networks and private school systems: elementary, middle and high schools, district transportation, nutrition services and athletics. Higher education stays in the Campuses pack.

Facts below were checked on 2026-10-24 for the research dossier (`docs/research/domains/k12_schools.md` in the Action Stations repository). Regulations and editions change; re-check a deadline before an exercise depends on it.

## Plans the exercise should draw on

There is no single federal mandate for a school emergency plan. Requirements come from state law, funding conditions and FERPA.

- **School / district Emergency Operations Plan (EOP)**: required by most states. The federal model is the REMS _Guide for Developing High-Quality School Emergency Operations Plans_ (ED, HHS, DHS, DOJ, 2013) with FEMA CPG 101: a basic plan; functional annexes for evacuation, lockdown, shelter-in-place, accounting for all persons, communications and warning, family reunification, continuity of operations, recovery, public health and mental health, and security; and threat- and hazard-specific annexes.
- **Drills** (fire, lockdown, severe weather, bus evacuation): frequency is set by state law and the locally adopted fire code.
- **Behavioral threat assessment program**: required by law in many states; the USSS NTAC model (2018) describes the team, the central reporting mechanism and the thresholds for law-enforcement involvement.
- **Silent panic alarm** linked to 911 ("Alyssa's Law" in several states).
- **Cyber incident response plan** and an annual tabletop: recommended by CISA's 2023 K-12 report and expected by the FCC Schools and Libraries Cybersecurity Pilot Program.
- **Section 504 / IDEA accommodations** in drills, evacuation, sheltering and reunification (34 CFR 104 and 300).

## Reporting deadlines and disclosure rules

- **FERPA health-or-safety emergency exception (34 CFR 99.36)**: disclosure of education records "to appropriate parties ... in connection with an emergency if knowledge of the information is necessary to protect the health or safety of the student or other individuals"; the disclosure is recorded (99.32). Verified against eCFR. FERPA itself sets no breach-notice clock.
- **Student data breach**: notice to parents, the attorney general or the state education agency on state timelines, which vary by state.
- **CIRCIA (not in force)**: the April 2024 proposed rule named LEAs, ESAs and SEAs with 1,000 or more students as covered entities, with 72 hours for a covered cyber incident and 24 hours after a ransom payment. The final rule was not published as of 2026-10-24.

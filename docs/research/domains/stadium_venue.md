## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** professional and collegiate stadiums, multipurpose arenas, ballparks, motor speedways, amphitheaters, convention centers and exhibition halls, festival grounds and temporary outdoor event sites, and the operator / tenant team / promoter on event day. Large retail, lodging and casinos are in `commercial_facilities`.
- **Aliases users might type:** stadium, arena, ballpark, venue, amphitheater, convention center, coliseum, speedway, raceway, fairgrounds, festival, concert, game day, event, sports complex, field house, NSSE, special event, World Cup, fan fest.
- **CISA sector:** **Commercial Facilities Sector**, **Sports Leagues** and **Public Assembly** subsectors (the CF sector has eight subsectors: Entertainment and Media, Gaming, Lodging, Outdoor Events, Public Assembly, Real Estate, Retail, Sports Leagues). **SRMA: CISA.** _The CISA Commercial Facilities Sector Profile ("As of August 2026", fetched) states the sector "is categorized into eight distinct subsectors"; the subsector names above match the profile's column headings as far as the PDF text extraction shows._
- Federal event designations: **National Special Security Event (NSSE)** (USSS lead; 18 U.S.C. 3056(e)) and the DHS **Special Event Assessment Rating (SEAR)** levels 1–5 for other events (_SEAR mechanics from general knowledge, not re-verified_).

## 3. Systems

**IT / business**

- Ticketing and mobile entry: Ticketmaster / AXS / SeatGeek-type platforms; barcode/NFC scanners at turnstiles; ticket-transfer and resale.
- Point-of-sale and concessions (cashless), payment terminals (PCI DSS scope), inventory.
- Credentialing system (staff, media, vendors, contractors; RFID or photo badges).
- Venue Wi-Fi and distributed antenna system (DAS), team/venue app, website, email, helpdesk, SIEM.
- IPTV / digital signage, videoboard and ribbon-board control (content management, Daktronics-type controllers).
- Public-safety comms: radio (P25 interoperable with police), Ops command post software (incident logging, e.g., commercial event-ops platforms).

**OT / building**

- Turnstiles and access control (PACS), credential readers, vehicle barriers / bollards (crash-rated, often hydraulic).
- CCTV / VMS with analytics (crowd density, perimeter), license-plate readers in lots.
- PA and emergency voice/alarm communication (EVAC), mass-notification on boards.
- Fire alarm, smoke control / atmospheric control in arenas, sprinklers, retractable roof control.
- Building automation (HVAC), lighting control (field/sports lighting), generators and UPS, elevators and escalators.
- Screening equipment: walk-through metal detectors and AI-assisted weapons detection, X-ray for bags (some venues).
- Counter-UAS detection (RF / radar sensors; mitigation authority limited to federal agencies, see §5).

## 4. Outside parties and media

- **Federal:** DHS/CISA Protective Security Advisor (venue assessments, SAFETY Act coordination, SEAR events), CISA Office for Bombing Prevention, FBI field office (special events coordinator, JTTF, WMD coordinator), USSS (NSSE lead), FAA (TFR, UAS; System Operations Security), TSA (VIPR teams at some events), ATF (explosives), FEMA (NSSE/SEAR support), HHS/ASPR for medical surge at large events.
- **State/local:** city police and sheriff (event command), fire department / fire marshal, EMS agency, county emergency management and EOC, public transit agency, state DOT (traffic), state fusion center, public health (food vendors, heat), hospitals (MCI notifications).
- **League / tenant:** team or league security (major leagues publish security standards to member clubs; documents are private), promoter, touring artist security, broadcast partners.
- **Vendors:** ticketing provider, POS/payment processor, credential vendor, contract security company, screening equipment vendor, concessionaire, parking operator, crowd-management consultants, counter-UAS detection vendor, cyber insurer.
- **Information sharing:** CISA Commercial Facilities Sector Coordinating Council, sports-league security directors' networks, NCS4 (National Center for Spectator Sports Safety and Security at the University of Southern Mississippi; copyrighted guides), Real Estate ISAC (covers some venues), FBI InfraGard.
- **Media:** live broadcast (game or concert on air while the incident unfolds), local TV, national sports media, X/Twitter, TikTok/Instagram fan videos from the stands, Reddit team subreddits, ticket-resale marketplaces (fraud complaints), the team's own social accounts.

## 6. Common operational workflows

**Command structure on event day**: venue command post (or Unified Command Post) with operations, security, police, fire, EMS, medical, facilities, guest services, parking, IT, PIO; CCTV room adjacent; police may run a separate event EOC. Decisions use pre-scripted **"hold / delay / evacuate / shelter-in-place / relocate"** messages on PA and boards.

**Severe weather / lightning**: NWS forecast and lightning detection → lightning within the venue's threshold (commonly 8–10 miles; _policy varies_) → suspend play, PA/videoboard shelter message to concourses → resume after 30 minutes without strikes (common NCAA/NWS guidance; _not verified this session_).

**Suspicious item / bomb threat**: report to command post → security isolates, police bomb technicians respond → decide on partial evacuation of sections → messaging that avoids panic → all-clear. (CISA Office for Bombing Prevention; FEMA IS-15 job aids.)

**Active assailant**: report via radio/CCTV/911 → lockdown or evacuation by zone → law enforcement in unified command → MCI triage at first-aid stations, casualty collection points → hospital notification → family assistance and reunification (fans separated from children) → media briefing. (CTEP _Large Sport Event Active Shooter_ SitMan; CISA _Active Assailant EAP_ guide.)

**Crowd crush / surge**: crowd density alarm from CCTV analytics or reports → hold gates, open additional egress, stop the show (artist and promoter coordination), PA instructions → EMS surge. (FEMA IS-15 job aids: critical crowd densities, crowd throughput capacities.)

**Ticketing / mobile entry outage or cyberattack**: scanners fail → switch to manual entry (print lists, visual validation) → long queues at gates create a crowd-safety hazard → decide whether to delay the start → PIO messages on app and social media; for a payment-card compromise, take POS offline, cash-only or free-concession decisions, PCI forensics.

**UAS incursion**: detection system alert or sighting → FAA/law enforcement notified → if the drone is over the bowl, hold play and decide on shelter → law-enforcement location of the pilot. (CTEP _UAS Stadium Crash_ SitMan; FAA TFR.)

**Forms used**: ICS 201, 202, 203, 204, 205, 206 (medical plan), 208 (safety), 213, 214, 215A; incident report / ejection report; lost child report; event action plan; MCI triage tags; FEMA IS-15 job aids (event profile, hazard analysis worksheets).

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Cyber**: mobile-ticketing platform outage 45 minutes before kickoff with 60,000 fans in queues, combined with a phishing-fed fake "free entry" link spreading on social media (CISA _Venue Guide for Mitigating Dependency Disruptions_; ticket-fraud patterns).
2. **Cyber-physical**: videoboard and ribbon boards hijacked to display false evacuation instructions while PA works (CISA _Venue Guide for Security Enhancements_; public cases of hijacked public displays; no venue named).
3. **Physical security**: active assailant at a perimeter gate during ingress; crowd flight into a lot with vehicles moving (CTEP _Large Sport Event Active Shooter_; USSS NTAC _Mass Attacks in Public Spaces 2016–2020_).
4. **Vehicle ramming**: vehicle drives through a fan-fest street closure two hours before the game (CISA _Vehicle Ramming Action Guide_ 2024; Vehicle Incident Prevention and Mitigation Security Guide 2024).
5. **IED / suspicious item**: unattended backpack in an upper-level concourse plus a phoned bomb threat at halftime (CTEP _Outdoor Events Active Assailant IED_; CISA OBP).
6. **UAS**: a drone overflies the bowl during a nationally televised game and drops leaflets; the second flight is unidentified (CTEP _UAS Stadium Crash_; FAA sports TFR).
7. **Mass gathering / crowd crush**: post-concert surge at a narrow exit when a rideshare zone is closed (FEMA IS-15 job aids: "Critical Crowd Densities", "Crowd Throughput Capacities"; public after-action reviews of festival crowd crushes exist but none was fetched this session; no victim named in the seed).
8. **Natural hazard**: lightning and severe storm with 70,000 in the stands, followed by a partial power outage that drops concourse lighting (venue weather policies; NWS).
9. **Insider**: a contract security screener is paid to pass a prohibited item; a credential-printing vendor account is abused to create counterfeit field-access credentials (CISA _Public Venue Credentialing Guide_ 2026).
10. **Infrastructure failure / dependency**: regional transit failure and a cell-network overload stop fans leaving and stop staff radios relying on LTE (CISA _Venue Guide for Mitigating Dependency Disruptions_, 2025).

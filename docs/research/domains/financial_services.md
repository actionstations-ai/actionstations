## 1. Scope

**Covers:** a US community financial institution with roughly $100 M to $10 B in assets that runs its own branches and outsources its core processing:

- community banks (national banks / federal savings associations under the OCC, state member banks under the Fed, state non-member banks under the FDIC, plus the state banking department);
- federally insured credit unions (federal charters under NCUA, state charters under NCUA and the state regulator);
- by extension, a small bank holding company, a thrift, or a CUSO (credit union service organization) acting as a service provider.

**Aliases users might type:** bank, community bank, credit union, CU, FCU, thrift, savings bank, savings and loan, S&L, financial institution, FI, depository institution, community financial institution, CFI, BHC, banking, fintech partner bank, member-owned cooperative.

**Out of scope (would need their own profiles):** broker-dealers and asset managers (SEC/FINRA, Reg S-P, Reg S-ID), insurers (state DOI / NAIC model law), payment processors and card networks as the exercising organization, financial market utilities (Fedwire, CHIPS, DTCC: they are "designated FMUs" and fall outside the bank notification rule), large complex banks (LCBO-supervised; Sheltered Harbor and the Hamilton-style sector exercises fit them better).

**CISA critical infrastructure sector:** Financial Services Sector. CISA's sector page says Treasury is the SRMA and that PPD-21 renamed the earlier "Banking and Finance Sector" ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/critical-infrastructure-security-and-resilience/critical-infrastructure-sectors/financial-services-sector)). Dependent sectors to show in the exercise: Communications (telecom carriers, DDoS scrubbing), Information Technology (core and cloud providers), Energy (branch and data-center power), Commercial Facilities (branches).

**Sector Risk Management Agency:** **U.S. Department of the Treasury**, through its Office of Cybersecurity and Critical Infrastructure Protection (OCCIP). The statutory basis for SRMAs is 6 U.S.C. 665d (FY2021 NDAA §9002). Treasury's January 2025 _Financial Services Sector Risk Management Plan_ and its October 2024 _Roles and Responsibilities Report_ cite NSM-22 for the designation. The SRMA coordinates with the **FBIIC** (Financial and Banking Information Infrastructure Committee, the regulators' council) on the government side and the **FSSCC** (Financial Services Sector Coordinating Council) on the industry side. FS-ISAC is the sector ISAC.

**Primary supervisors (the exercise's "regulator" role):**

| Charter                                           | Federal prudential regulator                                                                                | Incident notice rule |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| National bank / federal savings association       | OCC                                                                                                         | 12 CFR Part 53       |
| State member bank, BHC / SLHC                     | Federal Reserve (via the regional Reserve Bank)                                                             | 12 CFR 225 Subpart N |
| State non-member bank / state savings association | FDIC                                                                                                        | 12 CFR 304 Subpart C |
| Federally insured credit union                    | NCUA                                                                                                        | 12 CFR 748.1(c)      |
| All of the above                                  | State banking / credit union regulator (state law; many states run their own breach and cyber notice rules) | state law            |

The CFPB enforces Reg E against institutions over $10 B; for smaller institutions the prudential regulator examines Reg E compliance.

## 4. Outside parties and media

**Regulators and government**

- Primary federal regulator: the OCC supervisory office, the Federal Reserve Bank, the FDIC regional office or the NCUA regional office (designated incident points of contact)
- State banking or credit union regulator (state Department of Financial Institutions / Banking)
- FinCEN (SAR filing; advisories), OFAC (sanctions screening; ransomware payment advisory), Treasury OCCIP (SRMA; sector coordination)
- Federal Reserve Financial Services (FRFS), which runs Fedwire, FedACH, FedNow and FedLine customer support (operational, not supervisory)
- FBI field office and IC3 (BEC, ransomware; IC3 Recovery Asset Team / "Financial Fraud Kill Chain" for wires), U.S. Secret Service Cyber Fraud Task Force (account takeover, ATM jackpotting, skimming), local police (robbery, ATM attacks), US Postal Inspection Service (mail-theft check fraud)
- CISA (incident reporting, regional cybersecurity advisor); CIRCIA reporting once the final rule takes effect (at OMB review as of October 2026; not yet in force) **[status per trade press]**
- State attorney general (state breach-notice statutes), consumer reporting agencies (bulk-notice duties under some state laws)
- County / city emergency management (natural hazard branch closures), the National Weather Service

**Industry and vendors**

- Core processor / bank service provider (the main third party), digital banking vendor, card processor and EFT network, ATM servicer and armored carrier (cash-in-transit), item processor, MSSP / SOC, IR retainer firm, cyber insurer and breach coach (panel counsel), DDoS mitigation provider, telecom carrier
- Correspondent bank / corporate credit union (settlement, liquidity, wires for the institution)
- FS-ISAC (sector ISAC, including its community institution and CU councils), state bankers or credit union league, ICBA / America's Credit Unions, Nacha and regional payments associations (ACH rules), card networks (Visa / Mastercard compromise programs), PCI forensic investigator (PFI) for card breaches
- Sheltered Harbor (data vaulting standard; members only)
- Business customers whose payroll ACH files are in flight; the municipal depositors (public funds)

**Media channels**

- Local TV assignment desk and the local business journal
- Facebook community groups and Nextdoor ("is the bank down?")
- X / Reddit posts with screenshots of the online-banking error page
- Downdetector-style outage trackers **[product named as a pattern only]**
- Branch lobby signage, drive-up sign, ATM screen message
- The institution's own website banner, mobile-app push notification, IVR greeting and email/SMS alerts
- Trade press (American Banker, Banking Dive, CU Times) and security bloggers who track ransomware leak sites
- Ransomware leak site post naming the institution or its vendor

### Verbatim definitions (eCFR, current as of 2026-10-01)

**Bank rule (identical text in 12 CFR 53.2(b)(7), 225.301(b)(7), 304.22(b)(7)):**

> _Computer-security incident_ is an occurrence that results in actual harm to the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an information system or the information that the system processes, stores, or transmits.

> _Notification incident_ is a computer-security incident that has materially disrupted or degraded, or is reasonably likely to materially disrupt or degrade, a banking organization's—
> (i) Ability to carry out banking operations, activities, or processes, or deliver banking products and services to a material portion of its customer base, in the ordinary course of business;
> (ii) Business line(s), including associated operations, services, functions, and support, that upon failure would result in a material loss of revenue, profit, or franchise value; or
> (iii) Operations, including associated services, functions and support, as applicable, the failure or discontinuance of which would pose a threat to the financial stability of the United States.

**Timing (12 CFR 53.3; same text in 225.302 and 304.23):** "The OCC must receive this notification from the banking organization as soon as possible and no later than 36 hours after the banking organization determines that a notification incident has occurred." The notice goes "through email, telephone, or other similar methods."

**Bank service provider (12 CFR 53.4(a); same in 225.303, 304.24):** "A bank service provider is required to notify at least one bank-designated point of contact at each affected banking organization customer as soon as possible when the bank service provider determines that it has experienced a computer-security incident that has materially disrupted or degraded, or is reasonably likely to materially disrupt or degrade, covered services provided to such banking organization for four or more hours." Where no contact was designated, notice goes "to the Chief Executive Officer and Chief Information Officer of the banking organization customer, or two individuals of comparable responsibilities." Scheduled maintenance communicated in advance is excluded (§53.4(b)).

**Rulemaking:** 86 FR 66424 (Nov. 23, 2021), _Computer-Security Incident Notification Requirements for Banking Organizations and Their Bank Service Providers_. "Effective date: April 1, 2022; Compliance date: May 1, 2022" (Federal Register API). No 2025–2026 amendment was found; the Fed renewed FR 2231 without revision in 2025.

**NCUA (12 CFR 748.1(c)):** "The NCUA must receive this notification as soon as possible but no later than 72 hours after a federally insured credit union reasonably believes that it has experienced a reportable cyber incident or, if reporting pursuant to paragraph (c)(1)(i)(C) of this section, within 72 hours of being notified by a third-party, whichever is sooner."

> (1)(i) A reportable cyber incident is any substantial cyber incident that leads to one or more of the following: (A) A substantial loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability of a network or member information system ... that results from the unauthorized access to or exposure of sensitive data, disrupts vital member services as defined in § 749.1 of this chapter, or has a serious impact on the safety and resiliency of operational systems and processes. (B) A disruption of business operations, vital member services, or a member information system resulting from a cyberattack or exploitation of vulnerabilities. (C) A disruption of business operations or unauthorized access to sensitive data facilitated through, or caused by, a compromise of a credit union service organization, cloud service provider, or other third-party data hosting provider or by a supply chain compromise.

Good-faith testing requested by the owner is excluded (748.1(c)(1)(ii)). Rule: 88 FR 12811 (Mar. 1, 2023), effective **Sept. 1, 2023** (Federal Register API). NCUA Letter 23-CU-07 (Aug 2023) gives the reporting channels: the online form at cyberreports.ncua.gov, 1-833-CYBERCU (1-833-292-3728), or secure email to cybercu@ncua.gov. Its Quick Reference Guide lists what to report and says not to send PII, IOCs, specific vulnerabilities or attachments in the first notice.

**Vital member services (12 CFR 749.1):** "the essential financial services that a credit union provides to its members, such as member access to their accounts, share withdrawal and deposit facilities, and loan payments and disbursements."

**GLBA customer notice (12 CFR 30 App. B, Supp. A, III.A):** "If the institution determines that misuse of its information about a customer has occurred or is reasonably possible, it should notify the affected customer as soon as possible. Customer notice may be delayed if an appropriate law enforcement agency determines that notification will interfere with a criminal investigation and provides the institution with a written request for the delay." The response program includes "Notifying its primary Federal regulator as soon as possible when the institution becomes aware of an incident involving unauthorized access to or use of sensitive customer information." _Sensitive customer information_ means "a customer's name, address, or telephone number, in conjunction with the customer's social security number, driver's license number, account number, credit or debit card number, or a personal identification number or password that would permit access to the customer's account." The notice should describe the incident, the types of information involved and what was done, give a phone number, and remind customers to stay vigilant "over the next twelve to twenty-four months." (Part 30 App. B was last amended at 91 FR 18293, Apr. 10, 2026, by the OCC/FDIC rule that removes reputation risk; the notice text is unchanged.)

**Bank SAR timing (31 CFR 1020.320(b)(3)):** "A bank is required to file a SAR no later than 30 calendar days after the date of initial detection ... If no suspect was identified ... a bank may delay filing a SAR for an additional 30 calendar days to identify a suspect. In no case shall reporting be delayed more than 60 calendar days ... In situations involving violations that require immediate attention, such as, for example, ongoing money laundering schemes, the bank shall immediately notify, by telephone, an appropriate law enforcement authority in addition to filing timely a SAR." Robbery and burglary reported to law enforcement are exempt (1020.320(c)). Records are kept 5 years. Credit unions: 12 CFR 748.1(d).

**Reg E (12 CFR 1005.6(b)):** the consumer's liability is capped at $50 if they notify within two business days of learning of a lost or stolen access device, and at $500 otherwise. Unauthorized transfers on a statement must be reported within 60 days of transmittal. **12 CFR 1005.11(c):** investigate within 10 business days, or up to 45 days with provisional credit given within 10 business days. Report results within 3 business days of finishing; correct within 1 business day of finding an error.

**Bank Protection Act (12 CFR 326.3(a)):** the security program shall "Establish procedures for opening and closing for business and for the safekeeping of all currency ... (2) ... procedures that will assist in identifying persons committing crimes ... (i) Retaining a record of any robbery, burglary, or larceny ... (ii) Maintaining a camera that records activity in the banking office; ... (3) Provide for initial and periodic training ... in proper employee conduct during and after a robbery." 326.3(b) lists the minimum devices, starting with a vault or safe.

## 6. Common operational workflows

1. **Detection → triage → declaration.** An alert comes from the SOC/MSSP, the core provider, a customer call, a fraud-queue spike or a vendor notice. The ISO and CIO triage, then the IRP is activated and the IMT convenes (CEO, COO, CIO, ISO, CRO, GC, PIO, BCM). Most community institutions use the IRP's own structure rather than full ICS. Where they use ICS, the COO acts as Incident Commander and forms are ICS 201 / 202 / 213 / 214.
2. **Engage counsel, insurer and IR firm.** Call the cyber insurer hotline; panel breach counsel retains the forensic firm under privilege. This is a typical first-hour decision point.
3. **Regulatory clocks** (start each one in the MSEL):
   - **Bank → primary federal regulator: ≤ 36 h** after _determining_ a notification incident occurred (12 CFR 53.3 / 225.302 / 304.23).
   - **Credit union → NCUA: ≤ 72 h** after _reasonably believing_ a reportable cyber incident occurred, or after third-party notice (748.1(c)).
   - **Bank service provider → bank: "as soon as possible"** once a disruption of covered services lasts, or is reasonably likely to last, ≥ 4 h (53.4).
   - **GLBA:** primary regulator "as soon as possible" for unauthorized access to sensitive customer information; customer notice "as soon as possible", which law enforcement can delay in writing (Supp. A).
   - **SAR:** ≤ 30 calendar days from initial detection (≤ 60 with no suspect); telephone law enforcement immediately for ongoing violations (31 CFR 1020.320). Cyber-event SARs follow FIN-2016-A005 (cyber-event indicators, IP addresses with timestamps, hashes). Ransomware follows FIN-2021-A004 and deepfakes FIN-2024-Alert004, each with its keyword in field 2.
   - **OFAC:** screen any ransom payment; OFAC's Sept 21, 2021 advisory treats prompt reporting to and cooperation with law enforcement and CISA as mitigating factors.
   - **NCUA catastrophic act:** ≤ 5 business days (748.1(b)).
   - **State regulator and state breach-notice statutes:** vary by state; some set fixed day counts.
   - **Card brands / PCI:** notify the acquirer or networks right away on suspected card-data compromise (network rules, contractual; not public law).
   - **CIRCIA:** 72 h incident / 24 h ransom payment to CISA once the final rule takes effect (not yet in force; final rule at OMB as of Oct 2026 **[per trade press]**).
   - **SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05:** within 4 business days of a materiality determination, for a publicly traded holding company (88 FR 51896).
4. **Payments containment.** Halt outbound wires and set callback verification. Contact the Fed (FRFS customer contact center) to suspend FedLine credentials. Ask the beneficiary bank for a wire recall. File an IC3 complaint quickly so the Recovery Asset Team can try the Financial Fraud Kill Chain. Handle ACH returns and reversals within Nacha time frames. Freeze accounts, block card BINs or ranges, and switch the card processor to stand-in limits.
5. **Business continuity.** Run offline teller / store-and-forward, cap cash withdrawals, use manual signature cards, extend lobby hours, script the contact center, switch the website banner, call business customers about payroll files, and fall back to the core provider's DR site.
6. **Customer and member communications.** Use the holding statement first. Then the GLBA notice letter if misuse is reasonably possible, Reg E dispute handling, and any state AG / CRA notices.
7. **Physical security.** After a robbery: 911, lock down, preserve the scene, run the witness description forms, notify the FBI (federal bank robbery statute 18 U.S.C. 2113), keep the Bank Protection Act record, and support employees through the EAP. No SAR is required when the robbery was reported to law enforcement.
8. **Recovery and after-action.** Restore and reconcile from the last good end-of-day. Assess the vendor, report to the board, write an HSEEP-style AAR/IP and track corrective actions for the next exam.

**Forms and documents seen at the table:** ICS 201 / 202 / 213 / 214 (optional), the institution's IRP incident log, the regulator notice email, the NCUA Cyber Incident Reporting Card, the FinCEN SAR (BSA E-Filing), the IC3 complaint, the Reg E written dispute / affidavit, the wire recall request, the bank robbery description form, the vendor incident notice, the board notice.

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

Fictional institution throughout. The public patterns are cited; no real victim is named in a seed.

1. **Ransomware at the core processor / CUSO (cyber, third party).** The cloud-hosted core provider is encrypted on a Friday afternoon. Online banking, ACH posting and debit authorization fall to stand-in. The provider's first notice is vague. Players must decide whether this is a notification incident, start the 36 h / 72 h clock off the vendor notice, and choose whether to switch to manual processing for Monday payroll. _Pattern:_ FDIC Cyber Challenge vignettes 4 and 9; CISA FS CTEP Module 2; 748.1(c)(1)(i)(C) third-party prong; a Dec 2023 CUSO cloud-host ransomware event reported to affect about 60 credit unions **[per NCUA statements in trade press, not verified here]**.
2. **BEC / wire fraud against a commercial customer (cyber-enabled fraud).** A title-company customer's email is compromised. Three "updated instructions" wires go out through business online banking, a fourth is pending, and the beneficiary bank is not responding. _Pattern:_ FinCEN FIN-2019-A005; the IC3 2024 report gives BEC losses of $2,770,151,146; FDIC Cyber Challenge vignette 2 (corporate account takeover).
3. **Deepfake voice of the CEO authorizes an urgent wire (cyber / insider-social engineering).** The wire room gets a call in the CEO's cloned voice, then a text from a "new number", asking for an out-of-pattern correspondent wire before cutoff. _Pattern:_ FinCEN FIN-2024-Alert004 (deepfake media); the FBI's May 2025 PSA on AI voice impersonation of senior officials.
4. **ATM jackpotting / cash-out (cyber-physical).** "Low cash" alerts from three drive-up ATMs overnight. CCTV shows "technicians" opening the top hat, and there is a parallel card cash-out across the network. _Pattern:_ FDIC Cyber Challenge vignette 6 (ATM malware); FFIEC 2014 joint statement on unlimited-operations ATM cash-out; the Jan 2018 Secret Service jackpotting warning to financial institutions.
5. **DDoS on online banking during a data-theft diversion (cyber).** The website and mobile app go down at month-end, and the DDoS provider's mitigation fails. The IT manager then finds a second intrusion exfiltrating customer data. _Pattern:_ FDIC Cyber Challenge vignette 5; FFIEC 2014 DDoS joint statement; the 2012–13 hacktivist DDoS campaign against US banks **[DOJ 2016 indictment, URL not verified]**.
6. **Branch takeover robbery with a hostage threat (physical security).** A takeover robbery at a branch at opening. The holdup alarm triggers and a teller is injured. Social media live-posts; the PIO, BSO and Retail must decide on other branches. _Pattern:_ FBI Bank Crime Statistics 2023 (1,263 robberies, Friday the peak day).
7. **Credential-stuffing account takeover plus a P2P / FedNow drain (cyber).** Hundreds of retail logins from a residential-proxy pool. Instant payments leave before the fraud team reacts. Reg E claims and the contact center queue explode. _Pattern:_ FFIEC 2021 Authentication and Access guidance; Reg E 1005.6 / 1005.11.
8. **Hurricane or flood closes the operations center and four branches (natural hazard).** The data center floods, the telecom carrier is down, and staff cannot reach the alternate site. Cash demand spikes and the NCUA catastrophic act clock applies. _Pattern:_ FDIC Cyber Challenge vignette 8 (flood); FFIEC BCM booklet; 12 CFR 748.1(b), 749 App. B.
9. **Insider: a departing loan officer exports the member list (insider).** DLP fires on a personal-email upload two days before resignation, and a competitor's marketing hits members the following week. GLBA notice and SAR decisions follow. _Pattern:_ FIN-2016-A005 (insider-enabled cyber events); Interagency Guidelines Supp. A.
10. **Mail-theft check fraud wave plus a misinformation spike (infrastructure / fraud, PRESSURE).** Altered business checks clear through RDC. A viral post claims "the bank was hacked, pull your money", which causes lines at two branches (run-on-the-bank pressure). _Pattern:_ FinCEN FIN-2023-Alert003 (mail theft-related check fraud).

Threat families for the profile (default first): `cyber`, `insider`, `physical_security`, `cyber_physical`, `infrastructure_failure`, `natural_hazard`. `uas_drone` and `mass_gathering` have weak fit; leave them out, or include `mass_gathering` only as a "bank run / lobby crowd" pressure beat.

Decision-point examples for the MSEL author: pay or refuse the ransom (OFAC exposure, insurer position); declare a notification incident now from the vendor's partial notice, or wait for facts (the 36 h clock); halt all outbound wires on payroll day; close branches versus drive-up only; send GLBA notices now, or honor a law-enforcement delay request; reimburse BEC losses for a top commercial customer that are not covered by Reg E; disconnect FedLine.

Noise examples: a teller drawer out of balance by $20, a recycler jam, a jammed night-drop, a customer upset about an overdraft fee, a cheque-imaging scanner offline, a printer driver failure at the loan desk, an elevator inspection at HQ.

Pressure examples: "is my money safe?" posts with an error screenshot; a reporter asking whether the bank paid a ransom; a municipal treasurer demanding confirmation that public funds are secure; a business customer threatening to move payroll; the board chair demanding hourly updates; the state regulator calling before the bank has notified anyone.

# Domain dossier: `healthcare` (hospital / health system)

Researched 2026-10-24. Extends the existing profile `src/shared/domains/profiles/healthcare.ts`. "Verified" means checked against the primary source during this session; "unverified" is marked inline.

## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** acute-care hospitals, multi-hospital health systems, critical access hospitals, academic medical centers, freestanding EDs, ambulatory surgery centers attached to a system. Also useful for large clinics and dialysis/LTC operators (CMS has parallel emergency preparedness rules for 17 provider types; see §5).
- **Aliases users might type:** hospital, health system, medical center, clinic, ED, emergency department, trauma center, health_system, healthcare_system, hospital_system, IDN, AMC, critical access hospital.
- **CISA sector:** Healthcare and Public Health (HPH) Sector.
- **Sector Risk Management Agency:** HHS. Within HHS, ASPR runs the sector-cybersecurity coordination role and the HHS 405(d) program. HC3 (Health Sector Cybersecurity Coordination Center) was the HHS threat-sharing office; `https://www.hhs.gov/hc3` now redirects to `https://tech.hhs.gov/` (verified 2026-10-24), so treat HC3 products as archival and don't name HC3 as a live injector without checking. The CISA HPH CTEP (Oct 2023) still names HC3 in its Module 1 alert.
- **Overlap:** `public_health` covers health departments; this domain stays with the care-delivery organization.

## 3. Systems

**IT (vendor-neutral category, example products where useful):**

- Electronic health record (EHR): e.g. Epic, Oracle Health (Cerner), MEDITECH. Includes ADT (admission/discharge/transfer), CPOE, eMAR, clinical documentation, a downtime "BCA" read-only viewer.
- Interface engine (HL7 v2 / FHIR feeds between EHR, lab, radiology, pharmacy, billing): e.g. Rhapsody, Cloverleaf, Mirth.
- PACS / RIS (radiology imaging and scheduling), VNA.
- Laboratory information system (LIS), blood bank system.
- Pharmacy information system and automated dispensing cabinets (e.g. Omnicell, BD Pyxis).
- Revenue cycle, claims clearinghouse, eligibility (a third-party clearinghouse outage is a public incident pattern; see §7).
- Patient portal, telehealth platform, public website, call center / nurse line.
- Identity: Active Directory, SSO / badge-tap, privileged access, help desk.
- Email, Teams/Slack, paging and secure messaging (e.g. Vocera, Spok), nurse call.
- Scheduling, OR management, bed management / patient flow board.
- SIEM / EDR / SOC (often an MSSP).

**OT / clinical engineering / building:**

- Networked medical devices: infusion pumps, physiological monitors and central stations, ventilators, CT/MRI, lab analyzers.
- Building automation: HVAC with pressure-controlled isolation rooms and ORs, medical gas alarms, emergency generators and automatic transfer switches, fire alarm panels.
- Physical security: access control (PACS, the security kind; distinct from imaging PACS), infant / wander-guard tagging, CCTV, duress buttons.
- Pneumatic tube system, elevators, water treatment for dialysis.

**Identifiers seen in hospital documents (fact categories):**

- Units / wards (`4 West`, `MICU`, `L&D`, `5N Tele`): existing category `units`.
- Towers / buildings / pavilions: existing `towers`.
- Operating rooms (`OR 7`): existing `ors`.
- **Beds / rooms** (`Room 412-B`, `ED Bed 14`, `Trauma Bay 2`): proposed category `beds`, alnum.
- **ED zones / tracks** (`Fast Track`, `Pod C`, `Resus 1`): proposed `ed_zones`, not linted.
- **Generators / ATS** (`Gen 2`, `ATS-3`): proposed `generators`, token.
- **Overhead codes** (`Code Blue`, `Code Pink`, `Code Silver`, `Code Gray`, `Code Yellow`, `Code Black`): code colors are facility-defined and vary by state hospital association (unverified across states). Use as a vocabulary list, not a fact category.
- **HICS form numbers** (HICS 201, 202, 203, 204, 205A, 213, 214, 251, 254, 255, 257). Numbers are from the HICS 2014 forms set (unverified this session).

## 4. Outside parties and media

- **Regulators / federal:** CMS (Conditions of Participation; state survey agency acts for CMS), HHS Office for Civil Rights (HIPAA), HHS ASPR (Hospital Preparedness Program, healthcare coalitions), FDA (medical device recalls / safety communications), DEA (controlled-substance loss reporting), FBI field office, CISA regional office and Cybersecurity Advisor, U.S. Secret Service (financial fraud), OFAC (ransom sanctions screening).
- **State / local:** state department of health (licensure, reportable events), state attorney general (state breach law), local public health, local emergency management / EOC, EMS agency and medical direction, regional trauma / EMS council, fire and police.
- **Accreditor:** The Joint Commission (or DNV / HFAP equivalent). Its standards are copyrighted.
- **Coalition / mutual aid:** regional healthcare coalition (HPP-funded), neighboring hospitals (diversion, transfer agreements), regional medical coordination center.
- **Vendors:** EHR vendor, cloud / hosting provider, interface engine vendor, device manufacturers, MSSP / incident response firm, cyber insurer and breach coach, forensic firm, clearinghouse, blood supplier, pharmacy wholesaler, linen/food, oxygen supplier.
- **ISAC:** Health-ISAC (private membership organization).
- **Media channels:** patient-family Facebook post, nurse's anonymous Reddit post, local TV assignment desk, health reporter email, patient-portal banner, hospital's own X/Instagram, a ransomware group's leak-site post (render as text template, never as a real group's branding), a union statement, a state legislator's post.

## 6. Common operational workflows

- **Downtime activation:** IT declares an unplanned EHR downtime. The house supervisor / AOC is paged, units switch to paper downtime forms, and the BCA read-only viewer is used for MAR and allergies. Lab and radiology go to manual call-back of critical results. Pharmacy switches cabinets to override / manual profile. On recovery, back-entry ("reconciliation") of orders, results and charges.
- **HICS activation:** AOC activates HICS and opens the HCC. IC assigns section chiefs and starts the HICS 201 briefing and HICS 202/203/204. Each operational period ends with a HICS 251 facility system status report.
- **Diversion / ED closure:** ED physician and IC request diversion. The regional EMS / medical control and the healthcare coalition update the bed-status board (e.g. EMResource / Juvare, a common product). Neighboring hospitals absorb, and transfer agreements are invoked under 482.15(b)(7).
- **Ransomware response:** isolate (pull the internet connection, disable VPN, kill accounts), engage the IR firm through counsel for privilege, notify the cyber insurer, and report to the FBI (IC3 / field office) and CISA. Run an OFAC sanctions check before any payment. Communications decide on patient-portal, website and press messaging.
- **Regulatory reporting deadlines:**
  - **HIPAA Breach Notification Rule (verified, eCFR):** individuals "without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 calendar days after discovery of a breach" (45 CFR 164.404(b)). Media notice for a breach "involving more than 500 residents of a State or jurisdiction" on the same 60-day clock (164.406). HHS Secretary: 500 or more individuals on the same clock (164.408(b)); fewer than 500 by log, "not later than 60 days after the end of each calendar year" (164.408(c)). Business associate to covered entity: 60 calendar days after discovery (164.410). Law-enforcement delay: written statement, or an oral statement "no longer than 30 days" (164.412). Ransomware encryption of PHI is presumed a breach unless a low-probability-of-compromise risk assessment shows otherwise (OCR Ransomware Fact Sheet).
  - **CIRCIA (6 U.S.C. 681b):** covered cyber incidents to CISA within 72 h, ransom payments within 24 h. **Not yet in force.** The final rule was sent to OMB 2026-10-01 per trade press (secondary sources, unverified against the Federal Register).
  - **State breach laws and state AG:** vary (unverified per state).
  - **DEA Form 106:** theft or significant loss of controlled substances "within one business day" (21 CFR 1301.76(b); not re-fetched this session).
  - **FDA:** device malfunction reporting is MedWatch / MDR (user-facility reporting of device-related death within 10 working days under 21 CFR 803.30; not re-fetched).
  - **CMS / state survey:** most states expect a call to the state survey agency on facility evacuation or a major service disruption (state-specific, unverified).
- **Forms:** HICS 201/202/203/204/205A/213/214/251/254/255/257, ICS 213RR (resource request), patient tracking (HICS 254), downtime census, OCR breach portal submission, FBI IC3 complaint, DEA 106, MedWatch 3500A.

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Cyber:** Ransomware encrypts the EHR and the interface engine on a Friday night. ED goes on diversion, paper downtime runs past 72 h, and the group posts a sample of patient records on its leak site. (Pattern: ASPR TRACIE _Healthcare System Cybersecurity_ (2022 update) and _Select Lessons Learned_ (TA response, 2022).)
2. **Third-party / supply chain:** A national claims clearinghouse goes offline after a cyberattack. Eligibility checks, prior auth and e-prescribing fail, cash flow stalls for weeks, and OCR breach-notification questions follow. (Pattern: HHS OCR _Change Healthcare Cybersecurity Incident FAQ_, 2024–2025.)
3. **Cyber-physical:** Malware on the clinical network spreads to imaging modalities and an infusion-pump server. Biomed must isolate devices mid-procedure and decide whether to cancel cases. (HICP Tech Vol. 2 practice 9, medical device security.)
4. **Insider:** A recently terminated registration employee's account is still active and is used to look up a celebrity patient's chart. The chart contents appear on social media. (HIPAA privacy plus the 164.404 clock.)
5. **Physical security:** Infant-security alarm at a stairwell exit during a shift change. Code Pink, lockdown and a police response, while CCTV recording has been lost to a storage failure. (FEMA / ASPR TRACIE infant abduction resources; Joint Commission sentinel event, copyrighted.)
6. **Infrastructure failure:** Utility outage plus failure of one of two generators. Bed and ventilator triage, medical gas, a 96-hour fuel question, and patient transfers. (FEMA _Healthcare Facilities and Power Outages_, Aug 2019; 482.15(e).)
7. **Natural hazard:** Hurricane landfall forecast at 48 h. Shelter-in-place vs. partial evacuation of a NICU, 1135 waiver, and coalition patient movement. (482.15(b)(3),(8).)
8. **Mass casualty / mass gathering:** A stadium-adjacent mass casualty incident brings 40 patients in 90 minutes during a partial EHR downtime, and the patient-tracking system is unavailable. (HICS medical surge; ties to the `stadium_venue` pack.)
9. **UAS:** A drone loiters over the helipad and then crashes onto the roof near an air handler intake. Helicopter diversion and a possible-surveillance question. (Ties to `unmanned_systems`.)
10. **Cyber + public information:** A DDoS takes the patient portal and the website down while a false social-media rumor says the hospital "lost all records". The PIO, privacy officer and CISO must align on one message. (HICP main document, threat 3/5; CISA HPH CTEP Module 1.)

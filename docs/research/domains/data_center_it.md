# Domain dossier: `data_center_it`

IT service provider, managed service provider (MSP/MSSP), colocation provider or enterprise data center.
Researched 2026-10-24. Every URL in section 9 was fetched with `curl` on that date; NIST's `nvlpubs` host answers `HEAD` with 404 but `GET` with 206/200, so sizes there come from a ranged `GET` (`Content-Range` total).

## 1. Scope

**Covers**

- **MSP / MSSP**: runs customer IT remotely through RMM, PSA, backup and EDR consoles. One compromise reaches many tenants. CISA describes this "trust relationship" risk in AA22-131A and in CISA Insights (2021).
- **Colocation / wholesale / retail data center**: sells space, power, cooling, cross-connects and remote hands. Tenants own their servers; the operator owns the building, the MEP plant (mechanical, electrical, plumbing), physical security and the meet-me room.
- **Hosting / private cloud / IaaS provider**: runs multi-tenant hypervisor clusters (VMware ESXi / vSphere, Hyper-V, Proxmox, OpenStack), storage, backup-as-a-service and DRaaS.
- **Enterprise data center / corporate IT**: the in-house data center and IT operations of a large company, university or government agency.

**Aliases users might type**: `msp`, `mssp`, `managed services`, `it provider`, `it services`, `colo`, `colocation`, `data center`, `datacenter`, `data centre`, `hosting`, `cloud provider`, `private cloud`, `noc`, `soc`, `enterprise it`, `server room`, `hyperscale`.

**CISA critical infrastructure sectors**

- **Information Technology Sector** (primary). The CISA sector page says the Department of Homeland Security (CISA) is the Sector Risk Management Agency. It describes the sector as "hardware, software, and information technology systems and services".
- **Communications Sector** (secondary): carrier-neutral meet-me rooms, IXPs and fiber. CISA is also the SRMA here.
- **Dependencies**: Energy (utility feed, generator fuel) and Water (evaporative cooling, chillers). Tenants sit in every other sector, so a data center incident cascades. Finance, healthcare and government tenants each bring their own reporting clocks (section 6).
- Not verified this pass: whether the national policy that assigns SRMAs (NSM-22, April 2024, which replaced PPD-21) has since been amended. The CISA sector page still names DHS.

## 3. Systems

### IT and management plane (the "OT" of an MSP)

- **RMM (remote monitoring and management)**: agent-based control of every customer endpoint (examples: ConnectWise Automate, N-able N-central, NinjaOne, Datto RMM, Kaseya VSA). This is the highest-impact system in the domain.
- **PSA / ITSM ticketing**: ConnectWise PSA, Autotask, ServiceNow, Jira Service Management. Holds tickets, SLAs and change records.
- **Remote access / privileged access**: ScreenConnect, TeamViewer, BeyondTrust, CyberArk, jump hosts, bastions, VPN concentrators.
- **Identity**: Active Directory, Entra ID / Okta, MFA, GDAP/delegated admin into customer Microsoft 365 tenants.
- **Virtualization and hyperconverged**: VMware vCenter/ESXi, Hyper-V/SCVMM, Nutanix, Proxmox. ESXi was the target in CISA/FBI AA23-039A (ESXiArgs).
- **Storage**: SAN/NAS arrays, object storage, snapshots and replication. NIST SP 800-209 covers this.
- **Backup / DR / DRaaS**: Veeam, Commvault, Rubrik, Cohesity, Datto BCDR, immutable/air-gapped copies, a DR site.
- **Security tooling**: EDR/XDR (CrowdStrike Falcon, SentinelOne, Defender), SIEM (Splunk, Sentinel), SOAR, email security, vulnerability scanner.
- **Network**: core/edge routers, firewalls, load balancers, DNS/DHCP, IPAM, BGP to transit carriers, DDoS scrubbing.
- **Monitoring / observability**: NMS (PRTG, LogicMonitor, Zabbix), APM, synthetic checks, a public status page (Statuspage, Instatus), on-call paging (PagerDuty, Opsgenie).
- **Business systems**: billing, CRM, customer portal, email/collaboration (Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace), documentation vault (IT Glue, Hudu), password vault.
- **Cloud**: AWS/Azure/GCP accounts and regions the provider resells or depends on.

### Facility / critical-infrastructure systems (OT)

- **Utility service and medium-voltage switchgear**, ATS/STS (automatic / static transfer switches).
- **Standby diesel generators** with fuel day tanks, bulk tanks and fuel-polishing. Fuel delivery contracts.
- **UPS systems** (static or rotary/DRUPS), battery strings (VRLA / Li-ion) and battery monitoring.
- **Power distribution**: PDUs, RPPs, busway, rack PDUs (metered/switched).
- **EPMS / power monitoring** (electrical power monitoring system).
- **Cooling**: chillers, cooling towers, CRAC (computer room air conditioner, DX) and CRAH (computer room air handler, chilled water) units, in-row coolers, rear-door heat exchangers, direct-to-chip liquid cooling and CDUs for AI/GPU halls, economizers.
- **BMS / BAS** (building management / automation, often BACnet or Modbus), **DCIM** (data center infrastructure management: Schneider EcoStruxure, Vertiv, Sunbird, Nlyte).
- **Fire detection and suppression**: VESDA aspirating smoke detection, pre-action sprinkler, clean-agent suppression (FM-200/HFC-227ea, Novec 1230/FK-5-1-12, inert gas IG-541) with abort station. **EPO** (emergency power off) buttons; NFPA 75/70 govern EPO (copyrighted standards, link only).
- **Leak detection**, temperature and humidity sensors.
- **Physical security**: PACS (badge readers, biometrics), mantraps / man-traps, CCTV/VMS, perimeter fencing and bollards, loading dock, visitor management, cage locks.

## 4. Outside parties and media

**Government / law enforcement**

- **CISA**: report@cisa.gov, 1-844-729-2472 (voluntary; mandatory once the CIRCIA final rule takes effect). CISA regional Cybersecurity Advisors and Protective Security Advisors.
- **FBI**: field office cyber squad and IC3. The **U.S. Secret Service** Cyber Fraud Task Force where payment data is involved.
- **Sector regulators of tenants**: SEC (public-company tenants and broker-dealers), HHS OCR (health tenants), FTC, state attorneys general.
- **DoD Cyber Crime Center (DC3) / DIBNet** for DFARS 7012 tenants.
- **FedRAMP PMO** (fedramp_security@fedramp.gov) and agency customers' SOCs.
- **Local agencies**: fire department (suppression discharge, battery fire, generator fuel spill), local emergency management, the electric utility's key-account rep / outage desk, the water utility, and county/state DEQ (fuel spill, generator air permits).

**Commercial**

- Tenants and managed customers, especially the largest and most regulated ones.
- Vendors: RMM/PSA vendor, hypervisor vendor, EDR vendor, backup vendor, UPS / generator / chiller OEM service (field service engineers under maintenance contract), the fuel supplier, the BMS/DCIM integrator.
- Carriers and transit providers, the IXP, the cloud provider (AWS/Azure/GCP).
- The incident response retainer firm, breach coach / outside counsel, the cyber-insurance carrier, the ransomware negotiator, the forensic accountant.
- SOC 2 / ISO auditor; PCI QSA.
- Landlord / REIT and the property manager if the building is leased.

**ISACs and sharing bodies**

- **IT-ISAC**; **MS-ISAC** (for SLTT tenants; its membership model changed in 2025, check current status); **FS-ISAC** and **Health-ISAC** (tenants' sectors); the **Communications ISAC** (NCC). Vendor partner communities (e.g. MSP peer groups, the vendor's security advisories).
- **Data center trade groups**: 7x24 Exchange, AFCOM, Uptime Institute network (membership and copyrighted).

**Media channels** (text-bearing, for injects)

- The provider's own **public status page** and the RSS/email/SMS subscriber feed.
- **Reddit** r/msp and r/sysadmin threads. These surfaced the RMM-supply-chain pattern within hours in 2021.
- **X / Mastodon / Bluesky** infosec researchers posting IOCs; a ransomware group's **leak-site post** (text template, not AI pixels).
- **Trade press**: DataCenterDynamics, Data Center Knowledge, The Register, BleepingComputer, CRN / ChannelE2E.
- **Customer LinkedIn posts** complaining about downtime; a downstream business's Facebook post ("our POS is down because our IT company got hacked").
- **Local TV** for a generator fire, fuel spill or a major regional outage.
- **Analyst inquiries** (Gartner/IDC) and investor relations questions for a public provider.

## 6. Common operational workflows

### Notification chain (typical MSP / colo)

1. **Detection**: NOC alert, SOC alert, customer ticket, vendor advisory, a Reddit post.
2. **Triage**: on-call engineer opens a P1/SEV1 ticket and pages the Major Incident Manager.
3. **Bridge**: the MIM opens the bridge (Teams/Zoom/Slack war room). Out-of-band comms move to Signal or a phone tree if the identity or collaboration platform is suspected.
4. **Escalation**: CISO and CIO; the executive crisis team at SEV1.
5. **Legal**: General Counsel engages outside counsel. Counsel retains the IR firm so the work stays under privilege, and gives the insurer notice. The policy often requires notice "as soon as practicable" and use of panel vendors.
6. **Customers**: status page first post (often within 15–30 minutes of a confirmed outage by internal policy), then customer emails, then named-account calls by the CSMs.
7. **Regulators and government**: through the clocks below.
8. **Hot wash / PIR**: post-incident review, RCA published to customers (often contractually required within 5–10 business days for a major outage).

ICS is uncommon in pure IT shops. A Major Incident Management (ITIL) structure maps onto it: IC = MIM, Ops = technical leads, Planning = scribe/status, Liaison = CSM, PIO = comms. For a facility emergency (fire, fuel spill, flood) the fire department sets up ICS on scene. The data center then fills a liaison / technical specialist role, and ICS 201 / 202 / 205 / 213 / 214 apply.

### Facility workflows

- **Loss of utility**: UPS carries load, ATS transfers to generators within seconds, the EOP checklist runs, fuel burn rate gives "hours of runtime". Call the fuel vendor for priority delivery and the utility for an ETR (estimated time of restoration). Retransfer to utility.
- **Cooling failure**: high-temp alarms (ASHRAE TC 9.9 recommended inlet range 18–27 °C, copyrighted publication) open the hall doors, bring portable spot coolers and shed load. The decision point is to **shut down customer equipment under the contract's emergency clause** before hardware damage.
- **Clean-agent discharge / fire**: evacuate, close the abort window, call the fire department, decide on EPO, then reconstitute.
- **Remote hands / access**: emergency badge revocation, escorted access, chain of custody for seized drives.

### Forms and artifacts

- ICS 201 / 202 / 205 / 213 / 214 when the fire department or emergency management runs ICS. The existing `ics_201_prefill` and `ics_203_prefill` templates cover two of these.
- ITIL: major incident record, change record / emergency change (ECAB approval), problem record and RCA.
- **CISA Incident Reporting System form** (web), **DIBNet Incident Collection Format (ICF)** fields, **SEC 8-K Item 1.05** filing text, **FedRAMP Initial Incident Report** email.
- Facility: EOP checklist, generator run log, UPS event log, fuel delivery ticket, hot-work permit, MOP sign-off sheet, visitor/access log.

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

All exercising organizations are fictional; patterns are public.

1. **Cyber (supply chain via RMM)**: a zero-day in the provider's on-premises RMM server is used to push a ransomware "update" to every managed endpoint of ~40 small-business customers on a holiday Friday. Pattern: CISA-FBI guidance for MSPs after the July 2021 VSA supply-chain ransomware attack; AA22-131A.
2. **Cyber (hypervisor ransomware)**: an unpatched, internet-exposed ESXi management interface lets an affiliate encrypt VM configuration and disks across a multi-tenant hosting cluster. Backups sit on the same vCenter domain. Pattern: CISA/FBI AA23-039A (ESXiArgs); #StopRansomware advisories on ESXi-focused groups.
3. **Infrastructure failure (bad vendor update)**: a faulty EDR content update blue-screens every managed Windows server and workstation. BitLocker recovery keys are in a vault that is itself down; there is no malice, but phishing sites impersonating the vendor appear within hours. Pattern: CISA alert of 2024-07-19 on the CrowdStrike content update.
4. **Infrastructure failure / natural hazard (cooling)**: a record heat day trips two of three chillers. Data Hall 2 inlet temperatures climb past threshold and facilities must decide whether to power down tenant racks under the emergency clause. Pattern: July 2022 London heatwave cooling failures reported by two cloud providers.
5. **Natural hazard (utility loss and fuel)**: a regional ice storm or hurricane causes a multi-day utility outage. Generator 3 fails to start, fuel trucks are diverted to hospitals, and runtime math drives a controlled migration to the DR site. Pattern: 2012 Hurricane Sandy, when lower Manhattan data centers lost basement fuel pumps (public trade reporting; not re-verified this pass).
6. **Insider**: a recently terminated NOC engineer keeps a personal API token for the PSA/RMM and the documentation vault, deletes customer backups and posts screenshots. Badge logs show tailgating into the colo the night before. Pattern: CISA Insider Threat CTEP Situation Manual (Tier A #13); DOJ insider-sabotage prosecutions (generic).
7. **Cyber-physical**: attackers reach the BMS/DCIM through a vendor's remote-support VPN and change CRAH setpoints and alarm thresholds. The hall heats while the NOC dashboard shows green. Pattern: building-automation exposure advisories (CISA ICS advisories on BMS products).
8. **Physical security**: a person in contractor hi-vis talks past the lobby guard as "remote hands", reaches a tenant cage and plants a cellular-modem device in a top-of-rack switch. A tenant later reports exfiltration. Pattern: social-engineering intrusion tests reported in trade press; PACS / mantrap controls.
9. **Infrastructure failure (cloud region outage)**: the hyperscaler region hosting the provider's PSA, identity provider and status page goes down for 9 hours. The provider cannot open tickets, log in to the RMM or post a status update. Pattern: repeated public cloud-region post-incident reports.
10. **Cyber (identity / help desk)**: a voice-phishing call to the service desk resets a privileged engineer's MFA. The attacker uses delegated admin (GDAP) into 12 customers' Microsoft 365 tenants for email theft and BEC. Pattern: CISA/FBI advisories on help-desk social engineering (2023, Scattered Spider).

Threat families for the profile: `cyber` (default), `infrastructure_failure`, `insider`, `natural_hazard`, `cyber_physical`, `physical_security`. `uas_drone` and `mass_gathering` do not fit, except a drone over a hyperscale campus as a physical-security variant.

Decision-point examples for the MSEL author

- Shut down the RMM and remote-access platform for every customer, which stops managed service, or keep it up to push remediation.
- Pay or refuse the ransom; restore from backups of unknown integrity.
- Power down tenant equipment in a hot hall under the emergency clause (SLA credits and tenant fury) or risk hardware loss.
- Declare a material incident for the 8-K clock now, or wait for forensics.
- Fail over to the DR site and accept data loss up to the RPO.

Clue kinds: EDR telemetry, RMM audit log entries ("script pushed to all sites"), new admin accounts, vCenter task logs, BMS setpoint-change log, badge-reader log, a backup job failure report, a Reddit thread.

Hurdle kinds: backups encrypted, vault or identity provider down, BitLocker keys unavailable, generator fail-to-start, fuel delivery delayed, vendor support queue at 6 hours, an expired support contract.

Key coordination: tenants/customers and the upstream vendor (RMM/EDR/hypervisor/utility/fuel); CISA/FBI for cyber; the fire department for facility emergencies.

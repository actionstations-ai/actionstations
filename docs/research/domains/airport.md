## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** the operator of a commercial-service airport holding a 14 CFR Part 139 Airport Operating Certificate (Class I–IV) and a TSA-approved Airport Security Program under 49 CFR 1542. That covers city/county aviation departments, airport authorities, port authorities that run airports, and state-run airports. Tenants (airlines, FBOs, concessions, ground handlers) are outside parties, not the exercising org.
- **Aliases users might type:** airport, commercial airport, Part 139 airport, aviation authority, airport authority, aviation department, international airport, regional airport, hub airport, terminal, airfield, AOC.
- **CISA sector:** Transportation Systems Sector, Aviation mode. **SRMA:** DHS (TSA) and DOT, co-SRMAs for Transportation Systems. FAA is the safety regulator (Part 139); TSA is the security regulator (49 CFR 1542).
- Overlaps: Emergency Services (ARFF, airport police), Communications (radio/911), Energy (fuel farm). A large-hub airport may also be a port authority. The `port_maritime` profile covers the seaport side.

## 3. Systems

**IT:** AODB (Airport Operational Database), resource management system (gates, stands, belts), FIDS/BIDS/GIDS, CUPPS/CUSS (common-use passenger processing and self-service kiosks: real examples SITA, Amadeus, Materna), airport website and app, email and identity (Entra/AD), helpdesk/ITSM, SIEM/SOC, Wi-Fi, parking revenue control, lease/billing, GIS, the badging system's identity management (IDMS), and the radio/dispatch CAD.

**OT / building / airfield:** BHS SCADA and PLCs (inline EDS for checked bags), PACS / ACAMS (access control and alarm monitoring), CCTV / VMS, airfield lighting control and monitoring system (ALCMS / ALCS), fuel farm and hydrant SCADA (often tenant-operated by a fuel consortium), passenger boarding bridges and pre-conditioned air / 400 Hz ground power, BAS/HVAC, power distribution and standby generators, vertical transport (elevators, escalators, moving walks), PA and visual paging, UAS detection sensors (where installed), fire alarm.

**Identifiers these documents state (fact categories):**

- Runways ("Runway 16L/34R") ✔ already in profile

- **Baggage carousels / claim devices** ("Carousel 7"), **BHS lines / EDS units** ("EDS 3", "Make-up Belt M4"): candidate `bhs_units`

- **ARFF stations and vehicles** ("ARFF 1", "Rescue 3"): low-lint candidate
- **Airport identifier** (three-letter IATA / four-letter ICAO, e.g. a fictional "KXYZ"): needed in NOTAMs

## 4. Outside parties and media

- **Federal:** TSA (FSD, Transportation Security Operations Center, Compliance inspectors), FAA (ATCT, Airports District Office, Part 139 certification inspector, Regional Operations Center), CBP (FIS / international arrivals), FBI (JTTF, cyber task force, field office), CISA (Central, regional cybersecurity advisor, protective security advisor), NTSB (accidents), FEMA region, USDA Wildlife Services, NWS.
- **State/local:** local police and fire (mutual aid under the AEP), county/city emergency management and EOC, the 911 PSAP, hospitals (139.325(c)(2)), medical examiner, state aviation office, National Guard.
- **Tenants:** airlines (station managers, corporate SOCs, system operations control), ground handlers, the fuel consortium, FBOs, concessionaires, rental car companies, cargo operators.
- **Vendors:** AODB/FIDS/CUPPS vendors, BHS integrator, PACS vendor, ALCMS vendor, MSP / MSSP, insurance carrier and breach coach (cyber).
- **ISAC:** Aviation ISAC (A-ISAC); MS-ISAC/EI-ISAC for city or county-run airports.
- **Media channels:** passenger posts on X/Instagram/TikTok, a community Facebook group, the local TV assignment desk, aviation press and bloggers, airline customer-service accounts, flight-tracking sites, the airport's own app push notifications.

## 6. Common operational workflows

- **Alerting and notification:** ATCT crash phone or alert → ARFF and the AOC → AEP notification list (police, EMS, hospitals, airlines, FAA, TSA, NTSB for accidents) → ICS activation. The AOC issues NOTAMs through the FAA NOTAM system (NOTAM Manager / FNS) for closures and outages (139.339, AC 150/5200-28G).
- **ICS:** Airport IC from Operations or ARFF depending on incident type, then unified command with police and fire. Airport EOC plus a JIC for the PIO. HSEEP and NIMS apply.
- **Cyber incident reporting:**
  - **TSA (aviation):** under the ASP amendments, airport operators report cybersecurity incidents to CISA as soon as practicable and **no later than 24 hours** after identification (law-firm summaries of the Dec 2021 ASP changes; the amendment text is SSI). **(Unverified against primary text, which is not public.)** Note that TSA moved the rail SDs from 24 h to 72 h in October 2025 (see `freight_rail.md`). The aviation window may differ now. **(Unverified.)**
  - **CIRCIA:** NPRM published 4 Apr 2024 (FR doc 2024-06526, verified via the Federal Register API): 72 h for covered incidents, 24 h for ransom payments. **Final rule not yet published as of Oct 2026** (secondary reporting: sent to OMB 1 Oct 2026, **unverified**).
  - **TSA security incidents:** report to TSA per the ASP (1542.307), to the TSOC. **(Unverified detail.)**
  - **FAA:** runway incursions, aircraft accidents (NTSB 49 CFR 830 "immediate" notification for accidents and listed incidents).
  - State breach-notification law if passenger PII leaks. Payment card data falls under PCI DSS contracts (not law).
- **Forms:** ICS 201 (incident briefing), 202 (objectives), 203 (organization), 205 (radio plan), 213 (general message), 214 (activity log). FAA Form 7460-1 is not incident-related. NTSB Form 6120.1 (operator accident report) is the airline's. Airport self-inspection checklist (AC 150/5200-18D sample). NOTAM text in ICAO/FAA domestic format.
- **Time pressures to model:** a gate hold or ground stop decision within minutes; the TSA reporting clock; the airline's decision to cancel a bank of flights; the media "going live in 15".

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Cyber / ransomware:** Ransomware encrypts the airport's AODB, FIDS and Wi-Fi back end during a holiday peak. Common-use kiosks fall back to manual check-in and baggage tags are handwritten. _Pattern: the 2024 ransomware attack on a West Coast port-authority-run airport, publicly reported; the FEMA 2023 cyber planning guide covers the cascading effects._
2. **Cyber-physical:** The BHS SCADA network is compromised from a vendor remote-access path, and the inline EDS stops sorting. Bags pile up, TSA faces a screen-or-hold decision for checked bags, and the decision on whether to fly without bags lands on the table.
3. **Insider:** A badged contractor uses a cloned credential to open a SIDA door. CCTV shows a second person tailgating to the ramp. Badge revocation and audit, ASC/TSA reporting, airline crew concern.
4. **UAS:** Repeated drone sightings over the approach end of a runway at dusk. ATCT suspends arrivals, and a detection system shows a track but no Remote ID broadcast. Who can do what (FAA, FBI, local police; the 2020 interagency advisory and the 2026 SAFER SKIES rule). _CertAlert 21-04 requires AEP UAS instructions._
5. **Infrastructure failure:** A utility feeder fault plus a standby-generator transfer failure darkens the movement-area lighting at night (AEP item 139.325(b)(8)) and half a concourse. NOTAMs, diversions and passengers stranded in the terminal follow.
6. **Physical security:** Unattended bag at a checkpoint queue during a bank. A partial terminal evacuation and the bomb-incident procedure (139.325(b)(2)) run while a hoax threat posts on social media.
7. **Natural hazard:** A forecast ice storm then a power-loss cascade. Overnight stranded passengers (tarmac delay rules are the airline's), snow-and-ice plan, shelter in place.
8. **Mass gathering:** A major event (finals, festival) doubles departing passengers. A coordinated disinformation post about a "security breach" drives crowd surges at checkpoints.
9. **Third-party IT outage:** A global airline-IT or endpoint-agent update failure takes down common-use systems for several carriers at once (the July 2024 global endpoint-agent outage pattern, publicly reported). The question is whose problem it is, and whether the airport comms carry airline messages.

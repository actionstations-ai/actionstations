# Domain dossier: `unmanned_systems` (UAS operator or university drone program)

Researched 2026-10-24. Every file URL in §9 was fetched in full on that date; size, page count, title and date come from the downloaded PDF, and each file was text-searched for copyright / SSI / FOUO / TLP markings. Claims marked **(unverified)** were not checked against a primary source.

> **Fetch note:** faa.gov, cisa.gov, dhs.gov, govinfo.gov and congress.gov all served to plain `curl` with a browser User-Agent on 2026-10-24.

## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** an organization that flies UAS: a commercial Part 107 operator (inspection, survey, media, delivery), a public safety drone program (police/fire DFR, under Part 107 or a public-aircraft COA), a utility or infrastructure owner's in-house fleet, or a **university drone program** (research lab, flight test range, curriculum). The org may be both an **operator** (its own flights can go wrong or be hijacked) and a **defender** (it hosts or detects UAS over its sites).
- **Aliases:** UAS, UAV, drone, drone operator, drone program, sUAS, Part 107 operator, flight test range, UAS lab, DFR (drone as first responder), BVLOS operator, university drone program, counter-UAS.
- **CISA sector:** no single sector. Operators fall under Transportation Systems (Aviation) for airspace; universities under Government Facilities (Education Facilities subsector); public safety programs under Emergency Services. **SRMA:** DHS/TSA and DOT for aviation; ED and DHS for education facilities **(education SRMA unverified)**. The FAA is the regulator; DOJ/DHS (and, since 2026, certified SLTT agencies) hold counter-UAS authority.
- **Boundaries:** the airport-side drone response belongs to `airport`. This domain is about the UAS program itself and its sites.

## 3. Systems

**IT:** fleet management and flight logging (e.g. DroneDeploy, Aloft, AirData; real names allowed as examples), mission planning, LAANC / B4UFLY-type authorization clients, Remote ID compliance records, the vendor cloud (telemetry and imagery sync), data processing (photogrammetry, LiDAR), evidence management (public safety), email/identity, ITSM, SIEM, the university research data store.

**OT / aircraft side:** UAS airframes and flight controllers (autopilots such as PX4/ArduPilot-based or proprietary), firmware and companion computers, **ground control stations (GCS)**, C2 data links (2.4/5.8 GHz, LTE/5G, satellite), video downlinks, GNSS receivers (and RTK base stations), Remote ID broadcast modules, batteries and chargers, docking stations / drone-in-a-box for DFR, ADS-B In receivers, payloads (EO/IR, LiDAR, multispectral, drop mechanisms). On the defense side: **UAS detection systems** (RF detection, radar, EO/IR, acoustic, Remote ID receivers). Mitigation (jamming, spoofing, takeover) is federally restricted; see §5.

**Identifiers (fact categories):** profile already has Flight sites/test areas, Airframes (tail/serial numbers), Ground control stations, Radio links/frequencies, Payloads/sensors, Airspace/LAANC ceilings, Hangars/labs. Candidate additions:

- **FAA registration numbers** (format "FA" + 8 alphanumerics, e.g. "FA3ABC1234"; **format unverified**), `token`
- **Remote ID serial numbers** (ANSI/CTA-2063-A serials), low-lint
- **Waivers / COAs** ("Waiver 107W-2026-01234"; **format unverified**)
- **NOTAM / TFR numbers** (e.g. "6/1234"), useful with `regulatory_notice`
- **Mission IDs / flight numbers**

- **State/local:** local police (and, since 1 Jul 2026, possibly a **certified SLTT C-UAS agency**), fire, county EM, the state aviation office, the fusion center, neighboring landowners, municipal parks (where takeoffs are regulated locally).
- **University-specific:** the Office of Research, the IRB (imagery of people), export control, risk management and insurance, athletics (stadium overflight requests), campus police.
- **Vendors:** airframe manufacturer, autopilot/GCS vendor, fleet software vendor, detection vendor, the cellular carrier (LTE links).
- **ISAC:** no dedicated UAS ISAC found. The A-ISAC (aviation), REN-ISAC (higher education) and MS-ISAC (public safety) cover parts.
- **Media:** a hobbyist forum and Reddit drone subs, a neighbor's Nextdoor post about "drones spying", local TV, the student newspaper, a YouTube crash video, an aviation safety blogger.

## 6. Common operational workflows

- **Flyaway / lost link:** RPIC applies the lost-link procedure (RTH / land) → notifies ATC if in controlled airspace, or the controlling facility named in the authorization **(unverified detail)** → notifies the DFO and ASO → searches and recovers → **107.9 report within 10 days** if the injury or property thresholds are met (verified) → NTSB notification for accidents meeting its criteria **(unverified)**.
- **Suspected compromise of the fleet** (malicious firmware, vendor cloud breach, GNSS spoofing): ground the fleet → preserve logs and GCS images → contact the vendor → report to CISA (voluntary unless CIRCIA, once final, covers the entity) and the FBI → assess data exposure (privacy, IRB, sponsor).
- **Rogue drone over our site** (from CISA Suspicious UAS Activity guidance, verified): observe and document (location, time, description, the operator's location, Remote ID data if received) → call local law enforcement and report to the FAA → do **not** attempt mitigation (federal law) → if a downed UAS is found, follow CISA's safe-handling guidance (treat as potentially hazardous, isolate, do not power on or move, preserve evidence).
- **Authorization:** LAANC or DroneZone airspace authorization; waivers; TFR checks (stadiums, VIP movements, wildfires). NOTAMs for operations where required.
- **Forms:** ICS 201/213/214 for larger incidents; the FAA DroneZone 107.9 accident report (online); internal flight log and pre-flight checklist; chain-of-custody form for a downed UAS; NTSB 6120.1 **(applicability to UAS unverified)**.

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Fleet firmware compromise:** A vendor-pushed firmware update bricks or alters several aircraft mid-mission. Ground the fleet? Data sync to an overseas cloud is suspected. _Grounded in the CISA/FBI Jan 2024 guidance on PRC-manufactured UAS (verified)._
2. **GNSS spoofing / C2 link jamming:** During a BVLOS-waivered inspection, an aircraft drifts off course and the RTH heads to a spoofed home point near a highway.
3. **Flyaway over a crowd:** A university demo flight at a football tailgate loses link and the aircraft crashes in a parking lot. There is an injury, a viral video and a 107.9 report clock.
4. **Rogue drones over the test range / campus:** Repeated night overflights of a sensitive research lab. Detection shows no Remote ID, and law enforcement asks what the detection system is legally allowed to collect. _Grounded in the CISA Suspicious UAS Activity guidance and the 2020 interagency advisory (both verified)._
5. **Downed unknown UAS with a payload:** A drone with an attached package lands on a building roof. Bomb squad, safe-handling, evidence. _CISA Safe Handling Considerations (verified)._
6. **Insider / data exfiltration:** A visiting researcher copies flight logs and payload imagery of a defense-sponsored project to a personal drive. Export control and sponsor notification follow.
7. **Mass gathering:** The program supports police DFR at a festival. A TFR is in effect, an unauthorized drone enters it, and the new SLTT C-UAS authority (SAFER SKIES IFR) and its limits come into play.
8. **Ransomware at the operator:** Fleet management and flight records are encrypted. Can we prove compliance (Remote ID, pilot currency) to the FSDO? Customer missions are cancelled.
9. **Natural hazard:** The program is requested to fly post-tornado damage assessment. Emergency COA / SGI request, coordination with responders' aircraft, a battery logistics shortage.
10. **Infrastructure failure:** The cellular network carrying LTE C2 links fails across the county during DFR operations. Fallback to radio links, VLOS limits.

# Domain dossier: `port_maritime` (port authority and marine terminal)

Researched 2026-10-24. Every file URL in §9 was fetched in full on that date; size, page count, title and date come from the downloaded PDF, and each file was text-searched for copyright / SSI / FOUO / TLP markings. Claims marked **(unverified)** were not checked against a primary source.

> **Fetch note:** dco.uscg.mil and fema.gov return HTTP 403 to plain `curl`. They serve normally with browser-like headers (`User-Agent`, `Accept`, `Accept-Language`, `Sec-Fetch-Mode: navigate`). www.uscg.mil and govinfo.gov serve with any client. USCG "Portals" URLs often carry a `?ver=` cache-buster; the files below resolve without it.

## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** a port authority (landlord or operating port) and the marine terminal operators on it (container, break-bulk, ro-ro, liquid bulk, cruise). That means any facility that must keep a Facility Security Plan under **33 CFR part 105** (MTSA), plus the port authority's own police, harbor master and IT.
- **Aliases:** port, seaport, harbor/harbour, port authority, marine terminal, container terminal, cruise terminal, maritime, terminal operator, waterfront facility, MTSA facility, inland river port.
- **CISA sector:** Transportation Systems Sector, Maritime mode. **SRMA:** DHS (TSA and USCG; the USCG is lead for maritime security) and DOT (MARAD). The USCG Captain of the Port (COTP) is the regulator and first responder for security incidents.
- Overlaps: Chemical (liquid bulk), Energy (fuel terminals), Emergency Services, Commercial Facilities (cruise). The 33 CFR part 104 (vessels) and part 106 (OCS) are out of scope except as outside parties.

## 3. Systems

**IT:** Terminal Operating System (TOS; examples: Navis N4, Tideworks, CyberLogitec OPUS), Port Community System, gate appointment and truck reservation systems, gate OCR / RFID and kiosks, berth scheduling, billing and tariff, vessel traffic information, email/identity, ITSM, SIEM, CBP-facing systems (ACE interfaces). TWIC reader back end (33 CFR 101 subpart F/G readers at risk-group-A facilities, **unverified** scope).

**OT:** Ship-to-shore (STS) crane PLCs, drives and crane management systems, including vendor remote access (the subject of the USCG 2025 "Chinese cranes" special edition); rubber-tyred / rail-mounted gantries and automated stacking cranes; straddle carriers; reefer monitoring; shore power; liquid bulk terminal SCADA (tank gauging, valves, pipeline transfers); PACS and gate barriers; CCTV/VMS (land- and waterside); AIS receivers and radar; harbor lighting; fuel; fire suppression; port electrical substations.

**Identifiers (fact categories):** profile already has Berths, Piers/terminals, Cranes, Truck gates, Yard blocks. Candidate additions:

- **Vessels by name / IMO number / voyage**: low-lint (names vary)
- **Anchorages** ("Anchorage 9"), **channels / reaches** ("Main Channel", "Reach 3")
- **Container IDs** (ISO 6346: four letters + seven digits, e.g. `ABCU 123456 7`), useful in TOS injects; `token` shape
- **TWIC reader / access points** ("Gate 4 Lane 2")
- **Tanks** ("Tank 12") for liquid bulk
- **MARSEC Level** (1/2/3), a state value rather than an id

## 6. Common operational workflows

- **Security incident:** detection → FSO → **NRC (1-800-424-8802) without delay** (33 CFR 101.305; NVIC 02-24 CH1) and the local COTP → MARSEC level change if the COTP sets one (101.300: the COTP communicates changes; facilities confirm attainment).
- **Cyber incident:** CySO/IT → for reportable cyber incidents, NRC "without delay" (33 CFR 101.620, verified) — or, for entities covered by 33 CFR 6.16-1, immediate reporting to FBI, CISA and the COTP (NVIC 02-24 CH1, verified). CIRCIA, once final, would add a 72 h report to CISA (NPRM; see `airport.md` §6).
- **COTP orders:** the COTP may issue Captain of the Port orders (movement restrictions, port closure, safety/security zones). A USCG Marine Safety Information Bulletin (MSIB) or Broadcast Notice to Mariners announces them.
- **ICS:** USCG-led unified command (USCG uses ICS; the Incident Management Handbook is an HTML/PDF publication, not fetched). Port EOC, JIC with the USCG PA.
- **Hazmat/oil:** NRC notification, the facility response plan, Area Contingency Plan.
- **Forms:** ICS 201/202/203/204/205/213/214; NRC report record; CG-2692 (Report of Marine Casualty, for vessels; **unverified number**); MARSEC attainment confirmation.

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Ransomware on the TOS:** The terminal operating system and gate OCR are encrypted mid-shift. Trucks queue onto city streets, and vessel stowage plans become unavailable. _Pattern: CGCYBER Maritime Cyber Alert 01-26 on INC Ransom targeting the MTS (verified); public 2023 attacks on Australian and Japanese container terminals._
2. **Crane OT, cyber-physical:** An anomalous outbound connection is found from STS crane management servers to the OEM's remote-access endpoint. Do we stop crane operations with a vessel half-worked? _Pattern: USCG 2025 CTIME special edition on Chinese-manufactured STS cranes (verified)._
3. **Insider / cargo crime:** A TOS user account is used to change container release status, and a drayage driver picks up a box with a valid-looking pin. _Pattern: drug-smuggling cases where terminal IT was manipulated at European ports (public reporting, **unverified URL**)._
4. **GPS/AIS spoofing:** Pilots report GNSS interference and AIS ghost targets in the channel at night. The COTP considers a movement restriction while two deep-draft vessels are inbound.
5. **Physical security:** A stowaway or unauthorized waterside approach to the cruise terminal on embarkation day. MARSEC 2 is set and passenger screening slows.
6. **UAS:** Drones repeatedly overfly the liquid-bulk tank farm and the cruise berth. Who has authority under the 2026 SAFER SKIES rule (state/local certified agencies), the USCG and the FBI?
7. **Natural hazard:** A hurricane with port condition ZULU forecast. Departure decisions, crane tie-downs, reefer power, and a post-storm channel survey before reopening.
8. **Infrastructure failure:** A substation fault takes down shore power and reefer racks during a heat wave. Spoiled cargo claims and a generator fuel shortage follow.
9. **Hazmat:** A container with a mis-declared lithium-battery cargo smokes in the yard. Fire department, isolation distance, vessel at berth.
10. **Supply-chain cyber:** A Port Community System vendor breach leaks shipping data and phishing follows. _Pattern: CGCYBER Maritime Cyber Bulletins (verified list on uscg.mil/MaritimeCyber)._

# Domain dossier: `generic` (CORE pack)

Researched 2026-10-24. Every URL in §9 was fetched during this session. Files on fema.gov answer `curl` with HTTP 403 (bot screening), but a browser-style fetch (WebFetch) downloads them; the page counts and dates below come from `pdfinfo`/`pdftotext` on the downloaded copies. cisa.gov, nist.gov, preptoolkit.fema.gov and training.fema.gov answer `curl -I` normally. **A bundler that fetches fema.gov must send a browser User-Agent and accept-headers, or ship the file inside the pack** (the pack format already hashes the files).

---

## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** any organization that uses the core HSEEP/NIMS method without a domain profile: local governments and agencies, businesses, nonprofits, schools, and any project whose documents describe the domain themselves. The core pack is also the **shared base layer** for every domain pack. HSEEP, NIMS/ICS, CPG 101, CSF 2.0, CTEP templates and the federal incident-reporting picture apply to every sector.
- **Aliases users might type:** other, organization, general, any, business, office, company, agency, nonprofit, enterprise, corporate, HQ.
- **CISA sector:** none specifically. Cross-sector across all 16 critical infrastructure sectors (PPD-21, as amended by NSM-22, April 2024). Sector risk management agency: whichever one the documents name. CISA is the national coordinator for critical infrastructure security and resilience.

## 3. Systems

No built-in identifier categories (the project's documents remain the source). Generic systems by category:

- **IT:** identity provider and directory (Active Directory / Entra ID, Okta), email and collaboration (Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace, Teams/Slack), endpoint protection / EDR, SIEM / SOC, backup and recovery (immutable or offline copies), virtualization (VMware ESXi / Hyper-V), file shares, ERP / finance (SAP, Oracle, Workday), HR and payroll, CRM, website and CMS, VPN and remote access, phone system / VoIP, a mass-notification system (Everbridge, Rave, AlertMedia), helpdesk / ITSM (ServiceNow, Jira SM), cloud tenants (AWS/Azure/GCP).
- **Facility / OT:** building automation (BAS/BMS), PACS badge access, CCTV/VMS, fire alarm panel, elevators, generator / UPS, HVAC.
- **Identifier categories that could be added without lint risk** (lint: false, as with the existing `areas`): `buildings` ("Building 3", "Bldg A"), `floors` ("Floor 4"), `sites` ("Site 2", "Campus North"). Recommendation: add `buildings` only, with lint off, because generic documents often state building numbers and flagging them would be noisy.

## 6. Common operational workflows

**ICS activation:** first-on-scene incident briefing (**ICS 201**), then objectives (**ICS 202**), organization (**ICS 203**/**207**), assignments (**ICS 204**), communications plan (**ICS 205/205A**), medical plan (**ICS 206**), safety message (**ICS 208**), status summary (**ICS 209**), messages (**ICS 213**), resource requests (**ICS 213RR**), activity log (**ICS 214**), planning worksheet (**ICS 215/215A**). The forms are v3/v3.1 in the FEMA 502-2 booklet (Sept 2010 edition; the PDFs were rebuilt in 2017–2018 as fillable AcroForms).

**EOC activation levels:** the NIMS 3rd ed. (2017) EOC activation levels are 3 Normal Operations/Steady-State, 2 Enhanced Steady-State/Partial Activation, and 1 Full Activation.

**Federal cyber reporting (cross-sector):**

- **CIRCIA (6 U.S.C. §681 et seq.):** statute: report a covered cyber incident within **72 hours** after the entity reasonably believes it occurred, and a ransom payment within **24 hours**. NPRM published 4 April 2024 (89 FR 23644; comments closed 3 July 2024). **Status as of 24 Oct 2026:** CISA's CIRCIA page says it "continues to work on the final rule" and that reporting "will not be required until the CIRCIA final rule goes into effect." Secondary reports (Security.io, 5 Oct 2026; Paubox) say the final rule went to OMB/OIRA review on 1 Oct 2026. _That OIRA submission is unverified against reginfo.gov._ Exercises in late 2026 should treat CIRCIA as **not yet in force**; a forward-looking scenario can play it as "the rule took effect last month."
- **Voluntary reporting now:** CISA at https://www.cisa.gov/report, report@cisa.gov, 1-844-729-2472; FBI field office or IC3.
- **CISA Federal Incident Notification Guidelines** (effective 1 Apr 2017): federal civilian agencies report within **1 hour**; for others they are voluntary. The impact taxonomy is useful for injects: functional impact (No Impact … Denial of Critical Services/Loss of Control), information impact (… Core Credential Compromise, Destruction of Critical System), recoverability (Regular / Supplemented / Extended / Not Recoverable), and attack vectors (Attrition, Web, Email/Phishing, External/Removable Media, Impersonation/Spoofing, Improper Usage, Loss or Theft of Equipment, Other).
- **Ransomware payments:** OFAC advisory (21 Sept 2021): the sanctions risk falls on the payer, and prompt reporting to the government is a mitigating factor.
- **Public companies:** SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05, within **4 business days** after the incident is determined to be material (17 CFR 229.106; effective 18 Dec 2023).
- **State breach laws:** all 50 states. Deadlines range from "without unreasonable delay" to fixed windows of 30–60 days (for example, Colorado and Florida 30 days). _Lists of individual state deadlines are copyrighted law-firm material; link only._

**Forms beyond ICS:** HSEEP Exercise Evaluation Guides (EEGs, by core capability), Participant Feedback Form, AAR/IP, Master Task List, MSEL (.xls), Extent of Play Agreement.

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Ransomware with data theft:** phishing of an admin, encryption of file servers and ESXi hosts, a leak-site post 48 h later threatening to publish the HR files (CISA Ransomware CTEP; #StopRansomware advisories).
2. **Business email compromise and vendor-payment fraud:** a lookalike-domain invoice change diverts a large payment; the bank recall window is closing (FBI IC3 annual report: BEC is the costliest category).
3. **Third-party SaaS or MSP compromise:** the remote-management vendor pushes a malicious update to every endpoint (CISA Vendor Supply Chain Compromise CTEP; 2021 RMM supply-chain pattern).
4. **Telecom and identity outage:** the SSO provider and the carrier are both down, so staff cannot log in or be reached (CI Fortify isolation and recovery CTEP, 2026).
5. **Insider data exfiltration:** a departing employee syncs the customer database to personal cloud storage, and a later tip says the data is being sold (Insider Threat CTEP).
6. **Bomb threat by email to multiple sites at once,** paired with a swatting call (Bomb Threat CTEP 2025; CISA/FBI bomb-threat guidance).
7. **Severe weather / tornado** strikes headquarters during business hours; staff accountability, a damaged data closet, COOP relocation (Tornado CTEP).
8. **Active assailant in the lobby,** followed by reunification and media (CISA active-shooter CTEPs; FEMA reunification ESK).
9. **Deepfake executive voice** on a call to finance, asking for an urgent transfer and a password reset (FBI PSA I-051525-PSA on AI voice impersonation, May 2025 — _PSA number unverified_).
10. **Building systems compromise:** the BAS contractor's VPN account is used to disable HVAC in the server room; the room overheats and systems shut down (cyber-physical convergence).

Examples are active shooter by venue type, bomb threat (2025), IED/VBIED, UAS (airport, stadium, multi-option), civil unrest, insider, hazmat, freight-rail explosion, tornado, wildfire, hurricane/flood/winter supply chain, crowd surge, cruise ship and election security.
  - **Cyber-Physical Convergence** page: https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/cyber-physical-convergence-scenarios. **No packages are listed there** as of this date; it links back to the program page.
  - Package documents (8 PDFs): https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/ctep-package-documents
- **Formats:** SitMans are DOCX, built to be edited ("[Enter Organization Name]", `<Exercise Date>`). The package documents are PDF. Older (2023-01) physical CTEPs do not have the 2024 TLP handling page.
- **Public-domain status:** federal publication with no copyright notice, TLP:CLEAR by default. The handling page offers TLP:GREEN/AMBER/RED blocks for the _filled-in_ exercise, and the user deletes the ones that do not apply. A bundled blank is TLP:CLEAR. _Not verified:_ who owns the cover photos.
- **Domain mapping (for the other packs):** Critical Manufacturing / ICS → `industrial`; Communications → `telecom`; Information Technology / Vendor Supply Chain → `data_center_it`; Financial Services → `financial_services`; Ransomware / Insider / Executives / CI Fortify → `generic`.

### FEMA Exercise Starter Kits (ESKs)

- **Not bundle-able as of this check.** The ESKs live in the Emergency Management Toolkit of FEMA's **Preparedness Toolkit** (https://preptoolkit.fema.gov) and, per FEMA and secondary sources, **require an approved PrepToolkit account** to download. FEMA's old landing page (`fema.gov/.../exercises/starter-kits`) returns 404. The Jan 2024 release added 10 kits under the 2023–2026 NEP Principals' Exercise Priorities: climate resilience, climate adaptation, complex incident coordination, large-scale population movement, social isolation/loneliness, opioid crisis, supply chain resilience, recovery, community profile and reunification. Other sources list critical-infrastructure cybersecurity, IPAWS alert and warning, and large-scale events kits. **The topic list is unverified against the toolkit itself.** Each kit contains a facilitator and evaluator guide, conduct slides, a situation manual and a customizable placemat.
- **Public copies exist for some older DHS ESKs** (for example, the _DHS ESK Cyber Breach Facilitator Guide_ for academic campuses, hosted on schoolsafety.gov: https://www.schoolsafety.gov/sites/default/files/2023-06/4.%20DHS%20ESK%20Cyber%20Breach%20Facilitator%20Guide_508-v2.docx). _Not fetched or size-checked._ They could join a `campus` pack.

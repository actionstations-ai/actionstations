# Domain dossier: `k12_schools` (new: school district)

Researched 2026-10-24. Every URL in §9 was fetched in this session with a ranged GET (HTTP 206); the type and byte size come from `Content-Range`. Page counts, dates and public-domain statements come from `pdfinfo`/`pdftotext` on downloaded copies. `rems.ed.gov` **does not resolve** (DNS failure on 2026-10-24); the REMS K-12 guide is served from `www.ed.gov/media/document/...`. `www.ed.gov` returns 403 to `curl -I` but 206 to a browser-style ranged GET.

## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** public school districts (local education agencies, LEAs), charter networks, county offices / educational service agencies (ESAs, BOCES, intermediate units), private and parochial schools, and individual elementary, middle and high schools, plus district transportation (school buses), nutrition services and athletics. Higher education stays in `campus`.
- **Aliases users might type:** school district, K-12, K12, school, public schools, ISD, USD, unified school district, LEA, charter school, elementary school, middle school, high school, academy, BOCES, ESA, county office of education, school board.
- **CISA sector:** **Government Facilities Sector, Education Facilities Subsector**. SRMA for Government Facilities: CISA and GSA (FPS); the **Department of Education** leads the Education Facilities subsector partnership. _Subsector SRMA wording not re-fetched this session._
- Related federal programs: ED Office of Safe and Supportive Schools (REMS TA Center), DHS/CISA School Safety Task Force, USSS National Threat Assessment Center (NTAC), DOJ COPS (school resource officers), FEMA (EOP planning).

## 6. Common operational workflows

**Standard response protocol**: many districts use five named actions (Hold / Secure / Lockdown / Evacuate / Shelter, from the "I Love U Guys" Foundation SRP; copyrighted program, name only) or the REMS functional annexes (lockdown, shelter-in-place, evacuation, reverse evacuation). The principal or SRO initiates via PA, panic app or radio; the district EOC is notified; police arrive and assume unified command.

**Threat to a school** (social-media post or bystander report):

1. Tip arrives (state tip line, SRO, parent, Snapchat screenshot to an administrator).
2. Immediate safety assessment: imminent danger → 911 and lockdown/secure; otherwise threat assessment team convenes (same day).
3. Law enforcement identifies the poster; FERPA 99.36 disclosure as needed.
4. Decision on school operations (normal / heightened presence / closure) by the Superintendent with police.
5. Parent message via mass notification, typically before the evening news cycle; rumor control on social media.
   (CISA/USSS _K-12 Bystander Reporting Toolkit_ 2026; CISA _Social Media Threat Guidance for School Staff and Authorities_ 2023; CISA _K-12 Anonymized Threat Response Guidance_ 2024.)

**Family reunification**: students moved to the reunification site (another school, church, arena), parents directed to a separate check-in, ID verification against SIS emergency contacts, "request" and "release" gates, unaccompanied students tracked, media staged away from the site.

**Cyber incident** (ransomware or SIS compromise): IT detects / vendor notifies → CTO and ISO activate IR plan → isolate, preserve → cyber insurer / breach coach / DFIR → FBI field office and CISA (report@cisa.gov) → MS-ISAC / K12 SIX → state education agency notice (several states require it; _timelines vary_) → decide on closures if phones, HVAC, attendance or bus routing are down → parent and staff notices under state breach law → student-record exposure triggers FERPA considerations (FERPA itself has no breach-notice clock).

**Bus incident**: driver radio call to dispatch → transportation director → police/EMS → AVL location → parent notification by route roster → student accountability through ridership scans.

**Forms used**: ICS 201, 202, 205, 205A, 213, 214; student accountability / "green-red card" sheets; reunification request and release cards; visitor log; threat assessment intake and management plan; FERPA disclosure log; incident report to the SEA (state form); HSEEP AAR/IP.

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Cyber**: ransomware locks the SIS, phones and HVAC controls on the first day of state testing; actors email parents directly threatening to publish IEP and health records (pattern: CISA _Protecting Our Future_ report 2023; #StopRansomware advisories on Vice Society AA22-249A, Rhysida AA23-319A citing K-12 targeting).
2. **Cyber / vendor**: a breach at the district's SIS vendor exposes student and teacher data across many districts; the vendor's notice is vague and parents learn first from the news (pattern: 2024–2025 SIS vendor breaches reported in national press; no victim named in the seed).
3. **Physical security**: anonymous Instagram threat names "tomorrow" and a high school; a copycat post names two elementary schools overnight (USSS NTAC _Averting Targeted School Violence_ 2021; CISA _Social Media Threat Guidance_ 2023).
4. **Active assailant**: armed intruder reported at a middle school during dismissal with buses in the loop and parents in the pickup line (CTEP _Middle School Active Shooter_ SitMan; USSS NTAC _Protecting America's Schools_ 2019).
5. **Swatting**: false active-shooter 911 call triggers a heavy police response while the real issue is a nearby police pursuit; misinformation spreads on TikTok (FBI public warnings on swatting waves 2023–2025).
6. **Insider**: a staff member with admin rights changes grades and exports student data before resigning; a student "hacker" with stolen teacher credentials is also suspected (CISA K-12 report incident types).
7. **Natural hazard**: tornado warning at 2:45 p.m. with buses already loaded; one route is cut off and a school becomes an overnight shelter (REMS K-12 guide hazard annexes; Campus Resilience ESK hurricane format reused).
8. **Infrastructure failure**: water main break and boil-water advisory hit three schools during lunch; nutrition services must switch to shelf-stable meals and a school must decide whether to close (local boil-water pattern; USDA FNS emergency meal flexibility).
9. **Mass gathering**: Friday-night football game fight escalates to shots fired in the parking lot; crowd flight onto the field (CTEP _Outdoor Events Active Threat_; USSS NTAC _Mass Attacks in Public Spaces_).
10. **UAS**: a drone hovers over an elementary playground during recess and later drops an object at the stadium (CISA K-12 _Bombing Prevention Assistance_; FAA UAS rules).

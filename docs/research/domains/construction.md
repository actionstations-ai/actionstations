# Domain dossier: `construction` (construction, engineering and the built environment)

Research for the construction epic (#704, spec 0471) and the toolkit framework (#648, spec 0448). Researched 2026-11-05. **[V]** = checked this session against a primary or official source. **[U]** = not verified this session (several FEMA, PHMSA and ASCE-style sites blocked automated access).

## 1. Scope

**Covers:** general contractors and subcontractors on active construction sites, construction safety managers, owners' representatives, design engineers (structural, geotechnical, civil) and the engineer of record, building and code officials, post-disaster building safety evaluation programs (volunteer engineers, architects and inspectors), and engineering and construction management programs.

**Extends, does not duplicate:**

- `industrial` carries manufacturing sites (OSHA reporting, EHS manager); `commercial_facilities` carries operating buildings; `municipal` carries the building department as a city function. This domain makes **the construction project and the built-environment professions** the exercising party.
- The earth sciences dossier lists "building safety placards and inspection reports" among the seismic toolkit's templates (spec 0450 FR-3). The structures toolkit here **owns** those templates and the inspection program play; the seismic toolkit references them.
- Technical rescue is a fire and EMS function (spec 0456); this domain supplies the structural and site side of a trench or collapse rescue.

**Exercise types this domain needs:**

- **Site emergencies:** crane collapse or dropped load, trench cave-in, partial structural collapse, fall, fire, utility strike, severe weather on site.
- **Trench or structural collapse rescue:** the contractor's role until rescue arrives, then supporting the rescue team.
- **Post-earthquake or post-storm rapid building inspection:** teams assigned to areas, placards posted, throughput and consistency.
- **Occupied building concerns:** partial collapse in an occupied structure, evacuation decisions, adjacent buildings.
- **Site security and theft:** material and equipment theft, trespass, vandalism (kept brief; the law enforcement field is #698).
- **Engineering ethics dilemma under schedule pressure:** an engineer finds a deficiency and the owner wants to proceed.
- **Education:** engineering and construction management programs run these as classroom cases.

**Aliases:** construction, contractor, general contractor, construction site, jobsite, construction safety, structural engineering, engineer of record, building official, building department, code enforcement, building safety evaluation, rapid assessment, crane, excavation, trench, scaffold, demolition, structural collapse.

## 3. Systems

- **Site:** cranes with load moment indicators and anemometers, trench boxes and shoring, scaffolds, temporary power, access control and sign-in logs, site cameras, daily logs and inspection checklists, drone surveys.
- **Structural monitoring:** crack gauges, tiltmeters, strain gauges, settlement points, load cells on shoring, vibration monitors, survey prisms; data loggers with alarm thresholds.
- **Project management:** schedule (critical path), requests for information (RFIs), submittals, change orders, daily reports, incident reports, subcontractor lists, drawings and as-builts.
- **Building department and inspection programs:** permit and inspection software, evaluation forms and placards, assignment maps, sign-in and credential records.
- **Communications:** radios on site, emergency notification of workers, 911.

## 7. Evaluation

- **Occupant and worker safety decisions:** time from first warning sign (an alarm threshold crossed, a crack reported, a trench wall slough) to stop work and evacuation; whether workers were removed from the hazard until precautions were taken (the standard's wording) **[V]**; muster accounting complete.
- **Rescue timelines:** time to call 911; time to first rescuers on scene; time to a rescue plan with shoring; secondary collapse avoided; scene preserved for investigation.
- **Reporting clocks:** OSHA fatality within 8 hours, hospitalization within 24 hours **[V]**; owner and insurer notice per contract **[U]**.
- **Inspection throughput and quality:** buildings evaluated per team per shift, placard consistency between teams for the same condition, priority buildings (hospitals, shelters, schools) first, owners informed.
- **Message consistency:** contractor, owner, building official and rescue agencies agree on the status of the structure and the exclusion zone.
- **Professional judgment:** the ethics dilemma is scored on process, not on one right answer (information gathered, escalation chain, documentation); it follows the cited codes' public-safety priority **[U]**.
- **HSEEP core capabilities:** Operational Coordination, Mass Search and Rescue Operations, Critical Transportation, Infrastructure Systems, Situational Assessment, Public Information and Warning, On-scene Security, Protection and Law Enforcement, Fatality Management Services, Planning.

## 8. Threat families and arcs

- **Site emergency:** warning sign or event → stop work and evacuation → 911 and first aid → rescue support and scene control → notifications (OSHA, owner, insurer) → investigation and restart decisions.
- **Collapse and rescue:** collapse → accounting and rescue request → structural hazard assessment and shoring → extrication → recovery and investigation.
- **Post-disaster inspection:** event → request for evaluators → assignments and rapid evaluations → placards → detailed evaluations and re-occupancy → program closeout.
- **Professional dilemma:** deficiency found → escalation → schedule pressure → decision and documentation.
- **Service degradation (shared family):** communications loss on site, data logger failure, evaluator shortage, conflicting placards.

## 9. Reuse by other domains

Collapse and site incidents reach `industrial`, `energy_utility` (substation and transmission construction), `water_utility` and `water_dam` (trench and dam work), `transit` and `freight_rail` (bridge and track work), `airport`, `stadium_venue`, `campus`, `k12_schools`, `healthcare` (hospital expansions and occupied building work), `data_center_it` (building outages), and `municipal` (the building department). Post-disaster inspection plays in `municipal`, `emergency_communications` and the earth sciences and weather exercises (post-earthquake, post-hurricane, post-tornado).

## Expert review (2026-11-11)

This is a model-led expert review, written from the standpoint of a construction safety and structural engineering lead with post-disaster building assessment experience. It is not a review by a licensed or employed practitioner.

**Checked:** the domain profile (roles, outside parties, threat families, prompt guidance), the structures toolkit (glossary, realism rules, decision forms, metrics and critical tasks), the OSHA reporting regulation entry, the five lint rules, the reporting and placard metrics, the site emergency generator's reportable-event timing, the previews of the OSHA report record, crane lift record, excavation inspection record, rapid evaluation form, unsafe building order, stop-work order and building placard, and the user guide.

**Sources read:**

- 29 CFR 1904.39, full text through the eCFR versioner API (current as of 2026-11-01): 8 and 24 hour clocks, the three reporting methods, no voicemail, fax or email, 30-day and 24-hour windows, clocks running from when the employer or its agent learns, in-patient hospitalization as formal admission for care or treatment.
- 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P through the same API: 1926.650 competent person definition, 1926.651(c)(2) and (k), 1926.652(a)(1) and (b), Appendices B, C and D (each limited to 20 feet; Appendix D footnote refers deeper trenches to 652(c)(2) and (c)(3)).
- OSHA fact sheet "Trenching and Excavation Safety": "Trenches 20 feet (6.1 meters) deep or greater require that the protective system be designed by a registered professional engineer or be based on tabulated data prepared and/or approved by a registered professional engineer."
- 29 CFR 1926.1417(n): the competent person adjusts equipment and operations for wind, ice and snow; Subpart CC sets no numeric wind limit.
- ATC-20 UNSAFE placard (atcouncil.org) and California city post-disaster ordinances: UNSAFE means "do not enter or occupy" and is not a demolition order; INSPECTED reads "lawful occupancy permitted"; placards are not to be removed or covered without the jurisdiction's authorization.
- 8 CCR 342 (Cal/OSHA): serious injury, illness or death reported within 8 hours of the employer knowing.

**Changed and why:**

- The 20-foot engineered-system rule. The dossier left it unverified and the toolkit forbade stating it. Appendix B and OSHA's own fact sheet confirm it, so the realism rules, prompt guidance and guide now state it in OSHA's words, and the `structures.excavation_rules` lint now flags a depth other than 20 feet tied to professional-engineer design instead of flagging the correct rule.
- Placard authority. "An evaluator recommends a placard" is not how ATC-20 programs work: evaluators post the placard in the field as the building official's agents, and the official owns the unsafe declaration, the vacate order and re-entry. The guide, evaluator role, glossary, prompt guidance and lint message now say so.
- UNSAFE placard wording. The placard template now says "This placard is not a demolition order" (an owner misreading a red placard as a demolition notice is a classic exercise inject) and carries the "do not remove, alter or cover" line. Golden image regenerated for that template.
- OSHA clock detail. The regulation note, toolkit realism rule and guide now say the clocks run from when the employer learns, give the 30-day and 24-hour windows, exclude observation-only admissions, list the reporting methods and name California as a stricter state plan. The fatality recipient now includes the online form, which 1904.39(a)(3)(iii) allows.
- OSHA record preview said "admitted for observation and surgery"; observation alone is not reportable, so the sample now says surgery only.
- Crane record preview gave a "20 sustained" limit and then held the pick on a 21 mph gust with 15 mph sustained, which contradicts its own limit. The sample limit is now a gust-or-sustained limit from the lift plan for the curtain wall's sail area.

**Defaults set:** none were open for this field. The placard trap defaults (all off) and the owner and insurer notice targets (unset, shown but not scored) were reviewed and kept: contract notice terms vary and should be the designer's choice.

**Caveats:** the damage relation remains an invented exercise model. The rescue and placard material still deserves a read by a structural engineer who has served as an evaluator. The appeal period on the unsafe building order sample (10 days) is a plausible local value; model codes and local ordinances differ.

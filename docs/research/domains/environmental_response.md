# Domain dossier: `environmental_response` (oil spill and environmental response management)

Research for the environmental response epic (#688, spec 0455) and the toolkit framework (#648, spec 0448). Researched 2026-11-05. **[V]** = checked this session against a primary or official source. **[U]** = not verified this session.

## 1. Scope

**Covers:** the teams that plan for and manage a release of oil or a hazardous substance to land or water: the responsible party's incident management team (IMT, formerly called the spill management team, SMT), vessel and facility response plan holders, pipeline operators, offshore operators, oil spill removal organizations (OSROs), Coast Guard and EPA federal on-scene coordinators (FOSCs) and their Area Committees, state environmental and wildlife agencies, tribal and local governments, and natural resource trustees.

**Extends, does not duplicate:** `port_maritime` already carries the oil transfer facility response plan (33 CFR 154 subpart F), the National Response Center call and ICS forms for a port; `chemical` carries RMP, EPCRA and CERCLA release reporting and the toxic plume decision; `gas_pipeline` carries PHMSA notice and the rupture workflow. This domain makes the **response organization** the exercising party: the unified command, its sections, and the resource and sensitive-area decisions that follow a release. The existing domains stay the place for the plant, port or pipeline control room.

**PREP vocabulary change:** the 2016 PREP Guidelines now call the team an **Incident Management Team (IMT)**; older plans and many practitioners still say spill management team **[V: PREP 2016.1, section 2.3.4]**. The domain accepts both.

**Exercise types this domain needs:**

- **IMT tabletop (annual):** the PREP workhorse. Organization, notifications, communications, access to resource providers, unified command, use of the Area Contingency Plan (ACP) for sensitive areas, and, once per triennial cycle, a worst case discharge (WCD) scenario **[V]**.
- **Worst case discharge:** a vessel's entire cargo in adverse weather, or the facility or pipeline WCD volume from the plan. The exercise design team may use an alternative scenario representative of a worst case, for example a pipeline segment WCD **[V]**.
- **Area exercise:** a government and industry exercise planned by the Area Committee, FOSC and industry, on a four-year cycle for each ACP **[V]**.
- **Countermeasure request:** a dispersant, chemical agent or in-situ burn request to the FOSC and Regional Response Team, because each countermeasure listed in a plan must be exercised during the triennial cycle **[V]**.
- **Pipeline release to a river, toxic plume, drinking water intake threatened downstream, wildlife and sensitive shoreline, marine firefighting and salvage.**

**Aliases:** oil spill, spill response, spill management team, SMT, incident management team, IMT, OSRO, oil spill removal organization, unified command, area committee, area contingency plan, ACP, facility response plan, FRP, vessel response plan, VRP, worst case discharge, WCD, PREP, environmental unit, FOSC, OSC, responsible party, RP.

## 2. Organization and roles

The IMT works inside a **unified command (UC)** of the FOSC, state, the responsible party and others with authority over the area **[V: PREP 2016.1 glossary]**. The response uses the ICS structure the plan names; PREP asks the exercise to show Operations, Planning, Logistics, Finance, Public Affairs (a Joint Information Center) and Safety **[V: PREP Appendix A.3]**.

## 3. Systems

- **Plan documents:** the vessel or facility response plan, the Area Contingency Plan, the Regional Contingency Plan and the National Contingency Plan (NCP). An ACP is prepared by an Area Committee and is meant to be implemented with the NCP and RCP, in part to address removal of a worst case discharge **[V: 40 CFR 300.5 definition, as shown in search results; statute 33 U.S.C. 1321(j)(4)]**.
- **Mapping and modeling:** ERMA (NOAA's Environmental Response Management Application, a common operational picture), WebGNOME/PyGNOME trajectory models, ADIOS oil weathering, the Trajectory Analysis Planner, Environmental Sensitivity Index (ESI) maps, CAMEO/ALOHA/MARPLOT for chemical plumes, DIVER for damage assessment data **[V: NOAA OR&R "Response Tools for Oil Spills" page; EPA ALOHA page]**.
- **Operations systems:** vessel and aircraft tracking, GPS-tagged field reports, drones for surveillance, tracking of boom, skimmers and storage, waste manifests, WebEOC-style board for the UC.
- **Source-side OT:** pipeline SCADA leak detection and valve closure, tank gauging, transfer system shutdowns (see `gas_pipeline`, `port_maritime`).

## 7. Evaluation

- **Regulatory frame:** PREP requires self-evaluation: "Owner or operator should conduct review to determine whether or not objectives have been met," with self-certification, verification by the Coast Guard COTP/FOSC for vessels, and three-year records retention **[V: PREP 2016.1, IMT exercise section]**. The Action Stations evaluation can produce the record.
- **IMT tabletop objectives from PREP (annual):** knowledge of the response plan; proper notifications; communications system; ability to access resource providers; coordination of internal personnel; the annual review of the transition from a local team to a regional, national and international team; coordination with the National Response System; ability to access information in the ACP for sensitive areas and resources; WCD once per triennial cycle; countermeasure request and usage plan **[V]**.
- **Core components (PREP Appendix A, 15 per cycle):** notifications, staff mobilization, operating within the response management system and unified command, source control (salvage, marine firefighting, lightering, well control), assessment, containment, mitigation (protective booming, water intake protection, wildlife recovery and rehabilitation, population protection), disposal, communications, transportation, and others **[V for A.1 to A.11 as read; A.12 to A.15 [U]]**.
- **Metrics suggested by the epic:** notifications within required times; resources deployed against the trajectory (threshold timing: protective boom in place before the oil reaches a site); protection of sensitive areas before landfall; PREP documentation completeness.
- **Lessons from Deepwater Horizon (ISPR):** Gulf ACPs were inconsistent in quality and content; most did not contain WCD scenarios for offshore exploration; plans had deficiencies in identifying and prioritizing environmentally sensitive areas and developing protective strategies; area committees relied heavily on vessel and facility plans; "worst case discharge" was widely misunderstood, including by SMT members; the review recommended future Spill of National Significance exercises be elevated to National Level Exercise status and inject a significant natural disaster **[V: ISPR text read]**.
- **Lessons from Marshall (NTSB):** the rupture of Line 6B on July 25, 2010 released an estimated 843,444 gallons of crude oil and was not recognized by the control center for over 17 hours; the NTSB issued 19 new recommendations **[V: NTSB investigation page]**. Exercise lessons: leak recognition and restart decisions belong in the pipeline operator's exercise; the spill response starts when somebody calls.

## 8. Threat families and arcs

- **Hazard-driven:** vessel casualty with cargo release; facility or tank release; pipeline release to a river or wetland; offshore well release; chemical release with toxic plume; marine firefighting and salvage; drinking water intake threatened; wildlife and sensitive shoreline impact.
- **Arc:** discovery and notification → initial assessment and activation → unified command established → source control and containment → trajectory and resources-at-risk planning → protective strategies and countermeasure decisions → recovery and shoreline cleanup → waste, claims and demobilization → lessons and plan changes.
- **Service degradation (shared family):** OSRO equipment or crew unavailable; communications failure; weather stops operations; media and rumor on social platforms.

## 9. Reuse by other domains

Spill and plume products and figures serve `port_maritime` (vessel casualties, terminal releases), `gas_pipeline` and `energy_utility` (hazardous liquid pipelines, transformer oil), `chemical` and `industrial` (toxic plumes, runoff), `water_utility` and `water_dam` (intake protection), `transit` and `freight_rail` (derailments with releases), `food_agriculture` (manure and chemical releases), `municipal` and `emergency_communications` (public notification), and the EMS and fire hazmat toolkit (spec 0456). The weather toolkit supplies winds; the flooding toolkit supplies river flows.

# Domain dossier: `emergency_communications` (911 PSAP / emergency communications center) — NEW

Researched 2026-10-24. "Verified" means checked against the primary source this session; anything else is marked unverified.

## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** primary and secondary Public Safety Answering Points (PSAPs), consolidated / regional Emergency Communications Centers (ECCs), county or city 911 dispatch, state 911 boards and NG911 ESInet operators (as an outside party), and public-safety radio system operators (as a co-player).
- **Aliases users might type:** 911, 9-1-1, PSAP, ECC, dispatch, communications center, comm center, emergency communications, ECD, public safety communications, 911 center, NG911, call center (public safety), radio shop.
- **CISA sector:** Emergency Services Sector (the Emergency Services Sector CTEP covers ECC/PSAP/9-1-1 explicitly). Also Communications Sector, for carriers and ESInets.
- **Sector Risk Management Agency:** CISA (DHS) for Emergency Services. CISA's Emergency Communications Division runs SAFECOM / NCSWIC support. FCC regulates the carriers and 911 service providers. NHTSA / NTIA run the National 911 Program (911.gov returned 403 to curl this session).
- **Overlap:** `municipal` already lists "911 CAD and records management" as a city system. This pack makes the ECC the exercising organization.

## 3. Systems

**IT / call handling:**

- 911 call-handling equipment (CPE): ANI/ALI display, ACD, i3-capable NG911 call handling (e.g. Vesta, Viper, Zuercher, Solacom; vendor names as category examples only).
- ALI database / location services: legacy ALI, NG911 LIS, device-based hybrid location (supplemental location feeds).
- NG911 core services: ESInet, ESRP / ECRF / LVF (geospatial routing), BCF (border control function), text-to-911 (TCC / MSRP), RTT.
- Computer-aided dispatch (CAD): unit status, call priority, run cards, mobile data terminals (MDT) in vehicles.
- Records management system (RMS) and jail management (shared with law enforcement).
- Mapping / GIS: map display, address points, road centerlines (NENA GIS data model, copyrighted).
- Logging recorder (voice / radio / text recording), with retention and records-request workflow.
- State message switch / NCIC / Nlets access (CJIS network).
- Alert and warning: IPAWS-OPEN interface (WEA, EAS), local mass-notification, outdoor sirens.
- Admin telephony: 10-digit non-emergency lines, VoIP PBX.
- Station alerting (fire / EMS), paging, CAD-to-CAD links with neighbors.
- Email, AD, county network, SIEM / EDR (usually county IT or MSSP).

**OT / radio / facility:**

- Land mobile radio (LMR): P25 trunked system, master site / zone controller, tower sites, microwave backhaul, dispatch consoles, conventional mutual-aid channels, gateways / patches.
- Facility: UPS and generator, HVAC for equipment rooms, access control (badges, mantrap), CCTV, fire suppression.
- Backup PSAP / mobile communications vehicle.

**Identifiers seen in ECC documents (fact categories):**

- **Console / position numbers** (`Position 6`, `Fire Dispatch 2`): proposed `positions`, alnum, lint yes.
- **Talkgroups / channels** (`PD Main`, `FD TAC 3`, `8CALL90`, `8TAC91`, `VCALL10`, `UCALL40`): the national interoperability names come from the NIFOG (verified that the NIFOG 2.02 exists; the channel list was not extracted). Proposed `talkgroups`, token, lint yes.
- **Tower / radio sites** (`Site 4 – Hilltop`): proposed `radio_sites`.
- **Incident / call numbers** (`CAD #26-118734`): token, scene detail.
- **Units / apparatus** (`Engine 12`, `Medic 4`, `Adam-21`): reuse `apparatus` from `municipal`.
- **ESZ / ESN** (emergency service zones / numbers) and PSAP IDs (FCC PSAP Registry ID): token, not linted.
- **Trunk groups / 911 trunks** (`CAMA trunk 3`, `SIP trunk group B`): token.

## 6. Common operational workflows

- **Call surge / TDoS:** the supervisor sees ACD queue growth and abandoned calls. Tech checks trunk utilization, the carrier or 911 service provider is engaged, and the state 911 office is asked to reroute overflow to a partner PSAP. Recorder evidence is preserved, and FBI / CISA are notified. (CISA _Cyber Risks to 911: TDoS_, 2020; TDoS case study, 2022.)
- **CAD failure:** the floor goes to manual mode: paper cards / run books, radio-only unit status, grease board, manual timestamping. Back-entry into CAD afterward. Neighbors may take overflow dispatching. (CISA malware case study, 2022: a regional ECC ran manual operations.)
- **Network / ESInet outage notification:** the provider calls the designated PSAP contact within 30 minutes under 47 CFR 4.9(h). The PSAP activates alternate routing, and the PIO publishes 10-digit emergency numbers through the county, social media and WEA if authorized.
- **Evacuation to backup PSAP:** the director orders it, calls are rerouted, a skeleton crew moves, and there is a time-to-operational target (agency-specific).
- **Cyber incident on the county network:** county IT isolates. The ECC asks which systems sit on the county network (CAD, recorder, admin phones) vs. isolated (CPE, radio). The TAC reports to the state CSA under CJIS policy incident-response requirements (Policy Area 5.3; section number from v5.9.x, unverified in v6.1). FBI and CISA are informed.
- **Swatting / fraudulent call:** call-takers apply caller-verification procedures and law enforcement chooses a response posture. Recordings are kept for the FBI. (CISA _Introduction to Swatting and Fraudulent Calls for ECC Administrators_, 2026.)
- **Alert and warning:** EM / the authorized originator drafts the WEA / EAS message. Character limits apply: WEA allows 360 characters on 4G LTE / 5G handsets per FCC rules (unverified this session).
- **Regulatory reporting the ECC itself does:** generally none to the FCC (the duty sits on providers). There is CJIS incident reporting to the CSA, state 911 board rules (vary), state breach law if PII / CJI is exposed, and CIRCIA once in force (not yet; see the healthcare dossier).
- **Forms:** ICS 205 (radio communications plan), ICS 205A (communications list), ICS 213 (general message), ICS 214 (activity log), ICS 201, CAD incident printouts, carrier outage notice, CJIS security incident response form (Appendix F.1 in CJIS Security Policy v6.1, per its table of contents: verified title "F.1 Security Incident Response Form").

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Cyber (TDoS):** An automated robocall flood ties up 911 trunks for 40 minutes during a severe storm, and a real structure fire call waits in queue. (CISA _Cyber Risks to 911: TDoS_ (June 2020) and _TDoS Attacks: Lessons Learned from a PSAP_ (2022); ES CTEP Module 1 uses AI-driven robocall TDoS as cover for CAD intrusion.)
2. **Cyber (ransomware / malware):** Malware on the county network forces CAD and the logging recorder offline, and the floor runs on cards for 3 days. (CISA _Malware Attacks: Lessons Learned from an ECC_ (2022); _Cyber Risks to Public Safety: Ransomware_ (Sept 2020).)
3. **Infrastructure failure (carrier):** A fiber cut at a 911 service provider stops wireless 911 delivery across several counties. The carrier's 30-minute notice arrives late, and the public is told to use 10-digit numbers. (Pattern: April 2024 multi-state 911 outage reported by CNBC and others, with the FCC investigating; FCC report not located this session.)
4. **Swatting / fraud:** A cluster of spoofed swatting calls targets schools and officials over one afternoon, using AI-voice and VoIP spoofing. (CISA _Introduction to Swatting and Fraudulent Calls for ECC Administrators_, May 2026.)
5. **Cyber-physical (radio):** A trunked radio master site fails over to site trunking during a large incident, while a console gateway shows unauthorized configuration changes. (CISA _Considerations for Cyber Disruptions in an Evolving 911 Environment_, 2023.)
6. **Insider:** A telecommunicator runs unauthorized NCIC queries on an ex-partner, and the state CSA audit flags the ECC. (CJIS Security Policy, audit and misuse provisions.)
7. **Natural hazard / facility:** A tornado damages the primary PSAP. Evacuation to the backup PSAP coincides with a generator transfer-switch failure. (CISA _911 Cybersecurity Best Practices Package_, COOP guidance.)
8. **Mass gathering:** A crowd crush at a concert causes a call spike of 300 calls in 10 minutes, text-to-911 overload, and incorrect device location from a cell site at the venue. (Ties to `stadium_venue`.)
9. **NG911 / supply chain:** A GIS data-provisioning error after a vendor update misroutes 911 calls from a new subdivision to the neighboring PSAP. (CISA/SAFECOM _Cyber Risks to Next Generation 911_, Nov 2019; GIS Lifecycle Best Practices Guide, 2020.)
10. **Cyber + public information:** A fake social-media post claims "911 is down, call this number", and the number belongs to a scammer. The PIO and the state 911 office must counter it.

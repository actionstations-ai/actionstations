# Domain dossier: `industrial` (manufacturing plant, discrete and process)

Research date: 2026-10-24. Existing profile reviewed: `src/shared/domains/profiles/industrial.ts` (5 roles, 4 fact categories, 7 IT + 7 OT systems). This dossier proposes changes against it; "PROPOSED" marks additions, "KEEP" marks items that already fit.

Verification legend: **[V]** checked against a primary source this session (regulation text, agency page or the document itself); **[U]** not verified this session (from general domain knowledge; check before relying on it).

---

## 1. Scope

**Covers:** single-site manufacturing plants owned by a company (the exercising organization is always simulated):

- **Process manufacturing** (continuous / batch): chemicals and specialty chemicals, plastics and resins, coatings, fertilizer, pharmaceuticals / API, food and beverage processing, pulp and paper, glass, cement, refining-adjacent petrochemicals.
- **Discrete manufacturing**: automotive and parts (stamping, body, paint, assembly), aerospace components, machinery, electrical equipment, appliances, metal fabrication, electronics assembly, primary metals (foundry, rolling mill).
- **Hybrid** (batch processing feeding packaging lines) is the common case for food, pharma and consumer products.

**Aliases users might type** (PROPOSED additions to `aliases`; the existing list is `plant, factory, manufacturing, chemical, refinery, process`): `manufacturer`, `manufacturing plant`, `production plant`, `chemical plant`, `petrochemical`, `process plant`, `mill`, `steel mill`, `paper mill`, `foundry`, `assembly plant`, `auto plant`, `factory floor`, `food processing`, `food plant`, `pharma plant`, `pharmaceutical manufacturing`, `bottling plant`, `packaging plant`, `industrial facility`, `OT site`, `ICS site`. Leave `refinery` here only until an oil-and-gas profile exists (refineries are Energy Sector).

**CISA critical infrastructure sectors:**

- **Critical Manufacturing Sector**. Its four core industries are Primary Metals; Machinery; Electrical Equipment, Appliance and Component; Transportation Equipment **[V]** (cisa.gov/critical-manufacturing-sector). CISA's 2026 sector profile puts the sector at about 400,000 firms, with its closest dependencies on the IT, Transportation Systems and Water sectors **[V]**.
- **Chemical Sector**, a separate sector **[V]**, for chemical, plastics, fertilizer and similar plants.
- **Food and Agriculture** (food plants; USDA and HHS/FDA are the SRMAs) and **Healthcare and Public Health** (pharma) for those sub-types **[U, SRMA assignments from PPD-21 / NSM-22]**.

**Sector Risk Management Agency:** **CISA** for Critical Manufacturing. The CISA SRMA fact sheet says CISA "serves as the Critical Manufacturing Sector Risk Management Agency" **[V]**. CISA is also the Chemical Sector SRMA **[U on the current page, long-standing under PPD-21/NSM-22]**.

**Security regime note:** CFATS (6 CFR Part 27) has been **lapsed since 28 July 2023**. CISA's page says Congress let the statutory authority expire and CISA "cannot enforce" it, and it points facilities to the voluntary **ChemLock** program **[V, cisa.gov CFATS page as fetched 2026-10-24]**. No 2026 reauthorization was found **[V, search; no reauthorization located]**. Scenarios should treat CFATS-era Site Security Plans as legacy voluntary documents, not live obligations.

## 3. Systems

### IT / business (PROPOSED list, replaces the 7 current items)

- **ERP**: orders, inventory, procurement, shipping (e.g. SAP S/4HANA, Oracle, Infor, Epicor).
- **MES / MOM**: production orders, recipes, genealogy, OEE, label/serialization release (e.g. Rockwell FactoryTalk ProductionCentre / Plex, Siemens Opcenter, AVEVA MES, Critical Manufacturing MES). An MES outage alone can stop a plant whose OT is clean.
- **WMS** and yard / dock scheduling; **TMS**; truck-rack or weighbridge ticketing.
- **LIMS**: lab results and certificates of analysis; **QMS** (deviations, CAPA).
- **CMMS / EAM**: work orders, permits, spares (e.g. IBM Maximo, SAP PM).
- **Label and serialization printing** (barcode, GS1, pharma DSCSA serialization). This is a text-bearing artifact source.
- **Engineering document management**: P&IDs, PSI, MOC records.
- Email / M365, **Active Directory** (often shared IT/OT, a known lateral path), VPN, helpdesk / ITSM, SIEM / SOC (corporate or MSSP), EDR, backup.
- **Supplier portal / EDI** and **customer portal** (just-in-time and just-in-sequence feeds for automotive suppliers).
- Website and community notification list.

### OT / ICS (PROPOSED list; Purdue levels in brackets)

- **DCS** for process plants [L1–2]: e.g. Emerson DeltaV, Honeywell Experion PKS, Yokogawa CENTUM VP, ABB 800xA, Siemens PCS 7 / PCS neo.
- **PLCs and PACs** for discrete lines, packaging and utilities [L1]: e.g. Rockwell ControlLogix / CompactLogix, Siemens S7-1500, Schneider Modicon, Mitsubishi, Omron; **remote I/O**; **VFDs**.
- **HMIs / SCADA** [L2]: e.g. AVEVA (Wonderware) InTouch / System Platform, Rockwell FactoryTalk View, Ignition, WinCC.
- **Safety Instrumented System (SIS) / Emergency Shutdown (ESD)** [L1, safety zone]: e.g. Schneider Triconex, Honeywell Safety Manager, Yokogawa ProSafe-RS, HIMA. Also the **Burner Management System (BMS)** on furnaces and boilers, and **machine safety PLCs / light curtains** on discrete lines.
- **Engineering workstations (EWS)** and OT jump hosts, the most common attack staging point [L2–3].
- **Process historian** [L3 / L3.5 mirror]: e.g. AVEVA PI. **Batch execution** (ISA-88 recipes); **Advanced Process Control (APC)**.
- **OT DMZ (L3.5)**: patch server, historian replica, remote-access broker (vendor remote access is a recurring entry vector).
- **OT network monitoring / IDS**: passive asset discovery (e.g. Claroty, Dragos, Nozomi, Microsoft Defender for IoT).
- **Robotics and motion**: industrial robots, AGV / AMR fleet manager, CNC machines, vision inspection, torque / test stands (discrete).
- **Tank gauging and terminal automation**: radar gauges, overfill protection, loading rack.
- **Fire and gas (F&G) detection**, toxic gas detectors and fenceline monitors, deluge.
- **Continuous emissions monitoring (CEMS)**, wastewater pretreatment and outfall samplers. These are regulatory data sources.
- **Utilities**: boilers / steam, cooling towers, chillers, compressed air, nitrogen, the plant substation and switchgear, emergency generators and UPS.
- **Physical security**: PACS (badge readers, turnstiles), CCTV / VMS, perimeter intrusion detection, gatehouse visitor management, mass notification / plant alarm and PA, and the muster accountability (badge-reader headcount) system.
- **Building automation (BAS)** for clean rooms, warehouses and offices.
- Wireless instruments (WirelessHART / ISA100) and IIoT gateways / cloud connectors for condition monitoring.

## 4. Outside parties and media

**Emergency response and government** (PROPOSED list, replaces the current 10):

- Local fire department / HAZMAT team, county 911 / PSAP, EMS, nearby hospitals (decontamination capability).
- **LEPC** and county emergency management; the **SERC** (state emergency response commission).
- **National Response Center (NRC)**, 1-800-424-8802. The NRC is a call center that forwards reports and "is not a response agency" **[V]**.
- **EPA Region**: the On-Scene Coordinator for releases, and the RMP program.
- **State environmental agency**: air permit deviations, spill line, wastewater.
- **OSHA Area Office**; the state-plan OSHA where applicable.
- **U.S. Chemical Safety and Investigation Board (CSB)**. Its 8-hour reporting rule is 40 CFR 1604 **[V]**.
- **CISA** (regional Protective Security Advisor and Cybersecurity Advisor; CISA Central / incident report form at myservices.cisa.gov/irf **[V URL resolves]**) and the **FBI field office** (cyber task force, WMD coordinator for chemical threats).
- Local police / sheriff; **ATF** where explosives or precursors are present; USCG Captain of the Port for waterfront plants (MTSA).
- **DoD / DC3** for defense-supplier plants; DFARS 252.204-7012 sets a 72 h report **[V]**.
- FDA / USDA-FSIS for food and pharma (recall, adulteration).
- The local electric and gas utility and the water utility (supply interruption, pretreatment discharge).

**Mutual aid and industry**

- Neighboring plants' ERTs and the industrial mutual-aid association (common in Gulf Coast and Midwest chemical corridors).
- **MFG-ISAC**, the Manufacturing ISAC launched 2022 by Global Resilience Federation and CyManII **[V, press release; site mfgisac.org resolves]**. ONG-ISAC applies to petrochemical sites **[U]**.
- Critical Manufacturing Sector Coordinating Council / GCC **[V, CISA fact sheet]**.
- CHEMTREC (the industry hazmat emergency call center, private) **[U]**.

**Vendors and contractors**

- DCS / PLC / SIS vendor support and field service; the system integrator.
- OT monitoring vendor; the outside incident response firm (often chosen by the insurer's breach counsel); the cyber insurer.
- Maintenance contractors and turnaround crews.
- Rail carrier and trucking (hazmat shipments in transit).
- Key customers (JIT assembly plants), key suppliers.

**People**

- The union local and its steward; employees' families.
- Fenceline neighbors, schools and a nursing home in the evacuation zone.
- Environmental-justice community group.

**Media channels** (PROPOSED additions to the current 5):

- A neighbor's phone video of the flare or smoke column (Facebook, TikTok).
- Nextdoor and the community alert group.
- Local TV and the assignment desk; a helicopter shot; the local newspaper.
- A trade press reporter (manufacturing / chemical industry outlets) asking about a ransomware listing.
- The ransomware gang's leak-site post (rendered as a screenshot; already covered by `screenshot_generic` / `ransom_note`).
- An employee posting on Reddit or a worker forum ("we were sent home, plant hacked?").
- An investor / analyst call or a stock-ticker chyron (publicly traded parent).
- The union's Facebook page.
- The county's reverse-911 / WEA shelter-in-place alert.

## 6. Common operational workflows

1. **Alarm → board operator → shift supervisor.** A DCS or F&G alarm goes to the board operator, who acknowledges it and sends an outside operator to check. The shift supervisor decides to curtail, trip or ESD. The plant alarm then sounds and the PA announces "shelter in place" or "evacuate to Muster Point N".
2. **Accountability.** Badge-reader muster reports plus floor wardens. Unaccounted persons go to the ERT for search and rescue. A headcount is due to the IC typically within 15–30 minutes (plant policy) **[U]**.
3. **ICS activation.** The shift supervisor or senior emergency response official is IC (1910.120(q)). When the fire department arrives, unified command forms with the FD IC. The plant ICP is often the emergency response center or the gatehouse; the corporate CMT joins remotely. Forms: **ICS 201** (initial briefing), **202** (objectives), **203/204** (organization and assignments), **205** (radio plan; plant radio channels), **206** (medical plan), **207**, **208** (safety message; PPE and air monitoring), **213** (general message), **214** (activity log), **215A** (safety analysis). The existing templates cover 201 and 203 prefill.
4. **Regulatory notification clock** (the EHS Manager runs it):
   - NRC "immediately" for an RQ release (302.6); SERC and LEPC immediately (355.40), then written follow-up.
   - CSB within 8 h on a fatality, serious injury or substantial damage (1604).
   - OSHA within 8 h for a fatality and 24 h for hospitalization (1904.39).
   - The state spill line and air permit deviation report (state-specific; often "immediately" plus written notice within days) **[U]**.
   - RMP accident-history update within 6 months (68.195) **[V]**.
   - PSM / RMP incident investigation started within 48 h (1910.119(m), 68.81) **[V 1910.119; U 68.81]**.
5. **Cyber incident clock** (OT Security / CISO):
   - Corporate SOC triage → isolate the IT/OT boundary (the "cut the link" decision) → engage the IR firm via insurer counsel.
   - Voluntary CISA and FBI report (CIRCIA 72 h / 24 h once final).
   - DFARS 72 h for defense suppliers.
   - SEC 8-K 4 business days after the materiality determination.
   - State data-breach notices if HR or PII data is exfiltrated.
   - The insurer notice clause (often "as soon as practicable").
6. **Manual operations and safe state.** Decide whether the plant can run on local panels and field operators with the DCS or HMIs compromised. PSM operating procedures for emergency shutdown, and MOC for any temporary workaround (bypassing an interlock needs an MOC and a bypass permit).
7. **Restart.** Engineering verifies controller logic against a known-good backup (version compare), a SIS proof test or functional check, the **PSSR** (1910.119(i)), and a staged restart by unit or line. QA releases product made during the event (food/pharma).
8. **Business continuity.** Customer allocation and force majeure letters; shift cancellation and union notice; finished-goods inventory runway (days of supply); alternate plant.
9. **Community.** Reverse-911 / WEA shelter-in-place via county EM; the plant community hotline; the fenceline monitoring data release; LEPC briefing.

**Sector-specific forms and artifacts:**

- MOC form; bypass / override permit; hot work permit; lockout-tagout (LOTO) log.
- Shift log / operator logbook; alarm summary / alarm journal; SOE (sequence-of-events) report from the SIS.
- First-report-of-release worksheet (the NRC call sheet); written EPCRA follow-up letter.
- OSHA 301 incident report; muster accountability report.
- Product hold / quarantine tag.

## 7. Exercise scenarios

1. **cyber**: Ransomware encrypts ERP, MES and the label-print server on a Sunday night. The DCS is untouched but lines cannot release work orders or print compliant labels. Decide whether to sever the IT/OT link and run blind or idle the plant. (CISA #StopRansomware; NIST SP 800-82r3 §on IT/OT dependency.)
2. **cyber_physical**: Unauthorized logic download to a packaging-line PLC via an internet-exposed HMI with default credentials. The line runs erratically and machine guarding faults appear. (CISA AA23-335A on Unitronics PLC defacement across sectors; CISA/FBI/EPA/DOE "Primary Mitigations" fact sheet, May 2025.)
3. **cyber_physical (SIS)**: The SIS controller in a reactor area goes into a fault state and trips the unit. Forensics find unexpected programs on the SIS engineering workstation. Decide whether to restart with the SIS bypassed under MOC. (CISA MAR-17-352-01 HatMan / TRITON.)
4. **cyber (pre-positioning)**: CISA notifies the plant that its VPN appliance was used by a state actor living off the land, with AD credentials harvested and OT jump-host access. No disruption yet. Decide whether to rebuild AD with production running. (CISA AA24-038A, which lists Critical Manufacturing among affected sectors.)
5. **cyber (hacktivist)**: A pro-Russia hacktivist channel posts a video of someone moving setpoints on the plant's remote-access HMI (wastewater pretreatment or a boiler). Local media call within the hour. (CISA et al. "Defending OT Operations Against Ongoing Pro-Russia Hacktivist Activity", May 2024.)
6. **natural_hazard**: Hurricane flooding takes out grid power and backup generators, and loses refrigeration for temperature-sensitive organic peroxides. The decision is whether to move trailers and how wide the evacuation zone should be with the county. (CSB investigation of a 2017 Crosby, TX organic-peroxide plant fire.)
7. **physical_security / insider**: An active assailant (a terminated employee) enters through a contractor gate during shift change. Covers lockdown versus evacuation, accountability with PACS down, then reunification, media and recovery. (CISA Critical Manufacturing Sector Active Assailant Post-Incident Best Practices Guide, Mar 2024; CISA Insider Threat Mitigation Guide.)
8. **insider (sabotage)**: A disgruntled controls contractor whose access was not revoked changes alarm limits and disables historian data collection. A toxic release goes undetected on the board until a fenceline monitor alarms. Covers NRC and LEPC reporting under uncertainty. (Pattern from CISA insider-threat guidance; NIST SP 800-82r3 on account management.)
9. **uas_drone**: Repeated night drone flights over the tank farm and rail spur during a labor dispute, and one drone crashes inside the fence. Is it reconnaissance, a journalist or a hobbyist? Covers police, FAA and security posture. (CISA "Be Air Aware" UAS guidance for critical infrastructure.)
10. **infrastructure_failure**: A utility substation fault causes a plant-wide power dip. Multiple VFDs and PLCs fail to restart cleanly, and a cooling-water loss on an exothermic batch forces an emergency vent. Is it a cyberattack or an equipment failure, and who has the authority to call it? (Pattern; NIST SP 800-82r3 on OT resilience and manual operations.)

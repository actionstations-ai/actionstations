# Domain dossier: `freight_rail` (freight railroad), a new domain

Researched 2026-10-24. Every file URL in §9 was fetched in full on that date; size, page count, title and date come from the downloaded PDF, and each file was text-searched for copyright / SSI / FOUO / TLP markings. Claims marked **(unverified)** were not checked against a primary source.

> **Fetch note:** tsa.gov, govinfo.gov and ntsb.gov serve to any client. Use `https://www.ntsb.gov/…`, because the `data.ntsb.gov/…` form of the same report redirects to an HTML page. phmsa.dot.gov and railroads.dot.gov sit behind the same Akamai front as transit.dot.gov; send browser-like headers.

## 1. Scope

- **Covers:** a freight railroad: a Class I, a regional (Class II) or a short line (Class III), including its yards, terminals, intermodal ramps, dispatching center, mechanical shops and the hazmat it hauls. Switching and terminal railroads also fit. The exercising org is a fictional railroad. Real railroads may appear only as interchange partners or vendors under the simulated-orgs rule.
- **Aliases:** freight rail, railroad, railway, Class I, short line, regional railroad, switching railroad, terminal railroad, rail carrier, yard, intermodal, hazmat rail.
- **CISA sector:** Transportation Systems Sector, Freight Rail mode. **SRMA:** DHS (TSA) and DOT. FRA (49 CFR 200–299) is the safety regulator, PHMSA (49 CFR 171–180) the hazmat regulator, TSA (49 CFR 1570/1580 and SDs) the security regulator. STB (economic) is out of scope.
- Shares TSA's 1580/82 SD series with passenger rail; see `transit.md`.

## 3. Systems

**IT:** transportation management system / car management (waybills, car location, "car accounting"), crew management and calling, train dispatching back office, customer portal and EDI (waybill / car location messages with shippers and interchange partners), Railinc-type industry data exchanges (Umler equipment registry, car hire, AskRail-style responder apps; real names allowed as examples), the hazmat train consist system, email/identity, ITSM, SIEM.

**OT / field:** Computer-Aided Dispatch and **CTC** (centralized traffic control) with code lines to field signal locations; wayside signal systems and track circuits; interlockings; **PTC** (I-ETMS on most freight railroads: locomotive onboard segment, wayside interface units, back office server, 220 MHz radio network; 49 CFR 236 subpart I); highway-rail grade crossing warning systems; wayside defect detectors (hot box / hot bearing, dragging equipment, wheel impact load, acoustic bearing); remote-control locomotive systems in yards; hump yard process control (retarders, switches); locomotive control systems and event recorders; end-of-train devices; voice radio (VHF AAR channels); fueling facilities; yard PACS and CCTV.

**Identifiers (fact categories, new domain):**

- **Subdivisions** ("Sandhill Subdivision"): `pattern` `\b([A-Z][a-z]+) Sub(?:division)?\b`, complete
- **Mileposts** ("MP 123.4"): `pattern` `\bMP ?(\d+(?:\.\d)?)\b`, lint off or low
- **Trains** (train symbols like "M-ABCXYZ-24", "Q123"): `token`, low-lint
- **Yards / terminals** ("North Yard", "Hollis Terminal"): pattern like transit depots
- **Control points / interlockings** ("CP 214", "CP Ames"): `token`
- **Tracks** ("Main 1", "Main 2", "Siding", "Track 7")
- **Cars** (reporting mark + number, e.g. "ABCX 123456"): `token`, low-lint
- **Locomotives** ("unit 4512")
- **Grade crossings** (DOT crossing inventory number, "123456A"), low-lint
- **Bridges / tunnels** ("Bridge 87.2")

## 4. Outside parties and media

- **Federal:** FRA (regional office, hazmat and signal inspectors), PHMSA, TSA Surface Operations (TSOC; Transportation Security Inspectors–Surface), CISA, FBI (field office, JTTF), **NTSB** (major accidents), **National Response Center** (1-800-424-8802), EPA (removal action, On-Scene Coordinator), US Army Corps (bridges over navigable water), USCG (if at a waterway), FEMA.
- **State/local:** fire departments (often volunteer in rural areas), county emergency management and LEPC, state emergency management, state environmental agency, state police, the 911 PSAP, school districts (evacuations), hospitals, a state public utilities commission (some states regulate rail safety).
- **Industry:** interchange railroads (Class I partners, short lines), shippers and consignees (chemical plants: emergency contact numbers on shipping papers), hazmat response contractors, CHEMTREC (shipper emergency response line, **name verified from ERG cover text only**), tank car owners and lessors, the AAR Bureau of Explosives (**unverified role**), TRANSCAER (outreach).
- **Vendors:** PTC / signaling suppliers, dispatching system vendor, locomotive OEMs, wayside detector vendor, telecom carriers (code lines, 220 MHz).
- **ISAC:** Surface Transportation ISAC / AAR's Railway Alert Network (RAN). **(Names from industry sources, unverified against primary pages.)**
- **Media:** residents' Facebook groups and Nextdoor near the derailment, local TV with drone footage, national cable news, environmental advocates, rail-enthusiast forums and scanner feeds, elected officials' posts.

## 6. Common operational workflows

- **Derailment with hazmat:** crew emergency call on the road channel → dispatcher stops trains in the area and protects the track → railroad hazmat and police notified → **consist provided to the 911 / fire IC** (HM-263 real-time consist) → NRC within 12 h (171.15) and FRA telephonic report (225.9) → NTSB notified for major accidents → unified command with fire, county EM, state environmental agency, EPA OSC → shelter-in-place or evacuation decision using ERG isolation distances → vent-and-burn / transfer decision (the NTSB RIR-24-05 lessons).
- **Cyber incident:** SOC → Cybersecurity Coordinator → CISA ≤72 h (SD 1580-21-01E) → CIRP; dispatching may fall back to manual track warrants / paper and degraded PTC (restricted operations). TSA reporting under the SD is satisfied by the CISA report (verified: "TSA will accept a report to CISA").
- **Security concern** (tampering with switches, trespass on a bridge, a suspicious device on a tank car): railroad police → TSA within 24 h (1570.203) → FBI.
- **Embargo / service interruption:** customer service issues an embargo notice to shippers and interchange partners; reroutes via partner railroads.
- **Forms:** ICS 201/202/203/205/208 (safety message), 213, 214; train consist (hazmat sections); shipping papers / waybill emergency contact; NRC report; FRA F 6180.54 (**unverified**); track warrant / track bulletin forms (railroad-specific; GCOR-style operating rules are copyrighted).

## 7. Exercise scenarios (seeds)

1. **Derailment with a vinyl-chloride-type release:** A mainline derailment of a mixed train near a small town at night. Volunteer fire is first on scene, the consist is late reaching the IC, and a vent-and-burn is under pressure. _Grounded in NTSB RIR-24-05 (verified), which cites a late consist, incomplete information to the IC and the vent-and-burn decision._
2. **Ransomware in the transportation management system:** Waybills and car locations are unavailable. Hazmat consist generation fails, and the railroad must decide whether to stop hazmat trains while the HM-263 consist obligation stands.
3. **Dispatching / CTC cyber-physical:** False indications on the CTC display for a subdivision. Dispatchers move to track warrants and PTC goes into restricted mode. Is it a cyber attack or a code-line telecom failure? _TSA SD 1580/82-2022-01E focus on segmentation between IT and OT._
4. **Wayside detector data tampering / failure:** A hot-bearing detector reports normal while a bearing fails (cf. RIR-24-05 detector timeline). An insider modified alarm thresholds.
5. **Insider / sabotage:** A rail fastener or switch tampering is found on a bridge approach after a threat letter. Track inspection, train holds, TSA and FBI reporting.
6. **Physical security / theft:** Organized cargo theft at an intermodal ramp, with posts on social media of looted containers. Police and press attention follow. _Public 2022 reporting of train looting in Southern California (**URL unverified**)._
7. **Natural hazard:** Flooding undermines a bridge and a subdivision is out for days. A reroute pushes hazmat onto a partner's line through a city; the route risk analysis (172.820) comes into question.
8. **Infrastructure failure:** The 220 MHz PTC radio network or a telecom carrier backbone fails across a region. Do we run under PTC exemption rules or stop?
9. **UAS:** Drones hover over a hump yard and a tank-car storage track. Is it surveillance or recreational?
10. **Grade-crossing failure / mass casualty:** A crossing warning system failure leads to a collision with a school bus. Crossing system records are subpoenaed and media converge.

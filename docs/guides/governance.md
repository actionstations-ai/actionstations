# Corporate governance, risk and business continuity

The governance toolkit gives an exercise the decisions of the people who run a whole organization when something goes wrong: the board and its audit committee, the executive team, general counsel and the disclosure committee, risk and compliance, and the business continuity manager. It suits boards and executive teams practicing a cyber incident, business continuity programs testing recovery objectives, and business and law classes working a case.

Exercise aid, not legal advice. Check the current rule and your counsel. The toolkit does not decide materiality for you and files nothing; it records the decisions your players make and times them.

Turn it on in the scenario's **Toolkits** tab (see [Toolkits](toolkits.md)). The app proposes it when the brief mentions materiality, a Form 8-K, a ransom payment, a business impact analysis, a legal hold or executive succession. The **Corporate governance, risk and business continuity** kind of organization describes the decision makers themselves; any other kind of organization can add the toolkit on top of its operating scenario. Every company, person and adviser in an exercise is fictional, and documents show no stock ticker.

## What it adds

- **Documents:** board briefing, materiality worksheet, Form 8-K draft (Item 1.05 or Item 8.01), business impact analysis table, ransom payment decision paper, legal hold and privilege notice, regulator inquiry or subpoena summary, insurer notice and auditor inquiry, investor questions and call talking points, decision log, and succession notice with an authority matrix.
- **Charts** drawn from data: the business impact analysis table, a recovery tracker that shows each function against its recovery time objective (status is a word and a symbol, not only a color), and a decision timeline from discovery to determination to filing with the disclosure clocks. Each has a text summary and a data table.
- **Simulated incident** (labeled simulated): ransomware, data theft, loss of a site, or a cloud outage. Early facts are incomplete or wrong and later facts correct them, the financial impact range widens when the scope is revised and then narrows, and functions recover only after the functions they depend on.
- **Decisions** players record: materiality determination, disclosure, ransom payment, activate the continuity plan, escalate to the board, regulator or insurer notice, and succession and delegation. Each asks who decided, what was known and the basis.
- **Clocks:** the SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05 clock (four business days after the determination, or after an Attorney General delay ends) and the New York DFS notices (72 hours for an incident, 24 hours for an extortion payment, 30 days for the written explanation). Recording a "Material" determination starts the Form 8-K clock by itself, at the exercise time of the decision, and recording a ransom payment starts the NYDFS payment clocks; the regulation must be switched on for the project. You can still fire any trigger by hand from the regulatory clocks panel, for example when an Attorney General delay ends.
- **Measures:** time from discovery to determination, determination to filing against the clock, discovery to board notice, functions restored within their recovery time objective at each hour and whether recovery followed the business impact analysis, decisions documented with owner, facts and basis, and message consistency across the board briefing, filing, press release and employee message.

- **Real disclosure language for the facilitator:** from the Case data panel you can search public SEC Form 8-K filings that report Item 1.05 or Item 8.01 in a period, and read the item text of the first few. It is shown to the facilitator only, never put in player documents. The SEC asks every caller for a contact. The app sends help@actionstations.ai, or your own email if you add one under Settings, Case data. It goes only to sec.gov, only after you agree to a fetch, and the app keeps to the SEC's limit of 10 requests a second.

## Things the toolkit knows

- The determination follows discovery without unreasonable delay. It cannot be put off by delaying a meeting, and the investigation does not have to finish first.
- An Item 1.05 draft states the nature, scope and timing of the incident and its material impact or reasonably likely impact. An Item 8.01 draft describes an incident without claiming it is material.
- Paying or refusing a ransom does not remove the duty to decide on materiality, and the payment size is one factor.
- Recovery time objectives and impact grades are your numbers; the toolkit checks recovery against them and does not set them.

## Limits

The toolkit does not give legal advice, calculate materiality, or file anything. Real filings on EDGAR and real company text are not used in exercises. A review of the documents and decisions by a general counsel and a business continuity professional is recommended before a team relies on them.

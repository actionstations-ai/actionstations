# PREP exercises (oil spill response plans)

If you hold an oil spill response plan (a facility, vessel, pipeline or offshore plan, or an Area Contingency Plan), the National Preparedness for Response Exercise Program (PREP) Guidelines describe the exercises that plan should have, and what each exercise record should say. Action Stations helps you plan one of those exercises and produce its record. The guidelines are voluntary and they are not regulations; what this guide says is generic exercise guidance, not legal advice. Your plan and your regulator decide what satisfies the plan.

The objectives and core components follow the 2016 PREP Guidelines, version 2016.1. Check the current edition before you rely on a date or a retention period.

## Set up the exercise

1. Open the scenario and choose **Exercise details**.
2. Under **PREP exercise (oil spill response)**, choose **PREP IMT tabletop** (your Incident Management Team's annual exercise) or **PREP area exercise** (an Area Contingency Plan exercise).
3. Choose the **plan type**: tank vessel, marine transportation-related facility, inland facility, onshore pipeline or Area Contingency Plan. The objectives, the cycle length and the records rules follow the plan type.
4. Enter the **plan holder** and the **first day of the plan's exercise cycle**. A facility, vessel or pipeline plan has a three-year cycle; an Area Contingency Plan has a four-year cycle.
5. Tick whether this exercise simulates a **worst case discharge** scenario and say which one. The guidelines ask for one in each cycle. A pipeline operator can use a representative scenario, such as the worst case discharge of one pipeline segment.
6. List the **countermeasures** your plan names (dispersant, a chemical agent, in-situ burning) and tick the ones this exercise covers. The guidelines ask that each countermeasure in your plan is exercised during the cycle.

## Objectives

The objectives are filled in from the guidelines for your plan type: knowledge of the plan, proper notifications, the communications system, access to an oil spill removal organization, coordination of the people responsible, the transition from a local to a regional and national team, coordination with the National Response System, access to the sensitive-area information in the Area Contingency Plan, and a worst case discharge scenario. After the exercise, mark each one **Met**, **Partially met**, **Not met** or **Not evaluated**. Action Stations does not judge the result for you.

## The 15 core components

Each exercise cycle should exercise the 15 core components of the guidelines' Appendix A: notifications; staff mobilization; operating within the response management system and a unified command; source control; assessment; containment; mitigation; protection (protective booming, water intake protection, wildlife recovery and population protection); disposal; communications; transportation; personnel support; equipment maintenance and support; procurement; and documentation. They may be exercised in segments over the cycle, as long as each applicable component is exercised at least once. Not every component applies to every plan: tick **does not apply to the plan** for the ones you have decided to leave out.

The injects in your scenario mark the components they exercise. An NRC report record exercises notifications, a countermeasure request exercises mitigation, and a water intake closure exercises protection. If the discussion covered a component without an inject, tick **also exercised in discussion**.

Choose **Check coverage of the cycle** to see how many of the applicable components the project's PREP exercises have covered in the cycle, and which are still missing. Coverage counts only the exercises that fall inside the cycle you entered.

## The exercise record

Choose **Create exercise record (PDF)**. The record is saved in the project's exports folder. It follows what the guidelines say proper documentation should include: the exercise type, the date and time, a description, the objectives met, the core components exercised and lessons learned, with a block for the signature of an authorized representative of the plan holder. It also shows the plan holder, the participants' roles, where the exercise falls in the cycle, whether a worst case discharge was simulated, the countermeasures covered, the self-evaluation, and a reminder of how long to keep the record and where.

- **Participants:** the record lists the roles that played. Add the names by hand before you sign.
- **Signature:** Action Stations leaves the signature line empty. The plan holder certifies the exercise, not the app.
- **Keeping the record:** facility and vessel records are normally kept for three years, an inland facility record for five, and an Area record for four years (Coast Guard) or five (EPA). Documentation is usually completed within 60 days of the exercise.
- **Credit for a real response:** you may claim credit for an actual response when its objectives are met, it is evaluated and a proper record is made. You can use the same record for that.

Every product carries an exercise marking, and the operators in the examples are fictional.

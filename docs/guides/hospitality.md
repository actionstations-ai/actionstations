# Hospitality, tourism and cruise

This guide is for people who run exercises for the organizations that look after guests away from home: a cruise line and its ships, a hotel or resort, or a destination tourism board. It covers the **Hospitality, tourism and cruise** kind of organization and two toolkits any organization can turn on: **Outbreak at sea** and **Guest emergency**. See [Toolkits](toolkits.md) for how toolkits are chosen and used.

Every ship, line, hotel, destination organization and person in the material is fictional. Real ports and cities are fine and often help. The toolkits keep to coordination, accounting and notification. They give no medical advice beyond the cited guidance and no detail about weapons or attacker methods.

## The organization

One kind of organization holds three variants. Pick the roles that match your group.

- **Cruise line:** the Master and Staff Captain, the Ship's Doctor and the Environmental Health Officer, the Hotel Director, the Security Officer, and the shoreside team (Designated Person Ashore, fleet operations, a medical director, guest care and communications, legal).
- **Hotel or resort:** a front office manager, an executive housekeeper and a revenue manager. For the general manager, security director and technology roles, use the lodging roles of the **commercial facilities** kind of organization alongside.
- **Tourism board:** the chief executive, visitor services, an industry liaison and an emergency management liaison.

Six kinds of incident come with their own phases, decisions to weigh and clues: a gastroenteritis outbreak at sea or in a property, a hurricane affecting a resort or an itinerary, a guest death or serious injury (including a person overboard), a cyber attack on reservations and keycards, a security incident in a lobby, and a fire in a hotel tower.

## Outbreak at sea

Turn on **Outbreak at sea** for a ship, or for any place where many people stay together (a campus, a long-term care home, an airport hold room). It adds:

- **Documents:** the cumulative gastroenteritis report, the outbreak response checklist, guest and passenger letters (illness notice, cabin isolation, itinerary change, compensation offer), the port health notification and the boarding decision memo for the next voyage.
- **Charts:** an outbreak curve of cases by day with the 2 and 3 percent levels drawn, and a port status board.
- **Decisions:** report to an authority, isolate and clean, change the itinerary or a port, decide boarding for the next voyage, and issue a statement.

### What the toolkit assumes about reporting

These points come from the federal rule for ships bound for a US port and from the CDC Vessel Sanitation Program standards, read on 2026-11-05. Check the current documents before you rely on a number.

- A ship carrying 13 or more passengers reports its cumulative count of acute gastroenteritis cases (zero included) 24 to 36 hours before arrival, and again at least 4 hours before arrival if the count changed.
- Within 15 days of a US port, a special report goes to the CDC when the cumulative reportable cases reach 2 percent of the passengers or of the crew, with a telephone call, and a second at 3 percent. Three percent, or an unusual pattern below it, is an outbreak.
- A reportable case is diarrhea (three or more loose stools in 24 hours, or more than normal for the person), or vomiting with one more symptom. Nausea alone is not counted. Crew who fell ill up to 3 days before boarding count.
- After a special report the CDC may ask for daily updates, and the 24-hour and 4-hour reports carry on.
- Ill passengers are advised to stay in their cabins until 24 hours after their symptoms end. Ill food employees stay isolated for 48 hours after, other ill crew for 24 hours, and return to work only when the medical staff clear them. Medical staff begin collecting specimens at 2 percent.
- The CDC may ask a line to tell port authorities, delay boarding the next voyage and arrange cleaning in the terminal.

Separately, the master of a ship bound for a US port reports any death, or any ill person as the federal rule defines one, at once to the CDC quarantine station at or nearest the arrival port. This covers the 15 days before arrival, or the time since leaving a US port if that is shorter.

The standards give no time limit for the special reports, so the clocks use a short allowance that you can change. See **Compliance** in Settings for the clocks and how to place the arrival time.

## Guest emergency

Turn on **Guest emergency** for a muster, a person overboard, a serious crime report, a tower evacuation, a hurricane departure plan, a guest death, a hotline or a system outage. It adds:

- **Documents:** muster and accounting report, man overboard checklist, alleged crime report, floor evacuation status, hurricane guest departure plan, guest death or serious injury notification checklist, hotline script, and a property management system outage board.
- **Charts:** a muster board, a floor-by-floor status board and a departures timeline against road and airport closures.
- **Decisions:** evacuate or shelter, guest accounting, notify family or consulate, and issue a statement.

A cruise line must telephone the nearest FBI field office as soon as possible after a homicide, suspicious death, a missing US national, a kidnapping, a serious assault, certain sexual offenses or a theft over $10,000, and then file a written report on the Department of Transportation website. The line keeps a complaint log open to the FBI and the Coast Guard, and keeps camera recordings at least 20 days.

A guest death on a ship bound for a US port also goes at once to the CDC quarantine station. Required reports like this one, and the FBI call, do not wait for the family; the family is told before any public statement.

Accounting boards say when the count was made and when the guest list was printed, because a printout is older than the live list. Totals always add up: expected equals accounted for plus not accounted for.

Simulated muster, hurricane, tower fire and reservation outage timelines are available too. Each is labeled simulated, and persons are codes.

## How you are scored

- **Authority reports on time:** each report point is checked against its window. A report filed before the 24-hour window opens does not count.
- **Time to account for every person:** from the alarm to the declaration that all are accounted for, with a flag when the declaration came while persons were still missing.
- **Time from a level to an action:** for example, from the 2 percent level to isolation and cleaning.
- **Message consistency:** guest, family, media and employee messages agree on the numbers and times.

Critical tasks are set for public health and medical services, mass care, operational communications, public information, security and law enforcement, and fatality management.

## Before you use it

Ask a cruise or hotel safety manager and a public health professional with maritime experience to review the documents and the numbers. The material is a teaching aid and not a substitute for the line's own plans.

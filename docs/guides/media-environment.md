# Media environment and crisis communications

Every incident has a media environment: reporters call, a post goes viral, someone shares a video of uncertain origin, and the organization has to say something before it knows everything. The **Media environment** toolkit adds that to any exercise. The **Crisis communications and journalism** domain is for exercises where communications is the point: a communications team, a joint information center (JIC), a press office or a newsroom.

All outlets, handles, reporters, spokespeople and organizations in an exercise are fictional, and the app is told never to imitate a real outlet or person. A false claim in an exercise is always about a fictional organization and a fictional event. Exercise material is marked as such in every export.

## Turn it on

Open the scenario's **Toolkits** tab and tick **Media environment**. The app suggests it for every kind of organization, and also when your brief mentions the press, a reporter, social media, a rumor, misinformation, a viral post or a holding statement. For a communications exercise, choose **Crisis communications and journalism** as the kind of organization. It has four variants of one role set:

- **Communications team:** director, spokesperson, media relations lead, social media manager, writer, legal counsel, the executive who approves release, and customer service.
- **Joint information center:** Lead PIO, agency PIOs, rumor control and the briefing coordinator.
- **Press office:** the communications team facing hostile and off-topic questions.
- **Newsroom:** news director, desk editor, reporter, verification editor, producers and the standards editor.

The domain offers five kinds of incident: organizational crisis, disaster public information, information disorder (viral misinformation, impersonation, a hacked official account, manipulated media), newsroom breaking news, and a failure of the communications function itself (a compromised social account, an alert system outage, an unavailable spokesperson).

## What it adds

- **Documents players receive:** holding statement and full statement, message map with hard questions and answers, media advisory, correction or retraction notice, wire alert (flash, bulletin or update, from a fictional wire service), broadcast rundown, verification note, JIC briefing schedule, live blog and push alert. Social posts, news articles, press releases, reporter emails and call slips come from the templates you already have. All of them are drawn from templates, so the text is exact and legible.
- **People the facilitator plays:** reporters, the wire desk, the local TV desk, a fact-checking organization, a platform trust and safety team, the public and community group administrators. Two advisor roles are available for any domain: a media relations liaison and a social media monitor.
- **Decisions players record:** issue a holding statement, approve a release, respond to a reporter (on the record, background, decline or no comment), issue a correction, schedule a press conference, escalate to legal or the executive, request a platform takedown or label, hold or publish, request a second source, and request a provenance check. They are available in live play and to the facilitator.

## Simulated coverage and spread

The news cycle and social spread are **teaching models**. They are labeled **simulated** wherever they appear, and they do not predict how real news or real posts behave.

**The news cycle** schedules how coverage develops from the first public signal, using the severity and reach you choose: a wire flash within minutes, a bulletin, local TV at the next hour (with the producer's deadline 15 minutes before), a web article, a push alert for a severe story, the morning print edition, and reporter calls and emails that each state a deadline. Follow-up stories react to what your players do: a story saying the organization "has not commented" if no holding statement comes in time, a story quoting the statement if one does, coverage of a correction or a press conference. The same settings always give the same schedule, and you can adjust any item before it becomes an inject.

**Social spread** grows a post's views, reposts, likes and replies along a curve from the minute it appears. A response changes the growth that comes after it:

| Response          | Within an hour of the post    | Within four hours | Later |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------- | ----- |
| Correction        | removes 40% of further growth | 15%               | 5%    |
| Platform label    | 30%                           | 10%               | 3%    |
| Platform takedown | 50%                           | 20%               | 8%    |

A statement, or silence, changes nothing. Several responses add up but never slow growth below 35% of what it would have been. Replies are a mix of supportive, skeptical, hostile, false-claim and joke replies; a timely correction cuts the false-claim share, and two hours of silence makes hostile replies likelier. Counters never go down.

The spread and sentiment appear as charts with a plain-text summary and a data table, and sentiment is labeled in words as well as color. A social listening report gives the volume, sentiment mix and top narratives at any moment.

## Verification tasks

A verification task is an item (a photo, a video, a screenshot, an account or a document) with a hidden truth: authentic, misattributed, manipulated, synthetic, satire or unverifiable. Players can request checks, and each one costs exercise time:

- reverse image result
- metadata and file properties
- geolocation clues
- account history
- first-appearance timeline
- original-source contact
- credential check (Content Credentials)

A check that finds nothing is still a result and still costs its time. The credential check always shows the same rule: **a missing credential proves nothing, and a valid credential says who signed, not that the content is true.** Players then record a verdict (verified, false, unverified or hold) and a confidence. The clues are plain text; they never contain links to real sites or the image or voice of a real person.

## How communications teams are scored

The Evaluation tab can show these measures, each with the inputs behind it:

- **Time to holding statement** from the first public signal, against the window in the organization's communications plan if you set one.
- **Statement accuracy:** each claim in a statement checked against the scenario's fact sheet. You confirm every check; a suggested check does not count until you do.
- **Message consistency** across spokespeople, channels and agencies.
- **Corrections:** errors found, corrections issued, time from discovery to correction, and whether the correction went out on the channel the error spread on.
- **Reporter handling:** queries answered before the stated deadline and how they were answered.
- **Verification before publication:** published items with a recorded verification step, false items published, false items correctly held, and the time from receipt to verdict.

These are discussion aids for the hot wash, not grades, and they score the group. The exercise evaluation guide for communications uses Public Information and Warning, Operational Communications and Situational Assessment, with tasks such as issuing a holding statement within the time in the communications plan, coordinating messages across agencies before release, and correcting a false statement through the channel it spread on.

## Reference documents

The pack includes the FCC's report on the 2018 Hawaii false missile alert as a communications case study: it shows how long a correction took and why a cancellation is not the same as a correction.

Documents the pack links but does not include:

- CDC, Crisis and Emergency Risk Communication (CERC) manual: https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/php/cerc-manual
- FEMA, NIMS Basic Guidance for Public Information Officers (December 2020): https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_nims-basic-guidance-public-information-officers_12-2020.pdf
- Society of Professional Journalists, Code of Ethics: https://www.spj.org/ethicscode.asp
- Verification Handbook, European Journalism Centre: https://verificationhandbook.com/

If your project includes the organization's own communications plan, the evaluation guide cites it.

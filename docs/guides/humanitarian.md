# Humanitarian and international response

The humanitarian toolkit gives an exercise the work of aid organizations in a crisis abroad: situation reports, a shared picture of who is doing what where, coordination gaps, staff security decisions, supply pipelines and camp health. It suits international and national NGOs, UN coordination offices and cluster partners, response site teams, headquarters crisis management teams, humanitarian studies classes, and voluntary organizations active in disasters at home that want the same products in a community exercise.

Turn it on in the scenario's **Toolkits** tab (see [Toolkits](toolkits.md)). The app proposes it when the brief mentions a cluster, a camp, a refugee influx, a flash update, a 3W table, a kidnapping or detention, or a pipeline break. The **Humanitarian and international response** kind of organization has four role sets: a country office, a coordination office, a response site and a headquarters crisis management team. Every organization, person and crisis it produces is fictional.

## What it adds

- **Coordination documents:** flash update, 3W table, cluster meeting minutes, gap list, needs overview and response plan summary, funding status, and the Humanitarian Country Team record.
- **Field security documents:** incident report, risk assessment summary, movement request and approval, inter-agency security alert, crisis management team log and family update. They stay at the level of decisions and communications.
- **Supply and needs documents:** pipeline dashboard, shipment status, customs hold notice, procurement request, warehouse report, access note, rapid assessment, distribution report, camp health report, community feedback log and misinformation tracker.
- **Charts** drawn from data, never as pictures from an image model: a 3W matrix and map with the areas nobody reported in marked, needs against coverage, the pipeline projection with its break day, funding status, a staff incident timeline and an access constraints map. Each has a text summary and a data table.
- **Decisions** for players to record, such as requesting cluster activation, assigning a gap to a partner, stopping movements, hibernating or relocating staff, notifying a family, sharing a security alert, rerouting a shipment and declaring an outbreak response.
- **Measures:** how long each coordination gap took to assign and to cover and how many were still open at the end; people reached against the plan's targets; staff safety decisions against when a threat began; pipeline decisions against the break; and whether the country office, headquarters, the Humanitarian Country Team and donors say the same thing.

## Things the toolkit gets right

- A cluster is requested by the Humanitarian Coordinator and activated by the Emergency Relief Coordinator with the IASC Principals' endorsement. A refugee setting uses the refugee coordination model instead. The app flags drafts that say otherwise.
- Taking part in the inter-agency security framework does not make the UN responsible for an NGO's security.
- Days of stock are stock on hand divided by daily consumption, and the figure, the text and the measures all use the same arithmetic.
- A blank in a 3W table is a gap or a reporting failure, not proof that nobody is working there.
- Staff security and kidnapping scenarios never include negotiation tactics, ransom amounts or mechanics, or techniques for evading or resisting detention. Add a content notice and a wellbeing check for facilitators when a scenario involves harm to staff.

## Data you can bring in

Two sources can seed a scenario with real structure. Both are off until you enter your own application name in **Settings**; it identifies your copy of the app and is not a secret. Without it the app uses sample data.

- **Operational presence (3W) by country** from the Humanitarian Data Exchange: which organizations report work in which sector and admin area. The licence of the source dataset is read when you fetch and kept with the data; data under a licence that does not allow redistribution stays in your scenario and is left out of exported packs.
- **ReliefWeb report titles** for a country and period, for a timeline. Only titles, dates, sources and links are kept.

You see an estimate of the size before you agree, and the app sends nothing about your plans to either service.

## Simulated data

The app can generate, for any seed and settings, arrivals and registration at a site, the impact of a sudden-onset disaster by admin area, partners reporting into a 3W table over time, an outbreak in a camp, a supply pipeline with lead times and a break, and a rising threat level on a road. Every set is labeled **simulated**, repeats exactly with the same seed, and writes the events the measures score decisions against. They are teaching models, not forecasts.

## Reference documents

Most humanitarian doctrine is not public domain, so the toolkit links rather than bundles. Look for the current edition of these on their publishers' sites:

- IASC publications on cluster coordination, the Transformative Agenda and emergency scale-up, at interagencystandingcommittee.org.
- The Saving Lives Together framework and the security guides of the Global Interagency Security Forum, at gisf.ngo.
- The Core Humanitarian Standard, at corehumanitarianstandard.org.
- The Sphere Handbook, at spherestandards.org. Sphere indicators are referenced by name and never copied, because the handbook's licence terms are unclear.
- INSARAG guidance for urban search and rescue exercises, at insarag.org.
- The UNHCR Emergency Handbook, at emergency.unhcr.org.

## Before you use it for real

The rules the toolkit follows were checked against IASC and UNHCR pages on 5 November 2026, and the cluster system can change. Have a humanitarian operations lead who has run cluster or NGO coordination review a scenario, and a security manager review any staff security scenario, before you use it with participants. Every product is marked as an exercise product.

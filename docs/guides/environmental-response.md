# Environmental response and oil spills

The environmental response pack gives an exercise a release of oil or a hazardous substance to land or water, and the organization that manages it: the responsible party's incident management team and its qualified individual, the oil spill removal organization, the Coast Guard or EPA federal on-scene coordinator and the state on-scene coordinator in a unified command, and the wildlife agencies, trustees, water utilities and communities downstream. It suits facility, vessel, pipeline and offshore plan holders, response contractors, Area Committees, state spill programs and anyone who needs a spill in an exercise.

Everything here is exercise content. It is not response guidance or legal advice, and the ICS products are modeled on the public forms without being the official forms; use the current Coast Guard or EPA version in a real incident. The responsible party, people and phone numbers are fictional; real geography is fine.

## Domain or toolkit

The pack has two parts, and you can use either one.

- The **Environmental response (oil and hazardous releases)** domain makes the response organization the exercising party: the unified command, its sections, and the resource and sensitive-area decisions that follow a release. Choose it for a PREP incident management team tabletop, an Area exercise or a state spill program exercise. The terms spill management team (the name older plans use), IMT, OSRO, unified command and worst case discharge all lead here.
- The **Spill and plume** toolkit adds the same expertise to any other organization. A port, a chemical plant, a pipeline operator, a power utility, a water utility, a dam owner, a railroad, a transit agency, a farm cooperative, a city or a 911 center can turn it on without choosing the domain. See [Toolkits](toolkits.md). The app proposes it when the brief mentions an oil, fuel or chemical spill, a worst case discharge, boom, skimmers, dispersant, in-situ burn, a pipeline or tank release, a vessel casualty, oiled shoreline or wildlife, a water intake, a toxic plume, unified command, an Area Contingency Plan or an OSRO.

The port, chemical and pipeline domains stay the place for the terminal, the plant or the pipeline control room; this domain picks up when the response organization stands up. The domain also suggests the **Media environment** toolkit. Turn on the **Weather** toolkit for winds and storms, the **Flooding and hydrology** toolkit for river flows, and the **Hazardous materials response** toolkit for protective actions around a chemical plume (see [EMS, fire and hazardous materials](ems-fire.md)).

## Roles and scenario families

The domain's roles are the qualified individual; the federal and state on-scene coordinators and the responsible party incident commander in the unified command; the operations, planning, logistics and finance section chiefs; the environmental unit leader; NOAA's scientific support coordinator; the safety officer; the public information officer at the Joint Information Center; the wildlife branch director; and the OSRO duty manager. The unified command works from the Area Contingency Plan the Area Committee writes; the Area Committee is not part of the unified command.

Eight scenario families follow the same arc (discovery and notification, activation and unified command, trajectory and protection, recovery and demobilization): a vessel casualty with a cargo or fuel release, a facility or tank release, a pipeline release to a river or wetland, an offshore release, a chemical release with a toxic plume, marine firefighting and salvage, a drinking water intake threatened, and wildlife and sensitive shoreline impact.

## What the toolkit knows

- **Notifications.** The person in charge of the vessel or facility reports a release to the National Response Center immediately (33 CFR 153.203). The plan's call-out reaches the qualified individual, the state and the OSRO in the order the plan sets; the law does not make the NRC call wait for the qualified individual. A call to the NRC is a report, not a request for resources: the NRC relays it to the on-scene coordinator and the state, sends no responders and approves nothing.
- **Countermeasures.** Only the federal on-scene coordinator authorizes a dispersant, another chemical agent or an in-situ burn (40 CFR 300.910). Inside a Regional Response Team preauthorization area, and within its conditions on water depth, distance from shore and quantity, no incident-specific concurrence is needed. Outside it, the EPA and state representatives on the Regional Response Team must concur after consulting the Interior and Commerce natural resource trustees. A request states the weathering, wind and distance-from-shore criteria.
- **Closures.** A state agency or local authority closes a fishery or beach and a utility closes its intake; the responsible party does not.
- **Boom.** Boom set square to the current loses oil under it above about 0.7 knot (0.6 to 1 knot depending on the oil and the boom). In faster water it is angled to deflect, and it fails in steep or breaking waves. A boom plan gives the site, length, type, anchoring, who deploys it and when.
- **Trajectories** give a time window for each site, with the model run time, not a single time.
- **Volumes** use one unit per document (barrels, gallons or cubic meters), with the basis of the estimate.
- **Worst case discharge** is a release the plan's scenario defines: a vessel's entire cargo in adverse weather, or a facility's or pipeline segment's plan volume. Call a release one only when the plan's scenario says so. When the worst case discharge volume changes, a pipeline operator changes the response plan at once and submits the change within 30 days (49 CFR 194.121).
- **Wildlife counts** separate oiled live, oiled dead, hazed, in rehabilitation and released.

## Products

Templates for the ICS 202 incident objectives, ICS 204 assignment list, ICS 232 resources at risk summary, ICS 233 open action tracker, ICS 234 work analysis matrix, a unified command meeting agenda, the aerial observation report, the shoreline survey report, the countermeasure request and decision, the wildlife status report, a closure order (fishing, shoreline or water intake), a Joint Information Center claims line notice, the National Response Center report record and the PREP exercise record.

- The **ICS 232** follows the layout of the Coast Guard's ICS 232-CG: the incident location and operational period, a table of environmentally sensitive areas and wildlife concerns, and a separate table of archeo-cultural and socio-economic sites such as intakes, marinas and leases, each with a site number, priority and a narrative that carries the predicted contact and the protection strategy. The environmental unit leader prepares it with input from the trustees.
- The **ICS 234** follows the ICS 234-CG: objectives, the strategies for each and the tactics with who, what, where and when, prepared by the operations section chief before the tactics meeting.
- The **ICS 233** open action tracker keeps generic columns.
- The **aerial observation report** uses the oil appearance terms an observer would use (for example metallic, discontinuous true color and rainbow sheen for diesel).
- The **claims line notice** says a claim goes to the responsible party first, and to the Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund if it is denied or not settled within 90 days.

Two format checks run as the products are written: in a notification record the times do not run backward and an NRC call is not written as a request for resources, and a document never mixes barrels, gallons and cubic meters. The ICS 201, 203, 213 and 214, press releases, field reports, radio calls and social posts come from the templates already in the app. For the exercise record, see [PREP exercises](prep-exercises.md).

## Figures

Figures are drawn from data, never from AI images, each with a text summary, a data table and its source ("Simulated for the exercise" or the model it came from). Layers and zones differ by pattern and label, not only by color.

- **Trajectory map:** where the oil is forecast to be at chosen times, as the best-estimate outline and its uncertainty envelope over the shoreline drawn by ESI class, with the oil's center and the first contact with each shore named.
- **ESI excerpt:** the shoreline classes and resources at risk for an area, numbered in protection priority on the map and listed in a table, the way an ICS 232 reads.
- **Boom plan:** each boom by strategy (containment solid, deflection dashed, exclusion dotted) and state, with the sensitive sites that still have no boom named in the summary.
- **Oil weathering:** the mass balance over time (recovered, remaining on the water, naturally dispersed, evaporated), adding up to the volume released.
- **Plume footprint:** a toxic plume's threat zones by concentration level around the source, with the wind, a scale bar, a north arrow and the places in each zone.

## Simulated and imported data

Every simulated set is labeled **simulated** and repeats exactly from the same inputs.

| You want                                 | Use                   | What you set                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A slick that drifts, weathers and lands  | Simulated oil spill   | Release point and time, product (diesel, light, medium or heavy crude, heavy fuel oil), volume and unit, release duration, wind, current, sea state, recovery rate, shorelines with ESI classes, notifications |
| A toxic cloud with threat zones          | Simulated toxic plume | Source, release rate, wind, stability class, substance and molecular weight, up to four concentration levels                                                                                                   |
| The output of a trajectory model you ran | Trajectory import     | A GeoJSON file from PyGNOME or WebGNOME, the model name, run date and inputs                                                                                                                                   |

The **simulated oil spill** drifts with the current plus a share of the wind (3 percent by default), spreads as the slick thins, and weathers each parcel of oil by its age, with an uncertainty envelope around the best estimate. It gives the landfall time of each shoreline you draw. It also saves two datasets for scoring: when the oil reaches each shoreline and when each notification the plan requires is due, given in minutes after the release. The shorelines become the places the decision forms offer. The weathering limits by product are plausible teaching values, not the output of an oil weathering model; import real model output when the numbers matter.

The **simulated toxic plume** uses the Gaussian plume for a continuous ground-level release with the Briggs open-country dispersion coefficients. It is an exercise illustration, not a forecast and not a protective action distance; the concentration levels are yours to set.

A **trajectory import** keeps, for each output time, the outline of the best estimate and of the uncertainty and the center of the oil, and a note says the app did not compute it. See [Case data](case-data.md) for the file format.

You can also fetch three public NOAA sources once, after you see the size and agree: tides and currents (CO-OPS), buoy observations (National Data Buoy Center) and the Environmental Sensitivity Index shoreline classes for your area. See [Case data](case-data.md).

## Decisions in live play

Players record structured decisions, each with a time:

- **Make notifications:** who was notified and what was said.
- **Activate the OSRO and assign equipment:** the equipment and crews requested and the staging area.
- **Set protection priorities:** a sensitive area, its priority and the strategy.
- **Deploy protective boom:** the site, length in feet, strategy and the time it will be in place.
- **Request a countermeasure:** dispersant, chemical agent or in-situ burn, the zone and the net environmental benefit.
- **Close a water intake**, with the alternate supply, and **Close fishing or a shoreline.**
- **Set a safety zone** for vessels or the public, with its radius.
- **Escalate the IMT** from a local team to a regional or national team.
- **Issue a public statement** through the Joint Information Center.

Four advisor roles can sit with the players: the scientific support coordinator (the trajectory, its uncertainty, weathering and the trade-offs of each countermeasure; advises and never decides), the wildlife branch director, the environmental unit leader and the OSRO duty manager.

## What the evaluation shows

Open the scenario's Evaluation tab and choose **Compute field metrics** after a live session (see [Evaluation metrics](evaluation-metrics.md)). Each measure shows its inputs and caveats.

- **Notification timeliness:** each required notification against a Make notifications decision made in the hour before it was due. A decision after the due time does not count. The measure does not check who was called, so read the notification log beside it.
- **Protective boom before oil arrives:** a Deploy protective boom decision for a shoreline against the time the oil reaches it.
- **Sensitive areas protected before landfall:** boom, an intake closure or a fishery or shoreline closure that names the area, made before the oil arrives. A decision that names no area protects nothing.
- **Message consistency** across the responsible party, the unified command and the Joint Information Center, for the key facts you enter.

The timing measures need a simulated or imported spill in the scenario's case data. For a PREP exercise, mark each objective and core component after the exercise and create the exercise record, as described in [PREP exercises](prep-exercises.md).

## Rules and their caveats

Facts were checked in November 2026. Rules change, so check the current text before you rely on a deadline.

- The PREP Guidelines (2016, version 2016.1) are voluntary, not regulations. They describe an annual incident management team tabletop, quarterly qualified individual notification exercises, a worst case discharge scenario once in each three-year cycle, each countermeasure in the plan exercised during the cycle, and Area exercises on a four-year cycle.
- Facility response plans carry an exercise program (33 CFR 154.1055 for marine transportation-related facilities, 40 CFR 112.21 for facilities under EPA), and following PREP satisfies those sections. How long records are kept depends on the plan type; see [PREP exercises](prep-exercises.md).
- The preauthorization criteria in the sample products (at least 3 nautical miles from shore and 10 m deep) are written as the exercise area plan's. Each Regional Response Team sets its own.
- No order is enforced between the NRC report and the qualified individual's call. Plans differ, and the regulation sets none.
- The app gives no legal advice; your plan and your regulator decide what satisfies the plan.

## Tips

- Start with the call. A release discovered at night, a person in charge who must call the NRC, and a qualified individual who cannot be reached test the notification chain before anything else.
- Put the trajectory and the ESI excerpt on the screen together. The protection priority decision is where the environmental unit leader, the trustees and the OSRO disagree in useful ways.
- Script a countermeasure request outside the preauthorization zone. Whether the request goes to the Regional Response Team, and what the scientific support coordinator advises, is a strong discussion point.
- A diesel spill behaves differently from a crude or heavy fuel spill: much of it evaporates and disperses naturally, and dispersant in shallow water by a marsh is a poor trade. Match the product to the story.
- Real places, real agencies and real rivers are fine. Keep the responsible party, the facility and the people fictional.

## Limits

- The simulated spill and plume are illustrations with simple published relations. They are not oil spill or air dispersion models and teach no response tactics.
- Live data is never fetched during a session.
- The ICS products are exercise products, not the official forms.

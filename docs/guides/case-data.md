# Case data

Case data is information about an event that a scenario carries with it: readings over time,
areas on a map, images, text products such as advisories, and reports. Injects can show it, so an
inject at 1:15 into the exercise shows what the data said at the matching moment. It is optional.
A scenario without case data works as before.

## Historical and simulated

Every dataset is labeled one of two ways, and the label appears wherever the data does: on the
injects that show it, on the Facilitate page and in the notes the generator receives.

- **Historical** data is a record of something that really happened. It names its source, the
  date it was retrieved, the terms it was published under and, when it was cropped or otherwise
  changed, what was done to it. Historical data is never altered without saying so.
- **Simulated** data was generated by the app from parameters you gave, for example a storm track
  and an intensity. It is never presented as a record of a real event and has no retrieval date.

A simulated oil spill also saves two datasets for scoring: when the oil reaches each shoreline, and the times the plan requires each notification. The toolkit's evaluation measures read them, and the shorelines become the places the decision forms offer.

A model run made outside the app counts as simulated too. The spill and plume toolkit can import the output of a trajectory model such as PyGNOME or WebGNOME: a GeoJSON file of points with a time on each (PyGNOME's `current_time`, or a `time` property in ISO form). Points marked `UNCERTAIN` form the uncertainty cloud. For each output time the app keeps the outline of the best estimate and of the uncertainty, and the center of the oil, active until the next output time. The file is read as data only, up to 8 MB and 200,000 points. You give the model name, the run date and the inputs the run used; they are saved in a note beside the trajectory, which says the app did not compute it.

The generator is told which is which. It treats historical data as history, not as current
conditions, keeps its dates, places and values as given, and never calls simulated data real.

## Fetching a real past event

A toolkit can offer to find a real past event by date and place. Open the scenario's **Case data** tab. It lists the datasets the scenario already carries, each labeled historical or simulated with its source, retrieval date and size, and lets you remove one. Fetching happens once:

1. Under **Fetch a real past event**, choose a source. The list holds the archives the scenario's toolkits name; turn a toolkit on under **Toolkits** to add its sources.
2. Give the period (in UTC), the area and the source's own settings, such as a station number, a two-letter state code or a three-letter country code. Each says what it expects, and the form tells you what is missing.
3. Choose **Check size and terms**. The app shows each dataset it would fetch, its size, the room left in the scenario, the archive it would contact and the terms the data is published under. Nothing has been requested yet.
4. Choose **Agree and fetch**, and only then does the app contact that archive. Most archives need no account or key, and nothing about your plans is sent. The request says only which app is asking.
5. The data is saved in the scenario. From then on the scenario works offline, including during
   the exercise.

Each request is recorded in a fetch log in the scenario's case folder, with the address (without
its query), the status and the number of bytes.

### Sources for oil and chemical spills

The spill and plume toolkit can fetch three public NOAA sources, each from one fixed address:

- **Tides and currents** (CO-OPS): water levels, winds and tidal currents at a station for up to about three months, in metric units and UTC.
- **Buoy observations** (National Data Buoy Center): winds, air pressure and wave height at a buoy or coastal station, for a past year or the last 44 days. Readings the buoy did not report stay missing.
- **Shoreline classes** (Environmental Sensitivity Index): the shoreline types of one ESI region, cropped to your area, which must be under half a square degree. The saved lines keep only the shore class and shore type, and the data says it was cropped.

Each shows its size before anything is requested, and each is saved as historical data with its source and the date you fetched it.

### Keys for archives that need one

Most sources need no account. The FBI Crime Data Explorer, used by the justice toolkit, needs a free api.data.gov key. Save it under **Settings, Case data**; it is stored in your computer's keychain, never in the project, and sent only to that archive when you agree to a fetch. Without it that source refuses to fetch.

### Size limit

One scenario may hold up to 500 MB of case data by default. Change the limit under **Settings,
Case data**. A fetch that would not fit is refused before anything is requested, and the size
shown in step 2 tells you in advance. Images are cropped to your area and times.

## Exercise clock and case clock

A scenario with case data maps exercise time to case time. Set it under **Case clock** on the
**Case data** tab:

- a **start offset**: how far into the event the case data stands at the start of the exercise;
- a **speed**: case minutes per exercise minute. At 1 the case clock keeps pace with the
  exercise clock; at 4, one exercise minute shows four minutes of the event;
- the scenario's **time jumps**: when the exercise clock reaches a time jump, the case clock skips
  ahead by the amount set for that jump.

The Facilitate page shows both clocks. The case clock is labeled historical, simulated or both.

## Using case data in an inject

An inject field can point at a dataset instead of holding text. Give the dataset and either a
case time, an offset in minutes from the start of the dataset, or nothing: with nothing, the
inject's own exercise time is turned into case time. When the inject is rendered the app looks
up the value at that moment, such as the last reading at or before it, the warning areas in force
or the latest image, and prints the historical or simulated label on the document. If the data
has nothing at or before that moment the render stops with a message rather than inventing a
value.

## What stays on your computer

Case data is stored inside the scenario folder and travels with the project. Files are written
only there and only as data (JSON, GeoJSON, PNG, WebP, text). An archive address is fixed in the
app for each source and is never taken from a pack or from text you paste.

# Nursing and clinical care

The nursing pack gives an exercise the work of the nurses who run a unit in an emergency and the programs that teach them: charge nurses and nurse managers, the house supervisor, staff nurses, rapid response nurses, infection prevention, nurse educators, long-term care nursing staff, and prelicensure and graduate nursing programs with a simulation lab. It plays two ways from one library of synthetic patients:

- **Tabletop play:** a facilitated group exercise with injects, decisions and a hot wash, such as a unit surge, an evacuation, an outbreak or a downtime shift.
- **Unfolding case studies:** one patient's story revealed in stages. At each stage the learner sees new data (vital signs, labs, a family concern) and answers questions for each step of clinical judgment.

Everything here is exercise content, not clinical guidance, a drug reference or decision support for real patients. Every patient, hospital, unit and facility is fictional. The app writes its own questions and never reproduces licensure exam items.

## Domain or toolkit

The pack is included with the app and has two parts, and you can use either one.

- The **Nursing** domain is the unit-level and classroom view of a hospital. It repeats what a hospital exercise inherits from the healthcare domain, so a project can run on either, and adds three role sets: a **hospital nursing unit** (chief nursing officer, nurse manager, charge nurse, house supervisor, staff nurse, LPN and nursing assistant, rapid response nurse, infection prevention nurse, nurse informaticist), a **long-term care facility** (administrator, director of nursing, wing charge nurse, maintenance director) and a **simulation lab** (clinical instructor, simulation operator, learner team lead, program director, clinical placement coordinator). Choose it when the people you are exercising are nurses or nursing students.
- The **Clinical care** toolkit adds bedside care to any other organization. A hospital command center exercise, a health informatics downtime, a pharmacy, a health department, a campus or school health office, a stadium's first aid station or a commercial facility can turn it on without choosing the nursing domain. See [Toolkits](toolkits.md). The app proposes it when the brief mentions a nursing unit, a charge nurse, rapid response, a deteriorating patient, triage, a mass casualty incident, an early warning score, SBAR, a medication error, isolation, an evacuation, long-term care or a case study.

The nursing domain also suggests the **Media environment** and **Pharmacy and drug supply** toolkits. For a downtime shift, turn on the **Clinical systems** toolkit as well (see [Health informatics and clinical IT](health-informatics.md)): it brings the downtime declaration, the paper forms and the restore decisions. For triage in the field rather than on the unit, the **Mass casualty and triage** toolkit (see [EMS, fire and hazardous materials](ems-fire.md)) uses the same START and SALT tags.

## Scenario families

Nine families come with the domain, each with its own arc of phases:

- **Unit surge and mass casualty:** notification, unit readiness, arrivals and triage, sustained care, decompression.
- **Unit evacuation, horizontal and vertical:** warning and decision, patient priority and preparation, movement, accounting and handoff.
- **Outbreak on a unit:** a cluster, recognition and isolation, staffing and spread, notification.
- **Medication error and disclosure.**
- **Staffing crisis and agency nurses.**
- **Active threat or violence on a unit.**
- **Downtime care with paper charts.**
- **Heat and power loss in long-term care:** outage, resident checks, shelter or move, outcomes and notification.
- **Clinical event (case study):** presentation, first assessment, change in condition, escalation, treatment and reassessment, handoff and debrief.

## What the toolkit knows

- A vital-signs entry has a time, heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation (on room air or a stated oxygen flow), temperature with its units and level of consciousness. Values stay within human ranges and change gradually unless an event explains a jump, and vital signs, labs and findings tell one coherent story.
- A lab result shows the value, units, reference range and a flag (H, L or critical) that agrees with the range; a critical value records who was called, when and the read-back.
- An **SBAR** handoff has all four elements (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) and a read-back. When a case deliberately leaves one out, the product shows the gap rather than hiding it.
- **Triage** follows the findings under the stated method. Under START a casualty who walks is minor; one who breathes only after repositioning, has no radial pulse or cannot follow commands is immediate. Expectant is a resource decision and is stated as one.
- A nurse does not diagnose or prescribe. Rapid response is called for deterioration, a code for arrest.
- **Doses come from you.** When the scenario gives no dose, products read "dose as ordered" and are flagged for you to fill in. The writer never supplies a dose range from memory.
- Ratios, beds, census, arrivals and discharges add up across injects. The safe nurse-to-patient ratio depends on acuity, unit type and state law.
- Content involving patient death, violence or harm to children carries a content notice and an opt-out for learners.

## Products

Templates for the clinical lab results panel, the medication administration record, the SBAR handoff with read-back, the rapid response call record, the triage tag (START or SALT), the staffing grid and assignment sheet, the isolation and cohort board, the evacuation priority list and tracking sheet, and the patient event report with disclosure notes. For simulation teaching there are the simulation prebrief, a facilitation guide with cue cards, the debriefing guide, a learner feedback form, a learner report, a facilitator summary of class patterns and the simulation program record.

The products are checked as they are written: lab flags must agree with the ranges shown, an SBAR must have its four elements and its read-back, a dose of a few common drugs may not exceed a generous single-dose ceiling or use a route that does not exist for the drug (a guard against invented doses, not a drug reference), a triage category must agree with the findings, staffing numbers must add up, and an evacuation list may not list a patient twice.

## Figures

Clinical figures are drawn from data, never from AI images, each with a text summary and a data table:

- **Bedside monitor** and **vital-signs flowsheet** with trends over hours.
- **Early warning score:** each parameter with its points, the total, the trigger band and the recommended response, with the arithmetic done from the configuration. The app ships a neutral score whose bands are its own and are not a published or validated score; the chart prints its source. A named published score is used only when its terms allow, with its source cited.
- **Lab trend** with the reference range.
- **Staffing against demand**, **unit census** and **evacuation progress**.

## Simulated patients

The **simulated patient trajectory** generator draws a patient's vital signs and labs over 30 minutes to 8 hours, at a step of 5 to 60 minutes, from a named course: sepsis, hemorrhage, respiratory failure, a cardiac event, post-operative deterioration or hypoglycemia. You can schedule what the nurse does (reassess, escalate, treat or delay) at given minutes, and the course changes with it. The patient's name and details come from the seed, and the same seed gives the same patient. Everything is labeled **simulated**; it is a teaching course, not a physiologic model.

## Unfolding case studies

Open **Case studies** in the project.

1. **Make a draft.** Choose a story (sepsis after surgery, bleeding after surgery, worsening breathing, chest pain and a cardiac event, post-operative deterioration, or low blood sugar on insulin) and a seed, and add a content notice if the story involves death, violence or harm to children. The draft has six stages with questions for each step of the NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model: recognize cues, analyze cues, prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions, take action and evaluate outcomes. You can also import a case study file; the text is checked for direct identifiers such as phone numbers, record numbers, dates of birth and addresses before anything is saved.
2. **Review it.** Use **Preview as the author** to take the case, then **Record my review** with your name once you have read the stages, questions, keys and rationales. Only a reviewed case can be played; editing it later returns it to draft.
3. **Play it.** A learner reads the notices, says whether they are taking the case alone or as a small group with one answer, and starts. Each stage shows only what a nurse would know at that point. After each answer the learner sees a score for the question, the rationale and the strongest answer, and at the end a profile across the six steps.
4. **Run it with a class.** Start a live session for a scenario first so devices can join, then choose **Run with a class**. Send each stage to the players' devices, watch how many have answered, close the stage and show the room's answers. Detail appears only once enough devices have answered, so no one learner is singled out.

Each case also gives a prebrief, a facilitation guide, a debrief guide, a feedback form and a class summary as PDFs.

Scores use the app's own scale for practice. They are not NCLEX scores and make no claim of equivalence to them. A learner's answers stay on the computer unless the instructor runs a connected class session.

The **Education record** on the same page records, for each session, that the four cornerstones of simulation practice were done and by whom (prebriefing, facilitation, professional integrity, debriefing), with the date, facilitator, participants and simulation hours, and writes a program record as a PDF. Hours are counted for the program's own use: whether simulation counts toward clinical requirements is set by the program and its state board of nursing. NCSBN's guidelines allow programs to use simulation for up to half of clinical experience, and state boards set their own rules.

## Decisions in live play

Players record structured decisions, each with a time:

- **Assign nurse** to a patient, with the resulting patients per nurse.
- **Call rapid response** for a patient, with the trigger and whether SBAR was used.
- **Escalate staffing shortage:** to whom, how many nurses short and what is asked for.
- **Set evacuation order:** the patients ranked first to last, the reasons, horizontal, vertical or both, and the start time.
- **Triage arrival:** the category, the method and the findings.
- **Isolate or cohort** patients, with the precautions and the area.
- **Request transfer** to another unit or hospital, with the level of care needed.
- **Disclose event:** the disclosure lead, to whom, the time of the conversation and what is said.
- **Notify** a person or agency.

Five advisor roles can sit with the players: a clinical educator, a rapid response team lead, a physician advisor who plays the covering provider, an infection preventionist and a simulation facilitator.

## What the evaluation shows

Open the scenario's Evaluation tab and choose **Compute field metrics** after a live session (see [Evaluation metrics](evaluation-metrics.md)). Each measure shows its inputs and what it cannot tell, and says n/a when nothing was recorded.

- **Clinical judgment profile:** the case study runs scored by step of clinical judgment, on the app's own scale.
- **Handoff quality:** how complete each rapid response call was, from what the caller recorded on the form. The report itself is not read, and read-back is not scored here.
- **Time to escalation:** minutes from the first abnormal early warning score to the rapid response call (target 15 minutes), and from a critical lab to the provider notification.
- **Triage accuracy:** under-triage and over-triage against the category the scenario scripted for each victim. It shows agreement with the script, not clinical truth.
- **Evacuation order against the priority list:** the first three patients against a scripted list with reasons. A different order with sound reasons is a discussion point, not an error.
- **Staffing ratio exceedances:** assignments over the target ratio (5 patients per nurse by default, a medical-surgical figure) and the minutes to an escalation. Set your own number for an ICU, step-down unit or long-term care.
- **Infection control decisions:** the action and precautions against the scripted organism, and the minutes from recognition to the decision.
- **Disclosure timing:** minutes from a harm event to the disclosure conversation, against 60 minutes. The AHRQ CANDOR toolkit describes an initial conversation within an hour as a goal, not a rule.
- **Outage to downtime declaration:** minutes from the outage to the declaration, against 30 minutes. It covers recognition and escalation only; the time from the declaration to every unit on paper is a separate measure in the clinical systems toolkit.

The measures that compare decisions with scripted events (an abnormal score, a critical lab, a triage victim, an infection case, a harm event, an outage) read those events from the scenario's case data. When a scenario does not carry them, the measure says so and the others still run. Patients are matched by the name typed on the form, so use the names as they appear on the scenario's lists.

For evaluation guides, the toolkit names critical tasks a nurse can be observed doing, grouped under core capabilities: assign beds, nurses and supplies and keep ratios within the plan; triage and reassess casualties; isolate and cohort and notify public health; escalate a staffing shortage with a number and a request; give an SBAR report with read-back and notify the provider of a critical result; reassess vital signs and the score at the stated interval and keep the census current.

## Requirements

Hospitals must exercise the emergency plan at least twice a year (42 CFR 482.15(d)(2)), and the plan must include a system to track the location of on-duty staff and sheltered patients (42 CFR 482.15(b)). Long-term care facilities follow the parallel rule (42 CFR 483.73). The Joint Commission's exercise standard asks for two exercises a year as well. The app does not decide whether an exercise satisfies a requirement; your compliance office does.

## Tips

- Plan the prebrief and the debrief as parts of the session, not extras. The prebrief sets objectives, ground rules and a psychologically safe room; the debrief is where the learning is.
- For a downtime exercise, run both downtime measures. The after-action report can then say whether the delay was in recognizing the outage or in getting the procedures to the units.
- Give the evacuation priority list reasons, not only an order. Ventilators, infusions, bariatric patients and infants change what goes with each patient and how many staff it takes.
- Hand hygiene for C. difficile is soap and water; alcohol rub does not kill the spores. Small realism details like this are what nurses notice first.
- Have a nurse educator or a nurse leader read the scenario and any case study before learners use it.

## Limits

- Patients, trajectories and scores are teaching material, not calibrated to any population, and not a clinical reference.
- The CNO is labeled Operations Section Chief, which is common under the Hospital Incident Command System but not fixed; rename the role to match your plan.
- Nothing connects to a real EHR, monitor, staffing system or student record system.
